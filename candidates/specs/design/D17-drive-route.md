# D17-drive-route

The routing client: the one piece of candidates that asks Google's mapping services for the drive from the base to a company's record-chosen street address, for the `drive_route` kind of enrichment (drive route decisions 1 to 6). This document owns the package `internal/routes` whole: the client and its seam, the one call `Drive`, the outcome it answers, the precision words, the fail and why words, the per-request timeout, and how a call is classified as routed, nothing routable or failed, with what it counts and costs. What the kind names as its inputs, its subject, its found value and its `Ready` are `D16-enrichers`' (`enrichers.DriveRoute`, `enrichers.RouteFound`, `enrichers.Destination`); the targeted pass that runs it, its refusals, its bound on calls in flight and its counts are `D06-passes`'; the setting that names the origin, the key and how `cli.Run` builds the client over `Process.HTTP` and `Process.After` are `D01-layout-and-run-seam`'s; the manifest's `secrets` line is `D02-cli`'s; how a route result reaches the facts row's minutes, miles and route address is `D04-store`'s **drive decision**; the `enrich` and `candidate` tools are `D10-tools`'; the candidate page's view of a route result is `D08-pages`'. `GOOGLE_MAPS_API_KEY` also authorizes the walk's Places requests (walk outcome 11).

"Drive route decision N" is outcome N of the decisions document of the exact drive time change. "Drive route links outcome N" is outcome N of the decisions document of the drive route links change, and "links log DN" entry DN of the decisions log of its delivery.

## The call

`routes.Client` holds the `*http.Client` every request goes through, the API key, and the `After` every request's timeout waits on. `Drive(ctx, origin, destination)` takes the origin as the setting gives it and the destination as `enrichers.Destination` builds it from the subject (street, city, state and ZIP as one string), and answers one `routes.Outcome`. The subject's address is the candidate's record-chosen address: the address order applied to the candidate's records with the routed-address slot skipped (`D05-sources`, `D12-ingest`), not the display address, which a route result may itself supply; so the drive never asks for the address it answered, and a route result moves the display address without making the route due again. Which address that is, is `D16-enrichers`' and `D12-ingest`'s; `Drive` sees only the string. It never returns an error: a request that fails is part of the outcome, since the pass records it as a failed result (drive route decision 1) and counts it.

A call makes at most two requests, one after the other. The **geocoding request** asks Google's Geocoding API to place the destination string, which answers the address it resolved the string to, how precisely it placed it, and the place's coordinates. When the destination is placed closer than the town, the **routing request** asks the Routes API for the drive by car, without live traffic, from the origin string to those coordinates, asking for the duration and the distance and nothing else (drive route decision 4). The origin is sent as the string the setting holds and is never geocoded by a request of its own.

## Routed, nothing routable, failed

A call ends one of three ways (drive route decision 5).

- **Routed**: both requests answered and a route came back. The outcome holds the minutes, the route's duration rounded to whole minutes; the miles, its distance in meters divided by 1,609.344 and rounded to one decimal; the resolved address; the precision, `building` or `street`; and the service's status.
- **Nothing routable**: the service answered, but there is nothing to drive to. The geocoding answer placed nothing (`WhyNotPlaced`), or placed the destination no closer than the town (`WhyTooCoarse`), and then no routing request is made; or the routing answer held no route (`WhyNoRoute`). The outcome has no minutes and no miles and says why, with whatever address and precision the placement gave, and its error is empty: it is an answer, not a failure, and the ZIP estimate keeps deciding (`D04-store`).
- **Failed**: a request did not complete or its answer was not what was expected. The transport returned an error (`FailTransport`), the request's timeout fired (`FailTimeout`), the call's context ended while it was in flight (`FailCanceled`), the answer's HTTP status was not 200 (`FailStatus`), or its body was not the shape expected (`FailShape`). No further request is made; a failure of the routing request keeps the address and precision the placement gave.

The precision words `routes.PrecisionBuilding`, `PrecisionStreet` and `PrecisionTown` are contract values, stored in the found value and checked by its `Valid` (`D16-enrichers`); "town" stands for every placement no closer than the town. The fail and why words are copy constants: a person reads them on the candidate page and an agent in the `candidate` tool's answer, and no program parses them.

## Counts and cost

`Sent` is the number of requests the call made, `Failed` 1 when one of them failed and 0 otherwise, and `CostNanos` the cost of the call in nano-dollars. A request is charged only when the service answered it and it did not fail: a request that got no response, by a transport error, a timeout or a cancel, costs nothing, and so does one the service refused or answered in a shape not expected. A geocoding request that placed the destination or placed nothing is charged `GeocodePriceNanos`, and a routing request answered with a route or with nothing routable `RoutePriceNanos`, so a routed call costs both, a placement too coarse to route only the first. The pass sums `Sent` into its runs, `Failed` into its failed runs and `CostNanos` into its cost (`D06-passes`), so both requests show in the counts.

## The timer

Each request waits at most `routes.Timeout` (10 seconds), measured on the client's `After`, called once with `Timeout` as each request begins, as `website.Checker` measures `website.Timeout` (`D15-website-check`). A test hands in an `After` whose channels it holds and delivers on one to make a request time out, so no test waits on the clock. A context already done when `Drive` is called makes no request and no call of `After`.

## The key stays out of the record

The outcome's error and why are always one of the copy constants or empty, never the transport's own text. `net/http`'s client wraps a transport failure in a `*url.Error` that quotes the request's URL, and the geocoding request carries the key in its query; the stored found value, the candidate page, the `candidate` tool's answer and the trail would all show it. So, unlike the website check's error, which keeps the transport's text, a route's error is a word, and the cause is not kept.

## On the wire

The routing request is a `POST` to `routes.RoutesURL` carrying the key in the `X-Goog-Api-Key` header, the field mask `routes.duration,routes.distanceMeters` in `X-Goog-FieldMask`, and a JSON body naming the origin as an address, the destination as a latitude and longitude, `DRIVE` and `TRAFFIC_UNAWARE`; the key is never in its URL. A route comes back as a 200 whose body's `routes` array holds the route's `distanceMeters` and its `duration` as seconds followed by `s`, such as `4635s`. A drive the service cannot compute, to Honolulu from Sandstone or from an origin it cannot read, comes back as a 200 whose body is the empty object, which is nothing routable. A request the service refuses comes back with a status other than 200 and a body whose `error` object carries the service's `status` word, such as `INVALID_ARGUMENT`, which the outcome keeps in `Status` beside `FailStatus`. Each of these was observed by a live request while drafting. The Essentials price of a Compute Routes request, $5.00 per thousand at the first paid tier of Google's published global pricing list, is `routes.RoutePriceNanos`.

The geocoding request is a `GET` of `routes.GeocodeURL` whose query carries the destination string as `address` and the key as `key`. Its answer is a JSON object with a `status` word. `OK` comes with `results`, whose first element gives the resolved address as `formatted_address` and, under `geometry`, the coordinates as `location`'s `lat` and `lng` and the precision as `location_type`: `ROOFTOP` for a building (the Minnesota State Capitol's address), `RANGE_INTERPOLATED` for a number placed along a street (a rural County Highway address), `GEOMETRIC_CENTER` for a street with no number (Main St in Hinckley), and `APPROXIMATE` for a town (Pine City, MN). `ZERO_RESULTS` means it placed nothing (a nonsense string). Any other word is a refusal: `REQUEST_DENIED` came with a 200 for a key the project had not enabled the API for, and again for an invalid key; `INVALID_REQUEST` came with a 400 for a request with no address; each with an `error_message`, which is not kept. A refusal fails the call with `FailStatus` and keeps the word in `Status`. Each of these was observed by a live request while drafting. Geocoding Essentials is $5.00 per thousand at the first paid tier of the same price list, `routes.GeocodePriceNanos`.

## Map links

A routed drive carries two links the owner can open: the **place link**, which opens Google Maps on the address the service resolved the destination to, and the **directions link**, which opens Google Maps driving directions from the origin the route was measured from to that address (drive route links outcomes 1 and 2). `routes.PlaceURL` and `routes.DirectionsURL` build them from the two strings a route result already holds and the origin, each time a page or a tool shows them: they make no request, need no key, and nothing they return is kept. They use Google's Maps URLs, the documented, key-free form (`https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/urls/get-started`: search is `https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1` with a required `query`, directions `https://www.google.com/maps/dir/?api=1` with `origin`, `destination` and `travelmode`, one of whose values is `driving`; values must be encoded, a space as `+` or `%20` and a comma as `%2C`), each value escaped as `url.QueryEscape` escapes it, which writes a space as `+` and a comma as `%2C` (links log D3). An empty address, or an empty origin, gives no link, so a caller that passes what it holds never shows a link to nowhere.

## Concurrency

The pass runs up to `passes.RoutesInFlight` calls at once on one client (`D06-passes`), so `Drive` is safe for concurrent use and keeps no state between calls. Two clients with the same key, handed the same arguments and answered alike, send byte-identical requests, so a test of `cli.Run` can compare what `Run`'s client sent with what a client of its own sends (`D01-layout-and-run-seam`).

## Known limits

- **A coarse placement is not retried.** An address placed only to the town gets no route, and the ZIP estimate stands until its street, city, state, ZIP or the origin changes; `force` asks again but will get the same answer.
- **The origin is not checked.** An origin the routing service cannot read gives every candidate a failed result or no route, and the owner sees it on the pass's failed count and on the cards.
- **No cap on an answer's body.** An answer is read whole; the routing answer is field-masked to two numbers and the geocoding answer is small, so no cap is declared, and a misbehaving answer is bounded only by `Timeout`.
- **Only a building supplies the address.** A routed result whose precision is `PrecisionStreet` gives minutes and miles like one at `PrecisionBuilding`, but only a result at `PrecisionBuilding` may supply the candidate's address through the routed-address slot (`D12-ingest`); the precision words are this document's (R-N37S-S93I), and an interpolated street placement is too loose to stand as the company's address.
- **The precision is the geocoder's.** A building-level placement of the wrong building routes to it, and an interpolated one may land in the next town (a Sandstone County Highway address resolved to Finlayson); the resolved address is shown beside the minutes so the owner can see.

## Recorded decisions

- **Departure: two requests per candidate, not one** (drive route decision 4 says one request asks for the drive). The result must hold the resolved address and how precisely it was placed (decision 5), and a town-level placement must count as nothing routable, not as a route to the town's centre. A geocoding request answers both directly, and stops before the paid routing request when the placement is too coarse; the routing request then goes to coordinates. Both requests are counted and charged on the pass. Decided by the delivery.
- **The origin is sent as a string**, never geocoded: the setting is one string the owner wrote, the routing service places it itself, and a request of our own for it would double its cost for nothing kept.
- **The error is a word, not the transport's text**: see above. A transport failure's cause is lost; the cost is that a failed route says only which way it failed.
- **One package owns the client**, `internal/routes`, beside `internal/website`: the HTTP and the vendor's shapes stay out of `enrichers`, which wraps the client as it wraps the checker (`D16-enrichers`), and out of `store`.
- **The timeout reuses `Process.After`** (as `website.Timeout` does) rather than a new seam. `routes.Timeout` equals `website.Timeout`, so a test tells a route request's wait from a website fetch's by the work it started, not by the duration; a targeted pass of `drive_route` makes no website fetch.
- **No loopback stub.** Under `IKIGENBA_SANDBOX` the real client is used over `Process.HTTP`, since `drive_route` runs only when the owner asks, and the one sandbox check the finish line names is a real request (decision 6). Tests reach Google never: they hand the client an `*http.Client` over a transport of their own. The walk departs from this: its Places client searches an in-process fake under `IKIGENBA_SANDBOX` (`D19-walk`, walk decision 3).
- **A refused or misshapen answer is not charged**, a clarification of the sheet's rule that only answered requests are charged: Google's billing page does not say whether an error answer is billed, and charging the price for a refusal would overstate a pass's cost whenever the key or the request is wrong. An answer of nothing routable is charged, since the service did the work.
- **`GEOMETRIC_CENTER` is a street.** Google also answers it for the centre of an area such as a park, but a town comes back `APPROXIMATE`, and a street is the case a company's address meets; so it maps to `PrecisionStreet` and is routed. A `location_type` other than the four is a shape not expected, and fails the call with `FailShape`.
- **Every number taken from Google is bounded, and one out of bounds is a shape not expected.** A number the answers carry becomes an `int64`, a `float64` or a member of a JSON body, and one far beyond any real value would overflow the first, turn the second into an infinity that `json.Marshal` refuses, or ask the routing service for a place that is not on the globe. So each is read with `strconv.ParseFloat` at the bit size 64, and one for which it returns an error, or a number outside its bounds, fails the call with `FailShape`, rather than being saturated, wrapped or passed on: a route's duration not above 1e9 seconds, so its minutes fit an `int64` (R-CDXU-VBB3); its distance a whole number of meters not below 0 and not above 1e9, so its miles, at most 621,371.2, are finite and marshal (R-CDXU-VBB3); a place's latitude within -90 to 90 and its longitude within -180 to 180, the ranges a coordinate has, so the routing request's body always marshals and names a point on the globe (R-CHLK-0MJ6). The other values the answers carry are strings, which always marshal, and the counts and costs are the client's own, at most 2 requests and 10,000,000 nano-dollars a call. R-CHLK-0MJ6 is stated beside R-7JFG-FDWG and in place of its rule for the coordinates, as `D04-store` layers the drive decision over the facts projection, since re-minting R-7JFG-FDWG would re-mint every requirement citing it in `D16-enrichers` and `D06-passes` without changing what any of them asks; R-7JFG-FDWG's own cases, all within the bounds, read the same under both.
- **The first result is the place.** When the geocoder answers several results, as it did for the rural address, the first is taken and the rest ignored, and `partial_match` is not read; the resolved address shown beside the minutes is how the owner sees what was routed to.
- **Cost is the list price.** `CostNanos` prices each request at the first paid tier and ignores the free monthly usage Google grants, which candidates cannot see; the pass's cost is an upper bound.
- **Nil `After` means `time.After`**, as for `website.Checker`. It is declared with the field in R-N6VH-XKBL, following that precedent, and no test proves it, since proving it would wait on the real clock; every test hands in an `After` of its own. A nil `HTTP` makes no request and fails the call, since `enrichers.DriveRoute`'s `Ready` keeps a pass from calling it.
- **The links are built, never stored** (drive route links outcome 2 and its rejected list): the route result is unchanged, so a change in how Google's links are made is a release, not a pass over every result. They live in `internal/routes`, beside the client, since both are Google's mapping services' shapes; the pages and the tools call them (`D08-pages`, `D10-tools`).
- **The origin of a link is the route's, read through its inputs** (links log D1 and D8): a route result does not hold its origin, only the fingerprint of the address and the origin in its inputs (`D16-enrichers`). A result whose inputs equal its candidate's inputs now was measured from the store's origin now, so only such a result, and the card's deciding route, which is one by the drive decision (`D04-store`), gets a directions link from that origin; an older result whose address or origin has since changed keeps its place link and has no directions link, since its origin cannot be known.

## Consumer tasks

`cli.Run` builds `&routes.Client{HTTP: p.HTTP, Key: s.MapsAPIKey, After: p.After}` when the key is not empty and hands it to `passes.Config.Routes`, which the engine hands to `enrichers.Config.Routes` (`D01-layout-and-run-seam`, `D06-passes`). `enrichers.DriveRoute{Routes: c}.Route(ctx, s)` calls `o := c.Drive(ctx, s.Origin, enrichers.Destination(s))` and copies `o.Minutes`, `o.Miles`, `o.Address`, `o.Precision`, `o.Status`, `o.Why`, `o.Error` and `o.CostNanos` into a `RouteFound`, and `o.Sent`, `o.Failed` and `o.CostNanos` into the `Tally` (`D16-enrichers`). The candidate page and the `candidate` tool call `routes.PlaceURL(a)` and `routes.DirectionsURL(o, a)`, `a` a routed result's resolved address and `o` the store's origin (`D08-pages`, `D10-tools`).

A test of the client: `c := &routes.Client{HTTP: &http.Client{Transport: rt}, Key: "test-key", After: after}`, `rt` a `RoundTripper` of its own that records each request and answers, fails or holds it, `after` handing out channels it holds; `o := c.Drive(ctx, "Sandstone, MN", "1 Main St, Hinckley, MN 55037")`, compared field by field, `o.Error` with `routes.FailTransport` and its siblings and `o.Why` with `routes.WhyNotPlaced` and its siblings.
