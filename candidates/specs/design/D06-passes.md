# D06-passes

The engine: `internal/passes`, the one place a pass is started, worked, finished, settled at start and abandoned at the stop, and the place an event delivered on the bus becomes a pass. A pass is one of five kinds (`D04-store` records its kind and its origin). An **ingest** pass, started by a `scrape.finished` delivered on the bus, reads the run's rows from scrapes over the socket through appkit's `scrapes` client, hands them to the store a batch at a time to be resolved (`store.Attach`, `D12-resolution`) and marks gone the links the run no longer holds (`store.MarkGone`, `D12-resolution`); it judges nothing. A **judge** pass, started by a `cron.judge_candidates.fired` delivered on the bus, judges every active candidate from scratch (`store.JudgeAll`, `D13-scoring`) and reads nothing from scrapes. A **rebuild** pass, started by a `rebuild` call (`D10-tools`), ingests every registered scraper's current run, then records a fresh result of every derived kind for every active candidate (`store.Derive` forced, `D04-store`, `D16-enrichers`), and then judges every active candidate, as one pass; it fetches nothing. An **enrich** pass, started by an `enrich` call with no target (`D10-tools`) or by an ingest pass that ended `done`, walks the enrichers' registry (`D16-enrichers`) over the whole corpus: the one kind the call named, or every kind in registry order but those run through prompts, the derived kinds (`drive_minutes`, `link_facts`) first and then the fetched one (`website_check`, `D15-website-check`) when its checker is ready; for each kind it records a result for every candidate due for it, due meaning that the candidate's inputs for the kind are not empty and differ from those of its newest result of the kind, or it has none. An **enrich_cohort** pass, started by an `enrich` call with a target, one cohort or one candidate, walks the one kind the call named over the target's candidates alone: those due for it or, with `force`, every one whose inputs are not empty. For `site_text` (`D16-enrichers`) that is one run of the caller's `site-text` prompt per candidate in the prompts app, through appkit's `prompts` client, at most `RunsInFlight` at once; the agent in the run keeps pages on the company through `add_page` (`D10-tools`), and when the run ends the pass records a result saying how it ended and what it cost. Neither enrich kind reads anything from scrapes, and each writes nothing but results and the facts rows beside them. An enrich of either kind that wrote at least one derived or website result starts a judge, unless a judge is already running, so the new facts reach the opinions without waiting for the morning; a `site_text` pass writes neither and starts none. Whatever starts it, the engine records the pass `running` (`D04-store`), tells `pass.started` (`D07-events`), answers at once, works it in the background, and finishes it `done` with its nineteen counts or `failed` with its reason, telling `pass.finished`. One rule says what runs together: a pass is refused `busy` only while another pass of the same kind over the same scope is running, the scope being an ingest's run, an `enrich_cohort`'s target, and the corpus for a judge, a rebuild and an enrich (`D04-store`); every other combination runs together. Nothing refreshes a stored judgment: a pass's end writes nothing but its own record. The engine also declares the pass record's JSON, which the tools answer, every refusal word the tools answer, the identity every read of scrapes is made as (decisions 12 and 21), and that every request to prompts is made as the caller of the `enrich`. It is scrapes' run engine with a pass in place of a run, scrapes' sibling read path in place of a source, and one pass per kind and scope in place of one run per scraper.

`D01-layout-and-run-seam` declares the run seam, the settings, `cli.SweepInterval` and the names that cross packages (`passes.Config`, `Engine`, `New`, `Rebuild`, `Enrich`, `EnrichCohort`, `Deliver`, `Handlers`, `Recover`, `Drain`, `Refusal`, `Record`, `RecordOf`); `D16-enrichers` the registry, its kinds, which are derived, which fetched and which run through prompts (an `enrichers.Prompter`), each kind's inputs and result, and a prompts kind's prompt name, run input and result; `D15-website-check` what the website kind checks and records, its batches and what its five counts mean; `D04-store` the due rule, `Derive`, `DueAmong` and `DeriveAmong`, the facts row, a candidate's subject and its pages, and the pass records, their kinds, origins, targets and words, the busy rule in `AddPass`, the redelivery refusal and the prune; `D14-cohorts` a cohort's members; `D12-resolution` what `Attach` and `MarkGone` do to candidates and links; `D13-scoring` what `Judge` and `JudgeAll` write, the assessment of a candidate at a time; `D05-sources` the registry and how a source reads a row; `D07-events` the two pass events, their identity and cause, the two names candidates accepts, and `/declarations`; `D09-trail` `sibling.called` and secrecy; `D10-tools` the tools, `add_page` among them, and the sentences a refusal word is shown with; `D03-serve` builds the engine, mounts the delivery and calls `Recover` and `Drain` in its start and stop. The prompts client is appkit's `prompts` package (D27 of appkit), installed as an interface: `Show` reads a prompt by name, `Run` starts a run with an input object, `Result` reads a run, a refusal is a `*prompts.Refusal` carrying prompts' text, and the client waits for nothing.

## Starting a pass by `rebuild`

`Rebuild` takes the caller. Once the drain has begun it is refused `stopping`; with no scrapes client (the services file names no `scrapes` entry) `unavailable`, since a pass could read nothing; while another rebuild is `running`, whoever started it, `busy` (the store refuses the record, so two `rebuild` calls racing cannot both win), a running ingest, judge or enrich of either kind being no bar; and the database failing is an error matching `store.ErrUnreachable`, which the tool answers `unavailable`. Otherwise the pass is recorded with kind `rebuild`, origin `rebuild`, trigger `rebuild` and the caller as its starter, `pass.started` is told, the work begins in a goroutine, and `Rebuild` answers the record, `running`, without waiting: the MCP gateway gives a call fifty seconds and a pass over 17,000 rows may take longer.

## Starting a pass by `enrich`

`Enrich` takes the caller and the kind the `enrich` tool was given with no target, empty for every kind (`D10-tools`). Once the drain has begun it is refused `stopping`; with a kind the registry does not hold, or a kind run through prompts (`site_text`, decision 6: a pass over every candidate would be thousands of paid runs by accident), `invalid`, though the tool refuses both before it calls; with a fetched kind named whose fetcher is not ready (`website_check` while `Config.Website` is nil, which `D15-website-check` says when) `unavailable`; and while another corpus enrich is `running`, whoever started it, `busy`. An empty kind is never `unavailable`: with no checker it walks the derived kinds and fetches nothing, and it never walks a kind run through prompts. Any other pass running is no bar, an `enrich_cohort` included: an enrich reads the candidates' display emails and ZIPs, their links and the ZIP table, and writes only results and facts rows (decisions document, decisions 15 and 16; see the recorded decision on the busy rule). The database failing is an error matching `store.ErrUnreachable`. Otherwise the pass is recorded with kind `enrich`, origin `enrich`, trigger `enrich` and the caller as its starter, `pass.started` is told, the work begins in a goroutine and `Enrich` answers the record, `running`: some 7,000 domains at the website check's pace take about an hour. The kind asked for is not stored on a corpus enrich; its `enrich_kind` is empty.

## Starting a pass by a targeted `enrich`

`EnrichCohort` takes the caller, the kind, the target, a `coh_` or `cand_` id, and `force`, as the `enrich` tool was given them with a `cohort` or a `candidate` (`D10-tools`, decision 12). It is refused, the first that holds: `stopping` once the drain has begun; `invalid` with no kind or one the registry does not hold, since one targeted pass walks exactly one kind; `not_found` for a target that is neither id, a cohort the store does not hold or has deleted, or a candidate it does not hold; `invalid` for a merged candidate, as a reject of one is; `unavailable` for a fetched kind whose checker is not ready or a prompts kind with no prompts client (the services file names no `prompts` entry, decision 21); for a prompts kind, the prompt's check below; `busy` while another `enrich_cohort` over the same target is running, whatever its kind; and the database failing is an error matching `store.ErrUnreachable`. A cohort and a candidate it holds are different targets, and a corpus enrich, a rebuild, an ingest or a judge running is no bar.

For a prompts kind, before it records anything, the call asks prompts for the caller's prompt by the kind's prompt name (`site-text` for `site_text`, decision 7). A refusal, which is how prompts answers a prompt the caller does not have, refuses the call `invalid` with a text of its own: `passes.NoPrompt`, the prompt's name and, when prompts said why, prompts' own text, so the caller reads which prompt is missing. Any other failure of that request refuses it `unavailable`. The model, the instructions and the tools are the prompt's; candidates reads none of them.

Otherwise the pass is recorded with kind `enrich_cohort`, origin `enrich`, trigger `enrich`, the target, the kind as its `enrich_kind` and the caller as its starter, `pass.started` is told, the work begins in a goroutine and `EnrichCohort` answers the record, `running`. The **targets** are fixed at the call: the cohort's current members in their order, as `ReadCohort` answers them (`D14-cohorts`: the first `size` matching candidates, standing rejects and excluded candidates already left out), or the one candidate.

## Passes started by a pass

Two endings start a pass, its **follow-on**, with origin `pass` and the starting pass's id as its trigger (decisions log A1):

- an ingest recorded `done` starts an enrich of every kind but those run through prompts, whether or not there is a checker, so a run's new and changed candidates get their derived facts and, with a checker, their websites; while a corpus enrich is already running the store refuses the record and nothing is started or recorded, not even a failed pass, since the running enrich re-reads the due set batch by batch (decisions log P8); nothing else refuses it;
- an enrich or an `enrich_cohort` recorded `done` with at least one derived or website result written (its `derived`, `https`, `http` and `none` summing to one or more) starts a judge, so the new facts reach the opinions at once; while a judge is running the store refuses it, nothing is recorded, and the next day's judge picks the results up. A `site_text` pass writes none of those four counts, so it starts no judge (decision 10): site text feeds no judgment.

Nothing else starts a follow-on: not a judge, a rebuild, a failed pass, an enrich of either kind that wrote no derived or website result, or anything once the drain has begun. A follow-on is started as no person: no user, the starter's request id, and on its events the starter's event cause, so the trail of a delivered `scrape.finished` runs through the ingest, the enrich and the judge. Its `pass.started` follows its starter's `pass.finished`.

## Starting a pass by a delivery

candidates accepts exactly two event names (`D07-events`): `scrape.finished` (`trail.Accepted`), which starts an ingest pass, and `cron.judge_candidates.fired` (`trail.AcceptedJudge`), the daily fire of the cron trigger `judge_candidates`, which starts a judge pass. They are exact names, as scrapes accepts its triggers' `cron.scrape_<scraper>.fired`: the events app delivers only on `*` or an exact name. appkit's delivery handler hands either to `Deliver` with the bus event's id and depth already on the context as its cause, and the caller appkit's telemetry middleware built from the request: the events app's request id, or one minted for it, and no user. `Deliver` tells the two apart by the event's name; there is no second entry point.

### An ingest delivery

The bus event carries scrapes' attributes `scraper`, `run`, `status`, `reason` and the counts; the engine reads `scraper`, `run` and `status` and the event's id.

A delivery is skipped (`Skip`, nothing to retry) when it is neither a delivery for candidates nor a judge delivery: an event of another name, one with no id, a `scrape.finished` whose `status` is not `done`, one whose `scraper` the registry does not hold (decision 3), or one with no `run`. It is skipped too when a pass the store holds already carries its event id as its delivery or already names its run as its trigger, whatever became of that pass: redelivery is safe, and a second `scrape.finished` for one run under another event id is the same run (decision 11). That check reads only candidates' own records, so a redelivery is skipped even while scrapes cannot be reached.

Otherwise the delivery reads the run's record from scrapes before it answers (orchestrator ruling): a run scrapes does not know (404, unknown or pruned) or a record whose status is not `done` is skipped. Any other failure of the read, scrapes unavailable, a dead socket, another status, an answer that is not a record, or no scrapes client at all, is a pass that failed: the pass is recorded, failed `scrapes` with its counts 0, `pass.started` and `pass.finished` told, and `OK` answered, so the bus does not retry and the failure is on the record where a person sees it; a later delivery of the same run is skipped as a redelivery, and a rebuild repairs. A record that is read and `done` is recorded as an ingest pass of origin `run`, with the run's id as its trigger and the bus event's id as its delivery, `pass.started` told and `OK` answered, all before the rows are read. The store never refuses an ingest `busy`: the only pass that could block it, an ingest of the same run, is found first and refused as a redelivery, whatever its status, so an ingest runs beside a judge, a rebuild, an enrich of either kind and another run's ingest (decisions 15 and 17). Stopping and the database failing answer `Fail`; `Fail` is kept for those two, and the store's `busy`, which an ingest never gets, would be answered `Fail` with `passes.Busy` as a judge delivery's is (see the recorded decision on the second delivery).

### A judge delivery

A `cron.judge_candidates.fired` with an event id is a judge delivery, whatever its attributes. It reads nothing from scrapes, so it needs no scrapes client. It is skipped when a pass the store holds already carries its event id as its delivery: the redelivery check asks the store with the event id and no run, since a cron slug fires many times and only the event id names one fire. Otherwise the pass is recorded with kind `judge`, origin `cron`, trigger `judge_candidates` (`store.TriggerJudge`) and the bus event's id as its delivery, `pass.started` told and `OK` answered once it is recorded, the judging left to the background. The store lets one trigger stand on many judge passes; only a run id is unique as a trigger (`D04-store`). A judge fired while an ingest or a rebuild runs now runs beside it (decision 17). Only while another judge runs does the delivery answer `Fail` with `passes.Busy`, and the bus retries: a judge takes seconds, so the fire is not lost. Stopping and the database failing answer `Fail`.

A pass a delivery started is started as the delivery: its request id is the delivery's, and its user is whatever user the delivery's request named, which is none; the starter shown is what the trigger says, scrapes and the run for an ingest pass (decision 11) and the cron slug for a judge pass, as scrapes' run started by a cron fire shows `cron` and its slug.

## Identity on the read

scrapes' read path requires an identity, and appkit's `scrapes` client forwards the caller on the call's context. A pass a `rebuild` started reads scrapes as its caller: the request carries the caller's user id, email and request id, as every sibling call forwards the caller. A delivery carries no person, so every read an ingest delivery makes, its record read and its pass's rows, is made as the **service identity** (decision 12): `X-User-Id` `candidates`, no `X-User-Email`, and the delivery's request id, with the delivered bus event as the cause of the pass's events. scrapes trusts any process at its socket, so the read is answered and its trail names `candidates` as the user (decision 21). It is the suite's first service-to-service call made as no person, and recorded as such below. A judge delivery makes no read at all.

prompts is reached only by an `enrich_cohort` of a prompts kind, and always as the caller of the `enrich`: the check of the prompt at the call and every show, run and result request of the work carry the caller's user id, email and request id, as every sibling call forwards the caller, so the prompt run is the caller's own and prompts refuses one they do not have (decision 7). appkit's client records each request as `sibling.called` with the target `prompts` (`D09-trail`); the engine records nothing of its own for it.

## The work

A pass's work runs in a goroutine and is cancelled by the drain and by nothing else: the call or delivery that started it may end while it runs. Its runs are, for an ingest pass, the run the record named; for a rebuild, each registered scraper's current run (`fmcsa`, then `ppp`), read with `Current` in the registry's order, a scraper with no current run (scrapes answers 404) contributing no rows and marking nothing gone; and for a judge pass, none. For each run the work streams the rows (`Rows`) and has the source the registry holds for the run's scraper read each one (`D05-sources`), handing the store a batch of `passes.BatchSize` observations at a time, as they arrive, so a run of any size passes through with one batch held (decision 1). Once a run's stream has ended, and the number of rows it handed equals the record's `rows`, the links of that scraper the run no longer holds are marked gone. A rebuild treats every row as changed, so normalized forms, keys and readings are recomputed while candidates, their ids and the rulings stay (decision 11). Rulings are applied throughout, by the store (`D12-resolution`).

An ingest pass stops there: it writes links and the candidates' display, and no estimate or assessment; a candidate it creates has none until a judge, and a candidate whose rows it changed keeps the judgment the last judge wrote. A rebuild, after its runs, walks each derived kind in registry order with `store.Derive` forced, a window of `passes.BatchSize` candidates by id at a time, so every active candidate gets a fresh result of each derived kind whose inputs are not empty, due or not, and its facts row with it; it fetches nothing. A judge pass and, after that, a rebuild call `JudgeAll` once: every active candidate's estimates and assessment are rewritten from every link not gone, from every source, and from its facts row, measured at one time the store's clock reads, the judge's date (`D13-scoring`). So a filing's `fmcsa_active` weight is the one it had on the day of the last judge, and between judges nothing decays. A judge started by an enrich is a judge pass like the cron's.

An enrich pass walks its kinds in registry order: the one kind the call named, or every kind but one run through prompts and a fetched one whose fetcher is not ready. Every derived kind is walked before the first fetch, so the derived facts are in place while the hour of fetching runs. A derived kind is walked with `store.Derive` not forced, a window of `passes.BatchSize` candidates by id at a time, each call recording a result for each candidate in its window that is due and writing its facts row in the same write; the count `derived` sums what the calls recorded. The fetched kind is the website check's (`D15-website-check`): it selects the due candidates in keyset batches of `passes.BatchSize` by candidate id, one batch held at a time, hands each batch's domains not yet checked in the pass to the fetcher, and never selects one candidate twice within a pass, so a domain whose fetch failed is not retried until the next pass. An enrich has no runs, makes no request to scrapes, and writes only results and facts rows, never a candidate, link, estimate or assessment. Fetching nothing is not a failure: a domain that answers nothing is a `none` result. An enrich over a corpus whose inputs have not changed since the last records nothing, its counts all 0, and starts no judge.

An `enrich_cohort` walks its one kind over its targets, which it selects once, when it begins: each target that is active, whose inputs for the kind are not empty and that, without `force`, is due for it (decision 13). The rule is the same for every kind. A derived kind is recorded for the selected targets in one `store.DeriveAmong` call, a fresh result for each when forced, as a rebuild records one, and its count is `derived`. The website kind selects with `store.DueAmong` and checks each selected domain once, at the checker's pace, with the counts `D15-website-check` gives an enrich pass. So `website_check` over a cohort without `force` checks only the members whose domain changed, likely none, and the record says so with `due` 0; with `force` it checks every member once and each domain once.

A prompts kind, `site_text`, starts one prompts run per selected target, with the input the kind gives for the candidate's subject (`candidate`, `name`, `city`, `state` and `website`, `D16-enrichers`), keeping at most `RunsInFlight` runs going and starting the next as soon as one ends. A run is started once prompts accepts it. When prompts refuses a start, which is how it answers a full queue, the work waits `QueueWait` on `Config.After`, asks for the prompt again, so a prompt deleted meanwhile ends the work rather than looping, and tries again, at most `StartTries` starts per target. It learns that a run ended only by asking prompts for its result, each time a wait of `PollInterval` on `Config.After` ends, and by no other means; a run that is queued or running is asked again at the next interval, for as long as it takes: prompts' own bound on a run, `PROMPT_SECONDS`, is the only time limit (decision 8). When a run has ended, however it ended (`exited` with any exit code, `failed`, `killed`, `timed_out` or any status prompts adds), the pass records the kind's result for the target, unless the kind's `Valid` rejects it, which is a failure of prompts: the run's id, its ending, the number of distinct URLs the company then holds and the cost prompts reports, the result's cost in dollars (`D16-enrichers`). A failed run is a result (decision 9): the company is not due again, the failure shows on the candidate page, the pages the agent added stay, and the owner redoes it with `force`. The pages themselves are added by the agent through `add_page` while the run goes on, never by the pass; the pass counts the copies the company gained during each run.

The pass then finishes `done` with its nineteen counts: `rows` the rows streamed, and the rest the sums of what the store's writes answered (`new`, `changed`, `unchanged`, `created`, `linked` from the batches, `gone` from the marking, `judged` and `unranged` from `JudgeAll`, 0 for an ingest pass, `derived` from the `Derive` calls of an enrich or a rebuild and the `DeriveAmong` call of an `enrich_cohort`, 0 for every other kind), for an enrich that walked the website kind and an `enrich_cohort` of it `due`, `fetched`, `https`, `http` and `none` as `D15-website-check` pins them, for an `enrich_cohort` of a prompts kind `due` the targets selected, `runs` the runs started, `runs_failed` those that ended other than `exited` with exit code 0, `pages` the page copies the targets gained during their runs and `cost` the runs' cost in nano-dollars, and 0 for every other pass. What each counts is the owning document's.

## Cost

A pass costs in proportion to its rows (orchestrator ruling). A pass over the real FMCSA run of 16,706 rows never finished: 1,970 rows took 82 seconds and a re-scrape with every row unchanged four minutes, the pages timing out meanwhile, because every batch reloaded and compared the whole database and every row re-derived every candidate. Now each batch is one write transaction touching its own rows and the candidates they attach to, by key (`D04-store`); an unchanged row costs a lookup, a touch of its last seen, and its share of its candidate's display re-derived once per batch; and the gone-marking is one bounded statement per `MarkGone` call, over that call's scraper's links last seen in a run other than that call's run, after which the display of each candidate it touched is re-derived by key from that candidate's own links. An ingest pass judges nothing, so its cost is its rows alone. A judge covers every active candidate by definition, from each candidate's record, its own links and rulings, and its facts row: a full judge of the 16,580 candidates on `us` took 18 seconds. A pass's work holds no lock of the engine's while it calls the store, and a read of the store answers while a batch is being written (`D04-store`), so the pages and the tools answer throughout.

What is required is the time: a delivery of a **generated run** of 10,000 rows into an empty store, a second delivery of a run alike to it with every row unchanged, and a rebuild over that run, which judges its 5,000 candidates, each finish `done` within 120 seconds, under the race detector the gate runs, with the counts the attach rule gives. The work the pass asks of scrapes is counted too: one rows read per run (R-T3S6-08IX). Each of the three passes is timed with every pass the engine started before it ended, the ingests' follow-on enrich and judge included, so that each pass is measured alone; the rebuild's 120 seconds hold its derived step, two results for each of the 5,000 candidates, as well as its judge.

A pass ends `failed` with one reason (decision 11):

- `interrupted`: the drain cancelled the work before it ended, whatever else then failed;
- `store`: a write the work handed the store failed, `JudgeAll` and `Derive` included; for an enrich of either kind, any call of the store failed, a read of the due set included;
- `prompts`: for an `enrich_cohort`, any failure of prompts in the work: the prompt asked for again before a retried start refused or not answered, a start that fails other than by a refusal, the `StartTries`-th start of one target refused, a run's ending that the kind's `Valid` rejects (an empty status or a negative cost), or a result request that fails or is refused (an unknown run reads the same as an unreadable one, and a run whose ending cannot be read cannot be recorded);
- `scrapes`: any other failure, which neither enrich kind, reading nothing from scrapes, ever has: a read of scrapes that failed or answered something that is not a record or a row, a current run not `done`, or a stream whose row count disagrees with its record's `rows`, a stream cut short at a line boundary being told this way (appkit's `scrapes` client leaves that check to its caller).

A failed pass keeps its record and its counts at 0 and leaves in place every batch it had already handed the store; the next pass or a rebuild repairs them, since every write is idempotent. A stream that failed marks nothing gone, so a short read never takes evidence away. A judge that fails leaves the judgments as `JudgeAll` left them (`D13-scoring`); its delivery was already answered, so the next day's fire, or a rebuild, judges again. An `enrich_cohort` that fails keeps every result it recorded and abandons its runs in flight: the client offers no cancel, prompts ends them under `PROMPT_SECONDS`, and the pages their agents add still land; the targets it did not finish are due again at the next pass. `pass.finished` is told once the ending is recorded; an ending the store cannot record tells no event and leaves the pass `running` for the next start to settle, as scrapes leaves a run.

## Starting and stopping

`Recover` runs at start, before ready (`D03-serve`): every pass still `running` was cut off by a dead process and is failed `interrupted`, its `pass.finished` told as its starter with no cause. A running enrich of either kind is settled so too, as every kind is; the next enrich finds what is still due. `Drain` is the stop: from its call `Rebuild`, `Enrich` and `EnrichCohort` refuse `stopping`, `Deliver` answers `Fail` and no pass starts a follow-on, and a call already under way that has not recorded its pass records none; every running work is cancelled at once, its read of scrapes, its fetches, its requests to prompts and its waits ended, and its pass failed `interrupted` (decision 11). A drained `enrich_cohort`'s runs are abandoned as a failed one's are. `Drain` returns once every pass the engine started has ended, or when its context ends, which `D03-serve` sets to the drain deadline, `DRAIN_SECONDS` after the signal.

## The sweep

Once before ready, after `Recover`, and then each time a wait of `cli.SweepInterval` on `Process.After` ends, the passes past keeping are pruned with `store.PrunePasses` and `CANDIDATES_KEEP_PASSES`: the newest that many `done` passes are kept and, separately, the newest that many `failed` passes, so a page still shows what went wrong; a `running` pass is never pruned (decision 16). A pruned pass is gone from the tools and the pages; candidates, links, estimates, assessments and rulings are never pruned. A prune that fails is silent and tried again at the next interval. The website check waits on the same `Process.After`, with its own durations (`D15-website-check`), during a pass and during an `add_page` call's one fetch; the engine's own waits, the result poll's `PollInterval` and the queue-full `QueueWait`, end on `Config.After`, which `cli.Run` sets to the same `Process.After` and which, nil, is `time.After`; only a wait of `cli.SweepInterval` prunes.

## The pass record

The record the tools answer is `passes.Record`, made from a stored pass by `passes.RecordOf`: `id`, `kind`, `origin`, `trigger`, `target` and `enrich_kind` when not empty, which is for an `enrich_cohort` alone, `status`, `reason` when failed, `started`, `finished` once ended, the nineteen counts, the fifteen with `judged` where `rescored` stood, `derived` after `unranged` and the five website counts after it, then `runs`, `runs_failed`, `pages` and `cost`, always present as every count is, and the starter: `user` and `email` for a pass of origin `rebuild` or `enrich`, an `enrich_cohort` included, nothing for a delivery, whose trigger names the run or the cron slug, or for a follow-on, whose trigger names its starter. The delivery and the request id are not part of it. The record is a tool's and a page's answer, not a persisted event payload, so it renames `rescored` to `judged` at once; the pass events keep `rescored` beside `judged` for one release (`D07-events`). The members it adds are added keys only (decision 14).

## Refusals and failures

A refusal is a `*passes.Refusal`, a word and, rarely, a text. All of candidates' refusal words are declared in `internal/passes` (`D01-layout-and-run-seam`), so the tools tell every refusal apart with one `errors.As` (`D10-tools`): `not_found`, `invalid`, `busy`, `stopping` and `unavailable` (decision 14). The engine answers `busy`, `stopping` and `unavailable`, `invalid` for a kind given to `Enrich` that the registry does not hold or that runs through prompts, and every refusal of `EnrichCohort`, `not_found` and `invalid` included, since only the store can say whether a target exists or is merged; `not_found` and `invalid` are otherwise the tools' (`D10-tools`), and the database failing reaches them as an error matching `store.ErrUnreachable`, which they answer `unavailable`. A refusal's `Text` is empty but for the missing prompt, whose text the tools answer in place of the word's fixed sentence. A delivery's `Fail` carries a fixed message, a copy constant naming no value: `passes.Stopping` while stopping, `passes.Busy` while a pass of its own kind and scope runs, which for a judge delivery is another judge, `store.Unreachable` when the database fails.

## Recorded decisions

- **Ingesting and judging are separate passes** (decisions document, Ingest and judge): an ingest pass stores rows and judges nothing; a judge pass judges every active candidate and reads nothing; a rebuild ingests every current run, records every derived kind afresh and then judges, as one pass of kind `rebuild`, so its record says what started it and a judge never runs between its ingests. Nothing else computes a judgment but an owner's ruling, which judges what it touches in the same call (`D13-scoring`).
- **The judge runs daily, not on change and not on read** (decisions document, decision 26). The candidates whose `fmcsa_active` weight is moving are those whose filing nobody updated, so their rows never change: 2,431 of the 16,706 active MN and WI carriers are in the two-to-five-year band and 2,663 change weight within 30 days, so judging only the candidates a pass touched would never revisit them. Judging on read would make the first read after a quiet stretch wait for a full judge and put a staleness check on every read path, to save 18 seconds of CPU a day. A daily cron trigger is the pattern the suite already has, and it keeps a stored judgment within a day of true.
- **No pass refreshes** (decisions document, decision 25): the SQL statement that rewrote every score at each pass's end, on failure and at start, and its agreement test with Go, are gone, so the judgment formulas live once, in Go (`D13-scoring`). A pass's ending writes only its record, whatever the ending, and `Recover` writes nothing but the endings it settles.
- **A judge delivery is skipped only by its event id**: a cron slug fires many times, so the trigger `judge_candidates` stands on many passes, and the redelivery check asks the store with the event id and no run. A judge delivery needs no scrapes client and reads nothing, so it is recorded even when the services file names no `scrapes` entry.
- **A judge fire during another judge fails `busy`** (as before, rather than skipping): the bus retries for minutes and a judge takes seconds, so the fire runs after the judge and the day's judgment is not lost; a fire whose retries are spent is caught by the next day's. A fire during an ingest or a rebuild is no longer refused (decision 17): it runs beside it, reading what the ingest has attached so far, and the next judge corrects the rest.
- **A judge delivery is answered once its pass is recorded**, the judging left to the background as an ingest pass's rows are. That the answer comes before the judge ends is stated here, not required: unlike an ingest, whose first read a fake scrapes can hold, a judge has no seam a permitted test can hold open, and `JudgeAll` over a test's store ends in milliseconds.
- **The service identity on a delivery's reads** (decision 12, binding): `X-User-Id` `candidates`, the service's own name, no email, the delivery's request id. It is the suite's first service-to-service call made as no person. The pass's own starter stays the delivery's caller, as scrapes' delivery-started run keeps the delivery's caller, so the pass's events carry the delivery's request id and no user (`D07-events`) while its reads' `sibling.called` carry `candidates` (`D09-trail`).
- **The record is read before the delivery is answered** (orchestrator ruling, superseding coordinator decision C1): an unknown run or one not `done` is skipped; any other read failure records a pass failed `scrapes` and answers `OK`, so a scrapes outage shows as a failed pass rather than as retries and a paused subscriber, and `Fail` stays reserved for busy, stopping and the database. A delivery with no scrapes client is treated as an unavailable read. The redelivery check comes first and reads only the store, so a retry of a run already passed is skipped without asking scrapes.
- **One busy rule, in the store** (decisions 15 and 16, replacing the slots): a pass is refused `busy` only while a pass of the same kind over the same scope is running, the scope being an ingest's run, an `enrich_cohort`'s target, and the corpus for a judge, a rebuild and an enrich; `AddPass` keeps it (`D04-store`), so two calls racing cannot both win. The slots managed contention SQLite already handles: it serializes writes, so the rule protects no data, only paid or heavy work started twice, two website checks over the corpus, two `site_text` passes over one cohort, two rebuilds. Every other combination runs together: two ingests of different runs, a judge beside an ingest, a rebuild beside an `enrich_cohort`, a corpus enrich beside an `enrich_cohort`.
- **The busy scope ignores the kind walked** (decision 15 as written): two `enrich_cohort` passes over one target are `busy` whatever their `enrich_kind`, so a `website_check` and a `site_text` over one cohort wait for each other though they would not pay twice. A rule that let them run together would need a scope the decisions document does not name; the cost is a second call after the first ends.
- **A candidate target and a cohort holding it are different scopes**, accepted: an `enrich_cohort` over one candidate and one over a cohort holding it may both start the candidate's run of a prompts kind, the one paid twice. Comparing the members of every running target would make the busy rule read cohorts, and the owner names a candidate alone to redo it, not beside a cohort pass.
- **The second delivery of the same scrape run** (decision 17's "a delivery whose own kind and scope is already running") is the existing redelivery skip: the store holds every pass that names a run as its trigger, whatever its status, so a second `scrape.finished` of a run already running is skipped (`Skip`) by `FindDelivered` and `ErrDelivered` before `AddPass` could answer `busy`, as the sandbox check expects ("skipped as today"); `AddPass` itself answers `ErrDelivered` before `ErrBusy` for a pass naming the run, so even two deliveries racing never see `busy`. `Fail` with `passes.Busy` is then answered only to a judge delivery while a judge runs. That an ingest delivery would answer `ErrBusy` with `Fail` and `passes.Busy` is described, not required: no store a permitted test can build gives an ingest `ErrBusy`, so no test could tell that mapping from another.
- **A scraper with no current run contributes nothing to a rebuild** rather than failing it: the registry may hold a scraper whose first run has not landed, and the rebuild still judges every candidate. A current run that is not `done` fails `scrapes`, since scrapes answers only `done` runs as current.
- **The line count is the stream's check** (decision 2): appkit's client cannot tell a stream cut at a line boundary from a whole one; the pass compares the rows handed with the record's `rows` and fails `scrapes` on a mismatch, before marking anything gone.
- **The batch size, 500, is a constant of the rules version** (decision 17), declared as `passes.BatchSize`; a failed pass keeps every full batch it handed over. Whether a partial last batch of a failed stream is kept is left open: the next pass repairs either way.
- **Precedence of endings**: a work the drain cancelled is `interrupted` whatever then failed; a refused write, or for an enrich of either kind any failed call of the store, is `store`; for an `enrich_cohort`, any other failure is `prompts`; anything else is `scrapes`. The drain cancels at once, as scrapes' does: the pass cannot be resumed, and the next delivery, enrich or a rebuild repairs it.
- **An ending the store cannot record leaves the pass `running`**, as scrapes leaves a run, for the next start's `Recover` to fail `interrupted`; until then every new pass it blocks is refused as busy, but an ingest of the same run, which is skipped as a redelivery. Retrying the ending is not designed: the database failing is a broken host.
- **No queue**: the busy rule is the whole concurrency rule, and the bus's retries are the queue for a judge fire during a judge. An ingest that ends while a corpus enrich runs starts nothing and records nothing (decisions log P8): the running enrich re-reads the due set per batch and window, and the next ingest or an `enrich` call covers what it did not select. An `enrich` or `enrich_cohort` refused `busy` is the caller's to call again.
- **Origins `enrich` and `pass`** (decisions log A1): `enrich` mirrors `rebuild`, the tool naming itself; `pass` with the starter's id as trigger says which pass led to which.
- **A follow-on carries no person**: no one asked for it, so it has no user; it keeps its starter's request id and event cause, the honest provenance and the one a delivery's pass already has.
- **"At least one new result" is at least one derived or website result written** (enrichers decision 10, decision 10): every such result an enrich of either kind writes is a new row (`D04-store`), and its `derived`, `https`, `http` and `none` sum to them, so the rule reads off the pass's own counts. A pass that wrote none starts no judge: nothing a judge reads changed. A `site_text` pass writes none, so it starts no judge without the engine testing its kind; the change that adds a fact the judge reads from a prompts kind adds that kind's count to the sum.
- **An enrich's judge is refused only by a running judge** (decision 17): the daily judge picks the results up, so nothing is lost. A judge running beside an ingest or a rebuild is accepted (decision 16), and an enrich recorded after this one ended and before its judge is recorded does not stop the judge; an enrich writes only results and facts rows, and the judge's guarantees already exclude any `AddEnrichments`, `Derive` or `DeriveAmong` made during it; the next judge reads the later facts.
- **An enrich of either kind fails `store` on any store failure**, a read included, where the other kinds fail `store` only on a refused write: a corpus enrich has no other failing sibling, a failed fetch being a `none` result, so `scrapes` would mislead; an `enrich_cohort` of a prompts kind has one, and fails `prompts` for it.
- **`Enrich` and `EnrichCohort` refuse what the tool refuses** (decisions 6 and 12): a kind the registry does not hold, and for `Enrich` a prompts kind, for `EnrichCohort` an empty kind, so the engine's contract does not lean on its caller. There is no age: a candidate is due by its inputs alone (enrichers decision 3), and `older_than_days` fails the tool's schema like any unknown argument, so it never reaches the engine.
- **A targeted enrich names its kind** (orchestrator ruling): an `enrich_cohort` walks exactly one kind, its `enrich_kind`, so its record and its counts say what it did; a kindless target would run every kind, a prompts kind included, over a cohort, which no decision asks for.
- **A kindless corpus enrich, and the ingest's follow-on, leave out every prompts kind** (orchestrator ruling, from decisions 6 and 12): decision 12 leaves an untargeted enrich as release-44 left it, and a corpus walk of `site_text` would be the paid runs decision 6 forbids. Only a targeted enrich runs a prompts kind.
- **A merged candidate as a target is `invalid`**, as a reject of one is (`D14-cohorts`): it has no facts and no due results, so a pass over it would do nothing and say nothing.
- **The targets are fixed at the call**: the cohort's members as `ReadCohort` answers them while the call runs, so the pass's `due` and `runs` describe the cohort the caller saw; a member that joins later waits for the next call, and one that leaves is still worked.
- **`unavailable` only for a named kind that is not ready**: with no checker an enrich of every kind still walks the derived kinds, so a space without a checker still gets its link and drive facts, and the ingest's follow-on enrich runs whatever `Config.Website` is (enrichers decision 9); an `EnrichCohort` of a prompts kind with no prompts client is `unavailable` (decision 21), as one of `website_check` without a checker is.
- **Derived kinds before fetched ones** (enrichers decision 8): derived results take seconds and fetches an hour, so the derived facts land first, and a follow-on judge after the pass reads both. The registry's order puts them first; the engine does not reorder it.
- **The kind asked for is stored only on a targeted pass** (decision 14): an `enrich_cohort` records its `target` and `enrich_kind`, which its page and record show; a corpus enrich stores neither, its counts saying what was done, as the old age override was not stored.
- **The busy rule protects no data** (decision 16, generalizing enrichers decision 11): a pass reading a candidate another pass is writing records what it read. An enrich beside an ingest may derive a candidate's link facts while the ingest is between two batches touching it; its result records the inputs it read, so the ingest's own follow-on enrich finds the inputs changed and derives again. A judge beside an ingest judges what was attached so far, and the next judge corrects it; a rebuild beside an ingest redoes one run's rows. This is accepted, not prevented: preventing it would need passes to wait for each other, which the rule exists to avoid.
- **A rebuild derives forced, never fetches** (enrichers decision 9): a rebuild is the repair every failure points to and the way a new ZIP table or a new derivation reaches every candidate, so it records a fresh derived result for every candidate whatever its inputs; a fetch is an hour of outside requests, which a repair should not start.
- **The sweeper, the website check and the engine's waits share `Process.After`** rather than each having a field of the seam, told apart by the duration each asks for; only a `cli.SweepInterval` wait prunes. The engine takes it as `Config.After` so a test drives the poll and the queue-full wait with channels it holds; a call outside any pass, an `add_page` call's one fetch waiting `website.Timeout`, departs from the waits being a pass's alone, and R-TCBG-OMPS admits it.
- **Results recorded during a judge are excluded from the judge's guarantees**: a judge reads each candidate's facts row (`D13-scoring`), so R-SRL6-6J3Z and R-SV8V-BUC2 hold only when no `AddEnrichments`, `Derive`, `DeriveAmong` or `FillFacts` of another pass came between a pass's recording and its ending, as they already did for `Decide`. Because `GetAssessment` answers its facts from the facts row at the read (`D04-store`), R-SWGR-PM2R and R-SV8V-BUC2 exclude those writes up to the read, not only up to the pass's ending; an ingest's test holds an enrich `running` so that no follow-on starts.
- **The sweep's requirements are stated here, at the run seam**, as scrapes states its own; the prune is the store's (`D04-store`).
- **A failed prune is described, not required, at the run seam.** A store failure is provoked only with `SetFailing(true)` on a handle the test hands in (candidates/AGENTS.md), and `cli.Run` builds its store over its own handle, so no permitted test can fail a prune under `Run`. What is required is the store's half, that a failing `PrunePasses` answers `ErrUnreachable` and removes nothing (`D04-store`, R-OD8H-2EDG), and the sweep's, that the next interval prunes again (R-TCBG-OMPS); that the start goes on to ready and the failure is silent is this overview's.
- **The bound is 120 seconds for 10,000 rows** (orchestrator ruling): a probe of a keyed pass, SQL by key on decision 16's indexes over appkit's `db`, took 14 to 23 seconds per pass of 5,000 rows under `go test -race` and about one second without it on the developer's machine. At that rate 10,000 rows take about 30 to 45 seconds under the race detector, leaving the build's fuller work and a loaded machine room inside 120 seconds, and 16,706 real rows well under a minute without it: about 3.4 seconds at the probe's rate of about one second per 5,000 rows, and at most about 13 seconds even at the rate the bound allows, 120 seconds per 10,000 rows under the race detector divided by the probe's race factor of about 15, times 1.67, inside decision 20's minute. The store this replaces took 82 seconds for 1,970 rows, four minutes for a re-scrape with every row unchanged, and had not finished the real 16,706 rows after 14 minutes, each without the race detector. 10,000 rows rather than 5,000 makes a cost growing with the square of the rows four times as visible. A re-scrape with every row unchanged and a rebuild are held to the same bound, since the first was the slowest case seen and the second is the repair every failure points to; the rebuild's judge of 5,000 candidates is inside it, a full judge of 16,580 real candidates having taken 18 seconds.
- **The writes of a pass** are R-SQD9-SRDA: an ingest pass's are its batches and its marking alone, a judge pass's one `JudgeAll`, a rebuild's its batches and marking, then a forced `Derive` walk of each derived kind, then `JudgeAll` last, an enrich pass's a `Derive` walk of each derived kind it walks and then the website results it records (`D15-website-check`), and an `enrich_cohort`'s one `DeriveAmong` or the results its checks or runs record; R-SRL6-6J3Z requires the records those writes leave, R-SWGR-PM2R that an ingest leaves every assessment as it was, R-SV8V-BUC2 that a judge or a rebuild leaves every active candidate's assessment as of one time during its work, R-SNXH-17VW what an enrich's derived walks leave, R-TKUR-D0WN what an `enrich_cohort`'s leaves, and R-SU0Y-Y2LD what a rebuild's leave and that its judge reads the facts they leave.
- **A delivery is worked whatever run is current**: a delivery of an older run after a newer one marks gone what the older run lacks; the next delivery or a rebuild repairs it.
- **The prompt is checked at the call and before every retried start** (decision 7; appkit's client, D27 of appkit): a refusal of `show` at the call means the caller cannot use the prompt, so the call is refused and records nothing, and the owner learns at once which prompt to create; a refusal of `run` after a good `show` means not now, so the work waits and asks for the prompt again first, and a prompt deleted meanwhile fails the pass `prompts` rather than looping on `run`. Any other failure at the call is `unavailable`: prompts could not be asked.
- **Retried starts are bounded by `StartTries`** (20): appkit's client asks a consumer that retries `run` refusals to bound them, since a refusal of the arguments looks like a full queue. Twenty tries `QueueWait` (30 seconds) apart is ten minutes of a refusing prompts per company, longer than a deploy of prompts takes; past it the pass fails `prompts` rather than wait for ever.
- **The intervals** (orchestrator ruling): `PollInterval`, 5 seconds, because an agent run reads some fifteen pages and lasts minutes, so 5 seconds adds little to a pass and, with four runs in flight, is under one result request a second; `QueueWait`, 30 seconds, because a full prompts queue drains at the pace of whole runs. Each differs from `website.Interval`, `website.Timeout` and `cli.SweepInterval`, so a test tells the waits on one `After` apart by duration.
- **Every failure of prompts in the work fails the pass `prompts`** (orchestrator ruling, mirroring scrapes in a pass), a refused result included: an unknown run and an unreadable one read the same, and a run whose ending cannot be read cannot be recorded. So is an ending the kind's `Valid` rejects (an empty status, a negative cost): it is prompts' answer that is wrong, not the store, so nothing is recorded for that company and the pass fails `prompts`, never `store`. The pass ends at the first such failure, without waiting for its other runs in flight to end, which are abandoned as below. Results written before stay; the next targeted enrich does what is still due.
- **Runs are abandoned, not cancelled**, when a pass fails or is drained: appkit's client offers no cancel and prompts ends a run under `PROMPT_SECONDS`, its `add_page` calls still landing. A drained pass's unfinished targets are due again and are paid for again at the next pass, accepted: the drain is a stop, and a run that ended unseen has no result to record.
- **A pass's `pages` counts copies, a result's `pages` distinct URLs** (orchestrator ruling): the pass says how much the runs kept, a copy of a URL already held included, as the copies the company gained during each run; the result says what the company holds when its run ended (decision 5), which a page reads beside the run's ending.
- **The missing prompt's refusal carries text**, a departure from a refusal being a word and a fixed sentence (`D10-tools`): decision 7 asks the message to say which prompt is missing, so its `Text` is `NoPrompt`, the prompt's name and prompts' own text, which names no stored business data.

## Consumer tasks

`D03-serve` builds `eng := passes.New(passes.Config{Store: st, Sources: reg, Scrapes: sc, Prompts: pc, Telemetry: w, Events: em, Website: chk, After: p.After})`, `reg` being `sources.New(fmcsa.New(), ppp.New())`, `sc` nil when the services file has no `scrapes` entry, `pc` the `prompts.NewClient(prompts.ClientConfig{Service: e, Telemetry: w})` of its `prompts` entry `e`, nil when there is none, and `chk` the checker `D15-website-check` selects, nil when there is none; mounts `events.DeliveryHandler(eng.Handlers())` and `events.DeclarationsHandler(em, eng.Handlers())`, `eng.Handlers()` answering `events.Handlers{trail.Accepted: eng.Deliver, trail.AcceptedJudge: eng.Deliver}`; calls `eng.Recover(bg)` and `st.PrunePasses(bg, s.KeepPasses)` before ready, prunes again on each `p.After(cli.SweepInterval)`, and calls `eng.Drain(drainCtx)` at the signal. The `rebuild` tool (`D10-tools`), `c` the call's caller: `p, err := eng.Rebuild(ctx, c)`, answering `passes.RecordOf(p)`; a refusal told with `var rf *passes.Refusal; errors.As(err, &rf)`, reading `rf.Word` and, when not empty, `rf.Text`, and any other error, which matches `store.ErrUnreachable`, answered `unavailable`. The `enrich` tool (`D10-tools`): with no `cohort` and no `candidate`, `p, err := eng.Enrich(ctx, c, a.Kind)`, `a.Kind` empty for every kind; with one, `p, err := eng.EnrichCohort(ctx, c, a.Kind, a.Cohort+a.Candidate, a.Force)`; each answered and refused as `rebuild` is; the tool refuses an unknown kind, both targets, `force` with neither, a prompts kind with neither and a target with no kind `invalid` itself, and `older_than_days` fails its schema. The `pass` and `passes` tools answer `passes.RecordOf(p)` of what `st.GetPass` and `st.ListPasses` return, whose `Kind`, `Origin`, `Target`, `EnrichKind` and `Judged` the record carries.

The engine records an ingest pass with `st.AddPass(ctx, store.Pass{Kind: store.KindIngest, Origin: store.OriginRun, Trigger: run, UserID: c.UserID, RequestID: c.RequestID, Delivery: d.Event.ID})`, a judge pass with `st.AddPass(ctx, store.Pass{Kind: store.KindJudge, Origin: store.OriginCron, Trigger: store.TriggerJudge, UserID: c.UserID, RequestID: c.RequestID, Delivery: d.Event.ID})` after `st.FindDelivered(ctx, d.Event.ID, "")` answered `store.ErrNotFound`, a rebuild with `st.AddPass(ctx, store.Pass{Kind: store.KindRebuild, Origin: store.OriginRebuild, Trigger: store.TriggerRebuild, UserID: c.UserID, UserEmail: c.Email, RequestID: c.RequestID})`, an enrich with `st.AddPass(ctx, store.Pass{Kind: store.KindEnrich, Origin: store.OriginEnrich, Trigger: store.TriggerEnrich, UserID: c.UserID, UserEmail: c.Email, RequestID: c.RequestID})`, a targeted enrich with `st.AddPass(ctx, store.Pass{Kind: store.KindEnrichCohort, Origin: store.OriginEnrich, Trigger: store.TriggerEnrich, Target: target, EnrichKind: kind, UserID: c.UserID, UserEmail: c.Email, RequestID: c.RequestID})`, and a follow-on of a pass `s` with `st.AddPass(ctx, store.Pass{Kind: store.KindEnrich, Origin: store.OriginPass, Trigger: s.ID, RequestID: s.RequestID})`, or `Kind: store.KindJudge` after an enrich of either kind, `store.ErrBusy` meaning nothing is started. The engine builds its registry with `reg := enrichers.New(enrichers.Config{Website: cfg.Website, Prompts: cfg.Prompts})` and walks `reg.All()`, skipping every `enrichers.Prompter` and an `enrichers.Fetcher` whose `Ready()` is false unless a kind was named (then `Enrich` was refused `invalid` or `unavailable`). An enrich walks each derived kind `k` with `for after := ""; ; { next, n, err := st.Derive(ctx, k, after, false, passes.BatchSize); total.Derived += n; if next == "" { break }; after = next }`, then the website kind as `D15-website-check` says, `st.DueEnrichments(ctx, sources.WebsiteCheck, after, passes.BatchSize)` answering `[]store.Due` and the fetcher's `Fetch(ctx, unchecked, each)` writing each group with `st.AddEnrichments`, and ends with `st.FinishPass(ctx, p.ID, store.Counts{Derived: ..., Due: ..., Fetched: ..., HTTPS: ..., HTTP: ..., None: ...})`. A targeted enrich reads its targets with `st.ReadCohort(ctx, target)` or `st.GetCandidate(ctx, target)` during the call; for a prompts kind `pr := reg.Get(kind).(enrichers.Prompter)` it calls `cfg.Prompts.Show(identity.NewContext(ctx, c), pr.Prompt())` before `AddPass`. Its work calls `n, err := st.DeriveAmong(ctx, k, targets, force)` for a derived kind; `ds, err := st.DueAmong(ctx, k, targets, force)` for the website kind and the prompts kind, the website kind fetching each distinct input once and writing each group with `st.AddEnrichments`; and for the prompts kind, per `d` of `ds` with at most `passes.RunsInFlight` in flight and the caller on every context, `s, err := st.Subject(ctx, d.Candidate)`, `before, err := st.CountPages(ctx, d.Candidate)`, `start, err := cfg.Prompts.Run(cctx, pr.Prompt(), pr.Input(s))` (a `*prompts.Refusal` waiting `<-cfg.After(passes.QueueWait)`, `Show` again and trying again, up to `passes.StartTries`), then each `<-cfg.After(passes.PollInterval)` `out, err := cfg.Prompts.Result(cctx, start.ID)` until `out.Status` is neither `enrichers.RunQueued` nor `enrichers.RunRunning`, `after, err := st.CountPages(ctx, d.Candidate)`, `r := pr.Found(out, after.URLs)`, a failure of prompts when `!pr.Valid(r.Found)`, and otherwise `st.AddEnrichments(ctx, []store.Enrichment{{Candidate: d.Candidate, Kind: k, Inputs: d.Inputs, Source: r.Source, Found: r.Found, Cost: r.Cost}})`, summing `Runs`, `RunsFailed`, `after.Copies - before.Copies` into `Pages` and `out.CostNanos` into `Cost`; any store error ending the work with `st.FailPass(ctx, p.ID, store.ReasonStore)` and any failure of prompts with `st.FailPass(ctx, p.ID, store.ReasonPrompts)`, and otherwise with `st.FinishPass`. A rebuild walks each derived kind the same way with `force` true between its last `st.MarkGone` and `st.JudgeAll`, and finishes with `total.Derived` set.
