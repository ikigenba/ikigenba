# D06-passes

The engine: `internal/passes`, the one place a pass is started, worked, finished, settled at start and abandoned at the stop, and the place an event delivered on the bus becomes a pass. A pass is one of four kinds (`D04-store` records its kind and its origin). An **ingest** pass, started by a `scrape.finished` delivered on the bus, reads the run's rows from scrapes over the socket through appkit's `scrapes` client, hands them to the store a batch at a time to be resolved (`store.Attach`, `D12-resolution`) and marks gone the links the run no longer holds (`store.MarkGone`, `D12-resolution`); it judges nothing. A **judge** pass, started by a `cron.judge_candidates.fired` delivered on the bus, judges every active candidate from scratch (`store.JudgeAll`, `D13-scoring`) and reads nothing from scrapes. A **rebuild** pass, started by a `rebuild` call (`D10-tools`), ingests every registered scraper's current run and then judges every active candidate, as one pass. An **enrich** pass, started by an `enrich` call (`D10-tools`) or by an ingest pass that ended `done`, checks the website of every candidate due for one (`D15-website-check`) and records each result; it reads nothing from scrapes and writes nothing but those results. An enrich that wrote at least one result starts a judge, unless an ingest, judge or rebuild is running, so the new evidence shows without waiting for the morning. Whatever starts it, the engine records the pass `running` (`D04-store`), tells `pass.started` (`D07-events`), answers at once, works it in the background, and finishes it `done` with its fourteen counts or `failed` with its reason, telling `pass.finished`. One ingest, judge or rebuild runs at a time (decision 11), and at most one enrich runs beside it. Nothing refreshes a stored judgment: a pass's end writes nothing but its own record. The engine also declares the pass record's JSON, which the tools answer, every refusal word the tools answer, and the identity every read of scrapes is made as (decisions 12 and 21). It is scrapes' run engine with a pass in place of a run, scrapes' sibling read path in place of a source, and one ingest, judge or rebuild at a time, with at most one enrich beside it, in place of one run per scraper.

`D01-layout-and-run-seam` declares the run seam, the settings, `cli.SweepInterval` and the names that cross packages (`passes.Config`, `Engine`, `New`, `Rebuild`, `Enrich`, `Deliver`, `Handlers`, `Recover`, `Drain`, `Refusal`, `Record`, `RecordOf`); `D15-website-check` what an enrich pass checks and records, its due rule, its batches and what its five counts mean; `D04-store` the pass records, their kinds, origins and words, the busy rule in `AddPass`, the redelivery refusal and the prune; `D12-resolution` what `Attach` and `MarkGone` do to candidates and links; `D13-scoring` what `Judge` and `JudgeAll` write, the assessment of a candidate at a time; `D05-sources` the registry and how a source reads a row; `D07-events` the two pass events, their identity and cause, the two names candidates accepts, and `/declarations`; `D09-trail` `sibling.called` and secrecy; `D10-tools` the tools and the sentences a refusal word is shown with; `D03-serve` builds the engine, mounts the delivery and calls `Recover` and `Drain` in its start and stop.

## Starting a pass by `rebuild`

`Rebuild` takes the caller. Once the drain has begun it is refused `stopping`; with no scrapes client (the services file names no `scrapes` entry) `unavailable`, since a pass could read nothing; while an ingest, judge or rebuild is `running`, whoever started it, `busy` (the store refuses the record, so a `rebuild` and a delivery racing cannot both win), a running enrich being no bar; and the database failing is an error matching `store.ErrUnreachable`, which the tool answers `unavailable`. Otherwise the pass is recorded with kind `rebuild`, origin `rebuild`, trigger `rebuild` and the caller as its starter, `pass.started` is told, the work begins in a goroutine, and `Rebuild` answers the record, `running`, without waiting: the MCP gateway gives a call fifty seconds and a pass over 17,000 rows may take longer.

## Starting a pass by `enrich`

`Enrich` takes the caller and the age override the `enrich` tool was given, nil when none (`D10-tools`). Once the drain has begun it is refused `stopping`; with no checker (`Config.Website` nil, which `D15-website-check` says when) `unavailable`; with a negative override `invalid`, though the tool refuses one before it calls; and while another enrich is `running`, whoever started it, `busy`. An ingest, judge or rebuild running is no bar: an enrich reads only the candidates' display emails and writes only enrichments, which no other pass writes (decisions document, Concurrency). The database failing is an error matching `store.ErrUnreachable`. Otherwise the pass is recorded with kind `enrich`, origin `enrich`, trigger `enrich` and the caller as its starter, `pass.started` is told, the work begins in a goroutine and `Enrich` answers the record, `running`: some 7,000 domains at the website check's pace take about an hour. The override is not stored on the pass; the record has no field for it.

## Passes started by a pass

Two endings start a pass, its **follow-on**, with origin `pass` and the starting pass's id as its trigger (decisions log A1):

- an ingest recorded `done` starts an enrich, so a run's new candidates are checked; while an enrich is already running the store refuses the record and nothing is started or recorded, not even a failed pass, since the running enrich re-reads the due set batch by batch (decisions log P8);
- an enrich recorded `done` with at least one result written (its `https`, `http` and `none` summing to one or more) starts a judge, so the new evidence and facts show at once; while an ingest, judge or rebuild is running the store refuses it, nothing is recorded, and the next day's judge picks the results up.

Nothing else starts a follow-on: not a judge, a rebuild, a failed pass, an enrich that wrote nothing, an ingest when there is no checker, or anything once the drain has begun. A follow-on is started as no person: no user, the starter's request id, and on its events the starter's event cause, so the trail of a delivered `scrape.finished` runs through the ingest, the enrich and the judge. Its `pass.started` follows its starter's `pass.finished`.

## Starting a pass by a delivery

candidates accepts exactly two event names (`D07-events`): `scrape.finished` (`trail.Accepted`), which starts an ingest pass, and `cron.judge_candidates.fired` (`trail.AcceptedJudge`), the daily fire of the cron trigger `judge_candidates`, which starts a judge pass. They are exact names, as scrapes accepts its triggers' `cron.scrape_<scraper>.fired`: the events app delivers only on `*` or an exact name. appkit's delivery handler hands either to `Deliver` with the bus event's id and depth already on the context as its cause, and the caller appkit's telemetry middleware built from the request: the events app's request id, or one minted for it, and no user. `Deliver` tells the two apart by the event's name; there is no second entry point.

### An ingest delivery

The bus event carries scrapes' attributes `scraper`, `run`, `status`, `reason` and the counts; the engine reads `scraper`, `run` and `status` and the event's id.

A delivery is skipped (`Skip`, nothing to retry) when it is neither a delivery for candidates nor a judge delivery: an event of another name, one with no id, a `scrape.finished` whose `status` is not `done`, one whose `scraper` the registry does not hold (decision 3), or one with no `run`. It is skipped too when a pass the store holds already carries its event id as its delivery or already names its run as its trigger, whatever became of that pass: redelivery is safe, and a second `scrape.finished` for one run under another event id is the same run (decision 11). That check reads only candidates' own records, so a redelivery is skipped even while scrapes cannot be reached.

Otherwise the delivery reads the run's record from scrapes before it answers (orchestrator ruling): a run scrapes does not know (404, unknown or pruned) or a record whose status is not `done` is skipped. Any other failure of the read, scrapes unavailable, a dead socket, another status, an answer that is not a record, or no scrapes client at all, is a pass that failed: the pass is recorded, failed `scrapes` with its counts 0, `pass.started` and `pass.finished` told, and `OK` answered, so the bus does not retry and the failure is on the record where a person sees it; a later delivery of the same run is skipped as a redelivery, and a rebuild repairs. A record that is read and `done` is recorded as an ingest pass of origin `run`, with the run's id as its trigger and the bus event's id as its delivery, `pass.started` told and `OK` answered, all before the rows are read. While an ingest, judge or rebuild runs the store refuses the record and the delivery answers `Fail`, a running enrich being no bar: a pass takes seconds and the bus retries for minutes, so a run landing during a pass is resolved after it, not lost (decision 11). Stopping and the database failing answer `Fail` as well; `Fail` is kept for those three.

### A judge delivery

A `cron.judge_candidates.fired` with an event id is a judge delivery, whatever its attributes. It reads nothing from scrapes, so it needs no scrapes client. It is skipped when a pass the store holds already carries its event id as its delivery: the redelivery check asks the store with the event id and no run, since a cron slug fires many times and only the event id names one fire. Otherwise the pass is recorded with kind `judge`, origin `cron`, trigger `judge_candidates` (`store.TriggerJudge`) and the bus event's id as its delivery, `pass.started` told and `OK` answered once it is recorded, the judging left to the background. The store lets one trigger stand on many judge passes; only a run id is unique as a trigger (`D04-store`). While an ingest, judge or rebuild runs the delivery answers `Fail` with `passes.Busy`, as an ingest delivery does, and the bus retries: the judge waits for the ingest, and a fire is not lost. Stopping and the database failing answer `Fail`.

A pass a delivery started is started as the delivery: its request id is the delivery's, and its user is whatever user the delivery's request named, which is none; the starter shown is what the trigger says, scrapes and the run for an ingest pass (decision 11) and the cron slug for a judge pass, as scrapes' run started by a cron fire shows `cron` and its slug.

## Identity on the read

scrapes' read path requires an identity, and appkit's `scrapes` client forwards the caller on the call's context. A pass a `rebuild` started reads scrapes as its caller: the request carries the caller's user id, email and request id, as every sibling call forwards the caller. A delivery carries no person, so every read an ingest delivery makes, its record read and its pass's rows, is made as the **service identity** (decision 12): `X-User-Id` `candidates`, no `X-User-Email`, and the delivery's request id, with the delivered bus event as the cause of the pass's events. scrapes trusts any process at its socket, so the read is answered and its trail names `candidates` as the user (decision 21). It is the suite's first service-to-service call made as no person, and recorded as such below. A judge delivery makes no read at all.

## The work

A pass's work runs in a goroutine and is cancelled by the drain and by nothing else: the call or delivery that started it may end while it runs. Its runs are, for an ingest pass, the run the record named; for a rebuild, each registered scraper's current run (`fmcsa`, then `ppp`), read with `Current` in the registry's order, a scraper with no current run (scrapes answers 404) contributing no rows and marking nothing gone; and for a judge pass, none. For each run the work streams the rows (`Rows`) and has the source the registry holds for the run's scraper read each one (`D05-sources`), handing the store a batch of `passes.BatchSize` observations at a time, as they arrive, so a run of any size passes through with one batch held (decision 1). Once a run's stream has ended, and the number of rows it handed equals the record's `rows`, the links of that scraper the run no longer holds are marked gone. A rebuild treats every row as changed, so normalized forms, keys and readings are recomputed while candidates, their ids and the rulings stay (decision 11). Rulings are applied throughout, by the store (`D12-resolution`).

An ingest pass stops there: it writes links and the candidates' display, and no estimate or assessment; a candidate it creates has none until a judge, and a candidate whose rows it changed keeps the judgment the last judge wrote. A judge pass and, after its runs, a rebuild call `JudgeAll` once: every active candidate's estimates and assessment are rewritten from every link not gone, from every source, measured at one time the store's clock reads, the judge's date (`D13-scoring`). So a filing's `fmcsa_active` weight is the one it had on the day of the last judge, and between judges nothing decays. A judge started by an enrich is a judge pass like the cron's.

An enrich pass's work is the website check's (`D15-website-check`): it selects the due candidates in keyset batches of `passes.BatchSize` by candidate id, one batch held at a time, and never selects one candidate twice within a pass, so a domain whose fetch failed is not retried until the next pass. It has no runs, makes no request to scrapes, and writes only enrichments, never a candidate, link, estimate or assessment. Fetching nothing is not a failure: a domain that answers nothing is a `none` result.

The pass then finishes `done` with its fourteen counts: `rows` the rows streamed, and the rest the sums of what the store's writes answered (`new`, `changed`, `unchanged`, `created`, `linked` from the batches, `gone` from the marking, `judged` and `unranged` from `JudgeAll`, 0 for an ingest pass), and for an enrich `due`, `fetched`, `https`, `http` and `none` as `D15-website-check` pins them, 0 for every other kind. What each counts is the owning document's.

## Cost

A pass costs in proportion to its rows (orchestrator ruling). A pass over the real FMCSA run of 16,706 rows never finished: 1,970 rows took 82 seconds and a re-scrape with every row unchanged four minutes, the pages timing out meanwhile, because every batch reloaded and compared the whole database and every row re-derived every candidate. Now each batch is one write transaction touching its own rows and the candidates they attach to, by key (`D04-store`); an unchanged row costs a lookup, a touch of its last seen, and its share of its candidate's display re-derived once per batch; and the gone-marking is one bounded statement per `MarkGone` call, over that call's scraper's links last seen in a run other than that call's run, after which the display of each candidate it touched is re-derived by key from that candidate's own links. An ingest pass judges nothing, so its cost is its rows alone. A judge covers every active candidate by definition, from each candidate's record, its own links and rulings, and the ZIP table's minutes for its display ZIP: a full judge of the 16,580 candidates on `us` took 18 seconds. A pass's work holds no lock of the engine's while it calls the store, and a read of the store answers while a batch is being written (`D04-store`), so the pages and the tools answer throughout.

What is required is the time: a delivery of a **generated run** of 10,000 rows into an empty store, a second delivery of a run alike to it with every row unchanged, and a rebuild over that run, which judges its 5,000 candidates, each finish `done` within 120 seconds, under the race detector the gate runs, with the counts the attach rule gives. The work the pass asks of scrapes is counted too: one rows read per run (R-RO1G-CX1A).

A pass ends `failed` with one reason (decision 11):

- `interrupted`: the drain cancelled the work before it ended, whatever else then failed;
- `store`: a write the work handed the store failed, `JudgeAll` included; for an enrich, any call of the store failed, a read of the due set included;
- `scrapes`: any other failure, which an enrich, reading nothing from scrapes, never has: a read of scrapes that failed or answered something that is not a record or a row, a current run not `done`, or a stream whose row count disagrees with its record's `rows`, a stream cut short at a line boundary being told this way (appkit's `scrapes` client leaves that check to its caller).

A failed pass keeps its record and its counts at 0 and leaves in place every batch it had already handed the store; the next pass or a rebuild repairs them, since every write is idempotent. A stream that failed marks nothing gone, so a short read never takes evidence away. A judge that fails leaves the judgments as `JudgeAll` left them (`D13-scoring`); its delivery was already answered, so the next day's fire, or a rebuild, judges again. `pass.finished` is told once the ending is recorded; an ending the store cannot record tells no event and leaves the pass `running` for the next start to settle, as scrapes leaves a run.

## Starting and stopping

`Recover` runs at start, before ready (`D03-serve`): every pass still `running` was cut off by a dead process and is failed `interrupted`, its `pass.finished` told as its starter with no cause. A running enrich is settled so too, as every kind is; the next enrich finds what is still due. `Drain` is the stop: from its call `Rebuild` and `Enrich` refuse `stopping`, `Deliver` answers `Fail` and no pass starts a follow-on, and a call already under way that has not recorded its pass records none; every running work is cancelled at once, its read of scrapes or its fetches ended, and its pass failed `interrupted` (decision 11). `Drain` returns once every pass the engine started has ended, or when its context ends, which `D03-serve` sets to the drain deadline, `DRAIN_SECONDS` after the signal.

## The sweep

Once before ready, after `Recover`, and then each time a wait of `cli.SweepInterval` on `Process.After` ends, the passes past keeping are pruned with `store.PrunePasses` and `CANDIDATES_KEEP_PASSES`: the newest that many `done` passes are kept and, separately, the newest that many `failed` passes, so a page still shows what went wrong; a `running` pass is never pruned (decision 16). A pruned pass is gone from the tools and the pages; candidates, links, estimates, assessments and rulings are never pruned. A prune that fails is silent and tried again at the next interval. The website check waits on the same `Process.After`, with its own durations (`D15-website-check`); only a wait of `cli.SweepInterval` prunes.

## The pass record

The record the tools answer is `passes.Record`, made from a stored pass by `passes.RecordOf`: `id`, `kind`, `origin`, `trigger`, `status`, `reason` when failed, `started`, `finished` once ended, the fourteen counts with `judged` where `rescored` stood and the five enrich counts after `unranged`, always present as every count is, and the starter: `user` and `email` for a pass of origin `rebuild` or `enrich`, nothing for a delivery, whose trigger names the run or the cron slug, or for a follow-on, whose trigger names its starter. The delivery and the request id are not part of it. The record is a tool's and a page's answer, not a persisted event payload, so it renames `rescored` to `judged` at once; the pass events keep `rescored` beside `judged` for one release (`D07-events`).

## Refusals and failures

A refusal is a `*passes.Refusal`, a word. All of candidates' refusal words are declared in `internal/passes` (`D01-layout-and-run-seam`), so the tools tell every refusal apart with one `errors.As` (`D10-tools`): `not_found`, `invalid`, `busy`, `stopping` and `unavailable` (decision 14). The engine answers `busy`, `stopping` and `unavailable`, and `invalid` for a negative age given to `Enrich`; `not_found` and `invalid` are otherwise the tools' (`D10-tools`), and the database failing reaches them as an error matching `store.ErrUnreachable`, which they answer `unavailable`. A delivery's `Fail` carries a fixed message, a copy constant naming no value: `passes.Stopping` while stopping, `passes.Busy` while an ingest, judge or rebuild runs, `store.Unreachable` when the database fails.

## Recorded decisions

- **Ingesting and judging are separate passes** (decisions document, Ingest and judge): an ingest pass stores rows and judges nothing; a judge pass judges every active candidate and reads nothing; a rebuild ingests every current run and then judges, as one pass of kind `rebuild`, so its record says what started it and a judge never runs between its ingests. Nothing else computes a judgment but an owner's ruling, which judges what it touches in the same call (`D13-scoring`).
- **The judge runs daily, not on change and not on read** (decisions document, decision 26). The candidates whose `fmcsa_active` weight is moving are those whose filing nobody updated, so their rows never change: 2,431 of the 16,706 active MN and WI carriers are in the two-to-five-year band and 2,663 change weight within 30 days, so judging only the candidates a pass touched would never revisit them. Judging on read would make the first read after a quiet stretch wait for a full judge and put a staleness check on every read path, to save 18 seconds of CPU a day. A daily cron trigger is the pattern the suite already has, and it keeps a stored judgment within a day of true.
- **No pass refreshes** (decisions document, decision 25): the SQL statement that rewrote every score at each pass's end, on failure and at start, and its agreement test with Go, are gone, so the judgment formulas live once, in Go (`D13-scoring`). A pass's ending writes only its record, whatever the ending, and `Recover` writes nothing but the endings it settles.
- **A judge delivery is skipped only by its event id**: a cron slug fires many times, so the trigger `judge_candidates` stands on many passes, and the redelivery check asks the store with the event id and no run. A judge delivery needs no scrapes client and reads nothing, so it is recorded even when the services file names no `scrapes` entry.
- **A judge fire during another pass fails `busy`** (as an ingest delivery does, rather than skipping): the bus retries for minutes and the pass takes seconds, so the judge runs after the ingest and the day's judgment is not lost; a fire whose retries are spent is caught by the next day's.
- **A judge delivery is answered once its pass is recorded**, the judging left to the background as an ingest pass's rows are. That the answer comes before the judge ends is stated here, not required: unlike an ingest, whose first read a fake scrapes can hold, a judge has no seam a permitted test can hold open, and `JudgeAll` over a test's store ends in milliseconds.
- **The service identity on a delivery's reads** (decision 12, binding): `X-User-Id` `candidates`, the service's own name, no email, the delivery's request id. It is the suite's first service-to-service call made as no person. The pass's own starter stays the delivery's caller, as scrapes' delivery-started run keeps the delivery's caller, so the pass's events carry the delivery's request id and no user (`D07-events`) while its reads' `sibling.called` carry `candidates` (`D09-trail`).
- **The record is read before the delivery is answered** (orchestrator ruling, superseding coordinator decision C1): an unknown run or one not `done` is skipped; any other read failure records a pass failed `scrapes` and answers `OK`, so a scrapes outage shows as a failed pass rather than as retries and a paused subscriber, and `Fail` stays reserved for busy, stopping and the database. A delivery with no scrapes client is treated as an unavailable read. The redelivery check comes first and reads only the store, so a retry of a run already passed is skipped without asking scrapes.
- **One ingest, judge or rebuild at a time, refused by the store** (`D04-store`): a rebuild during one is `busy`, a delivery `Fail`; an enrich is no bar to either. scrapes' delivery skips when busy; candidates fails, because a lost `scrape.finished` is a lost month of evidence (decisions document, Rejected).
- **A scraper with no current run contributes nothing to a rebuild** rather than failing it: the registry may hold a scraper whose first run has not landed, and the rebuild still judges every candidate. A current run that is not `done` fails `scrapes`, since scrapes answers only `done` runs as current.
- **The line count is the stream's check** (decision 2): appkit's client cannot tell a stream cut at a line boundary from a whole one; the pass compares the rows handed with the record's `rows` and fails `scrapes` on a mismatch, before marking anything gone.
- **The batch size, 500, is a constant of the rules version** (decision 17), declared as `passes.BatchSize`; a failed pass keeps every full batch it handed over. Whether a partial last batch of a failed stream is kept is left open: the next pass repairs either way.
- **Precedence of endings**: a work the drain cancelled is `interrupted` whatever then failed; a refused write is `store`; anything else is `scrapes`. The drain cancels at once, as scrapes' does: the pass cannot be resumed, and the next delivery or a rebuild repairs it.
- **An ending the store cannot record leaves the pass `running`**, as scrapes leaves a run, for the next start's `Recover` to fail `interrupted`; until then every new pass is refused as busy. Retrying the ending is not designed: the database failing is a broken host.
- **No queue**: one ingest, judge or rebuild at a time, and one enrich beside it, is the whole concurrency rule, and the bus's retries are the queue. An ingest that ends while an enrich runs starts nothing and records nothing (decisions log P8): the running enrich re-reads the due set per batch, and the next ingest or an `enrich` call covers what it did not select.
- **An enrich runs beside the other kinds** (decisions document, Concurrency): it reads only display emails and writes only enrichments, so it cannot disturb an ingest, a judge or a rebuild, and an hour-long check would otherwise hold off a day's deliveries. Two enrich passes never overlap: they would fetch the same domains twice. The store keeps both rules in `AddPass` (`D04-store`).
- **Origins `enrich` and `pass`** (decisions log A1): `enrich` mirrors `rebuild`, the tool naming itself; `pass` with the starter's id as trigger says which pass led to which.
- **A follow-on carries no person**: no one asked for it, so it has no user; it keeps its starter's request id and event cause, the honest provenance and the one a delivery's pass already has.
- **"At least one new result" is at least one result written**: every result an enrich writes is a new row (`D15-website-check`), and its `https`, `http` and `none` sum to the results written, so the rule reads off the pass's own counts.
- **An enrich's judge is refused, not queued, while an ingest, judge or rebuild runs** (decisions document, Concurrency): the daily judge picks the results up, so nothing is lost. This departs from the document's word "no other pass is running": an enrich recorded after this enrich ended and before its judge is recorded does not stop the judge, since the store's busy rule admits a judge beside an enrich (`D04-store`) and closing that window would need a lock the store does not have. It is harmless: an enrich writes only results, never what a judge writes, and R-RSX1-W002 already excludes from the judge's guarantee any `AddEnrichments` made during it; the next judge reads the later results.
- **An enrich fails `store` on any store failure**, a read included, where the other kinds fail `store` only on a refused write: an enrich has no other failing sibling, a failed fetch being a `none` result, so `scrapes` would mislead.
- **`Enrich` refuses a negative age itself**, though the `enrich` tool refuses it first, so the engine's contract does not lean on its caller.
- **The sweeper and the website check share `Process.After`** rather than each having a field, told apart by the duration each asks for; only a `cli.SweepInterval` wait prunes.
- **Results recorded during a judge are excluded from the judge's guarantees**: a judge reads each candidate's newest result (`D13-scoring`), so R-RQH9-4GIO and R-RSX1-W002 hold only when no `AddEnrichments` of another pass came between a pass's recording and its ending, as they already did for `Decide`.
- **The sweep's requirements are stated here, at the run seam**, as scrapes states its own; the prune is the store's (`D04-store`).
- **A failed prune is described, not required, at the run seam.** A store failure is provoked only with `SetFailing(true)` on a handle the test hands in (candidates/AGENTS.md), and `cli.Run` builds its store over its own handle, so no permitted test can fail a prune under `Run`. What is required is the store's half, that a failing `PrunePasses` answers `ErrUnreachable` and removes nothing (`D04-store`, R-OD8H-2EDG), and the sweep's, that the next interval prunes again (R-S3W5-BXOB); that the start goes on to ready and the failure is silent is this overview's.
- **The bound is 120 seconds for 10,000 rows** (orchestrator ruling): a probe of a keyed pass, SQL by key on decision 16's indexes over appkit's `db`, took 14 to 23 seconds per pass of 5,000 rows under `go test -race` and about one second without it on the developer's machine. At that rate 10,000 rows take about 30 to 45 seconds under the race detector, leaving the build's fuller work and a loaded machine room inside 120 seconds, and 16,706 real rows well under a minute without it: about 3.4 seconds at the probe's rate of about one second per 5,000 rows, and at most about 13 seconds even at the rate the bound allows, 120 seconds per 10,000 rows under the race detector divided by the probe's race factor of about 15, times 1.67, inside decision 20's minute. The store this replaces took 82 seconds for 1,970 rows, four minutes for a re-scrape with every row unchanged, and had not finished the real 16,706 rows after 14 minutes, each without the race detector. 10,000 rows rather than 5,000 makes a cost growing with the square of the rows four times as visible. A re-scrape with every row unchanged and a rebuild are held to the same bound, since the first was the slowest case seen and the second is the repair every failure points to; the rebuild's judge of 5,000 candidates is inside it, a full judge of 16,580 real candidates having taken 18 seconds.
- **The writes of a pass** are R-RP9C-QORZ: an ingest pass's are its batches and its marking alone, a judge pass's one `JudgeAll`, a rebuild's both, the `JudgeAll` last, and an enrich pass's only the results it records (`D15-website-check`); R-RQH9-4GIO requires the records those writes leave, R-06IV-MIZ0 that an ingest leaves every assessment as it was, and R-RSX1-W002 that a judge or a rebuild leaves every active candidate's assessment as of one time during its work.
- **A delivery is worked whatever run is current**: a delivery of an older run after a newer one marks gone what the older run lacks; the next delivery or a rebuild repairs it.

## Consumer tasks
