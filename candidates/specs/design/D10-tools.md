# D10-tools

The seventeen MCP tools agents reach candidates by through the MCP gateway: `internal/tools` (decision 14). An agent lists the owner's cohorts with `cohorts`, reads one with `cohort` (its definition, `matching`, its members in rank order and its standing rejects), and keeps them with `create_cohort`, `edit_cohort` and `delete_cohort`; it keeps a candidate out of one cohort with `reject` and lets it back with `unreject`; it finds candidates by name, ZIP or phone with `candidates`, reads one with its evidence, estimates, assessment, facts, rulings, kept pages, enrichment results of every kind and reject records with `candidate`, reads the passes with `passes` and `pass`, starts a pass over every registered scraper's current run with `rebuild`, starts an enrich pass with `enrich`, over every candidate due one, of one kind or of every kind but site text, or over one cohort's members or one candidate, of one kind, keeps a page of a candidate's website with `add_page`, records a merge, split or not-same ruling with `decide`, and excludes a candidate from every cohort with `exclude` or brings it back with `include` (the cohorts change, its decision 17). Every signed-in caller sees and may change everything: the evidence, the rulings, the cohorts and the rejects are the space's, not a user's, so no read answer depends on who asks, and the caller a tool is handed is used only to start a rebuild or an enrich as that caller (`D06-passes`), which a targeted site text pass forwards to prompts, and to record who made a ruling, who added a page, who created or last changed a cohort, and who rejected or un-rejected. The pass record is `D06-passes`' (`passes.Record`), answered as that document encodes it. What a cohort is, its filters, its order, its members and `matching`, and what a standing reject is, are `D14-cohorts`'; what each store method answers is `D04-store`'s; which kinds an enrich pass walks and in what order is `D06-passes`'; what each kind takes as its inputs and what it finds is `D16-enrichers`', and what the website check and `add_page` fetch, and how a page's host is held to the candidate's website, is `D15-website-check`'s; what a page is and how its copies are kept is `D04-store`'s; and how the facts row projects the newest result of each kind, which `GetAssessment` and `ReadCohort` answer the facts from, is `D04-store`'s. This document states the tools' arguments and schemas, how each call maps onto one store method, the words a store error becomes, and the answers' shapes.

## The tools

- `cohorts` takes optional `page` and `limit` and answers `cohorts`, the held cohorts' definitions in ascending byte order of name and then of id, `limit` at a time with `next` when more remain (Paging, below). Read-kind.
- `cohort` takes `cohort` and answers `cohort` (the definition), `matching`, `members` and `rejects`, as `ReadCohort` answers (`D04-store`), the store reading its own clock once for the filing-age filter. A cohort is read whole: its size is at most 100, so it is not paged. Each member is the candidate with its display fields and tags, its operating confidence, revenue range and the confidence in it, its category names, fleet verdict, website state and the URL it landed on, when site text was last written for it (`null` when never), drive minutes, government match, known-since date, newest filing and whether it is excluded. Read-kind.
- `candidates` takes optional `name` (a case-insensitive substring of any of its names), `zip`, `phone` (matched on the phone key), `page` and `limit`, and answers `candidates`, active candidates newest first, `limit` at a time with `next`. Read-kind.
- `candidate` takes `candidate` and answers it with its links, estimates, assessment (its two judgments, operating confidence with the evidence behind it and the revenue range with its confidence, and the owner's exclusion, the rules version and when it was calculated as the last judge wrote them, beside its facts as the facts row holds them at the read: known-since date, newest filing, categories with their sources, government match, fleet verdict with the power units and drivers behind it, website state and the URL it landed on, and drive minutes), rulings, the pages kept on it (the newest copy of each URL, newest first, each with its number, URL, title, size read, when it was fetched, who added it and the path of its text, never the text itself), its enrichment results of every kind newest first (each with its kind, source, inputs and cost and when; a website check's with its state, landed URL, title, status code, size read, last error and domain; a derived kind's, and a site text result's, with its `found` object, which for site text names the prompts run, how it ended, the pages the company held when it ended and its cost in nano-dollars), and reject records, every reject and un-reject naming it, each with its cohort's name while the cohort exists; a merged id answers the merged record with its `merged_into`, no links, no estimates, no assessment, no pages, no results, and its rulings and reject records. Read-kind.
- `passes` takes optional `page` and `limit` and answers `passes`, pass records in pass order (`D04-store`), `limit` at a time with `next`; `pass` takes `pass` and answers its record. Read-kind.
- `rebuild` takes nothing and answers the pass record it started, `running`; the work lands in the background (`D06-passes`). Additive.
- `enrich` takes optional `kind`, one of `drive_minutes`, `link_facts`, `website_check` and `site_text`, and optional `cohort`, `candidate` and `force`, and answers the pass record it started, `running`, as `rebuild` does. Without a target it is the corpus enrich the previous release had: with a `kind` the pass runs that kind alone, and with none, or an empty one, every kind but site text, the derived ones first and the website check only when the engine has a checker (`D06-passes`); `site_text` and `force` are refused there. With a `cohort` or a `candidate`, never both, and a `kind`, it starts a pass of kind `enrich_cohort` over the cohort's members at the call (`D14-cohorts`) or the one candidate, running that kind for those that are due, or for every one of them with `force`; site text runs the caller's `site-text` prompt in prompts once per company (`D06-passes`). A candidate is due a kind when its inputs for it are not empty and it has no result of the kind or its newest one was computed from other inputs (`D04-store`). `older_than_days`, which an earlier release took, is no longer a member. A corpus enrich is `busy` only while another corpus enrich runs, and a targeted one only while another runs over the same target (`D06-passes`). Additive: a run adds results and never removes one.
- `add_page` takes `candidate` and `url`, fetches the URL itself with the website check's fetcher and rules (`D15-website-check`), and keeps the page on the candidate, its title, its text and the size read, with the caller as the one who added it; it answers the page it kept without the text, whose text is served at the page's own path (`D08-pages`). The URL's host must be the candidate's website host or a subdomain of it, and a candidate keeps at most `store.PageCap` distinct URLs; adding a URL it holds keeps a newer copy. It ties the page to no pass and no prompts run, so any signed-in caller may call it, an agent inside a prompts run or the owner's own. Additive: it adds a copy and never removes one.
- `decide` takes `kind`, `why` and `candidate`, and by kind `other` (`merge`, `not_same`) or `links` (`split`); it records the ruling with the caller as its maker and answers `ruling` and `candidates`, the candidates as they stand after it (`store.Decide`, `D12-resolution`). `exclude` and `include` are no longer its kinds. Additive.
- `exclude` and `include` take `candidate` and `why` and record the owner's exclusion or inclusion through the same `store.Decide`, answering as `decide` does. The ruling re-judges the candidate in the same call, so it leaves or rejoins every cohort that does not show excluded candidates at once. Additive.
- `create_cohort` takes `name` and optional `filters`, `order` and `size` (the default size, 30, when absent, `D14-cohorts`) and answers the new cohort's definition; called twice it makes two cohorts. Additive.
- `edit_cohort` takes `cohort` and any of `name`, `filters`, `order` and `size`; a passed `filters` or `order` replaces the whole of it (`{}` clears every filter, `[]` sets the default order), an absent or null one keeps it, and the answer is the definition after the call. An edit that changes nothing writes nothing and answers the definition as it was. Additive.
- `delete_cohort` takes `cohort` and answers the definition it removed; its reject records stay. Destructive.
- `reject` and `unreject` take `cohort`, `candidate` and `why` and answer the standing record for the pair after the call; repeating either writes nothing. Additive.

## Paging

`candidates`, `passes` and `cohorts` page as the suite's listing convention asks (`shape/`, Listing and Storage), on the contract of appkit's `paging` package (`appkit/paging`, appkit's D25), which this document cites and never restates. Each input declares `page`, an opaque string, and `limit`, an integer, as pointer fields carrying the shared descriptions `paging.PageDescription` and `paging.LimitDescription`; each answer is its list then `next`, an opaque string present exactly when at least one entry follows, whose field's tag carries `paging.NextDescription`. An absent, null or empty `page` is the first page, and `next` is handed back as `page` unchanged; an absent or null `limit` is `paging.DefaultLimit`.

Each tool's token is `paging.Token{Tool: <its name>}` over the key of the last entry the page answered, in the order the store windows by (`D04-store`): for `candidates` the candidate's created time as RFC 3339 in UTC to the second and its id; for `passes` the pass's started time so formatted and its id; for `cohorts` the cohort's name and its id. All three keys are two strings. The tool asks the store for one entry more than the page, from the decoded position, and sets `next` only when the extra entry came back. A token binds no filter. A token one of the three issued is refused by the other two, because the checksum covers the tool's name.

Before the store is read, a `limit` outside 1 to `paging.MaxLimit` is refused with the text of `paging.Window`'s error, then a `page` the tool's token does not decode with `paging.BadPage`; each refusal is that text alone, not one of the five words below, and is the same while the database is unreachable. Paging records nothing: no token appears in any event or log line (`D09-trail`).

## Arguments

Every argument type is typed, so appkit derives each tool's input schema and refuses malformed arguments before the handler runs. A cohort's filters are one nested object, `filters`, whose members carry the cohorts document's spellings: `revenue_min`, `revenue_max`, `max_minutes` and `max_filing_age_days` as non-negative integers, `categories` as a list of names, `fleet` and `class` as strings, `government` as a boolean that may be absent, and the switches and `show_excluded` as booleans, and `website` as a pointer to a list of states, so that absent or null is no filter and a passed `[]` an empty list, with its switch `website_unknown`. `enrich`'s arguments are flat: `kind`, `cohort` and `candidate`, strings, each absent and empty alike, and `force`, a boolean, absent and false alike. `add_page`'s are `candidate` and `url`, both required strings. An order is a list of entries, each an object with `field` and `direction`, both required. On both tools `filters` and `order` are pointers, so an absent or null member reaches the handler as nil and a passed `{}` or `[]` as an empty value; `size` is a pointer to an `int`.

The handler converts the arguments to the store's types (the filter set and the order list) and checks them with the store's validators before it reads the store: what each validator accepts is `D14-cohorts`' validation set, and a value it refuses is `invalid`. The schema already refuses a member `filters` does not name, an `order` entry missing `field` or `direction` or naming another member, and a negative number in `filters`. A `website` list's states, and that it is not empty and repeats none, are the store's validator's to check (`D14-cohorts`); an absent `website` reaches the store as nil and a passed `[]` as an empty list, which is `invalid`.

## Answers

Times are RFC 3339 UTC to the second; a date (a filing date, the known-since date, the newest filing) is `YYYY-MM-DD`, absent when there is none. Money is whole dollars. A confidence or an evidence weight is the stored `float64` itself, never rounded at the call. Missing is never zero: a candidate with no evidence answers no `operating_confidence`, one with no estimate no `revenue_low`, `revenue_high` or `revenue_confidence`, one with no known date no `known_since`, one with no FMCSA counts no `power_units` or `drivers`, and one whose rows give no category an empty `categories`. `website` is always present, `unknown` before the candidate's first website check result, and `website_url` is absent unless the state is `https` or `http`. A website check result's `url`, `title` and `error` are absent when empty, its `status_code` when no response came, and it carries no `found`; any other kind's result carries `found`, the object it stored (`{}` included), and none of the website members. Every judgment answered (operating confidence and its evidence, revenue range, exclusion) is the one the store holds, written by the last judge of the candidate (`D13-scoring`, `D06-passes`); every fact (dates, categories, government match, fleet verdict and counts, website, drive minutes) is the facts row's at the read, written by the newest result of its kind (`D04-store`).

A cohort's definition answers its `filters` exactly as the store holds them: the store's own filter type encoded with its members omitted when empty, so a cohort with no filters answers `{}`, and a stored `government` of false is present. Its `order` is never empty, since the store keeps the default order explicitly (`D04-store`). `created_by` and `changed_by` are user ids, beside `created_email` and `changed_email`. A member carries `excluded` always, since a cohort that shows excluded candidates lists them beside the rest. A reject record carries its cohort's name while the cohort exists, and omits `why`, `user`, `email` and `at` in the one answer that has none: an `unreject` of a pair that has no standing reject, which writes nothing.

A kept page answers its `number`, the store's number of that copy, its `url`, the URL the fetch landed on, its `title`, absent when empty, its `size`, its `fetched` time, `by` and `email`, the user who added it, and `path`, the candidate's page path for that number; it never carries the text, which the page path serves. A candidate's `pages` are every URL it holds, the newest copy of each, newest first, `[]` when none and for a merged candidate. A member's `site_text` is always present: the time the site text kind was last written for the member, `null` when never. A site text result answers its stored `found` as any non-website kind does.

A candidate is answered whole, phone and email included: a tool's answer is product, given to a signed-in caller, and the secrecy of decision 18 governs the trail, the bus, refusals and the logs (`D09-trail`), not the answer.

## Refusals

Apart from the two paging refusals above, a refusal is an MCP error result whose text is `<word>: <sentence>`. The words are the five `D01-layout-and-run-seam` declares as `passes.Refusal*`: `not_found`, `invalid`, `busy`, `stopping` and `unavailable`. Each sentence is a copy constant, `tools.Refuse*`: one paired with each word, used unless a requirement names another, and four more for `add_page`, `RefuseNoWebsite`, `RefuseOffSite`, `RefusePageCap`, which names the cap, and `RefuseFetch`, which opens a fetch failure's text. A refusal names no id, business name, cohort name, address, phone, email or reason the store supplied (decision 18), with two exceptions, recorded below: an `enrich` refused for a missing prompt carries the engine's text, naming the prompt and quoting prompts' refusal, and an `add_page` whose fetch failed carries the failure.

- `candidate` and `pass`: an id that is not well formed, and an unknown or pruned one, are `not_found`.
- `cohort` and `delete_cohort`: a malformed cohort id is `not_found` without reading the store; then an unknown or deleted one is `not_found`.
- `create_cohort`: a name, size, filter set or order a validator refuses is `invalid`, before the store.
- `edit_cohort`: a passed name, size, filter set or order a validator refuses is `invalid`; then a malformed cohort id `not_found`; both before the store; then an unknown or deleted cohort `not_found`.
- `reject` and `unreject`: a blank `why` is `invalid`; then a malformed cohort or candidate id `not_found`; both before the store; then what `Reject` refuses: an unknown or deleted cohort or an unknown candidate `not_found`, a merged candidate `invalid`.
- `exclude` and `include`: a blank `why` is `invalid`; then a malformed candidate id `not_found`; both before the store; then what `store.Decide` refuses.
- `decide`: a `kind` other than `merge`, `split` and `not_same`, a blank `why`, `other` absent for `merge` or `not_same` and `links` empty for `split` are `invalid`, decided first; then a malformed `candidate` or `other` is `not_found`, without reading the store; then what `store.Decide` refuses.
- `rebuild`: what `Engine.Rebuild` refuses, with its word, and with the engine's text when it carries one (`D06-passes`).
- `enrich`: a non-empty `kind` that names no kind, both `cohort` and `candidate`, `force` with neither, `site_text` with neither, and a target with no `kind` are `invalid`, decided first; then a malformed `cohort` or `candidate` is `not_found`; both without the store. Then, without a target, what `Engine.Enrich` refuses, with its word (`D06-passes`): `stopping` while draining, `unavailable` for `website_check` with no checker, `busy` while a corpus enrich runs; and with one, what `Engine.EnrichCohort` refuses, with its word and its text when it carries one: `stopping`, an unknown or deleted cohort or an unknown candidate `not_found`, a merged candidate `invalid`, a kind its engine cannot run `unavailable`, a caller with no prompt named for the kind `invalid` with a text naming it, and `busy` while a pass over the same target runs. `older_than_days` and any other member the arguments do not name are appkit's schema refusal.
- `add_page`: a malformed `candidate` is `not_found`, then a `url` that is not an absolute `http` or `https` URL with a host `invalid`, then no fetcher `unavailable`, all without the store; then, before fetching, an unknown candidate `not_found`, a candidate with no website, a merged one among them, `invalid` with `RefuseNoWebsite`, a URL off its website `invalid` with `RefuseOffSite`, and a twenty-first distinct URL `invalid` with `RefusePageCap`; then a fetch that fails `unavailable` with `RefuseFetch` and the failure, and one that was redirected off the website `invalid` with `RefuseOffSite`; each keeps nothing.
- `candidates`, `passes` and `cohorts`: the limit refusal, then the page refusal, before the store is read.
- Every tool: the database failing is `unavailable`. Malformed arguments, an unknown member in `filters` or in an order entry and a negative number in `filters` among them, are appkit's schema refusal.

## Descriptions

Every tool's description is one line, a copy constant `tools.Describe<Tool>`, which the gateway's `describe` answers as the tool's first line. `tools.Catalog()` answers each tool's name with that line, in the order `cohorts`, `cohort`, `candidates`, `candidate`, `passes`, `pass`, `rebuild`, `enrich`, `add_page`, `decide`, `exclude`, `include`, `create_cohort`, `edit_cohort`, `delete_cohort`, `reject`, `unreject`, for the tools page (`D08-pages`).

## Recorded decisions

- **Raw registration**, as scrapes: all seventeen tools are registered with appkit's `mcp.AddRawTool` and answer what `encoding/json` writes, the JSON as the one text block and as `structuredContent`; the answer types use `omitempty`, which appkit's typed `mcp.AddTool` refuses, so they carry no output schema, and their JSON is stated here. The argument types are typed: each field carries a `json:"<name>"` tag naming its property, with no `omitempty`, and each required property also `mcp:"required"`, the order entry's `field` and `direction` included; the paged tools' `Page` and `Limit` also carry a `description` tag repeating `paging.PageDescription` and `paging.LimitDescription` word for word, as `spec`'s one exception to copy living in one place allows.
- **The shortlist goes in one release** (the cohorts change, its decision 15): `shortlist`, its arguments, its operating-key token and its answer types leave with no expand period, for the reason the judgments change gave for its answers: a tool's arguments and answers are not persisted and cross no release boundary, and every caller is a model reading the schema `tools/list` gives at the call. The ranked view of everything is a cohort with no filters. A script kept in `repos` that calls `shortlist` breaks when this release is deployed.
- **`exclude` and `include` are tools of their own; `decide` keeps three kinds** (decision 12). Both record the same rulings through the same `store.Decide`, with the same effect and the same re-judge, and answer the same `Decision` `decide` does, so nothing about a ruling changes but the tool that takes it. `decide` refuses `exclude` and `include` as `invalid`, as it refuses any kind it does not take.
- **Filters are one nested typed object; an order is a list of typed entries** (log 15). The cutoffs are pointers to `uint32`: absent is not carried, `0` is a value, and appkit's schema gives each `"minimum":0`. A probe against appkit's released `mcp` at the release candidates requires showed, for the argument types stated here: the nested `filters` and order entry objects get `additionalProperties:false`; a negative cutoff, an unknown member and an entry missing `field` or `direction` are refused by appkit's schema before the handler; `filters` and `order` as pointers reach the handler as nil when absent or null, a passed `{}` as a non-nil empty filter object, and a passed `[]` as a non-nil empty list; `size`, `name` and the nested pointer members likewise reach it as nil when null. So log 8's "absent or null keeps" holds as written for `edit_cohort`. Decision 9 asks for every validation to be `invalid`; the unknown filter name and the negative value are appkit's schema refusal instead, a departure from its word and not its intent (log 15): both are refused before the store, and no caller is left without an answer. Every other check of decision 9 is the handler's `invalid`.
- **`size` has no schema bound**: it is a pointer to `int`, listed as a plain integer, and a value outside 1 to 100 is the handler's `invalid`, by `store.ValidCohortSize`, so the bound lives once, in the store's validator, which the store applies again for a direct caller (log 7).
- **The tools check with the store's validators, the store checks again** (log 7): one rule in one place, so a tool's `invalid` and the store's `ErrInvalid` cannot disagree, and a cohort part the tool lets through never fails at the store for its shape.
- **`edit_cohort` screens shape before the id**, as `decide` does: a passed part the validators refuse is `invalid` whatever the id, then a malformed id is `not_found`. **`reject` and `unreject` follow log 6**: blank `why` first, then the ids' form, then the store's precedence (`D04-store`). A blank `why` or name is one with no character but white space, kept verbatim otherwise (log 7).
- **`RefuseInvalid` is reworded** to a sentence that fits any argument refused `invalid`, a ruling's, a cohort's or a reject's (log 7). The requirement that declares it states no value, so it is re-minted to say what it now covers, and the definition of a refusal that cites it with it (log 29).
- **A member is narrower than the whole assessment** (log 17). Decision 17 says a member carries "its whole assessment, in the shape the shortlist entry had"; the log settles the shortlist entry's fields with `fleet_verdict` in place of `fleet`, plus `known_since`, `newest_filing` and `excluded`, which is what the cohort page's row and the dashboard's row need. The full assessment, its evidence, category sources, power units and drivers included, is `candidate`'s. The member's drive time keeps the assessment's member name `minutes`, while the order field is `drive_minutes` as the cohorts document spells it.
- **`fleet_verdict` replaces `fleet`** on the assessment and on the link (log 10): a string in `fleet` would pass a caller's truthiness check silently, a missing member fails loudly. A link a previous release stored has no verdict on its reading and answers `unknown`. The assessment answers `power_units` and `drivers` beside it (log 20), absent when not known.
- **A cohort's definition encodes the store's own filter type** (log 16), so the answered `filters` is the stored text and an agent reads back exactly the filters that apply. `created_by` and `changed_by` carry the user id, paired with the email as rulings carry `user` and `email`; creating stamps the changed fields equal to the created ones (log 24), so they are always present.
- **`unreject` of a pair with no standing reject** writes nothing and answers a record of kind `unreject` with the cohort, its name and the candidate, and no `why`, `user`, `email` or `at` (log 27): repeating it is harmless and the answer still says the pair is not rejected.
- **`cohorts` pages by name, then id**, in byte order (log 18), so its token is two strings, like the other two lists'. `cohort` is not paged: a cohort is at most 100 members (decision 2).
- **Effects**: the six reads are `mcp.Read`; `rebuild`, `enrich`, `add_page`, `decide`, `exclude`, `include`, `create_cohort`, `edit_cohort`, `reject` and `unreject` are `mcp.Additive`, since each adds a record, a page copy among them, or rewrites a cohort's definition and none removes a record; `delete_cohort` is `mcp.Destructive`, since it removes a cohort (prompts' precedent for a delete).
- **Every number shown is the store's**: the tools compute nothing at the call, so `tools.Config` carries no clock; the one clock a cohort read uses is the store's own, for the filing-age filter (`D14-cohorts`). The candidate answer, the cohort answer and the pages agree: judgments as of the last judge, facts as of the facts row.
- **A link answers its reading's common fields and keys**, so an agent can see why a row matched and what a later pass will match on, and not the estimate, evidence, known-since date, category or government match it carries, which the candidate's estimates and assessment answer.
- **`enrich` follows `rebuild`** in the catalog (log A3): both start a pass and answer its record, so they sit together. **`add_page` follows `enrich`** (site text decision 2), since the site text pass's agent calls it, and the tools page lists seventeen.
- **`older_than_days` leaves the schema** (site text decision 12). The previous release kept it as a member only so that a caller passing it was told `invalid` by the handler; it is now an unknown member like any other, refused by appkit's schema before the handler, whatever its value, and no pass is recorded. That is the departure from decision 9's word, and not its intent, that the nested filters already make (log 15): the call is refused before anything runs and no caller is left without an answer.
- **`enrich` takes its target flat** (site text decision 12): `cohort` and `candidate` are two strings rather than one id whose prefix says which, so the schema tells a caller what each names, and naming both is the handler's `invalid`. `kind` is a plain string, so absent and empty are one value; without a target it still means every kind (but site text, below) and a non-empty one that is not an element of `enrichers.Kinds()` is the handler's `invalid`, which `Enrich` and `EnrichCohort` refuse again for a direct caller. A nil checker makes only `website_check` `unavailable`, and a nil prompts client only `site_text`.
- **The tool decides the shape of a call before the engine** (site text decisions 6, 12 and 14): `site_text` with no target, `force` with no target and a target with no `kind` are `invalid`, and a malformed target `not_found`, all without the store, so a draining engine or a failing store answers them as it answers a malformed ruling. A pass over every candidate would be thousands of paid runs by accident (decision 6); a targeted pass records one kind, its `enrich_kind` (decision 14), and walks exactly that one, so a target with no kind has nothing to walk. The engine refuses each again for a direct caller (`D06-passes`); an unknown target and a merged candidate are its refusals, since they need the store; a merged candidate is `invalid`, as `reject` refuses one, since its pages and results have moved to the survivor.
- **Departure: refusals that carry variable text** (site text decisions 2 and 7): the parent's decision 18 and this document's rule make a refusal a word and a fixed sentence. Two now carry a detail, because the caller needs it to act: the missing prompt names the prompt the caller must create and quotes prompts' refusal, as `D06-passes` builds the engine's `Refusal.Text`, which the tool answers after the word; and a fetch that failed answers `RefuseFetch` and the failure `website.Page.Error` holds (`D15-website-check`), which may quote the URL the caller gave, so the agent can try another URL. `RefusePageCap` names the cap, the one copy constant whose content a requirement constrains. None carries stored business data; `D09-trail` keeps the trail's secrecy over them.
- **`add_page` guards before it fetches** (site text decisions 2 and 3): the host guard, the website it needs and the cap are checked against the store before any request leaves, so a refused URL costs no fetch; the host guard is checked again on the URL the fetch landed on, so a redirect off the site keeps nothing, since only the website's own pages describe the business; the host rule, one leading `www.` ignored and any subdomain accepted, is `website.OnSite`'s (`D15-website-check`). The cap check before the fetch is on the URL given; the store checks the cap again in the write (`store.ErrPageCap`), so two adds racing cannot pass it. A candidate with no facts row, a merged one among them, has no website and is `RefuseNoWebsite`.
- **A page is kept under the URL the fetch landed on** (site text decision 2), as `D15-website-check` hands it: the text is what candidates' fetcher saw at that URL, and two spellings of one page that land on the same URL keep copies of one page, not two. Accepted: the cap check before the fetch sees only the URL given, so at the cap a new spelling that would redirect to a URL the candidate already holds is refused `RefusePageCap` without a fetch; checking before the fetch keeps a refused URL from costing a fetch, and the caller can pass the URL it holds to keep a newer copy.
- **`add_page` answers the page without its text** (site text decision 2): a page's text may be up to `website.BodyCap` of markup stripped to text, which the caller, an agent, did not ask to read back; `path` names where it is served. Neither `add_page` nor `candidate` answers text, so the candidate answer stays small whatever the pages hold.
- **Departure: a candidate's pages are listed whole** (`shape/` Listing asks a list an agent can add to be paged): `candidate`'s `pages` hold the newest copy of each URL, at most `store.PageCap` (20), more only after a merge, as a cohort's size bounds its members. Copies are never listed; the store keeps them.
- **Departure: a member's `site_text` is `null` when never written** (site text decision 18), where every other member fact is absent when unknown: the decision asks for null, and a member that always carries the key tells an agent the cohort answer knows of site text, so its absence is never mistaken for an older release.
- **`add_page` belongs to no pass** (site text decision 4): it works whenever a signed-in caller calls it, inside a prompts run or not, records who added the page, and starts and finishes nothing; the site text pass counts the pages its runs added by what the store holds before and after each run (`D06-passes`).
- **The candidate answer carries every result, of every kind** (website-check decisions 17 and 18, its log A2; enrichers decisions 4 and 5, its settled item 7): `enrichments` is the history, newest first as the store orders it (`D04-store`), so the dashboard's card needs no second call. One answer type serves every kind and is a superset of the previous release's: a website check result answers every member it did, its state, URL, title, status code, size and error decoded from the stored `found` and its `domain` the store's `Source`, plus `source` and `inputs`, and no `found`; a derived result answers `kind`, `source`, `inputs`, its stored `found` object as it is (`{}` when the ZIP table lacks the ZIP), `cost` and `at`. The assessment's facts, `website` and `website_url` among them, are the facts row's at the read, since `GetAssessment` and `ReadCohort` answer them so (`D04-store`), and a member carries the same two, so a cohort row links to the site without a lookup; a new result changes them before any judge runs. The answer types grow by supplement: the assessment, member, enrichment and filter-set definitions add the new fields in requirements of their own beside the definitions they extend, which keep their ids and every requirement that cites them; so do the candidate answer's `pages` (R-OSJW-F07V beside R-RLA3-ZRZI and R-VRRJ-PPU1) and the member answer's `site_text` (R-ORC0-18H6). Only the type declarations whose field lists grow are re-minted.
- **Descriptions are one line**, one sentence of at most 120 characters ending with a full stop, which appkit's `mcp` requires; `DescribeDecide`'s words change with its kinds, which changes no requirement. **Refusal sentences are constants**, so decision 18's secrecy holds by construction for every refusal but the two that carry variable text, above.
