# D10-tools

The seven MCP tools agents reach candidates by through the MCP gateway: `internal/tools` (decision 14). An agent reads the ranked shortlist with `shortlist`, finds candidates by name, ZIP or phone with `candidates`, reads one with its evidence, estimates, assessment and rulings with `candidate`, reads the passes with `passes` and `pass`, starts a pass over every registered scraper's current run with `rebuild`, and records one of the owner's rulings with `decide` (decision 10's tool surface). Every signed-in caller sees every candidate: the evidence and the rulings are the funnel's, not a user's, so no answer depends on who asks, and the caller a tool is handed is used only to start a rebuild as that caller (`D06-passes`) and to record who made a ruling. The pass record is `D06-passes`' (`passes.Record`), and the tools answer it as that document encodes it, its kind, its origin and the candidates it judged included; the candidate, its evidence, its two judgments and its rulings are answered in this document's shapes.

## The tools

- `shortlist` takes optional `max_minutes`, `min_revenue`, `class`, `cargo`, `page` and `limit`, and answers `shortlist`, the shortlist of the filters passed (`D13-scoring`; the store's filter is `D04-store`'s `store.ShortlistFilter`), `limit` at a time with `next` when more remain (Paging, below). Each filter applies only when it is passed: an absent `max_minutes` or `min_revenue` applies no cutoff at all, not a default. With none passed the shortlist is every active candidate that has been judged and that the owner has not excluded, whatever its fleet verdict, government match, drive minutes or revenue. A passed filter leaves out a candidate whose value for it is unknown: `max_minutes` one with no drive minutes, `min_revenue` one with no revenue estimate (it compares `revenue_low`), `class` and `cargo` one with no class or cargo. The order is operating confidence, highest first, those with none last, the candidate id breaking ties. Each entry is the candidate with its display fields and tags, its operating confidence, its revenue range and the confidence in it, its category names, its fleet verdict, its drive minutes and its government match, as the last judge wrote them. Read-kind.
- `candidates` takes optional `name` (a case-insensitive substring of any of its names), `zip`, `phone` (matched on the phone key), `page` and `limit`, and answers `candidates`, active candidates newest first, `limit` at a time with `next`. Read-kind.
- `candidate` takes `candidate` and answers it with its links, estimates, assessment (its two judgments, operating confidence with the evidence behind it and the revenue range with its confidence, beside its known-since date, newest filing, categories with their sources, government match, fleet verdict, drive minutes, the owner's exclusion, the rules version and when it was calculated, all as the last judge wrote them) and rulings; a merged id answers the merged record with its `merged_into`, no links, no estimates, no assessment, and its rulings. Read-kind.
- `passes` takes optional `page` and `limit` and answers `passes`, pass records in pass order (`D04-store`), `limit` at a time with `next`; `pass` takes `pass` and answers its record. Each record says what kind of pass it is (`ingest`, `judge` or `rebuild`), what started it (`run`, `cron` or `rebuild`) and how many candidates it judged (`D06-passes`' `Record`). Read-kind.
- `rebuild` takes nothing and answers the pass record it started, `running`; the work lands in the background (`D06-passes`): every registered scraper's current run ingested, then every candidate judged. Additive.
- `decide` takes `kind`, `why` and `candidate`, and by kind `other` (`merge`, `not_same`) or `links` (`split`, the source keys to move out); it records the ruling with the caller as its maker and answers `ruling` and `candidates`, the candidates as they stand after it, the new candidate among them for a split (`store.Decide`, `D12-resolution`). The ruling is applied and the active candidates a merge, split, exclude or include touches judged again in the same call (a `not_same` judges none), so the shortlist reflects it at once. Additive.

## Paging

`shortlist`, `candidates` and `passes` page as the suite's listing convention asks (`shape/`, Listing and Storage), on the contract of appkit's `paging` package (`appkit/paging`, appkit's D25), which this document cites and never restates. Each input declares `page`, an opaque string, and `limit`, an integer, as pointer fields carrying the shared descriptions `paging.PageDescription` and `paging.LimitDescription`; each answer is its list then `next`, an opaque string present exactly when at least one entry follows, whose field's tag carries `paging.NextDescription`. An absent, null or empty `page` is the first page, and `next` is handed back as `page` unchanged; an absent or null `limit` is `paging.DefaultLimit`.

Each tool's token is `paging.Token{Tool: <its name>}` over the key of the last entry the page answered, in the order the store windows by (`D04-store`): for `shortlist` the assessment's operating key (an `int64`, `D13-scoring`) and the candidate's id; for `candidates` the candidate's created time as RFC 3339 in UTC to the second and its id; for `passes` the pass's started time so formatted and its id. The tool asks the store for one entry more than the page, from the decoded position, and sets `next` only when the extra entry came back, so a full last page carries no `next` and an entry sharing its key's first field with the page's last is neither repeated nor skipped. A token binds no filter: keeping the filters the same is the caller's part, as D25 says. A token one of the three issued is refused by the other two, because the checksum covers the tool's name.

Before the store is read, a `limit` outside 1 to `paging.MaxLimit` is refused with the text of `paging.Window`'s error, then a `page` the tool's token does not decode with `paging.BadPage`; each refusal is that text alone, not one of the five words below, and is the same while the database is unreachable, since retrying cannot help. Paging records nothing: a paged call is appkit's `tool.called` like any other, which carries no arguments, so no token appears in any event or log line (`D09-trail`).

## Answers

Times are RFC 3339 UTC to the second; a date (a filing date, the known-since date, the newest filing) is `YYYY-MM-DD`, absent when there is none. Money is whole dollars. A confidence or an evidence weight is the stored `float64` itself, never rounded at the call: the operating confidence is stored rounded to six decimal places (`D13-scoring`), the revenue confidence and the evidence weights as computed; an agent may compare or recompute with them, and the pages round to two decimals for people (`D08-pages`). Missing is never zero: a candidate with no evidence answers no `operating_confidence` and an empty `operating_evidence`, one with no estimate no `revenue_low`, `revenue_high` or `revenue_confidence`, one with no known date no `known_since`, and one whose rows give no category an empty `categories`. Every judgment answered is the one the store holds, written by the last judge of the candidate: a judge pass, a rebuild, or a ruling other than `not_same` that touched it (`D13-scoring`, `D06-passes`); between judges nothing decays, so no answer depends on the moment of the call, and with the daily judge an answer is at most a day behind. A candidate is answered whole, phone and email included: a tool's answer is product, given to a signed-in caller, and the secrecy of decision 18 governs the trail, the bus, refusals and the logs (`D09-trail`), not the answer.

## Refusals

Apart from the two paging refusals above, a refusal is an MCP error result whose text is `<word>: <sentence>` (decision 14, as scrapes'). The words are the five `D01-layout-and-run-seam` declares as `passes.Refusal*`: `not_found`, `invalid`, `busy`, `stopping` and `unavailable`. Each sentence is a copy constant, `tools.Refuse*`, one per word, so a refusal names no id, business name, address, phone, email or ruling text the call or the store supplied (decision 18).

- `candidate` and `pass`: an id that is not a well-formed candidate or pass id, and an unknown or pruned one, are `not_found`.
- `rebuild`: what `Engine.Rebuild` refuses, with its word (`D06-passes`): the drain `stopping`, no `scrapes` entry in the services file at the start `unavailable`, a pass already running `busy`; anything else `unavailable`.
- `decide`: a `kind` that is none of the five, a `why` with no character but white space, `other` absent for `merge` or `not_same`, and `links` empty for `split` are `invalid`, decided first, as `store.Decide` decides a malformed ruling (`D12-resolution`); an argument the kind does not take is ignored, as `store.Decide` ignores it; then a `candidate`, or an `other` the kind takes, that is not a well-formed candidate id is `not_found`, without reading the store; then what `store.Decide` refuses: an unknown candidate `not_found`, a ruling the records forbid `invalid` (a ruling on a merged candidate, a merge of a candidate into itself, a split naming keys the candidate does not hold or all of them; `D12-resolution`).
- `shortlist`, `candidates` and `passes`: a `limit` out of range is refused with `paging.Window`'s text, then an undecodable `page`, a token another tool issued among them, with `paging.BadPage`, before the store is read.
- Every tool: the database failing is `unavailable`. Malformed arguments, a `page` that is not a string and a `limit` that is not an integer or too large for a Go `int` among them, are appkit's schema refusal. A `class` or `cargo` no candidate carries answers an empty list.

## Descriptions

Every tool's description is one line, a copy constant `tools.Describe<Tool>`, which the gateway's `describe` answers as the tool's first line (Verification). `tools.Catalog()` answers each tool's name with that line, in the order `shortlist`, `candidates`, `candidate`, `passes`, `pass`, `rebuild`, `decide`, for the tools page (`D08-pages`).

## Recorded decisions

- **Raw registration**, as scrapes: all seven tools are registered with appkit's `mcp.AddRawTool` and answer what `encoding/json` writes, the JSON as the one text block and as `structuredContent`; the answer types use `omitempty`, which appkit's typed `mcp.AddTool` refuses, so they carry no output schema, and their JSON is stated here. The argument types are typed, so appkit derives the input schema and refuses malformed arguments before a handler runs: each field carries a `json:"<name>"` tag naming its property, with no `omitempty`, and each required property also `mcp:"required"`; the paged tools' `Page` and `Limit` also carry a `description` tag repeating `paging.PageDescription` and `paging.LimitDescription` word for word, as `spec`'s one exception to copy living in one place allows, which appkit copies into the schema.
- **`max_minutes` and `min_revenue` are pointers to `uint32`, and absent means not applied** (the two-judgments change, its decision 18; orchestrator decision 3): an absent or `null` member is no filter at all, and `0` is a value. The query has no defaults any more: the cutoffs of 120 minutes and ten million dollars, which hid most candidates before, are gone with `scoring.DefaultMaxMinutes` and `scoring.DefaultMinRevenue`, so a call with `{}` shows every judged candidate the owner has not excluded, and an agent that wants the old cutoffs passes them. A passed filter leaves out a candidate whose value is unknown, because a filter is a question the candidate cannot answer yes to: `min_revenue` 0 keeps every candidate with a revenue estimate and drops those without; `max_minutes` 0 keeps only those at minute 0. `uint32` holds every sensible cutoff (more than four billion dollars) and lets appkit's schema refuse a negative one; the conversion to the store's `int64` cannot overflow. The argument types and input schemas do not change. The proof: a probe against appkit's released `mcp` derived `{"type":"integer","minimum":0}` for a `*uint32` field and handed the handler nil for an absent and a `null` member.
- **The three lists follow D25 in place.** `page`, `limit` and `next` change shape in one release, with no expand period: a tool's arguments and answers are not persisted and cross no release boundary, and every caller is a model reading the schema `tools/list` gives at the call; an agent holding an integer `page` from the previous release is refused by appkit's schema and starts again from the first page. `PageSize` goes: the page size is the call's `limit`, read with `paging.Window`. The input schemas are stated as observed with appkit's released `mcp` at the release candidates requires: a `*string` field derives `{"type":"string"}` and a `*int` field `{"type":"integer"}`, each with the field's `description` tag copied, and an absent or `null` member reaches the handler as nil.
- **`Next` carries `paging.NextDescription` in its tag**, as D25 asks, though a raw tool has no output schema to show it; the tag is stated so a test can compare it with the constant.
- **The shortlist token is the operating key, not the operating confidence**: the key is the exact integer the store orders and windows by (`D13-scoring`: the confidence, already rounded to six decimal places, times a million and rounded to an integer, and −1 when there is none, so a candidate with no operating confidence sorts last), so a token decodes to a position the store compares exactly; appkit's tokens carry only `int64` and `string`, and a float in a token would not round-trip the comparison. Two confidences share a key only when they are equal, and then fall to id order. A token the previous release issued over a score key decodes, and positions the window somewhere in the new order; the caller starts again from the first page, as with any token after records change. The candidates and passes tokens carry the times as the store compares them, text to the second.
- **`decide` screens its arguments' shape before the store**, by the rule `store.Decide` applies to a malformed ruling (`D12-resolution`), so the word is the same whichever answers: `invalid` (decision 10); an id that cannot name a candidate is `not_found`, as scrapes answers a malformed run id; `store.Decide` decides the rest, an unknown candidate `not_found` before a forbidden ruling `invalid`, so a merge on `cand_0000000000000000` with any well-formed `other` is `not_found` (Verification). `why` is kept verbatim; one of only white space is no reason.
- **`decide` answers what `store.Decide` returns**, in its order: the candidates as they stand after the ruling, which for a split includes the new candidate (decision 14); which candidates and in which order is `D12-resolution`'s.
- **A merged candidate's `candidate` answer is its record**, display fields as the store keeps them (binding decision: a merged candidate keeps the display fields it had at the merge), with `merged_into`, no links, estimates or assessment, and its rulings, which record the merge (decision 10: "its record as merged with `merged_into` and no evidence"). The page blanks the display fields as its template asks (`D08-pages`); the tool answers the record.
- **Every number shown is the store's** (`store.Assessment`'s `Operating`, `Evidence` and revenue fields, `D04-store`, as `D13-scoring` judges them): `shortlist` and `candidate` compute nothing at the call, so `tools.Config` carries no clock. A judgment is written only by a judge: the daily judge pass, a rebuild, or a ruling other than `not_same`, for the active candidates it touches; an ingest pass changes none. The shortlist, the candidate answer and the pages agree, all as of the last judge.
- **The stored value, the operating confidence stored rounded** (orchestrator decision 8, amended): the operating confidence, the revenue confidence and each evidence weight are answered as the stored `float64`, which `encoding/json` writes as the shortest text that reads back to the same value, and the tools round nothing at the call. The operating confidence is stored rounded to six decimal places (`D13-scoring`), an orchestrator ruling after the sandbox answered one 0.2 piece as 0.19999999999999996; the revenue confidence and the weights are stored as computed. Rounding to two decimals is the pages' presentation, not the data's, and an agent reading 0.776 can check it against the weights it is given.
- **The old score members go in one release**: `low`, `high`, `confidence`, `filed`, `freshness`, `effective`, `point`, `score`, `base`, `exclusions` and `scored` leave both answers, and the freshness-as-scored definition with them; the filing's freshness now lives only in the weight of the `fmcsa_active` evidence. No expand period is needed, for the reason the paging change needs none: answers are not persisted and cross no release boundary, and the callers are models reading the answer as it comes. The cost is that a script kept in `repos` that parses an old member breaks as soon as this release is deployed, with no release of grace in which both shapes are answered. The `candidate` answer's `assessment` is shaped as the two judgments and the facts beside them; `newest_filing` is what `filed` was, under the store's new name for it.
- **A shortlist entry names its categories, the candidate answer sources them**: the entry carries each distinct category name once, in byte order, enough to read a list; the `candidate` answer carries each category with the scraper that gave it, as it carries each evidence item with its source, so an agent can see why.
- **Government match and fleet are facts, not gates** (the two-judgments change, its decisions 16 and 17): both are answered on the entry and on the assessment, and neither keeps a candidate off the shortlist; the cohort filters of a later change may use them. The owner's `exclude` still does, and `include` brings the candidate back.
- **A link answers its reading's common fields and keys**, so an agent can see why a row matched (`name_key`) and what a later pass will match on (`phone_key`, `domain_key`), and not the estimate, evidence, known-since date, category or government match it carries, which the candidate's estimates and assessment answer.
- **Effects**: `shortlist`, `candidates`, `candidate`, `passes` and `pass` are `mcp.Read`; `rebuild` and `decide` are `mcp.Additive`: a ruling adds a record and never removes one (decision 10).
- **Descriptions are one line**, one sentence of at most 120 characters ending with a full stop, which appkit's `mcp` requires; **refusal sentences are constants**, so decision 18's secrecy holds by construction.
