# D04-store

The records: `internal/store`, which owns candidates' thirteen tables in its SQLite database, `state/candidates.db`, and every read and write of them (decision 16): `candidates`, one row per candidate business, never deleted (decision 4); `links`, one row per scrapes row attached to a candidate, addressed by its source key (decision 6); `estimates`, one per source per candidate; `assessments`, one per candidate (decision 8); `decisions`, the owner's rulings (decision 10); `passes`, one row per pass until the prune removes it (decision 11); `zip_drive`, the ZIP drive-time table, replaced whole at every start (decision 9); `candidate_cargo`, one row per cargo tag of each candidate, kept beside the candidate's cargo for the previous release, which reads it, though nothing in this one does (log 30); `cohorts`, one row per cohort until it is deleted (cohorts decision 23); `rejects`, one row per reject or unreject record, never deleted (cohorts decisions 11 and 23); `enrichments`, one row per enrichment result recorded on a candidate, never deleted (website decisions 20 and 21); `facts`, one row per active candidate holding a result, the projection of its newest result of each kind (enrichers decision 5); and `pages`, one row per copy of a web page kept on a candidate, never deleted (site text decision 1). This document declares the store's whole public surface: its records, the words they are spelled with, every method with its signature, the errors every consumer tells apart and the one line every consumer answers when the database cannot be reached. It states what the pass records, the ZIP table, the reads and their windows, the cohort and reject writes and reads, the enrichment writes and reads, the derived kinds' runs, the targeted walks, the facts rows, the page writes and reads, the schema's eighth to thirteenth migrations, the prune and `db status` do. What a row does to the candidates and links when it is attached, what a gone run does, and what each ruling does (`Attach`, `MarkGone`, `Decide`) are `D12-resolution`'s; what a judge writes, the fleet verdict and its counts included (`Judge`, `JudgeAll`), is `D13-scoring`'s; what a cohort is, which validation set each `ValidCohort` function accepts, and which candidates a cohort's members and its matching count are, in which order, with which standing rejects, are `D14-cohorts`'; which pass calls which write is `D06-passes`'; what each kind's inputs are, what it finds and what makes its found valid are `D16-enrichers`'; what a website check finds and how a pass uses `DueEnrichments`, `EnrichmentMark` and `EnrichmentAfter` to fetch each inputs once are `D15-website-check`'s; which derived kinds a merge or a split re-runs, and that a merge moves the other candidate's pages to the survivor and a split leaves them, are `D12-resolution`'s; which targets an `enrich_cohort` walks, what a `site_text` run records and how its counts are summed are `D06-passes`' and `D16-enrichers`'; and which URL `add_page` may keep, and what it fetched, are `D10-tools`' and `D15-website-check`'s. Opening, migrating, transactions and the failure seam are appkit's `db`; `D01-layout-and-run-seam` resolves the path and declares the root package's `Migrations()`. It is scrapes' store in shape, with a pass in place of a run and the candidates, their evidence, the rulings and the cohorts beside it.

Decisions cited by number are the decisions document that delivered candidates; "judgments decision N" is decision N of the later one, *two judgments and the PPP reader*, which splits each candidate's one score into two judgments and splits storing rows from judging them; "cohorts decision N" is decision N of the next, *cohorts*, which replaces the shortlist with the owner's cohorts, and "log N" is entry N of its decisions log; "website decision N" is decision N of the next, *the website check*, which checks each candidate's own domain for a working website, and "website log X" is entry X of its decisions log; "enrichers decision N" is decision N of the next, *candidates enrichers*, which makes every fact an enrichment, derived or fetched, kept in a facts row, and "enrichers settled N" is item N of its settled answers; "site text decision N" is decision N of the latest, *candidates — site text*, which adds the `site_text` kind, the pages kept on a candidate, the targeted enrich and its pass kind, and one rule for which passes run together, and "settled for this delivery" marks a choice its delivery made where that document left it open.

## One database, one writer

candidates is the only writer of its database, and the database is the only record of its candidates, links, estimates, assessments, rulings, passes, cohorts, reject records, enrichment results, facts rows, pages and drive times; nothing else is kept on disk (decision 1). `store.New` builds a store over a handle and opens or creates nothing. What a store holds when its handle is closed is what a store over the next `db.Open` of the same path holds; a test proves the records that way, never by reading tables. A store whose handle is failing (`SetFailing(true)`) answers every call with `ErrUnreachable` and changes nothing. `Config.Now` is the clock every stamp is read from, the moment a judge measures freshness at, and the clock a cohort read measures filing age by (log 3); `Config.Rand` is the source pass ids, candidate ids and cohort ids are drawn from. `Run` hands in `Process.Now` and `Process.Rand`, and the store has no default for either.

## By key

A pass costs in proportion to its rows, and the pages and the tools answer while it runs (orchestrator ruling, after a pass over the real FMCSA run of 16,706 rows never finished: every batch loaded, copied and compared the whole database under one lock). The store reaches every record the pass path needs by key through SQL, on decision 16's indexes: a link by its source key (the links' unique scraper and source id), the active candidates one of whose links holds a match key by the links' name key, state and ZIP, the oldest first, a candidate's links by the links' candidate, a candidate by its id, its estimates, assessment and rulings by its id, a ZIP's minutes by the ZIP. No write on the pass path reads a whole table, but `JudgeAll`, which a judge pass and a rebuild run and which covers every active candidate by definition, and so enumerates the active candidates once. An `Attach` touches its batch's rows and the candidates they attach to: an unchanged row costs the lookup of its link and the write of its run, last seen and gone; a candidate's display is re-derived from its own links once per batch for each candidate one of whose links the batch wrote, since a link's last seen and gone order the display (`D12-resolution`). `MarkGone` is one bounded statement per `MarkGone` call, over that call's scraper's links last seen in a run other than that call's run, after which it re-derives by key the display of each candidate whose link it marked, since gone orders the display, bounded by those candidates. `Judge` reads and writes each candidate it is given by its id, reading the candidate's record, its own links, rulings and facts row, and writing its estimates and assessment. An ingest judges nothing, so its cost is its rows alone (judgments decision 23). A ruling works the same way, touching only the candidates it names, each by key (its record, its own links, its rulings, its results and facts row, and the ZIP table's minutes for its display ZIP), so its cost is bounded by those candidates' links: a `not_same` judges none and re-derives nothing; an `exclude` or `include` judges `r.Candidate`; a merge or a split re-derives the display of, re-runs the derived kinds for, and judges the candidates it touches (`r.Candidate` and `r.Other`, or `r.Candidate` and the split's new candidate). It reads no table whole and refreshes no other candidate (`D12-resolution`). A cohort write touches its one cohort row, and a reject or unreject its one pair's records, its cohort and its candidate, each by key. An enrichment write touches the candidates its results name, each by key, and rewrites their facts rows from their newest results; a `Derive` reads one window of the active candidates in id order and, for each, its subject by key (its links, its ZIP's minutes) and its newest result of the kind; and a read of a candidate's results, of the results of one inputs after a mark, or of one window of the due candidates in id order reads by key and by window on `0011`'s and `0012`'s indexes and the candidates' id (`D06-passes` and `D15-website-check` bound the pass's memory). `FillFacts` walks the active candidates once, at the start, writing a row only where one is missing. The upkeep the start and the sweep run touches only its own table (log 36, after candidates was killed at its start on the `us` space under `memory_max = 256M`, the two upkeep writes loading, copying and comparing every candidate, link, estimate, assessment and ruling of a database holding 43,000 candidates and 54,000 links): `ReplaceZipDrive` reads and writes `zip_drive` alone, and `PrunePasses` reads and deletes in `passes` alone, so the memory either takes does not grow with the candidates, links, estimates, assessments or rulings the store holds. A page write touches its one candidate's pages, by key on `0013`'s index on the candidate and the URL, which is also how the cap is counted inside the write; `Pages` and `CountPages` read one candidate's pages on that index and `GetPage` one page by its number; and `DueAmong`, `DeriveAmong` and `Subject` read and write only the candidates whose ids they are given, each by key, so a targeted pass costs in proportion to its targets. No call of the store loads every record.

Every write is one write transaction of appkit's `db`, and the store holds no lock of its own across one: the only lock it keeps guards `Config.Rand`. A read goes through `db.Read`, which answers from what was last committed while a write transaction is open, since appkit's handle reads beside its one writer; so a page or a tool never waits for a batch, a judge, a ruling or a cohort write. Calls racing still behave as one after another (`D12-resolution`, R-O0XX-2PW3, R-O9H7-R42Y), because every write goes through appkit's one writer. Which statements the store issues is not contract; that a read answers while a write is open is, and so is the time a pass over thousands of rows takes (`D06-passes`).

## The records

A **candidate** (`store.Candidate`) is one business: its id, `cand_` and 16 lowercase hexadecimal digits; its status, `active` or `merged`, with the id it was merged into; its display fields (name, every name, street, city, state, ZIP, county as `MN-115`, phone, email, people); its tags (class and cargo); and when it was created and last updated (decision 4). Which link the display fields come from and when they change is `D12-resolution`'s.

A **link** (`store.Link`) is one row's attachment to one candidate: the candidate, the source key (scraper and source id), the run the row was last seen in and its digest, the source's reading of the row as last seen (`sources.Reading`: the keys, the envelope's common fields, the tags, the filing date, the fleet test and the row's fleet verdict and counts, the estimate, the operating evidence, the date the row shows the business existing since, the category and the government name match), how it attached (`source_key`, `match_key` or `decision`) with its confidence, when it was first and last seen, and whether it is gone (decision 6). A source key is unique among links: a row attaches to one candidate at a time.

An **estimate** is one source's `sources.Estimate` for one candidate, at most one per source per candidate. An **assessment** (`store.Assessment`) is a candidate's two judgments as the last judge wrote them (judgments decision 1): its **operating confidence**, how sure we are that it is a business operating today, with the **operating evidence** behind it, and its **revenue estimate**, a low, a high and a confidence. Beside them it answers the facts the cohort filters read (`D14-cohorts`), which for an active candidate come from its facts row at every read, not from the assessment's own columns (enrichers decision 7): the date the business is known since, the government name pattern its name matched, the newest filing among its links not gone, its categories each with its source, the **fleet verdict**, `pass`, `fail` or `unknown`, with the power units and drivers of the FMCSA link it was read from (cohorts decision 13, log 20), the **website** state, `https`, `http`, `none` or `unknown`, with the URL the check landed on, empty unless `https` or `http` (website decision 14), the drive minutes from its display ZIP (nil when the table lacks it), whether the owner's standing ruling excludes it, the rules version and when it was calculated. The verdict replaces the boolean fleet test in the Go type, the tools and the pages under one name, `fleet_verdict` (log 10); the `fleet` column the store wrote beside it for the release before is dropped by `0011` (website decision 22). A judge still copies the facts row into the assessment's fact columns, which the previous release reads (`D13-scoring`); this release reads those columns only for a merged candidate, whose assessment answers what its last judge wrote, as it did at the merge. Its **operating key** is the operating confidence scaled by a million and rounded to an integer, -1 when it has none: what a cohort's order compares operating confidence by (`D14-cohorts`, `D13-scoring`). A judgment the evidence does not support is empty, never 0 (judgments decision 2). What a judge computes is `D13-scoring`'s; judgments are written only by a judge: a judge pass, a rebuild, or a ruling re-judging the candidates it touches and no other (`JudgeAll`, `Judge`, `Decide`). An ingest stores links and writes no judgment. A read shows what was last written and computes nothing, so a store read between two judges shows each judgment as the last judge wrote it and each fact as the last enrichment wrote it; nothing decays between writes (judgments decision 6).

A **ruling** (`store.Ruling`) is one of the owner's decisions: its kind (`merge`, `split`, `not_same`, `exclude`, `include`), the candidate it names, the other candidate (the other side of a merge or a `not_same`, the new candidate of a split), the source keys a split moved, why, who (user id and email) and when (decision 10). Rulings are never changed or removed. The `exclude` and `include` tools and `decide` all record theirs through `Decide`, unchanged (cohorts decision 12).

A **cohort** (`store.Cohort`) is one saved search: its id, `coh_` and 16 lowercase hexadecimal digits; its name, free text, not unique; its size; its filters (`store.CohortFilters`); its order (`[]store.CohortOrder`); who created it (user id and email) and when; and who last changed it and when, which creation sets to the creator and the creation time (cohorts decision 1, log 24). Its members are never stored: `ReadCohort` works them out at every read (cohorts decisions 3 and 25). The store keeps the filters and the order in their **normal form**, the categories de-duplicated and in byte order and an empty order as the default order, so an edit that changes nothing compares equal and writes nothing (log 8, log 16). An edit (`store.CohortEdit`) names the cohort and any of its name, size, filters and order, nil for each part it leaves alone; a passed filter set or order replaces the stored one whole, so `&CohortFilters{}` clears every filter and `&[]CohortOrder{}` sets the default order (cohorts decision 6, log 8). A delete removes the row; its reject records stay.

A **reject record** (`store.Reject`) is one reject or unreject: its kind (`reject` or `unreject`), the cohort and the candidate it names, why, who and when (cohorts decision 10). Records are never changed or removed, not at an unreject and not when their cohort is deleted (cohorts decision 11). Which record stands for a cohort and a candidate is `D14-cohorts`' **standing reject**. `CohortName` is filled on every read from the cohort the record names while that cohort exists, so it is empty once the cohort is deleted.

An **enrichment result** (`store.Enrichment`) is one thing found about a candidate, from its own links, from the ZIP table or from outside (website decision 20, enrichers decisions 1 and 4): the candidate it is on; its kind, one of `enrichers.Kinds()`, `drive_minutes`, `link_facts`, `website_check` and `site_text` (`D16-enrichers`); its **inputs**, what it was found from, a ZIP, a fingerprint of the candidate's links, a domain or a website URL; its source word (`zip_drive`, `links`, the domain checked, or `prompts`); what was found, as the kind's JSON object; what it cost and when it was recorded. Results are history (website decision 21): never changed but by a merge, which moves the other candidate's results to the survivor, and never removed; a redo adds a result, and a candidate's **newest result** of a kind is the one recorded last, as `Enrichments` orders them, not the one with the latest time, so a result moved by a merge keeps its place. A candidate is **due** for a kind when it is active, its inputs for the kind now are not empty, and it has no result of the kind or its newest one was found from other inputs (enrichers decision 3): no age makes a result due.

A **facts row** (`store.Facts`) is the projection of a candidate's newest result of each kind (enrichers decision 5): the link facts from its newest `link_facts` result, the website state and URL from its newest `website_check` result, the drive minutes from its newest `drive_minutes` result, each kind's facts at their unknown values when it has none, and, per kind written, when the result it came from was recorded; `site_text` gives no fact of its own, only that time (site text decision 11), which a cohort's member answers as when site text was last written for it (`D14-cohorts`). It is written in the same write as the result that changes it, so it is never behind its results, and it can be rebuilt from them at any time; it is what the cohorts, the tools and the pages read a candidate's facts from.

A **page** (`store.Page`) is one copy of a web page kept on a candidate (site text decision 1): the candidate it is on, its **number**, the URL it was fetched from, its title, its text, the bytes of its body read, when it was fetched, and who added it, by user id and email. The store mints the number, which names the copy among every page the store holds, not only the candidate's, so a merge that moves a page keeps its number and its text page's path, `/candidates/<id>/pages/<n>`, names it on the survivor (`D08-pages`, `D12-resolution`). Adding a URL a candidate already holds keeps a newer copy beside the old; the newest copy of each URL, the one with the highest number, is the one read. Pages are never changed but by a merge and never removed.

A **pass** (`store.Pass`) is one ingest, one judge, one rebuild, one enrich or one enrich over a target. Its id is `pass_` and 16 lowercase hexadecimal digits. It keeps its **kind**, what it does: `ingest` stores one run's rows as links and marks the vanished ones gone, `judge` judges every active candidate, `rebuild` ingests every source's current run, re-runs every derived kind for every active candidate and then judges, `enrich` records what the enrichers find for the candidates due, derived kinds before fetched ones, and `enrich_cohort` runs one kind for one cohort's members or one candidate, the due ones or, forced, all of them (`D06-passes`; site text decisions 12 to 14); its **origin**, what started it: `run`, a `scrape.finished` delivery, `cron`, a delivery of the daily trigger's event, `rebuild`, the `rebuild` tool, `enrich`, the `enrich` tool, for either enrich kind, or `pass`, another pass (website log A1); its **trigger**, the run id an ingest's delivery named, the trigger's slug `judge_candidates` for a judge, `rebuild`, `enrich` for an enrich or an `enrich_cohort` the tool started, or the id of the pass that started it (judgments decision 25, website log A1); for an `enrich_cohort` its **target**, the cohort or candidate id it walks, and its **enrich kind**, the kind it walks, both empty for every other pass; its **status**, one of `running`, `done` and `failed`, and when failed one of four **reasons**, `scrapes`, `interrupted`, `store` and `prompts` (which failure is which is `D06-passes`'; `prompts` settled for this delivery, S5); **started** and **finished**, each UTC to the second; the nineteen counts, each 0 until the pass is done, `cost` among them in nano-dollars; its starter's user id and email; the request id it was started under; and, for a delivery, the bus event's id, the **delivery**, an internal column no tool or page shows: it is how a redelivered event is told from a new one (`D06-passes`).

Every id the store mints, a pass's, a candidate's or a cohort's, is its prefix and 8 bytes read from `Config.Rand`, written as 16 lowercase hexadecimal digits, drawn again when it is the id of a record of its kind the store holds; a cohort id is drawn again also when a reject record names it, so a deleted cohort's id is never reused while its records stand (cohorts decision 1).

## Passes

A pass has three writes. `AddPass` records it `running`, stamping started and minting the id. It is refused `ErrDelivered` when its delivery is one an existing pass carries, or when its origin is `run` and its trigger is the run id an existing pass of origin `run` names, whatever that pass's status; and otherwise `ErrBusy` while a pass that blocks it is `running`: one of the same kind over the same **scope**, the scope of an ingest being its run, of an `enrich_cohort` its target, and of a judge, a rebuild and an enrich the corpus (site text decision 15). Every other pair runs together: two ingests of different runs, a judge beside an ingest, a rebuild or an enrich beside an `enrich_cohort`, and two `enrich_cohort` passes over different targets, a candidate and a cohort holding it among them; two over one target are busy whatever kinds they walk (settled for this delivery, S10). An ingest naming a run another ingest already names is refused `ErrDelivered` before its scope is looked at, so an ingest's scope refuses nothing an ingest delivery can send. A judge's trigger is the same slug every day, so the trigger refuses only run ids, and a redelivered trigger event is told by its delivery alone. `FinishPass` ends a `running` pass `done` with the nineteen counts its work gathered; `FailPass` ends a `running` pass `failed` with a reason and leaves everything the pass wrote in place, since every write is idempotent and the next pass or a rebuild repairs it (decision 11). Of two calls racing on the same pass one succeeds and the other is told it is no longer running (`ErrEnded`). Nothing moves a pass back.

The counts a pass finishes with are the sums of the `store.Counts` its writes answered: `Attach` answers `Rows`, `New`, `Changed`, `Unchanged`, `Created` and `Linked` for a batch; `MarkGone` answers `Gone`; `Judge` and `JudgeAll` answer `Judged` and `Unranged`. So an ingest finishes with `Judged` and `Unranged` 0 and a judge with only those two. `Derive` answers the results it recorded, which a pass sums as `Derived`. An enrich finishes with only `Derived`, `Due`, `Fetched`, `HTTPS`, `HTTP` and `None`, the last five counted by its work itself, since no store write answers them (`D06-passes`, `D15-website-check`); a rebuild with its ingest's and judge's counts and `Derived`; an `enrich_cohort` with the enrich counts of the kind it walked and, for a prompts kind, `Runs`, `RunsFailed`, `Pages` and `Cost`, which its work counts itself, every other pass leaving those four 0. What each counts is the owning document's.

`GetPass` answers any pass, whoever started it. `ListPasses` is one window of the passes in pass order, newest first: those after a position, the started text and id of the last pass of the window before, at most `n`; `RunningPasses` every pass still `running`, which the start finishes `failed` with `interrupted` (`D06-passes`). `PrunePasses` removes, in one write, every `done` pass beyond the newest keep count of `done` passes and every `failed` pass beyond the newest keep count of `failed` passes; a `running` pass is neither counted nor removed (decision 16). A pruned pass is gone from every read, its delivery and trigger with it. Candidates, links, estimates, assessments, rulings, cohorts, reject records, enrichment results and pages are never pruned; the prune touches none of them. A judge replaces a candidate's estimates and assessment (`D13-scoring`); a merge moves the merged candidate's links to the survivor, and its enrichment results and pages with them, deletes the merged candidate's facts row, and the merged candidate otherwise keeps what it had at the merge: its display fields, tags, estimates, last assessment and reject records (`D12-resolution`).

## The ZIP table

`ReplaceZipDrive` replaces the whole table with the map it is given in one write; the start hands it the root package's `ZipDrive()`, so the table is always the release's (decision 9). `ZipMinutes` answers one ZIP's minutes. `drive_minutes` reads the table for each candidate's display ZIP when it derives (`D16-enrichers`); a judge no longer reads it (`D13-scoring`). A new table reaches the facts only through a rebuild, which forces every derived kind, since a ZIP that did not change leaves its candidate's `drive_minutes` inputs unchanged.

## Cohorts

`CreateCohort` mints the id, stamps created and changed with the caller and the clock, and keeps the normal form of what it was given. `EditCohort` replaces each part the edit passes, and when the cohort that results is the one stored it writes nothing and keeps changed and its user as they were, so an empty edit or a repeated one is the same as none (log 8). `DeleteCohort` removes the cohort and answers it as it was; deleting it again is `ErrNotFound` (cohorts decision 7). `GetCohort` answers a definition without its members. Each write refuses a part a `ValidCohort` function refuses with `ErrInvalid` before it looks for the cohort, and an empty user id `ErrUnreachable`, as no consumer sends one (log 7). Creating twice makes two cohorts, as cohorts decision 18 accepts.

`ReadCohort` answers the definition, its **matching** count, its **members** in their order, each the candidate and its assessment, its judgments as the last judge wrote them and its facts the facts row's at the read, and its standing rejects, newest first, each with the cohort's name, all as `D14-cohorts` defines them, at the clock `Config.Now()` gives once for the read (log 3). Membership is one query over the assessments joined to the candidates and, by a left join, to the facts rows, every fact filter and order key reading the facts row and a missing row reading every fact unknown, cut by the size, beside one count (cohorts decision 25); the store holds no more than the members.

`ListCohorts` and `ListCohortsReversed` are the cohort windows the `cohorts` tool and the landing read, in **cohort order**, name then id, each in byte order (log 18), a window after a position or before it, as the passes' are.

`Reject` records a reject or an unreject. A reject of a pair that has no standing reject, or an unreject of one that has, records one record and answers it; a reject of a pair already rejected writes nothing and answers the record that stands; an unreject of a pair with no standing reject writes nothing and answers a record of kind `unreject` naming the cohort, the candidate and the cohort's name, with no why, user or time (log 27), so every reject and unreject is safe to repeat (cohorts decisions 10 and 18). It refuses a blank why `ErrInvalid`, a cohort the store does not hold, a deleted one included, `ErrNotFound`, then a candidate it does not hold `ErrNotFound`, then a merged candidate `ErrInvalid` (log 6). `Rejects` answers every record naming a candidate, active or merged, newest first (cohorts decision 11). A cohort's standing rejects are listed whatever the candidate's status (`D14-cohorts`), so a reject of a candidate that is later merged keeps standing on its cohort's read and cannot be cleared, since `Reject` refuses a merged candidate `ErrInvalid` either way; a merged candidate is no member of any cohort, so the row only shows.

## Enrichments

`AddEnrichments` records a pass's fetched results in one write, in the order given, each stamped with the clock, and rewrites each written candidate's facts row in the same write; it skips a result naming a candidate that is merged or unknown, so no result is ever on a merged candidate, as no link is, and it answers what it recorded; were every candidate sharing an inputs merged between the pass's selection and its write, that result is skipped and recorded nowhere, and the inputs may be fetched again for the survivor, in the same pass when its id lies after the cursor, or in a later one. It accepts what the registry's `Valid` accepts for the kind (`D16-enrichers`) with non-empty inputs and a finite, non-negative cost, and keeps `Found` compacted, as given otherwise; anything else, an unknown kind, a found that is not JSON or not the kind's, empty inputs, or a cost that is negative, NaN or infinite (which SQLite would store as NULL and `cost`'s not-null refuses), is a value no consumer sends, refused `ErrUnreachable` with nothing recorded. The store does no UTF-8 repair of its own: the enrichers make `Found` with `json.Marshal`, which already replaces each invalid byte with U+FFFD. That a merge racing it leaves the result moved or unrecorded, never on the merged candidate, is `D12-resolution`'s, which states the store's writes behave as one after another. `Enrichments` answers every result on a candidate, merged or not, newest first, each as it was stored, whichever write recorded it.

`Derive` runs one derived kind over one window of the active candidates in id order after a cursor, in one write: for each it builds the candidate's subject from the records (`D16-enrichers`), and records the kind's result, at the clock, when the inputs are not empty and either the candidate is due or the call is forced, rewriting the facts row beside it. It answers the last id it examined, empty once the walk is over, and how many results it recorded. An enrich derives what is due; a rebuild, and a merge or a split for the candidates it touches, force it, so every derived kind is recomputed whatever its inputs (`D06-passes`, `D12-resolution`). The derived kinds run inside the store (decided for this delivery; enrichers decision 9, which has a merge or a split re-run them in the same call, and decision 15 with its decisions log): a merge or a split re-runs them in the same write as its moves and its re-judge, as the store already judges, and the enrichers package is pure functions the store calls.

`DueEnrichments` is one window of the candidates due for a kind, in id order after a cursor, each answered with its inputs now, so a fetching pass groups them by inputs without reading them again. The store answers whatever inputs the kind names: a display email with no domain key (Gmail and the like) has empty inputs for `website_check` and is never due (website log P12, enrichers decision 3). `EnrichmentMark` answers an opaque mark of what is recorded so far, and `EnrichmentAfter` the last result of a kind with given inputs recorded after a mark, which is how a pass, reading the mark when its work begins, tells inputs it already fetched across batches without holding a set of them (`D15-website-check`).

`DueAmong` and `DeriveAmong` are the targeted forms (site text decision 13): each takes the ids an `enrich_cohort` walks, a cohort's members in their order or one candidate, and a force flag. `DueAmong` answers, in the order given and each once, the ids naming an active candidate whose inputs for the kind are not empty and that is due, or every such id when forced, each with its inputs, so a fetched or prompts kind gets its targets without a window; `DeriveAmong` records the derived kind's result for the same set in one write, as a `Derive` window over those ids alone would, and answers how many it recorded. Due and force mean the same for every kind, the same inputs rule a corpus enrich uses, so a forced derived kind records a fresh result for each target as a rebuild does. An id the store does not hold, or a merged candidate's, is skipped rather than refused, since the targets are fixed when the pass is recorded and a ruling may merge one while it runs. `Subject` answers the subject `D16-enrichers` defines for one candidate, which a prompts kind makes a run's input from; for `site_text` the inputs are the website URL its facts row holds, so a website check that lands elsewhere makes the candidate due again.

`GetFacts` answers a candidate's facts row, `ErrNotFound` when it has none: no result yet, merged, or not yet filled after `0012`. `FillFacts` writes the missing rows, once, at the start before `READY=1` (`D03-serve`), from the newest results each candidate already holds, and touches no row already written, so it is safe at every start; a failing store answers it `ErrUnreachable` and it changes nothing, as every store call does then (R-OD8H-2EDG). `GetAssessment` of an active candidate and every member a cohort read answers carry the facts row's facts, read in the same read, or every fact unknown when there is no row.

A merge moves the merged candidate's results to the survivor with its links, keeping the order they were recorded in, deletes the merged candidate's facts row, and then records on the survivor a fresh result of every derived kind whose inputs are not empty; a split leaves the results on the candidate it split and records fresh results, likewise, for both; every other write but `AddEnrichments`, `Derive` and `DeriveAmong` leaves the results alone, and only those, `FillFacts` and a merge or a split write a facts row (website decision 21, enrichers decision 15, `D12-resolution`).

## Pages

`AddPage` records one copy of a page on an active candidate (site text decisions 1 to 3): the URL, title, text and size the caller fetched, who added it, the clock as when it was fetched, and a number the database mints. It is refused `ErrNotFound` for a candidate the store does not hold, `ErrInvalid` for a merged one, and `ErrPageCap` when the URL is new to the candidate and the candidate already holds `PageCap` (20) distinct URLs; a URL it already holds is always accepted, since a newer copy adds no URL. The cap is counted inside the write, so two adds racing cannot carry a candidate past it. An empty URL or user id or a negative size is a value no consumer sends. `AddPage` writes nothing else, no enrichment result, no facts row and no pass, since a page is tied to no pass and no run (site text decision 4). The tool that calls it checks the host, the cap and the fetch first and answers each with its own sentence (`D10-tools`, `D15-website-check`).

`Pages` answers a candidate's newest copy of each URL, newest first by number, without the text, which the `candidate` tool and the candidate page list; `GetPage` one copy with its text, by the candidate and its number, `ErrNotFound` when that number is not on that candidate, so a number a merge moved is reached only through the survivor; and `CountPages` the distinct URLs and the copies a candidate holds, which a `site_text` run's result and its pass's `Pages` count read when the run ends (`D06-passes`; settled for this delivery, S6). A merge moves the other candidate's pages to the survivor, keeping each number, and a split leaves them on the candidate it split (`D12-resolution`); no other write touches a page.

## Reads

`GetCandidate` answers any candidate, active or merged: a merged candidate's record carries its status and the id it was merged into. `Links`, `Estimates`, `GetAssessment`, `Rulings` and `Rejects` answer what the store holds for one candidate; each tells an unknown candidate (`ErrNotFound`) from a known one with nothing to show, except `GetAssessment`, which answers `ErrNotFound` for a candidate not yet judged too, and `GetFacts`, which answers `ErrNotFound` for a candidate with no facts row. An active candidate's assessment answers its facts from its facts row at the read and its judgments from the last judge. Links come not gone first, then newest filing first, so the freshest evidence leads; rulings and reject records newest first. `ListCandidates` is one window of the active candidates newest first, those after a position, the created text and id of the last candidate of the window before, at most `n`, optionally only those one of whose names contains a text, case-insensitively, those at a ZIP, or those one of whose links has a phone key (decision 14). Beside `ListPasses` and `ListCohorts` sit their reversed windows, which the landing's backward step and its last window read (`D08-pages`): `ListPassesReversed` answers at most `n` of the passes that precede a position in pass order, nearest first, and `ListCohortsReversed` likewise in cohort order; an empty id there means the end of the order, so the reversed window from no position is the oldest passes, or the cohorts last in order, last first. The position need not be any entry's. The tools never call them. Each window is ordered and cut by the database on an index (appkit's D25, the suite's Storage convention): a read holds at most the window it was asked for, and the tools and pages ask for one more than they show to learn whether more follow. The store knows nothing of tokens; the tools and pages encode and decode them with `appkit/paging` from the position fields an entry carries (`D10-tools`, `D08-pages`). All lists are never nil.

## Errors

`ErrNotFound`, `ErrBusy`, `ErrDelivered`, `ErrEnded`, `ErrInvalid` and `ErrPageCap` are answers about the records; `ErrInvalid` is the answer of the writes whose arguments come from a person: `Decide`'s, a ruling that is malformed or one the records forbid (`D12-resolution`), `CreateCohort`'s and `EditCohort`'s, a part a validator refuses, `Reject`'s, a blank why or a merged candidate, and `AddPage`'s, a merged candidate. `ErrPageCap` is `AddPage`'s alone, a URL past the cap, which the tool answers with a sentence naming the cap (`D10-tools`). Every other error satisfies `errors.Is(err, ErrUnreachable)`, and every consumer answers it with `Unreachable` (a tool's `unavailable`, a page's unavailable answer, a delivery's `Fail`, a pass failed `store`), never quoting the cause. A done context, a failing handle, a random source that fails and a write given a value no consumer sends all end there, the last changing nothing. No error variable is added for cohorts.

## `db status`

`candidates db status` writes what appkit's `db.Status` writes for `state/candidates.db` and the root package's `Migrations()`, and creates nothing.

The operating confidence is stored and answered rounded to six decimal places and the key is derived from that rounded value, so the key is exact and two confidences share a key only when they are equal (orchestrator ruling, after the sandbox showed one 0.2 piece answering 0.19999999999999996; `D13-scoring`). The column is not null with default -1, so a row the previous release inserts reads as no confidence; a cohort's order puts an unknown last by the confidence being nil, not by the key (`D14-cohorts`), and the key stays the comparison among known ones.
- **A cohort keeps the normal form, and an edit compares it** (log 8, log 16): the filters and the order are stored as the JSON of the store's own types, `omitempty` on every filter member, so the zero filter set is `{}`, a `government` of false is kept and a nil one dropped, and the answer encodes the same type, so the stored text is the answered one. An empty categories list reads back as none, which `D14-cohorts` treats as the filter not carried. An edit builds the cohort that would result, in normal form, and writes only when its name, size, filters or order differ from the stored ones, so a repeated edit restamps nothing and `changed` says when the definition last changed. Create stamps changed as created, so every column is not null (log 24).
- **A deleted cohort is removed, not flagged** (cohorts decision 7): no column of log 16 is a deleted flag, the landing lists only cohorts that exist, and the candidate page shows a record's cohort as deleted by its missing name. Its reject records stay and keep its id, which minting never reuses.
- **One `Reject` method takes the kind**, as `Decide` takes a ruling's kind, since a reject and an unreject name the same three things and differ in what they record. `Decide` is unchanged and still takes `KindExclude` and `KindInclude`; that `decide` refuses them is the tool's (`D10-tools`).
- **The cohort store reads `Config.Now` itself** (log 3): a cohort read's filing-age date comes from the store's own clock, read once per `ReadCohort`, computed in Go and bound, never SQL's `now`, so the tool and the page answer on the day they are called and a test drives the date through the clock it hands in. A cohort read is one statement for the members, `LIMIT` the size, and one for the count (cohorts decision 25); which statements, and that the store holds no more than the members, cannot be observed by use, carry no requirement of their own, and are the build's.
- A write given a value no consumer sends answers an error matching `ErrUnreachable` and changes nothing, as in scrapes; `ErrInvalid` is kept for the writes whose arguments come from a person: a ruling, a cohort and a reject (log 7). The `ValidCohort` functions are exported so the tools refuse `invalid` before the store is read and the store refuses `ErrInvalid` by the same rule, which cannot disagree.
- **Keyed, never whole** (orchestrator ruling): the pass path reads and writes by key on decision 16's indexes, and no index is added for it (the indexes `0008_windows.sql`, `0009_judgments.sql` and `0010_cohorts.sql` add serve the windows, the phone filter and the reject lookups, `0011_enrichments.sql`'s and `0012_facts.sql`'s the enrichment reads, and `0013_site_text.sql`'s the page reads and the cap): the gone-marking's statement runs on the scraper prefix of the links' unique index and its display re-derivation on the links' candidate index, `JudgeAll` on the candidates' index on status, the match on the links' name key, state and ZIP joined to the candidates by id, and a ruling on the candidates' id, the links' candidate index and the rulings by candidate, for the candidates it touches alone. Only the time bound of `D06-passes` and the read that answers while a write is open (R-OXV7-EIWU) are required; the statements are the build's.
- **Ruling points 1 and 2 carry no requirement of their own** (orchestrator ruling): reads by key, work confined to a batch's rows and their candidates, and a bounded gone-marking cannot be observed by use, since a test never reads tables or counts statements; the cost requirements of `D06-passes` (R-00FD-PO9J, R-1YYP-WJMZ, R-206M-ABDO) are their proof, and the read during a write (R-OXV7-EIWU) is ruling point 4's.
- **Upkeep touches only its own table, and its memory is the bound** (log 36): `ReplaceZipDrive` and `PrunePasses` had gone through a write that read every record of every table into memory, copied it and compared the copy afterwards, so the start took 450 to 700 MB over the `us` space's 43,000 candidates and 54,000 links and was killed under `memory_max = 256M` (`D02-cli`). They were that path's only callers, so the path is gone with them; every write is now bounded by what it touches. Which statements they run is the build's; what use can observe is the memory a call allocates, which a test reads with `runtime.ReadMemStats` around the call. R-HJ5E-3SHV and R-HKDA-HK8K bind it over the store `D06-passes`' cost chain leaves behind, 5,000 active candidates judged and 10,000 links, generated as the time bounds' runs are, so no production data is needed: measured over that store, each call allocated about 174 MiB through the whole-database path, 0.7 MiB for the same call over an empty store, and reading the 5,000 candidates alone allocated 15.5 MiB, so the bound of 4 MiB fails a call that reads the candidates whole, and so any that reads the links, twice as many, and leaves the keyed calls room. The cost store holds no ruling, cohort or reject, so a call that read one of those tables whole would pass; this limitation is accepted, since those tables grow only by the owner's hand. `TotalAlloc` counts every goroutine of the test process and only grows, so a collection does not disturb it; the test that reads it does not call `t.Parallel`, waits for the pass's ending event in its capture before it measures, and makes no other call while it measures.
- **An unchanged row still re-derives its candidate's display once per batch**: a link's last seen orders the display (`D12-resolution`, the display link), so the touch an unchanged row makes can change which link shows, and `D12-resolution` requires the display of every candidate an `Attach` returns current after it; the cost stays one re-derivation per candidate per batch, from that candidate's own links. This goes beyond the ruling's "one lookup and a touch of last seen", deliberately.
- **A read never waits for a write**: appkit's `db.Read` answers from the last commit while `db.Write` holds the writer, which a probe against appkit's released `db` showed (a read returned while a test held a write transaction open), and the store adds no lock of its own across a transaction. The store this replaces held one store-wide lock across every transaction, so a read waited behind a write that was itself waiting for the writer; a test holding a `Write` open while an `Attach` waits tells the two apart. A lock the store took only inside its own write transaction, after appkit's writer is won, would escape that test, and this limitation is accepted: use cannot observe when an `Attach` is inside its own transaction, and a lock held there blocks reads only for as long as the batch's own write transaction, which the time bound of `D06-passes` keeps short. Likewise the test cannot confirm that its waiting `Attach`, `Decide` and `Judge` have begun waiting before it reads, since the design declares no hook that would tell it: a store lock held across a whole write call is caught only when a waiting call has taken it before the reads end, which the test makes likely, not certain, by starting the writes first and yielding between reads; this limitation is accepted too.
- `ListCandidates`' `phone` filter is matched on the links' phone keys, any link, gone or not, with the filter normalized by `sources.PhoneKey`: a phone is identity evidence, and a gone link still counts for identity (decision 6).
- **Windows by position, not offset** (appkit's D25): each list read takes the position of the last entry of the window before and a count `n`, as prompts' `Runs` does; a position is the sort key of the entry, the created text (`Created` as RFC 3339 UTC, which is how it is stored) and id for a candidate, the started text and id for a pass, the name and id for a cohort, and an empty id asks for the first window. The tools and pages pass `n` one more than the window `paging.Window` read. An `n` below 1 is refused `ErrUnreachable`, as every value no consumer sends is in candidates' store, where prompts answers an empty window. The landing no longer ranks a window, so no offset count is kept.
- **The order each window takes, and the plan that serves it**: candidates `status = 'active'`, `(created, id) < (?, ?)`, `ORDER BY created DESC, id DESC`, on `candidates_window`; passes `(started, id) < (?, ?)`, `ORDER BY started DESC, id DESC`, on `passes_window`; cohorts `(name, id) > (?, ?)`, `ORDER BY name, id`, on `cohorts_window`, `shape/`'s Storage row value, catalogs by name (log 18). The indexes carry `id` because a table whose primary key is text keeps its rows by rowid, so `candidates_created` and `passes_started`, ordered by rowid among equal times, cannot serve the id tie-break; those two are left in place, and a later contract migration may drop them. **Each window is two fixed statements** (orchestrator decision D28): the first window and the window after a position are separate statements, exported from `internal/store` as `CandidatesFirst` and `CandidatesAfter`, `StartedFirst` and `StartedAfter` for the passes (named for their order, since gate 5's `gosec` G101 flags a string constant whose name holds `pass` as a hardcoded credential), and `CohortsFirst` and `CohortsAfter`, each with every value bound (as scrapes' rows window and prompts' `Runs` are), and each window method runs the first of its pair for an empty id and the second otherwise: SQLite cannot take a disjunction guarded by a bound value as an index bound, so a folded statement walked its index from the start on every deep page (the sandbox check over 10,000 candidates and 2,000 passes). The filters may stay guarded by their bound values (`?='' OR ...`), since none of them is an index bound; the phone probe plans as a correlated subquery on `links_phone`. A first window has no position to seek to, so the passes' and the cohorts' are a `SCAN` of their index in its own order, stopped by the `LIMIT`, and the candidates' a `SEARCH` on `status = ?`. Observed through appkit's driver (`Read` on a handle `db.Open` returned, SQLite 3.53.4) and with the `sqlite3` CLI 3.46.1, over the nine migrations for the candidates and passes, as recorded for `0009` (`CandidatesAfter` `SEARCH candidates USING INDEX candidates_window (status=? AND (created,id)<(?,?))`, `CandidatesFirst` the same with `(status=?)`, `StartedAfter` `SEARCH passes USING COVERING INDEX passes_window ((started,id)<(?,?))` for a statement selecting the id alone, `StartedFirst` `SCAN passes USING INDEX passes_window`), and over the ten for the cohorts (`CohortsAfter` `SEARCH cohorts USING INDEX cohorts_window ((name,id)>(?,?))`, `COVERING INDEX` for a statement selecting the id and name alone, `CohortsFirst` `SCAN cohorts USING INDEX cohorts_window`, before and after `ANALYZE`); no `USE TEMP B-TREE` in any. appkit's driver refuses a statement given fewer arguments than its parameters, so the requirements declare each statement's parameters in order. The plans are required of the six window statements (R-CVLX-U974, R-CWTU-80XT, R-TGA0-W4TG), and each statement's rows are required to be its method's window (R-CUE1-GHGF). That a window method runs its statement rather than another giving the same rows cannot be observed by use, since no test reads source or traces statements; this limitation is accepted, and the sandbox check's deep-page timings and plans cover it on the real binary.
- **The reversed windows mirror the forward ones** (decided 2026-10-13, for the landing's Prev and Last, `D08-pages`) rather than taking a direction flag: each is two fixed statements, as each forward window is, with every value bound, and each reversed method runs the first of its pair for an empty id and the second otherwise. `ListPassesReversed` runs `StartedLast`, `ORDER BY started ASC, id ASC LIMIT ?`, and `StartedPreceding`, `WHERE (started, id) > (?, ?) ORDER BY started ASC, id ASC LIMIT ?`; `ListCohortsReversed` runs `CohortsLast`, `ORDER BY name DESC, id DESC LIMIT ?`, and `CohortsPreceding`, `WHERE (name, id) < (?, ?) ORDER BY name DESC, id DESC LIMIT ?`. They are named for the end they start from and the side of the position they read, mirroring `First` and `After`. Both read the forward windows' indexes in the other direction. Observed as above: `StartedPreceding` `SEARCH passes USING COVERING INDEX passes_window ((started,id)>(?,?))` for a statement selecting the id alone, `StartedLast` `SCAN passes USING INDEX passes_window`, `CohortsPreceding` `SEARCH cohorts USING INDEX cohorts_window ((name,id)<(?,?))`, `CohortsLast` `SCAN cohorts USING INDEX cohorts_window`; no `USE TEMP B-TREE` in any. They answer in the reverse of list order, the order a keyset read from a position backwards takes; the landing page reverses the window it draws. An `n` below 1 is refused `ErrUnreachable`, as the forward windows refuse it. The plans are required of the four (R-D458-INDZ, R-THHX-9WK5) and their rows to be their methods' windows (R-D1PF-R3WL); that a method runs its statement rather than another giving the same rows is accepted unobserved, as for the forward windows.
- **The name filter compares Go's lowering, not SQL's.** `names_lower` is written as Go's `strings.ToLower` of the names joined by line feeds, and SQLite's `lower()` folds only ASCII, so the filter is lowered with `strings.ToLower` before it reaches SQL and found with `instr(names_lower, ?) > 0`, a plain substring test: no `LIKE`, so `%` and `_` need no escape, and no `LIKE` pattern-length limit (50,000 bytes by default) can fail a long filter. The brief's `names_lower LIKE '%' || lower(?) || '%'` with an escape would differ from the Go semantics on non-ASCII letters; this does not. The one difference from the Go semantics the store had is the line feed that joins the names: a filter holding a line feed matches no candidate, where it matched a name holding the same line feed before; no source writes one into a name.
- **What still reads whole tables.** The windows and every read a page or a tool makes read by key or by window: `GetCandidate`, `Links`, `Estimates`, `GetAssessment`, `Rulings`, `Rejects`, `GetCohort`, `GetPass`, `ZipMinutes`, `Pages`, `GetPage` and `CountPages` read one candidate's, one cohort's, one pass's or one page's rows by index. `ReadCohort` reads the assessments of the active candidates its filters do not rule out, ordered by its order and cut by its size, and counts them, which `D14-cohorts`' definition asks of it; its standing rejects come from `rejects_cohort`. `RunningPasses` is no page's or tool's read: the start calls it once, and with no index on `status` it scans `passes`, whose size is the configured retention plus the passes recorded since the last sweep. What still reads a table whole is the judge path and upkeep: `JudgeAll`, which covers every active candidate by definition, and `PrunePasses` and `ReplaceZipDrive`, each its own table alone (log 36). `Decide` reads and writes by key the candidates it touches alone (orchestrator ruling, after the sandbox found one ruling taking eight minutes over 43,058 candidates and 53,787 links, past the MCP gateway's 50 seconds; `D12-resolution`'s requirements bind it). The engine's redelivery check (`D06-passes`, R-YRFA-2BM8) does not list the passes: `FindDelivered` asks for one pass carrying the delivery's event id or naming its run, on `passes_delivery` and on `passes_trigger`, which `0008` adds; for a judge delivery the engine passes no run, so only the delivery arm can match. `passes_delivery` is partial (`WHERE delivery <> ''`, `0006`), so the delivery arm states `delivery <> ''` for SQLite to use it; and since two passes can match at once (one by delivery, another by run), the first in pass order is chosen with `ORDER BY started DESC, id DESC LIMIT 1`. With the guard, `EXPLAIN QUERY PLAN` on SQLite 3.46.1 shows the two arms as `SEARCH passes USING INDEX passes_delivery (delivery=?)` and `SEARCH passes USING INDEX passes_trigger (trigger=?)`. That a list or page read holds no more than its window cannot be observed by use, since a test never counts statements or reads tables (the tests of R-CUE1-GHGF and the plan requirements run only the statements the store exports, never SQL of their own); it carries no requirement of its own, and the sandbox check measures it.
- **`candidate_cargo` is still written, and observed by nothing** (log 30): every write of a candidate's `cargo` replaces that candidate's `candidate_cargo` rows in the same transaction, so the previous release's shortlist cargo filter reads it right after a rollback; `JudgeAll` no longer re-derives the table, the repair having served only the shortlist (`D13-scoring`). Cargo is not a cohort filter (cohorts decision 4), so no read of this release reads the table, and the requirements only the shortlist could observe are gone with it; the upkeep is carried by this prose alone, since a test may not read tables. Dropping the table is a finding for the next contract.
- **The `fleet`, `low` and `high` columns are gone** (website decision 22): `0011` drops them, and the store writes none of them; their absence after the migration is proved by `0011`'s bytes (R-J19N-RJ4G), since no test reads tables, and checked in the sandbox.
- **Enrichment results are generic** (enrichers decision 4): `store.Enrichment` carries the kind, the inputs, the source, what was found as raw JSON, the cost and the time, one shape for every kind, and the registry's `Valid` is what the store checks a found against (`D16-enrichers`); the website check's fields left the store type and are decoded from `Found` by the consumers that show them into a `website.Result`. The website found stays `json.Marshal` of a `website.Result`, members spelled as the Go fields, since the previous release decodes `found` into its own `store.Enrichment` by those names; the derived kinds' members are snake_case and none equals one of that type's field names in any case, so the previous release decodes a candidate's derived rows without failing (`D16-enrichers`). `Cost` cannot be negative, NaN or infinite and `Inputs` cannot be empty; anything else is a value no consumer sends.
- **The facts row is the newest result of each kind** (enrichers decision 5): each kind sets its own columns and its own `_at` from its newest result, and a kind with no result leaves its columns unknown. A candidate whose inputs for a kind become empty (its links all gone from the store, its ZIP cleared, its domain lost) records nothing new for that kind and keeps the facts of its last result, as `D13-scoring` once kept a candidate that stops being checked at its last result; this departs from enrichers decision 3's "the facts projection shows unknown", because a facts row must stay the projection of the results, rebuildable from them alone (enrichers decision 5, enrichers settled 9). `drive_minutes` records a result with no minutes for a ZIP the table lacks, so a ZIP moving to an unknown one clears the minutes and is not examined again at every pass (`D16-enrichers`).
- **A facts row exists only beside a result**: `FillFacts` and every write build a row only for an active candidate holding a result; a candidate with none has no row and reads every fact unknown, and a merge deletes the merged candidate's row, one row per active candidate (enrichers decision 5). The row is keyed by the candidate and has no other index: the cohort query reaches it by key from each candidate, as it reached the assessments' facts, which had no index of their own either, so the primary key is all of the "indexes" the decisions ask `0012` to create for the facts.
- **The derived kinds run in the store** (decided for this delivery; enrichers decisions 9 and 15): `Derive` and a ruling's re-run call the pure functions of `internal/enrichers` inside the store's write, so a merge's moves, its fresh derived results and its re-judge are one write, as the judge already is; the store imports `enrichers`, which imports nothing of the store or the engine. A merge or a split records a fresh result of each derived kind whose inputs are not empty for each touched active candidate, whether or not that candidate is due for the kind (R-R7HY-562T, `D12-resolution`), so the survivor's facts reflect the moved links even where the moved results' order would leave an older one newest.
- **Newest is recorded later, not stamped later** (decided 2026-10-15): two results of one kind on a candidate are ordered by when they were recorded, which is deterministic where two share a second, and survives a merge that moves the other candidate's results among the survivor's, as the rulings are ordered. A survivor whose merged candidate's last check came after its own takes that result as its newest.
- **The due set is a keyset window in id order** (website log P8, P12, enrichers decision 3): `DueEnrichments` answers at most `n` due candidates after a cursor id, each with its inputs, so a pass holds one batch and a candidate already answered is never answered again in the same pass, whatever its results become; due is a comparison of inputs, never of ages, so `live`, `none` and their arithmetic are gone with the refresh ages. A candidate whose id falls after the cursor may still be reached in the pass that is running, since ids are random. The window's statement is not exported and its plan is not required: the due set is the pass's alone, read by no page or tool, and `D06-passes` and `D15-website-check` bound what the pass holds. The inputs of `link_facts` are computed in Go from the candidate's links, so the due window reads each candidate of the window and its newest result of the kind by key; which statements the build chooses is its own. `Derive` walks the active candidates the same way.
- **The mark is an opaque sequence** (decided 2026-10-15): `EnrichmentMark` answers the highest `seq` held and `EnrichmentAfter` the last result above it of one kind and inputs, on `enrichments_inputs`, which `0012` adds now that the reuse matches inputs rather than the source (for a website result the two are the same domain); results are never removed and a merge keeps their sequence, so a mark never reaches a result recorded after it. The value is not contract: only that results recorded before it are not found and results recorded after it are (R-GX77-7X5D).
- **`derived` sits after `unranged`** in `store.Pass` and `store.Counts`, as everywhere the counts are listed (`D06-passes`), since the pass page lists it first among an enrich's counts. `Runs`, `RunsFailed`, `Pages` and `Cost` follow `None`, and `Target` and `EnrichKind` follow `Trigger`, in the order the pass page, the trail and the tools list them (`D08-pages`, `D07-events`, `D06-passes`); `Cost` is an integer of nano-dollars, as prompts reports a run's cost, so the pass's sum is exact (site text decision 14).
