# D20-search

Search: finding an active candidate by part of its business name, possibly misspelled. `store.Search` answers the best matches first, each the candidate as `GetCandidate` answers it, the stored name it was matched on, verbatim, and a score from 0 to 1; the `search` tool (`D10-tools`) passes its `q` and `limit` through and converts each match, adding nothing. Behind it sits the **search index**, an FTS5 table with the trigram tokenizer in candidates' own database, one row per name of each active candidate, derived from the candidates table, created and filled by `0030_search_index.sql` and kept by every write of a candidate; beside it the same migration adds `candidates_names_lower`, a plain index on the candidates' `names_lower`, which serves a short query. Search is additive (settled for this delivery): the `candidates` tool, its substring `name` filter, `ListCandidates` and every other read answer exactly as before, and no page gains a search box.

This document owns, in package `internal/store`, `store.Match`, `(*Store).Search`, `store.SearchDefaultLimit`, `store.SearchMaxLimit`, `store.SearchShort`, the statement of a short query's read, and `store.SearchCandidates`, the statement that reads the answer's candidates, each with its plan; the file `0030_search_index.sql` and its bytes, the index `candidates_names_lower` among them; the words **query**, **word**, **short**, **names**, **tier**, **score** and **ranking**; what `Search` answers, at every size and exactly on a small store; how the index stands after the migration and after each write of a candidate, as `Search` shows it; and the time a call takes. `D04-store` counts the migration among the thirty and holds the Known limit of the mixed window; `D10-tools` the tool, its arguments, its answer and its refusals; `D09-trail` the rule that `q` is record text; `D11-on-a-space` the release that carries it to `us`.

## The query and the names

NUL is a space: before anything else happens to a query, to a name being ranked, or to a name the store writes into the index, each U+0000 in it becomes one space (Recorded decisions). The **query** of a `q` is `q` with each NUL a space, its leading and trailing white space then removed, and the rest lower-cased by Go's Unicode rules (`strings.ToLower`); a `q` whose query is empty, one of NULs and white space alone among them, is **blank** and refused, `ErrInvalid`, as is a `limit` outside 1 to `SearchMaxLimit` (50). The tool sends `SearchDefaultLimit` (10) when the caller names no limit. A query of one or two runes is **short**.

A **word** of a string is a maximal run of Unicode letters and digits in it, so `at&t` has the words `at` and `t` and `Smith-Jones Co.` the words `smith`, `jones` and `co` once folded. Every length here counts runes.

The **names** of a candidate are its display name, `Name`, and each other element of `Names` (every name its links use, `D12-ingest`), the empty string never one and each distinct string once; the display name is the one equal to `Name`, every other an **alternate**. A name is matched **folded**, each NUL a space and then lower-cased by the same `strings.ToLower`, and answered as stored, NUL and all; no length changes, a NUL and a space being one rune each.

## Tiers and scores

A name has, for a query that is not short, at most one **tier**, the first that holds, its folded form `n` and the query `Q`:

1. **equals**: `n` is `Q`;
2. **starts with**: `n` begins with `Q`;
3. **word prefix**: `Q` has a word, and every word of `Q` begins some word of `n`, in any order;
4. **substring**: `n` contains `Q`;
5. **near miss**: `Q` has a word, and every word of `Q` is within its **allowance** of some word of `n`: the Damerau-Levenshtein distance between them, adjacent transposition counted as one edit and edits allowed between and across transposed letters (the unrestricted distance, not optimal string alignment), is at most the name word's length divided by 5, rounded down.

Tiers 1, 2 and 4 compare the folded strings verbatim, punctuation and spacing included, a NUL already a space; tiers 3 and 5 compare words. So one error in a word of five letters or more is a near miss, two errors in one of ten or more is too, and ranks lower; a typo in a word of four letters or fewer is not found; a wrong first letter is found, but never reaches the prefix tiers. In tier 5 each query word is paired with a name word at the least distance from it, of those the one with the greatest allowance; the **edits** are the distances summed over the query's words and the **allowance** the pairs' allowances summed. A name in tier 5 holds at least one edit, since a query every word of which equals a name word is in tier 3.

A name's **score** is 1 in tier 1, and in tiers 2 to 5 lies in its band, starts with [0.8, 1), word prefix [0.6, 0.8), substring [0.4, 0.6) and near miss [0.1, 0.4): the band's low end plus its width times `w = 0.6 × display + 0.25 × coverage + 0.15 × closeness`, display 1 for the display name and 0 for an alternate, coverage the query's length over the name's, at most 1, and closeness 1 in tiers 2 to 4 and in tier 5 one less the edits over the allowance. A score the formula puts at its band's top, which only tier 3 reaches, a display name with every word of it begun by the query, is the greatest `float64` below the top. The bands do not overlap, so ordering by tier then score is ordering by score.

Worked examples, the display name or alternate stated:

- `acme` on the display name `Acme Trucking`: starts with, coverage 4/13, 0.8 + 0.2 × (0.6 + 0.25 × 4/13 + 0.15) = 0.965385; on an alternate `Acme Trucking`, 0.845385.
- `trucking acme` on the display name `Acme Trucking`: word prefix, coverage 1, `w` 1, so the score is the greatest `float64` below 0.8.
- `me truck` on the display name `Acme Trucking`: substring, 0.580769.
- `brown hawk` on the alternate `BROWN HAWK LLC`: starts with, 0.865714.
- `trujilo transport` on the display name `Trujillo Transport Services`: near miss; `trujilo` is one edit from `trujillo`, allowance 1, `transport` none, allowance 1; closeness 1 − 1/2; coverage 17/27; 0.1 + 0.3 × (0.6 + 0.25 × 17/27 + 0.15 × 0.5) = 0.349722.
- `mcuen` on the display name `MCCUEN CONSTRUCTION INC`: near miss, one edit of allowance 1, 0.296304.
- `cadefghij` on the display name `Abcdefghij`: near miss, two edits (`ca` for `abc`) of allowance 2, closeness 0, 0.3475; optimal string alignment counts three, past the allowance, and would not find it.
- `abcdefghi` on the display name `Abcdzefghi Abcdfghi`: one edit from each word, allowances 2 and 1; paired with `abcdzefghi`, closeness 0.5, coverage 9/19, 0.338026.
- `xbcdefghij` on the display name `Abcdefghij`: a wrong first letter, near miss, 0.3775, not starts with.
- `jnoe` on `Jone Co`: one transposition in a four-letter word, allowance 0, no tier.
- `A\u0000B` on the display name `A\u0000B` of a candidate written after the migration, by `Attach` say: both become `a b`, equals, score 1; recalled by pass 2 on the query's one trigram `a b`, which the index holds, pass 1 skipped since no word has three runes.

The **ranking** of a query over a set of candidates holds one entry per candidate with a name that has a tier: its **best name**, the one of lowest tier, then highest score, then first in the order `Name`, then `Names`, with that name's score; entries ordered by tier, then score descending, then candidate id ascending. `Search` answers a prefix of the ranking, at most `limit` entries, each a `Match`.

## Recall: the index and its two passes

Tiers 2 to 5 are decided in Go over the rows the index recalls; the index's own rank chooses which rows come back, never their order. A row the store writes holds a name with each NUL a space, and a row the backfill wrote the stored name as it is; a recalled row stands for its candidate, whose names are ranked as stored and folded by the rule above, and `Matched` is the stored name. The index is `search_index`, an FTS5 table with columns `name`, `candidate` and `display`, the last two unindexed, tokenized by `trigram`, beside `search_index_vocab`, its `fts5vocab` table of the `row` kind, which answers for each trigram the number of rows holding it (`doc`).

- **Pass 1** asks, for each word of the query of three runes or more, any of that word's trigrams, the words joined by an explicit `AND`: `("tru" OR "ruj" OR …) AND ("tra" OR "ran" OR …)`, each trigram a quoted phrase, a `"` inside doubled. A word of one or two runes has no trigram and is left out; a query with no word of three runes skips the pass. FTS5's implicit AND of whole phrases is not used: a misspelled word shares only some of its trigrams with the word it misspells.
- **Pass 2** runs only when pass 1 recalled fewer rows than `limit`: the query's own trigrams, white space and punctuation included, the rarest eight of them by `doc` (`SELECT term FROM search_index_vocab WHERE term IN (…) ORDER BY doc, term LIMIT 8`; a trigram the index lacks has no vocab row and drops out), joined by `OR`.

Each pass takes at most 200 rows, `ORDER BY rank LIMIT 200`, rank being FTS5's bm25. The rows recalled are read with their candidates, a candidate whose status is not `active` dropped, as a guard for the mixed window, and ranked by the rules above.

So what `Search` finds is exactly the ranking whenever the cap cannot bind: on a store whose active candidates hold at most 200 names in all, every name in tier 1, 2 or 4 is recalled, since it holds every trigram of the query; every name in tier 3 when the query has a word of three runes or more, since that word is a substring of it; and every name in tier 5 that holds at least one trigram of each such word of the query. A near miss of a five-letter word changed in its middle letter (`abcde`, `abxde`) shares no trigram with it and may be missed. On a large store the cap bounds the work and may leave out a match bm25 rates low; what is answered is still ranked by these rules, each candidate once.

The trigram tokenizer folds case for ASCII and other letters alike and keeps diacritics, as `strings.ToLower` does, and in a row the store writes never sees a NUL, the text holding the space the folded form holds, so recall and ranking agree; where a rune's folding differs between FTS5 and Go, recall follows FTS5 and ranking Go.

## Short queries

A short query does not use the search index, whose phrases of fewer than three runes match nothing. It answers the active candidates whose display name, folded, begins with the query, alternates never, each scored as a display name: 1 when the display name equals the query, else in the starts-with band, so a shorter name first, then the candidate id. Lower-casing is Go's, as `ListCandidates`' name filter folds through `names_lower`, which the store writes with `strings.ToLower`; SQLite's `lower()` folds ASCII alone and would leave `Ébène` unmatched by `éb`. `ab` on `Abc` scores 0.983333 and on `Ab` 1; `éb` on `Ébène Co` 0.9625.

The read is a range on `names_lower`, served by `candidates_names_lower`, the plain index `0030` adds: the active candidates whose `names_lower` is at least `Q`, the query, and less than `Q` followed by the one byte 0xFF, both bound as text, the lower bound inclusive and the upper exclusive. `store.SearchShort` is that statement, its two parameters the two bounds in that order. `names_lower` is `strings.ToLower` of the candidate's `Names` joined by line feeds, written only where a candidate is written (`D04-store`); `Names` begins with `Name` when it is not empty, so `names_lower` begins with the display name lowered, followed by a line feed when alternates follow. The range holds every string that begins with `Q` and no other, since `names_lower` never holds the byte 0xFF: `strings.ToLower` leaves an ASCII string as it is and writes any other as valid UTF-8, which never holds that byte; a bound of `Q` followed by U+10FFFF instead would miss a name holding `Q` followed by that rune.

So the range answers the candidates whose `names_lower` begins with `Q`, and for a candidate whose `Name` is not empty that is exactly the candidates whose folded display name begins with `Q`, though `names_lower` keeps a NUL where folding makes it a space: a short query holds no space, line feed or NUL, since a NUL becomes a space, white space at the ends is trimmed and a space within takes three runes, so where the display name holds a NUL neither form matches the query's rune; and lowering the names joined or one at a time gives the same bytes (probed with `İ`, `Σ` and NUL). A candidate whose `Name` is empty may come back on its first alternate; the ranking leaves it out (R-7D72-ZYXN), so `Search` does too. The rows are ranked in Go as the ranking orders a short query, `Score` descending, so a shorter display name first, then the candidate id, and at most `limit` entries are kept (Recorded decisions).

The planner needs the statement's help (probed with the `sqlite3` CLI 3.46.1 on a copy of the sandbox's database, 90,740 active candidates, no `ANALYZE`; the app's modernc.org/sqlite v1.59.0 bundles SQLite 3.53.4): `status = 'active' AND names_lower >= ? AND names_lower < ?` plans as `SEARCH candidates USING INDEX candidates_window (status=?)`, every active candidate read; with the status test kept from the index, as `+status = 'active'` keeps it, it plans as `SEARCH candidates USING INDEX candidates_names_lower (names_lower>? AND names_lower<?)`. After an `ANALYZE` the planner picks the range unaided, but nothing runs one. An `ORDER BY names_lower` sends it back to `candidates_window`, so the statement orders nothing and Go ranks. The statement's text is the build's; its plan is required (R-40I3-9W4D). Bound from Go, a string such as `"ma\xff"` reaches SQLite as text, its bytes unchanged (probed under modernc.org/sqlite v1.59.0).

## Reading the answer

Whichever way the matches were found, by the passes or by the short read, the candidates the answer keeps, at most `limit`, are read in one statement, `store.SearchCandidates`: the candidates whose `id` is in a JSON array of ids, `id IN (SELECT value FROM json_each(?))`, its one parameter that array as text. It filters no status: a candidate that is not active is dropped in Go, the guard on status above, and each `Match` carries the candidate as `GetCandidate` answers it (R-4HXU-477Y). Its rows come in no promised order; `Search` puts them back in the ranking's order. With the `sqlite3` CLI 3.46.1, on a scratch in-memory table `candidates(id TEXT PRIMARY KEY, ...)` and again over the real `0001` to `0030` files, it plans as `SEARCH candidates USING INDEX sqlite_autoindex_candidates_1 (id=?)` over a `LIST SUBQUERY` that scans `json_each`, so the plan required of it (R-7WK5-7X11) forbids a scan of `candidates`, not every `SCAN`.

## The index and its upkeep

`0030_search_index.sql` creates both tables, fills the index from the active candidates and, in its fourth line, creates `candidates_names_lower`, a plain index on `candidates(names_lower)`; the fill holds a row for `candidates.name` with `display` 1 when it is not empty, and a row with `display` 0 for each distinct text element of `candidates.names` that is not empty and differs from it, the `UNION` dropping an alternate the array repeats; each row's text is the stored name as it is, NUL and all (Known limits). The probe that proved it ran under modernc.org/sqlite, cgo off, over a database the real `0001` to `0029` files made: the FTS5 and `fts5vocab` tables are created, the trigram tokenizer folds case for ASCII and non-ASCII letters, a phrase under three characters matches nothing, a phrase holding a space or a doubled `"` matches, `ORDER BY rank LIMIT 200` works, and the backfill of its third line filled the index, an alternate the array repeated, the empty string and a non-text element giving no row. `candidates_names_lower` is a plain expand: SQLite keeps it on every write of `candidates`, the previous release's included, so it needs no upkeep of the store's and adds no Known limit.

The store keeps it in the one place a candidate is written, so every write path keeps it: `Attach`, through an ingest or a rebuild, `MarkGone`, `Decide` with a merge or a split, `AddOwnerRecord`, `RecordSweep` and `Rebuild`. Each write of a candidate replaces that candidate's rows with one per name it now holds, each distinct name once, its text the name with each NUL a space, the display name with `display` 1, or none when it is not active, in the same transaction; besides the paths above, any other write that re-derives a candidate, `AddEnrichments` and `FillFacts` among them, reaches that write. A full ingest rewrites every active candidate, so no reindex tool exists. `exclude`, `include` and `not_same` change no candidate (`D12-ingest`), and an excluded candidate stays active and is answered.

## Recorded decisions

- **The trigram index over a scan** (settled for this delivery): a misspelling search must be answered from a few hundred rows, not the 130,000 names a space holds, and the trigram tokenizer finds a word from part of it with no stemming or word list of its own; ranking stays in Go, where the tiers are exact.
- **The index's rank recalls, never ranks** (settled for this delivery): bm25 rewards short rows and rare trigrams, which says nothing of whether a name begins with the query; tiers and scores decide the order, and bm25 only which 200 rows a pass keeps.
- **Two passes, the second only when the first is thin** (settled for this delivery): the AND of the words keeps a two-word query from drowning in either word's matches; the OR of the rarest eight trigrams rescues a query whose AND recalls too little, a word misspelled past its trigrams, at the cost of a second statement only then. Rarity is the vocab's `doc`, the index's own count, so nothing is kept beside it.
- **Short words are left out of pass 1**: a word of one or two runes yields no trigram, and a phrase that short matches nothing (probed), so it would make the AND fail; the ranking still requires it in tiers 3 and 5.
- **The tie between pairings goes to the greater allowance** (decided in this design): where two name words are equally near a query word, the longer one, whose allowance is greater, is its pair, since the entry is the candidate's best and a candidate's names give it their best; minimizing edits first is the settled rule, this only breaks its ties.
- **The clamp is one `float64` below the top** (decided in this design): "strictly inside its band" is kept by the nearest value below the top, `math.Nextafter(top, 0)`, so a clamped score still sorts above every unclamped one of its band.
- **Departure from `shape/`'s Bounded work** (settled for this delivery): a ranked answer over every name cannot be paged by a key, so a pass's statement spans the index's matching rows and a short query's read spans the active candidates whose `names_lower` begins with the query, thousands for one common letter. Memory stays bounded, 200 rows a pass and `limit` entries for a short read; the time is bounded by the figure the build tests, a call within 100 ms at 100,000 candidates, a short query's among them (R-9JC7-61RU), and by the plans required of the short read and the answer's read; on real names a thin multi-word query may pass the figure (Known limits).
- **A B-tree on `names_lower` over a scan for a short query** (settled for this delivery): the short read first scanned the active candidates, `instr(names_lower, ?) = 1`, and a short `search` took about 275 ms on the sandbox's 90,740, past the 100 ms figure. A range on a plain index of `names_lower` answers the same rows: on a copy of that database, with the `sqlite3` CLI 3.46.1 and no `ANALYZE`, the read for `a` (6,556 rows) took 60 ms by `instr` and 12 ms by the range, for `ma` (1,865 rows) 67 and 3 ms, for `zz` (1 row) 59 and 0 ms, and the two answered the same set for `a`, `ma`, `co`, `th`, `jo` and `zz`. `GLOB` would need its metacharacters escaped, and `LIKE` uses no index while SQLite's `LIKE` folds case, as it does by default; the range needs neither. Its plan is required, since the timing test's synthetic store may not tell a scan from a search in time alone.
- **One statement for the answer's rows** (decided in this design): `Search` read each kept candidate with a statement of its own, up to 50, which cost about 30 to 40 ms at a `limit` of 50 on the sandbox's 90,740 candidates; one statement reads them all. `json_each` carries the ids because a constant cannot hold an `IN` list of varying length.
- **Kept in `putCandidate`, not by triggers** (settled for this delivery): the index is the store's derived data, written beside the candidate in the same transaction, as `candidate_cargo` is; the price is the Known limit below.
- **The guard on status** (settled for this delivery): rows are read with their candidates and a candidate that is not active is dropped, so a merge the previous release records in the mixed window, which leaves the merged candidate's rows, never answers a merged candidate.
- **NUL is a space** (settled for this delivery): the trigram tokenizer keeps no trigram holding NUL, so a name holding one could not be recalled, not even by its exact text; mapping NUL to a space before anything else, in the query, the folded names and the text the store's upkeep writes, makes recall and ranking agree. The backfill is raw: SQLite's `replace` cannot match an embedded NUL (probed under sqlite3 3.46.1 and modernc.org/sqlite v1.59.0), and the next full `ingest` heals what it leaves. Only NUL: no other control character is touched, and the stored name is answered as it is.
- **Diacritics are kept** (probed): the trigram tokenizer's default leaves them, and `strings.ToLower` does too, so `Ébène` matches `ébène` and not `ebene`, the same in recall and ranking.

## Known limits

- **A name stored with a NUL before `0030` is not found until its candidate is written again** (settled for this delivery): the backfill indexes stored names as they are and the trigram tokenizer keeps no trigram holding NUL, so such a name is not recalled until that candidate's next write by this release or the next full `ingest`, which rewrites every active candidate's rows.
- **A candidate the previous release writes is not reindexed** (settled for this delivery): the previous release names no search index, so a candidate it writes during a mixed window or after a rollback across `0030` keeps the rows this release last wrote for it, or none, until its next write by this release or the next full `ingest`; `search` may miss it or answer it matched on a name it no longer holds, the `candidate` beside it current (`D04-store`).
- **The cap may hide a weak match on a large store**: each pass keeps 200 rows by bm25, so a query common to thousands of names may leave out one that would rank in the top `limit`, typically a near miss on a long alternate.
- **A middle-letter typo in a short word may not be recalled**: `abxde` shares no trigram with `abcde`, so it is found only through pass 2's rarest trigrams of the rest of the query.
- **A thin multi-word query may pass the figure**: a query of several words whose pass 1 recalls fewer than `limit` rows runs pass 2, and on a store whose names share many common trigrams that pass can take longer than 100 ms. On the sandbox's 90,740 real candidates the four-word exact name `ANDERSON BUS COMPANY INC` took 82 to 135 ms, pass 1 alone 5 to 10 ms. The figure (R-9JC7-61RU) and the pass rules stand; the timing test's synthetic store does not show this.
- **A one-word query on a common fragment may pass the figure at a large `limit`**: a word many names share, `ander`, which pass 1 matches in about 12,800 index rows, ran 96 to 122 ms at a `limit` of 50 on the sandbox's 90,740 real candidates, while the same query at the default `limit` ran about 64 ms and the figure's own test passes. Pass 1 ranks every matching row to keep its top 200 whatever `limit` is, and decoding fifty rows adds the rest; a narrower pass 1 or a smaller recall limit would cut it, and is left for a later decision.

## Consumer tasks

The `search` tool, `a` its arguments, calls `ms, err := cfg.Store.Search(ctx, a.Q, l)`, `l` being `*a.Limit` or `store.SearchDefaultLimit`, and converts each `m` to its own match from `m.Candidate`, `m.Matched` and `m.Score` (`D10-tools`). Over a store holding the active candidates `Acme Trucking` (display, with the alternate `ACME TRUCKING LLC`) and `Zed Holdings` (display, with the alternate `Acme Trucking`), `st.Search(ctx, "  ACME Trucking ", 10)` answers two matches: `Acme Trucking`'s candidate on its display name, score 1, and `Zed Holdings`'s on its alternate `Acme Trucking`, score 1, in candidate id order; `st.Search(ctx, "acme", 1)` answers the first alone, at 0.965385, the second ranking below at 0.845385. A candidate `Attach` wrote with the display name `"A\x00B"` is answered by `st.Search(ctx, "A\x00B", 10)` on `Matched` `"A\x00B"`, score 1. `st.Search(ctx, " \t", 10)`, `st.Search(ctx, "\x00 ", 10)` and `st.Search(ctx, "acme", 51)` answer `store.ErrInvalid`.

A test opens its own database with `db.Open` and the root package's `Migrations()`, builds `store.New(d, store.Config{Now: clock, Rand: rnd})`, writes candidates through `Attach`, `MarkGone`, `Decide`, `AddOwnerRecord`, `RecordSweep` and `Rebuild`, reads them back with `GetCandidate`, and compares `Search`'s answer with a ranking it computes from those candidates by the rules above, a true Damerau-Levenshtein of its own included. A test of `0030` reads `fs.ReadFile(candidates.Migrations(), "0030_search_index.sql")`; one of the short read runs `store.SearchShort` through `d.Read`, bound to `"ma"` and `"ma\xff"` over candidates named `Ma`, `Ma Co` and `Mb`, an active and a merged among them, and to `"éb"` and `"éb\xff"` over one named `Ébène`, and `EXPLAIN QUERY PLAN ` followed by it the same way; one of the answer's read runs `store.SearchCandidates` through `d.Read`, bound to a JSON array such as `["<active id>","<merged id>","no-such-id"]`, and `EXPLAIN QUERY PLAN ` followed by it bound to `"[]"`; one of the backfill opens a database with a file system holding only the first twenty-nine files, inserts candidates with `d.Write` as the previous release writes them, opens it again with `Migrations()` and asks `Search`. The timing test builds its database the same way, 100,000 candidates from a fixed seed, and times each call.
