# D16-enrichers

The enrichers: the kinds of enrichment candidates keeps, each a kind word with a rule naming a candidate's inputs and a rule computing a result from them, held in one fixed registry the enrich pass walks without knowing any kind in particular (enrichers decisions 1 and 2). The site text decisions document adds a fourth kind, `site_text`, the first whose result is a run of a prompt kept in the prompts service (its decisions 5 to 9), and the brief decisions document a fifth, `brief`, a run of a prompt that reads the pages `site_text` kept on a company and writes a summary of it with its categories and labels (its decisions 1 to 6). This document owns the package `internal/enrichers` whole: the kind words and source words, the **subject** an enricher reads of a candidate, the result and the found shapes, the `Enricher`, `Deriver`, `Fetcher` and `Prompter` interfaces, the five enrichers `drive_minutes`, `link_facts`, `website_check`, `site_text` and `brief`, what each names as its inputs and, for the two derived kinds, what each computes, for `site_text` and `brief` the prompt each names, the input object of a run, the result recording one and which recorded results are of a run that failed, which found values each accepts, and the registry with what it is built from. The records, the due read, the derived walk, the facts row and the reads of results are `D04-store`'s (`store.Enrichment`, `DueEnrichments`, `Derive`, `AddEnrichments`, `GetFacts`, `FillFacts`), and so is how a brief result becomes the facts row's `brief` text and categories; the enrich pass, the rebuild, their counts and follow-ons are `D06-passes`'; what the website check fetches, how, and what its `Ready` and `Fetch` do are `D15-website-check`'s; the targeted enrich pass that runs a `Prompter` kind, asking the prompts service for the prompt, starting runs, waiting on them, counting the runs that failed and, for `site_text`, the pages they add, is `D06-passes`'; the pages themselves, and how the store reads them only for the kinds that read them, are `D04-store`'s; the list of the category words is `D01-layout-and-run-seam`'s (`sources.Categories()`); merge and split re-running the derived kinds are `D12-resolution`'s; what a judge makes of the facts is `D13-scoring`'s; the `enrich` tool is `D10-tools`'.

"Enrichers decision N" is entry N of the decisions document of the enrichers change, and "categories decision N" entry N of the decisions document of the categories change. "Site text decision N" is entry N of the decisions document of the site-text change, "brief decision N" entry N of the decisions document of the brief change, "P N" entry N of the decisions log of the brief change's delivery, and "category evidence decision N" entry N of the decisions document of the category evidence change.

## Fetched and derived

An enricher is one kind. A kind is **fetched** when it reaches outside the service and may cost money, and **derived** when it reads only what the service holds (enrichers decision 1). The distinction is the enricher's `Fetched`. A derived kind is a `Deriver`, whose `Derive` computes a result from the subject alone, so the store can run it inside its own writes. A fetched kind is one of two things. A `Fetcher`, the website check, computes many inputs at a pace of its own through `Fetch`, one result per distinct inputs. A `Prompter`, `site_text` or `brief`, is one run of a prompt per candidate: it names the prompt, builds the run's input object from the subject, turns the run's outcome into a result and says whether a recorded result of its kind is of a run that failed, and the pass does the running (`D06-passes`). Both have a `Ready` saying whether they have anything to fetch with. A rebuild treats every derived kind as due for every candidate and never a fetched one (`D06-passes`); a merge or split re-runs the derived kinds for what it touched (`D12-resolution`). Every enricher is exactly one of the three.

Five kinds are registered in this release, in this order, which is the order the enrich pass walks them: `drive_minutes` and `link_facts`, derived, then `website_check`, `site_text` and `brief`, fetched (enrichers decision 8: derived kinds first, since they finish in seconds and the fetched kinds can take hours; site text decision 5: `site_text` after `website_check`; brief decision 1: `brief` after `site_text`, whose pages it reads). It is no longer kind name order, since `brief` and `site_text` sort before `website_check` (recorded decision below). `enrichers.Kinds()` lists them. An enrich with no target never walks a `Prompter` kind, and `site_text` and `brief` run only over a cohort or one candidate (site text decision 6; brief decision 10; `D06-passes`).

## Inputs and due

Every kind names a candidate's **inputs** as one string: the display ZIP for `drive_minutes`, the link fingerprint for `link_facts`, the checked domain (`website.Domain` of the display email, `D15-website-check`) for `website_check`, the website URL the facts row holds for `site_text`, and a fingerprint of the pages kept on the candidate for `brief`. Every result records the inputs it was computed from (`store.Enrichment.Inputs`, `D04-store`). A candidate is due for a kind when it has no result of that kind, or its newest result's inputs differ from the inputs named now; there are no refresh ages (enrichers decision 3). Empty inputs mean not due and no result: no ZIP, no own domain, no link at all, no website, no page.

The inputs are computed from the candidate's **subject**, the one value an enricher reads: the candidate's id, display name, city, state, email and ZIP, the website URL its facts row holds (the URL the newest website check landed on, `D04-store`), the ZIP table's minutes for that ZIP, and every link with its scraper, source id, whether it is gone and the source's reading of it, in the order `store.Links` answers (`D12-resolution`: not gone first, then newest filing date, then newest last seen); the newest copy of each page URL kept on the candidate, with its number, URL, title, text and size, in the order the URLs were first kept (brief decision 3); and the link facts its facts row holds, its categories those of the newest `link_facts` result (the facts' `Records`, `D04-store`), nil when it has no facts row (brief decision 4). The subject is built by the store, which holds all of it; the enrichers never read the store, so they are pure functions a test drives with subjects it builds. The subject is defined with its pages whatever the kind it is built for; only a `Prompter` reads them, and only a targeted pass runs one, so the store reads a candidate's pages only when the kind it builds the subject for is a `Prompter`, and an untargeted walk never reads them. How it does so is invisible here and is `D04-store`'s recorded decision.

## drive_minutes

The inputs are the display ZIP, and the result is the minutes the ZIP table holds for it (enrichers decision 2), as `{"minutes":84}`, with source word `zip_drive` and cost 0. When the table lacks the ZIP, or holds a negative value for it, the minutes are unknown and the result is still written, as `{}`: a candidate whose ZIP moves from a known one to an unknown one must lose its stale minutes, and a result keeps it from being due on every pass forever (recorded decision below).

## link_facts

The result is the facts a judge derived from links before this release (enrichers decision 2), computed now once, when the links change, over the links not gone: **known since**, the earliest date any shows the business existing; the **newest filing**; the **government match**, the first pattern a link's name matched, in link order; the **categories**, each a link's category with the scraper that gave it, and beside them one from the government match with the source word `name`: `education` when the match is `SCHOOL DISTRICT` or `UNIVERSITY`, `government` for every other pattern (categories decision 3); and the **fleet verdict** with the **power units** and **drivers**, from the first FMCSA link, which in link order is the freshest. A date whose UTC year is outside 0 to 9999, a category from a link with no scraper and a verdict outside the three are absent or unknown, never copied (recorded decision below). The rules are `D13-scoring`'s of before, moved here unchanged but for the category from the government match, and so are their examples but for two, whose categories now hold that category; the categories are sorted by name, then source, and kept once each, which puts `government` from `name` before `trucking and logistics` from `fmcsa`, and keeps `government` from `name` beside `government` from `ppp` since the source differs; the result's source word is `links` and the cost 0. Link facts are one kind, not five, because they share one input and are always recomputed together.

`link_facts` copies each link's category as its source read it and is unchanged by the category evidence change (category evidence decision 10); what changes is the FMCSA reading, which gives `trucking and logistics` only to a carrier whose class is `for_hire` or `mixed` (category evidence decision 7, `D05-sources`). So a private carrier's FMCSA link adds no category here: one whose name matches `COUNTY` gives `government` from `name` alone, one beside a PPP link with code `236220` gives `construction` from `ppp` alone, and one with neither gives none and is unknown (category evidence decision 8), while a for-hire or mixed carrier still gives `trucking and logistics` from `fmcsa`.

## The fleet verdict

The fleet verdict is `pass`, `fail` or `unknown` (`sources.FleetPass`, `sources.FleetFail`, `sources.FleetUnknown`). Each FMCSA row already says which, on its reading (`D05-sources`). `link_facts` copies the verdict of the candidate's first FMCSA link not gone, `unknown` when it is not one of the three, and that link's power units and drivers beside it; with no such link the verdict is unknown and both counts are absent. Only FMCSA counts trucks, so a candidate known only from PPP is unknown, never fail, whatever its readings say; a link stored by an earlier release decodes with an empty verdict and gives `unknown`, never a guess from its old fleet boolean. A negative count on that link's reading is absent, as if the reading lacked it. Power units and drivers come from the same link as the verdict, so they never disagree.

## The link fingerprint

The inputs of `link_facts` are a fingerprint of every link of the candidate, gone ones included: the SHA-256, in lowercase hexadecimal, of one line per link holding its source key, whether it is gone and its reading as JSON, the lines sorted. Sorting makes the order of the links irrelevant; including the gone flag makes a link going gone change the inputs, so the facts drop it. The inputs are empty only when the candidate has no link at all.

## website_check

The website check is `D15-website-check`'s; here it is one enricher among five. Its inputs are the checked domain, its source word is that domain too, and its found value is the JSON of a `website.Result`. `Ready` is whether it holds a checker, so an enrich pass with no checker still runs the derived kinds; `Fetch` checks each domain once through the checker's `CheckAll` (`D15-website-check`).

## site_text

`site_text` records that the owner's prompt was run for a company (site text decision 5). Its inputs are the website URL the subject carries, exactly as the facts row holds it, so a company is due when it has no result yet or a new website check landed on another URL, and a company with no website is never due. Its source word is `prompts`. Its prompt is named after the kind, `_` made `-`, by `PromptName`: `site-text` (site text decision 7); the prompt itself, its model, instructions and tools, are the prompts service's, and candidates knows none of them. A run's input object holds the candidate's id, name, city, state and website URL, each always present (site text decision 8). `Ready` is whether it holds a prompts client, so a registry built without one still holds the kind, and an `enrich` of it is refused `unavailable` (site text decision 21; `D06-passes`, `D10-tools`).

Its found value records the run: the prompts run id, the status it ended with and its exit code when it has one, the number of distinct page URLs the company held when the run ended, and the cost in nano-dollars as prompts reports it; the result's cost is the same in dollars. `Found` copies these from the run's outcome (appkit's `prompts.Outcome`, D27 of appkit) and the count the pass gives it. The pages themselves are read off the company, never off the result. A run that ended `failed`, `killed` or `timed_out`, or exited with any code but 0, is still a result (site text decision 9), and `Failed` is the one rule for which those are: the pass counts its failed runs by it and the candidate page marks a failed result by it. Both reach it through the `Prompter`'s own `Failed`, which decodes a recorded found value of its kind and answers that value's `Failed`, so neither switches on the kind (recorded decision below).

## brief

`brief` turns the pages `site_text` kept on a company into a short summary for the owner, with the company's categories from the fixed list and labels of its own words (brief decisions 1 to 6). Its inputs are a fingerprint of the pages: one line per distinct URL, holding the URL and the number of its newest copy, the lines sorted, hashed as the link fingerprint is. So a company is due when it has no brief yet or `site_text` has since kept a new page or a newer copy of one, and a company with no page, one with no website among them, is never due. Nothing but the pages is an input: a changed fact, name, link or website alone makes no one due, and `force` is how a new prompt is applied (brief decision 2). Its source word is `prompts`, and its prompt is `brief`, by `PromptName`. It shares the one prompts client `site_text` runs with (`Config.Prompts`, brief decision 14), so a registry built without one holds both kinds and neither is `Ready`.

A run's input object holds the candidate's id, name, city, state and website URL, as the site text input does; its facts, from the subject's: the categories with the source of each, leaving out every category whose source is `brief`, so an earlier brief is never fed back to the model, the government match, the fleet verdict, the power units and drivers and the known-since and newest-filing dates, each left out when empty; the carrier class of the first FMCSA link not gone, the link the fleet verdict, power units and drivers come from, as that reading's class word, left out when there is no such link (category evidence decision 9); and the distinct people named on the links not gone; and its pages in subject order, each with its URL, title and text. Each page's text is cut at `BriefPageBytes` (12,288 bytes), the cut moved back so it never splits a UTF-8 sequence, and pages are included in order while their cut texts together stay within `BriefInputBytes` (98,304 bytes); the first page that does not fit and every later one are left out, so the largest input is bounded whatever the site (brief decision 4): twelve pages of 12,000 bytes give eight.

The run answers on its standard output the object the owner's prompt's schema describes: a summary, categories each with a name and a reason, and labels (brief decision 5). `Found` decodes it whatever status the run ended with. A category whose name is one of the 23 category words `sources.Categories()` lists is kept as a category; one whose name is not is moved to the labels, after the run's own, so nothing the model said is lost and nothing it invented reaches a filter. Standard output that does not decode gives an empty summary and no category or label. The found value records the run as `site_text`'s does, its id, status, exit code and cost in nano-dollars, but with no page count, and beside them the summary, the categories and the labels; the result's cost is the same in dollars. A brief is failed when the run did not exit with code 0, or exited 0 with an empty summary, which output that did not decode gives (brief decision 6). A failed run is still a result, valid for the kind, with the cost spent, so a company whose pages break the model is not paid for on every pass. The facts row takes its `brief` text and its brief categories from the newest brief that did not fail, each category with the source word `brief`, the kind word itself (`D04-store`).

## What a result holds

A result is the source word, the found value, JSON shaped by its kind, and the cost; the store adds the candidate, the kind, the inputs and the time (enrichers decision 4). `AddEnrichments` asks the registry whether a found value is valid for its kind instead of checking that the kind is `website_check` (`D04-store`). Every found value a `Derive` returns is valid for its kind, for every subject: `Derive` normalizes what it reads rather than copying a value its kind's `Valid` refuses. A negative minutes or count is absent; a time whose UTC year falls outside 0 to 9999, which `time.Parse` with `2006-01-02` cannot read back, is absent like a zero one; a category from a link whose scraper is empty is left out; and a fleet verdict other than `pass`, `fail` and `unknown` is `unknown`.

Release-43 decodes every row of a candidate's enrichments into its own `store.Enrichment`, whose fields are `Candidate`, `Kind`, `State`, `URL`, `Title`, `StatusCode`, `Size`, `Error`, `Source`, `Cost` and `At`, matching member names without regard to case. So the website found value keeps the Go member names release-43 reads, and the derived found values and those of the two `Prompter` kinds use snake_case members none of which equals one of those names, so a rolled-back release-43 decodes such a row into an empty website result and never fails on it.

## Known limits

- **A tie reordered by last seen alone is not seen.** `store.Links` breaks filing-date ties by last seen, and the fingerprint leaves last seen out, so two FMCSA links filed the same day that swap places only because one was seen again do not make `link_facts` due, and the fleet verdict may stay the other link's until something else changes. A rebuild recomputes every derived kind and puts it right.

## Recorded decisions

- **The registry is fixed and built by `New` from a `Config`** (sheet 0.1), in the order `drive_minutes`, `link_facts`, `website_check`, `site_text`, `brief`. Unlike `sources`, nothing forces an injected list: no import cycle, and the only runtime parts are the checker and the prompts client, which the `Config` carries. `New` took the checker alone before; a struct now, so the signature does not change as kinds that bring a runtime part of their own are added. The site text document expected the brief to bring a client of its own; it does not: `site_text` and `brief` share the one prompts client, the registry holding `SiteText{Prompts: cfg.Prompts}` and `Brief{Prompts: cfg.Prompts}`, and `Config` is unchanged (brief decision 14). Website search and exact drive time may still each bring one.
- **Registry order is no longer kind name order.** `site_text` sorts before `website_check`, but site text decision 5 puts it after, and the fetched kinds come last because they are slow; `brief` sorts first of all, but brief decision 1 puts it after `site_text`, whose pages it reads. Nothing depended on the two orders being equal: the facts row's written times are sorted by kind themselves (`D04-store`).
- **A fetched kind that is not a `Fetcher`.** The enrichers document made every fetched kind a `Fetcher`, and the site text document asked for one whose `Fetch` runs prompts. `Fetch` is handed inputs strings and answers one result per distinct inputs; a prompts run needs the candidate's id, name, city and state as well, and runs once per candidate, not once per website. So `site_text` is a `Prompter`: it names the prompt, builds the input and records the outcome, and the pass makes the calls to prompts through the client the `Config` holds, the same one the registry's `SiteText` and `Brief` hold. `brief` is one for the same reasons. A `Fetch` that took subjects instead would have changed the website check, which the document leaves unchanged (site text decision 22).
- **`Found` copies the outcome rather than normalizing it.** Unlike `Derive`, `Found` reads prompts' answer, not the store: a run id or status that is empty, or a negative cost, is prompts misbehaving, and its result is not valid for the kind, so the store refuses to record it rather than record a guess. What the pass does then is `D06-passes`'.
- **`Failed` is the found value's own rule**, not the pass's or the page's, so the count of failed runs and the mark on the candidate page cannot disagree. A status prompts may add later counts as failed unless it is an exit with code 0, and for a brief an exit with code 0 that gave a summary.
- **`site_text`'s and `brief`'s found members are snake_case and avoid release-43's field names**, like the derived kinds', and each refuses unknown members, since nothing old writes them.
- **`Prompter` gains `Failed`** (departure: P4 leaves the interface unchanged unless design finds a cleaner seam, and records it). The pass counting `runs_failed` and the candidate page marking a failed result each need every prompts kind's failed rule over a stored found value. Without the method each would switch on the kind and decode into that kind's found type, two switches to keep in step with every kind added; with it, each asks the `Prompter` the registry holds. `Failed` of a value that does not decode into the kind's found type is false; the store holds only values valid for their kind (`AddEnrichments`), so it never meets one. `Found` keeps its signature, page count included, though `brief` ignores the count.
- **A brief category's source is the kind word**, `enrichers.KindBrief`, with no source constant of its own (P4): as `fmcsa` and `ppp` name the scraper that gave a category and `name` the government match, `brief` names the kind whose result gave it, and the input's filter, the facts projection and the page compare against one constant.
- **The facts ride on the subject.** Brief decision 4 reads the input's facts from the facts row, but the enrichers never read the store; so the store carries them on the subject, as it already carries the facts row's website URL, in the `LinkFound` shape `link_facts` writes, dates as strings. Its categories are the facts' `Records`, what the newest `link_facts` result found (`D04-store`), not the row's `Categories`: once a good brief decides, the row holds only `brief` categories, and the input, which leaves them out, would carry none, where the previous release's input carried the records' categories; reading the records keeps the input's categories what the records say (category evidence decision 10). Nil means no facts row. Reusing `LinkFound` adds no type; the input still leaves out any `brief` category and every empty member.
- **`BriefFound` has no `pages` member** (departure from brief decision 6's "records the run as `site_text`'s does"): `site_text`'s `pages` is the count of page URLs the company held when its run ended; a brief keeps no page, and the decision lists the brief's members without one. So `Found` ignores the count it is handed and `Valid` refuses a `pages` member.
- **The brief's class comes from the links, not the facts row** (departure from category evidence decision 9's "as the facts row would carry it"): the facts row carries no class, and `LinkFound`, the shape the subject's facts ride in, does not change (category evidence decision 10). So `class` is read, like `people`, from the subject's links: the `Reading.Class` of the first FMCSA link not gone, the link whose verdict, power units and drivers `link_facts` copies (R-V4E6-1Y8G). While the facts row is stale the class may describe another FMCSA link than its verdict, power units and drivers, accepted because a derived walk brings the row current and they are then one reading. `Subject` gains no field, and the inputs stay the pages alone, so no brief becomes due by it.
- **The page cut never splits a UTF-8 sequence.** Brief decision 4 cuts a page's text at `BriefPageBytes` bytes; a cut inside a multi-byte character would hand `json.Marshal` invalid UTF-8, which it turns into a replacement character. Moving the cut back to the start of that character keeps the bound and the text valid, at the price of at most three bytes.
- **`Found` decodes the standard output whatever the status.** A run that was killed may have written nothing and one that exited 1 may have written something; decoding always keeps `Found` one rule, and `Failed`, not the decode, says whether the result counts. A failed result's categories never reach the facts row (`D04-store`), so recording them costs nothing and shows on the result what the run said.
- **`BriefFacts` leaves out empty members, and its people come from the links** (departure from brief decision 4's "an object read from the company's facts row (empty when there is none)"): the facts row holds no people, so `people` is read from the links not gone, which the subject carries whether or not there is a facts row; `facts` is then `{}` only when there is no facts row and no person, and the model is never handed a member that says nothing.
- **Derived work runs inside the store** (sheet 0.3): the store calls `Derive` within its own writes, so a merge or split re-runs the derived kinds atomically with the ruling and the judge that follows. `internal/store` imports `enrichers`; `enrichers` imports `sources`, `fmcsa` and `website` and never `store` or `passes`.
- **The website found value is `website.Result`'s JSON** (sheet 0.6), with Go's member names, because release-43 decodes it by those names. Rows release-43 wrote hold more members (`Candidate`, `Kind`, `Source`, `Cost`, `At`) and are still valid; a reader decodes them into `website.Result` and ignores the rest, so the website kind does not refuse unknown members.
- **Derived found members are snake_case and avoid release-43's field names** (sheet 0.7), so release-43 never fails on a derived row. The derived kinds refuse unknown members, since nothing old writes them.
- **`drive_minutes` writes `{}` for a ZIP the table lacks** (sheet 0.8), rather than writing nothing: otherwise a ZIP moving to an unknown one would keep stale minutes, and the candidate would be due on every pass forever.
- **The fingerprint hashes every link, gone ones included** (sheet 0.9; settled decision 2: each link's id, state and reading values, sorted), so a link going gone changes it; the inputs are empty only with no link at all.
- **A candidate whose inputs become empty keeps its last result's facts** (sheet 0.10), departing from enrichers decision 3's "the facts projection shows unknown": the facts row is the projection of the newest result of each kind and must be rebuildable from the enrichments alone (enrichers decision 5), and a candidate that has none newer keeps its newest. This is `D13-scoring`'s precedent for the website check: a candidate that stops being checked keeps its last result as the newest.
- **Derive is total; it normalizes rather than refuses.** A `Subject` is a plain value any caller can build, so `Derive` cannot assume what the store's readings guarantee (a scraper on every link, a verdict from `fmcsa.Read`, a date a scrape could hold). Rather than narrow the contract to an input domain, each value its kind's `Valid` would refuse is read as absent or unknown, the way an empty verdict already was: every found value it returns is then valid for any subject, and the store never fails on a derived write. For the store's own subjects nothing changes.
- **The rules moved, not changed** (enrichers decision 6): `link_facts` gives, field for field, what the judge wrote before for the same links, but for the category the government match adds (categories decision 3), and the rules version stays 5.
- **The government match's category comes from the one match `link_facts` keeps**, the first in link order, not from every link's match: the fact and the category then never disagree, and two links matching `COUNTY` and `CITY OF` give `government` from `name` once. The fact stays, and so does the `government` cohort filter. The fingerprint is unchanged: the match is already on each link's reading, which it hashes.

## Consumer tasks

The store builds a candidate's subject as `s := enrichers.Subject{ID: c.ID, Name: c.Name, City: c.City, State: c.State, Email: c.Email, Zip: c.Zip, Website: f.WebsiteURL, Minutes: m, Links: ls, Pages: ps, Facts: lf}`, `f` from `GetFacts` (empty on `store.ErrNotFound`), `m` from `ZipMinutes`, `ls` from `Links`, each `enrichers.Link{Scraper: l.Scraper, SourceID: l.SourceID, Gone: l.Gone, Reading: l.Reading}`, `ps` the newest copy of each URL among the candidate's pages, text included, in the order the URLs were first kept, each `enrichers.Page{Number: p.Number, URL: p.URL, Title: p.Title, Text: p.Text, Size: p.Size}`, and `lf` nil on `store.ErrNotFound` and otherwise `&enrichers.LinkFound{KnownSince: ks, NewestFiling: nf, GovernmentMatch: f.GovernmentMatch, Categories: cs, FleetVerdict: f.FleetVerdict, PowerUnits: f.PowerUnits, Drivers: f.Drivers}`, `ks` and `nf` the `2006-01-02` dates of `f.KnownSince` and `f.NewestFiling`, empty when zero, and `cs` holding `enrichers.Category{Name: x.Name, Source: x.Source}` for each `x` of `f.Categories`; it keeps `reg := enrichers.New(enrichers.Config{})` and validates with `reg.Valid(e.Kind, e.Found)`; for a derived kind `k` it takes `en, ok := reg.Get(k)` and `d, ok := en.(enrichers.Deriver)`, `in := d.Inputs(s)`, skipping an empty `in`, and `r := d.Derive(s)` gives the `Source`, `Found` and `Cost` it records beside `in`. For the facts row it takes the newest result `e` of kind `enrichers.KindBrief` for which `enrichers.Brief{}.Failed(e.Found)` is false, decodes `var bf enrichers.BriefFound; json.Unmarshal(e.Found, &bf)`, writes `bf.Summary` as `brief` and adds `sources.Category{Name: x.Name, Source: enrichers.KindBrief}` for each `x` of `bf.Categories` (`D04-store`).

The engine builds `reg := enrichers.New(enrichers.Config{Website: cfg.Website, Prompts: cfg.Prompts})` and walks `reg.All()`: a value whose `Fetched()` is false is run through `st.Derive`; one that is a `enrichers.Fetcher` is skipped when `Ready()` is false and otherwise handed each batch's unchecked inputs through `Fetch(ctx, inputs, func(in string, r enrichers.Result) { … })`; one that is a `enrichers.Prompter` is skipped by an untargeted pass. A targeted pass over `site_text` or `brief` takes `p, _ := en.(enrichers.Prompter)`, refuses it when `!p.Ready()`, calls `cfg.Prompts.Show(ctx, p.Prompt())`, and for each due subject `s` calls `cfg.Prompts.Run(ctx, p.Prompt(), p.Input(s))`, polls `Result` until the status is neither `enrichers.RunQueued` nor `enrichers.RunRunning`, and records `r := p.Found(out, n)` with `n` the distinct URLs the candidate holds, inputs `p.Inputs(s)`; it counts a failed run when `p.Failed(r.Found)` is true, whatever the kind. The `enrich` tool refuses a `kind` not in `enrichers.Kinds()`.
