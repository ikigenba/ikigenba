# D16-enrichers

The enrichers: the kinds of enrichment candidates keeps, each a kind word with a rule naming a candidate's inputs and a rule computing a result from them, held in one fixed registry the enrich pass walks without knowing any kind in particular (enrichers decisions 1 and 2). The site text decisions document adds a fourth kind, `site_text`, the first whose result is a run of a prompt kept in the prompts service (its decisions 5 to 9), and the brief decisions document a fifth, `brief`, a run of a prompt that reads the pages `site_text` kept on a company and writes a summary of it with its categories and labels (its decisions 1 to 6), and the website search decisions document a sixth, `website_search`, a run of a prompt that finds the website of a company no site has been found for, whose answer the service confirms with one fetch before recording it (its decisions 1 to 6), which the empty sites decisions document also makes due for a company whose website site text could keep no pages from, telling the run the site on record and recording which case its answer is (empty sites outcomes 1 to 7), and the exact drive time decisions document a seventh, `drive_route`, the drive Google's routing service gives from the service's origin to a company's street address, in minutes and miles (its outcomes 1 to 5). The revenue bands decisions document adds no kind: it adds to `link_facts` the **headcounts**, each a count a link's reading gives of the people a business employs, which the judge turns into a revenue estimate (its decisions 1 to 5). The OSHA ITA decisions document adds no kind either: each headcount `link_facts` copies carries the hours worked beside its count when the reading gives them, which only an OSHA ITA reading does, and the copies stay one per link, the sum of a business's sites being the judge's (OSHA ITA outcomes 11, 12 and 16). This document owns the package `internal/enrichers` whole: the kind words and source words, the **subject** an enricher reads of a candidate, the result and the found shapes, the `Enricher`, `Deriver`, `Fetcher`, `Prompter`, `Router` and `Failer` interfaces and the `Tally` a `Router` answers, the seven enrichers `drive_minutes`, `link_facts`, `website_check`, `website_search`, `site_text`, `brief` and `drive_route`, what each names as its inputs and, for the two derived kinds, what each computes, for `website_search`, `site_text` and `brief` the prompt each names, the input object of a run, the result recording one and which recorded results are of a run that failed, for `drive_route` the destination it asks the drive to, the result recording the drive and which recorded results are of a request that failed, which found values each accepts, and the registry with what it is built from. The records, the due read, the derived walk, the facts row and the reads of results are `D04-store`'s (`store.Enrichment`, `DueEnrichments`, `Derive`, `AddEnrichments`, `GetFacts`, `FillFacts`), and so is how a brief result becomes the facts row's `brief` text, categories and `brief_labels`, and how the newest `link_facts` result becomes its `headcounts`; the headcount a reading carries and its kind words are `D05-sources`'; the enrich pass, the full ingest, their counts and follow-ons are `D06-passes`'; what the website check fetches, how, and what its `Ready` and `Fetch` do are `D15-website-check`'s, and so is how `Checker.Get`, which confirms a website search's answer, fetches one URL; which result decides the facts row's website, the check's or a search's, is `D04-store`'s **website decision**; the targeted enrich pass that runs a `Prompter` kind, asking the prompts service for the prompt, starting runs, waiting on them, counting the runs that failed and, for `site_text`, the pages they add, is `D06-passes`'; the pages themselves, and how the store reads them only for the kinds that read them, are `D04-store`'s; what the routes client asks Google, how, and the outcome it answers are `D17-drive-route`'s; which result decides the facts row's drive minutes, the route's or the ZIP table's, is `D04-store`'s **drive decision**, and the origin the store carries on the subject is `D04-store`'s and its setting `D01-layout-and-run-seam`'s; the targeted pass that runs a `Router` kind, its counts and its cost, is `D06-passes`'; the list of the category words is `D01-layout-and-run-seam`'s (`sources.Categories()`); merge and split re-running the derived kinds are `D12-ingest`'s; what a judge makes of the facts, the headcounts and the brief labels among them, is `D13-judging`'s; the `enrich` tool is `D10-tools`'.

"Enrichers decision N" is entry N of the decisions document of the enrichers change, and "categories decision N" entry N of the decisions document of the categories change. "Site text decision N" is entry N of the decisions document of the site-text change, "brief decision N" entry N of the decisions document of the brief change, "P N" entry N of the decisions log of the brief change's delivery, "category evidence decision N" entry N of the decisions document of the category evidence change, "website search decision N" entry N of the decisions document of the website search change, and "S N" entry DN of the decisions log of the website search change's delivery. "Drive route decision N" is outcome N of the decisions document of the exact drive time change. "Revenue bands decision N" is entry N of the decisions document of the revenue bands change. "Website states outcome N" is outcome N of the decisions document of the website states change. "OSHA ITA outcome N" is outcome N of the decisions document of the OSHA ITA change, and "OSHA ITA P N" entry P N of the decisions log of its delivery. "Empty sites outcome N" is outcome N of the decisions document of the change that runs the website search after an empty site, and "empty sites decision N" entry N of the decisions its delivery settled.

## Fetched and derived

An enricher is one kind. A kind is **fetched** when it reaches outside the service and may cost money, and **derived** when it reads only what the service holds (enrichers decision 1). The distinction is the enricher's `Fetched`. A derived kind is a `Deriver`, whose `Derive` computes a result from the subject alone, so the store can run it inside its own writes. A fetched kind is one of three things. A `Fetcher`, the website check, computes many inputs at a pace of its own through `Fetch`, one result per distinct inputs. A `Prompter`, `website_search`, `site_text` or `brief`, is one run of a prompt per candidate: it names the prompt, builds the run's input object from the subject, turns the run's outcome into a result, with the subject the input was built from, since `website_search` records what that input told the run, and under the pass's context, since `website_search` fetches the URL its run names, and says whether a recorded result of its kind is of a run that failed, and the pass does the running (`D06-passes`). A `Router`, `drive_route`, is one drive asked of the routing service per candidate: `Route` hands the routes client the subject's origin and destination under the pass's context and answers the result with a `Tally` of the requests it took, which the pass adds into its counts and cost, and `Failed` says whether a recorded result of its kind is of a request that failed (`D06-passes`). All three have a `Ready` saying whether they have anything to fetch with. A `Prompter` and a `Router` each have `Failed`, and `Failer` names that one method, so the pass and the candidate page ask any registered kind whether a result failed by asserting `Failer`, never switching on the kind. A full ingest treats every derived kind as due for every candidate and never a fetched one (`D06-passes`); a merge or split re-runs the derived kinds for what it touched (`D12-ingest`). Every enricher is exactly one of the four, `Deriver`, `Fetcher`, `Prompter` and `Router`.

Seven kinds are registered in this release, in this order, which is the order the enrich pass walks them: `drive_minutes` and `link_facts`, derived, then `website_check`, `website_search`, `site_text`, `brief` and `drive_route`, fetched (enrichers decision 8: derived kinds first, since they finish in seconds and the fetched kinds can take hours; site text decision 5: `site_text` after `website_check`; brief decision 1: `brief` after `site_text`, whose pages it reads; `website_search` after `website_check`, whose sites stop it, and before `site_text`, which a site it finds makes due, recorded decision below; `drive_route` last, since it reads no other kind's result and none reads its). It is no longer kind name order, since `brief`, `drive_route` and `site_text` sort before `website_check` and `website_search` after it (recorded decision below). `enrichers.Kinds()` lists them. An enrich with no target never walks a `Prompter` or a `Router` kind, and `website_search`, `site_text`, `brief` and `drive_route` run only over a prospect list or one candidate (site text decision 6; brief decision 10; website search decision 1; drive route decision 1; `D06-passes`).

## Inputs and due

Every kind names a candidate's **inputs** as one string: the display ZIP for `drive_minutes`, the link fingerprint for `link_facts`, the checked domain (`website.Domain` of the display email, `D15-website-check`) for `website_check`, a fingerprint of the name, city and state for `website_search`, empty when the facts row holds a website the search did not find, and joined to the website and the site text result when site text kept no pages from it, the website URL the facts row holds for `site_text`, a fingerprint of the pages kept on the candidate for `brief`, and a fingerprint of the street, city, state and ZIP and the origin for `drive_route`, empty when there is no street or no origin. Every result records the inputs it was computed from (`store.Enrichment.Inputs`, `D04-store`). A candidate is due for a kind when it has no result of that kind, or its newest result's inputs differ from the inputs named now; there are no refresh ages (enrichers decision 3). Empty inputs mean not due and no result: no ZIP, no own domain, no link at all, a website the search did not find that site text did not show empty, no website, no page, no street.

The inputs are computed from the candidate's **subject**, the one value an enricher reads: the candidate's id, display name, city, state, street, ZIP, phone and email, the website URL its facts row holds and which source decided it, the check or the search (the website decision, `D04-store`), the result of the website check of its own email domain, nil when it has none or no check of it is recorded, the ZIP table's minutes for that ZIP, and every link with its scraper, source id, whether it is gone and the source's reading of it, in the order `store.Links` answers (`D12-ingest`: not gone first, then newest filing date, then newest last seen); the newest copy of each page URL kept on the candidate, with its number, URL, title, text and size, in the order the URLs were first kept (brief decision 3); and the link facts its facts row holds, its categories those of the newest `link_facts` result (the facts' `Records`, `D04-store`), nil when it has no facts row (brief decision 4); the found value of the newest `site_text` result against the website URL its facts row holds, nil when there is none, and the inputs of its newest `website_search` result, empty when there is none (empty sites decision 2); and the **origin**, the address string drives are measured from, the same on every subject a store builds, since the store is configured with it (`D04-store`; drive route decision 2). The subject is built by the store, which holds all of it; the enrichers never read the store, so they are pure functions a test drives with subjects it builds. The subject is a read bounded by one candidate: its links, pages and results are each one query on that candidate's key, folded as read, the largest candidate on the `us` space holding 3,224 links (`D04-store`, The units and their bounds; `shape/`'s Bounded work). The subject is defined with its pages whatever the kind it is built for; only a `Prompter` reads them, and only a targeted pass runs one, so the store reads a candidate's pages only when the kind it builds the subject for is a `Prompter`, and an untargeted walk never reads them. The site text result and the search inputs are read on the same terms, since only `website_search`, a `Prompter`, reads them (`D04-store`). How it does so is invisible here and is `D04-store`'s recorded decision.

## drive_minutes

The inputs are the display ZIP, and the result is the minutes the ZIP table holds for it (enrichers decision 2), as `{"minutes":84}`, with source word `zip_drive` and cost 0. When the table lacks the ZIP, or holds a negative value for it, the minutes are unknown and the result is still written, as `{}`: a candidate whose ZIP moves from a known one to an unknown one must lose its stale minutes, and a result keeps it from being due on every pass forever (recorded decision below).

## link_facts

The result is the facts a judge derived from links before this release (enrichers decision 2), computed now once, when the links change, over the links not gone: **known since**, the earliest date any shows the business existing; the **newest filing**; the **government match**, the first pattern a link's name matched, in link order; the **categories**, each a link's category with the scraper that gave it, and beside them one from the government match with the source word `name`: `education` when the match is `SCHOOL DISTRICT` or `UNIVERSITY`, `government` for every other pattern (categories decision 3); and the **fleet verdict** with the **power units** and **drivers**, from the first FMCSA link, which in link order is the freshest; and the **headcounts**, one from each link that carries one (revenue bands decision 4, below). A date whose UTC year is outside 0 to 9999, a category from a link with no scraper and a verdict outside the three are absent or unknown, never copied (recorded decision below). The rules are `D13-judging`'s of before, moved here unchanged but for the category from the government match, and so are their examples but for two, whose categories now hold that category; the categories are sorted by name, then source, and kept once each, which puts `government` from `name` before `trucking and logistics` from `fmcsa`, and keeps `government` from `name` beside `government` from `ppp` since the source differs; the result's source word is `links` and the cost 0. Link facts are one kind, not six, because they share one input and are always recomputed together. A places link's category is a weak hint: it counts only for a candidate whose every link not gone is a places link (R-F7H0-LOM5, `D19-walk`).

`link_facts` copies each link's category as its source read it and is unchanged by the category evidence change (category evidence decision 10); what changes is the FMCSA reading, which gives `trucking and logistics` only to a carrier whose class is `for_hire` or `mixed` (category evidence decision 7, `D05-sources`). So a private carrier's FMCSA link adds no category here: one whose name matches `COUNTY` gives `government` from `name` alone, one beside a PPP link with code `236220` gives `construction` from `ppp` alone, and one with neither gives none and is unknown (category evidence decision 8), while a for-hire or mixed carrier still gives `trucking and logistics` from `fmcsa`.

## The fleet verdict

The fleet verdict is `pass`, `fail` or `unknown` (`sources.FleetPass`, `sources.FleetFail`, `sources.FleetUnknown`). Each FMCSA row already says which, on its reading (`D05-sources`). `link_facts` copies the verdict of the candidate's first FMCSA link not gone, `unknown` when it is not one of the three, and that link's power units and drivers beside it; with no such link the verdict is unknown and both counts are absent. Only FMCSA counts trucks, so a candidate known only from PPP is unknown, never fail, whatever its readings say; a link stored by an earlier release decodes with an empty verdict and gives `unknown`, never a guess from its old fleet boolean. A negative count on that link's reading is absent, as if the reading lacked it. Power units and drivers come from the same link as the verdict, so they never disagree.

## Headcounts

A reading may carry one headcount, a count a source's row gives of the people a business employs, with its kind word and the date it is as of (`sources.Headcount`, `D05-sources`): PPP's jobs reported, FMCSA's drivers, Form 5500's participants (kind `participants`, scraper `form5500`), and an OSHA ITA 300A summary's annual average employees (kind `employees`, scraper `osha_ita`), as of the last day of its filing year, with the hours worked beside the count (OSHA ITA outcome 11). `link_facts` copies one for each link not gone whose reading carries one, in link order, as an `enrichers.Headcount`: the link's scraper, the kind and the count as the reading holds them, the date it is as of, written as the other dates are and empty when there is none (revenue bands decision 4), and the hours as the reading holds them, nil when it holds none, as it does for every source but OSHA ITA and for an OSHA ITA row that gives no hours (OSHA ITA outcome 16). It copies every one, not only the first FMCSA link's as it does the counts beside the verdict, since the judge ranks them by kind and compares them (revenue bands decision 6, `D13-judging`); it neither ranks, filters nor checks them, so a kind the judge does not know or a count below 1 is copied as given and the judge ignores it. The facts row projects the list as its `headcounts` (`D04-store`), and the judge reads it there.

`link_facts` does not sum (OSHA ITA P6.3). A business with several sites files one OSHA ITA row per site and gets one link per row, so it gets one `employees` entry per link, each with its own count and hours: Design Ready Controls' 2025 sites under its main EIN are an entry each, not one of 523, and Rosenbauer Minnesota's 2024 summary, entered twice, is two entries of 357 over 715864 hours, both listed, one per link. The facts row and the card then show every site, and the judge sums a year's sites and counts an entry made twice once (OSHA ITA outcome 12, `D13-judging`).

The facts row's `brief_labels`, the labels of the newest brief result that did not fail, are the store's projection of the brief's results (revenue bands decision 5, `D04-store`): `BriefFound.Labels` already holds them, and no enricher changes for them. The brief's inputs and its input object are unchanged; the headcounts do not reach the brief's facts.

## The link fingerprint

The inputs of `link_facts` are a fingerprint of every link of the candidate, gone ones included: the SHA-256, in lowercase hexadecimal, of one line per link holding its source key, whether it is gone and its reading as JSON, the lines sorted. Sorting makes the order of the links irrelevant; including the gone flag makes a link going gone change the inputs, so the facts drop it. The inputs are empty only when the candidate has no link at all. A reading's headcount is part of its JSON, so a changed count, kind, date or hours changes the inputs and makes the kind due; the fingerprint itself is unchanged (revenue bands decision 4).

## website_check

The website check is `D15-website-check`'s; here it is one enricher among seven. Its inputs are the checked domain, its source word is that domain too, and its found value is the JSON of a `website.Result`. `Ready` is whether it holds a checker, so an enrich pass with no checker still runs the derived kinds; `Fetch` checks each domain once through the checker's `CheckAll` (`D15-website-check`).

## site_text

`site_text` records that the owner's prompt was run for a company (site text decision 5). Its inputs are the website URL the subject carries, exactly as the facts row holds it, so a company is due when it has no result yet or its website changed, a new website check having landed on another URL or a website search having found one (the website decision, `D04-store`), and a company with no website is never due. Its source word is `prompts`. Its prompt is named after the kind, `_` made `-`, by `PromptName`: `site-text` (site text decision 7); the prompt itself, its model, instructions and tools, are the prompts service's, and candidates knows none of them. A run's input object holds the candidate's id, name, city, state and website URL, each always present (site text decision 8). `Ready` is whether it holds a prompts client, so a registry built without one still holds the kind, and an `enrich` of it is refused `unavailable` (site text decision 21; `D06-passes`, `D10-tools`).

Its found value records the run: the prompts run id, the status it ended with and its exit code when it has one, the number of distinct page URLs the company held when the run ended, and the cost in nano-dollars as prompts reports it; the result's cost is the same in dollars. `Found` copies these from the run's outcome (appkit's `prompts.Outcome`, D27 of appkit) and the count the pass gives it. The pages themselves are read off the company, never off the result. A run that ended `failed`, `killed` or `timed_out`, or exited with any code but 0, is still a result (site text decision 9), and `Failed` is the one rule for which those are: the pass counts its failed runs by it and the candidate page marks a failed result by it. Both reach it through the `Prompter`'s own `Failed`, which decodes a recorded found value of its kind and answers that value's `Failed`, so neither switches on the kind (recorded decision below).

## brief

`brief` turns the pages `site_text` kept on a company into a short summary for the owner, with the company's categories from the fixed list and labels of its own words (brief decisions 1 to 6). Its inputs are a fingerprint of the pages: one line per distinct URL, holding the URL and the number of its newest copy, the lines sorted, hashed as the link fingerprint is. So a company is due when it has no brief yet or `site_text` has since kept a new page or a newer copy of one, and a company with no page, one with no website among them, is never due. Nothing but the pages is an input: a changed fact, name, link or website alone makes no one due, and `force` is how a new prompt is applied (brief decision 2). Its source word is `prompts`, and its prompt is `brief`, by `PromptName`. It shares the one prompts client `site_text` runs with (`Config.Prompts`, brief decision 14), so a registry built without one holds both kinds and neither is `Ready`.

A run's input object holds the candidate's id, name, city, state and website URL, as the site text input does; its facts, from the subject's: the categories with the source of each, leaving out every category whose source is `brief`, so an earlier brief is never fed back to the model, the government match, the fleet verdict, the power units and drivers and the known-since and newest-filing dates, each left out when empty; the carrier class of the first FMCSA link not gone, the link the fleet verdict, power units and drivers come from, as that reading's class word, left out when there is no such link (category evidence decision 9); and the distinct people named on the links not gone; and its pages in subject order, each with its URL, title and text. Each page's text is cut at `BriefPageBytes` (12,288 bytes), the cut moved back so it never splits a UTF-8 sequence, and pages are included in order while their cut texts together stay within `BriefInputBytes` (98,304 bytes); the first page that does not fit and every later one are left out, so the largest input is bounded whatever the site (brief decision 4): twelve pages of 12,000 bytes give eight.

The run answers on its standard output the object the owner's prompt's schema describes: a summary, categories each with a name and a reason, and labels (brief decision 5). `Found` decodes it whatever status the run ended with. A category whose name is one of the 23 category words `sources.Categories()` lists is kept as a category; one whose name is not is moved to the labels, after the run's own, so nothing the model said is lost and nothing it invented reaches a filter. Standard output that does not decode gives an empty summary and no category or label. The found value records the run as `site_text`'s does, its id, status, exit code and cost in nano-dollars, but with no page count, and beside them the summary, the categories and the labels; the result's cost is the same in dollars. A brief is failed when the run did not exit with code 0, or exited 0 with an empty summary, which output that did not decode gives (brief decision 6). A failed run is still a result, valid for the kind, with the cost spent, so a company whose pages break the model is not paid for on every pass. The facts row takes its `brief` text and its brief categories from the newest brief that did not fail, each category with the source word `brief`, the kind word itself (`D04-store`).

## website_search

`website_search` asks the owner's prompt to find the website of a company no site has been found for, or a different one for a company whose site gave site text nothing to read (empty sites outcome 1), confirms the site the agent names with one fetch, and records the run, the answer and the confirmation, so `site_text` and the brief can follow by their own rules (website search decisions 1 to 8). It is a `Prompter`, as `site_text` and `brief` are, holding the one prompts client they share and the website check's checker (S1): the registry holds `WebsiteSearch{Prompts: cfg.Prompts, Checker: cfg.Website}`, so `Config` is unchanged. `Ready` is whether it holds both, so a registry built without either holds the kind and an `enrich` of it is refused `unavailable` before any request to prompts (`D06-passes`). Its source word is `prompts`, and its prompt `website-search`, by `PromptName`; the prompt itself, its model, its tools and the bounds on its searches are the prompts service's (website search decision 4), and candidates knows none of them.

Its inputs turn on the **empty-site condition** (empty sites outcome 1; empty sites decision 2): the subject carries a website, which the facts row holds only in the states `https` and `http` (`D04-store`); the newest `site_text` result against that URL, which the subject carries as `SiteText` (`D04-store`), ended, in any way, with its `pages` 0; and the subject holds no page, since its pages are every page the store holds on the candidate, those the `add_page` tool added by hand included (R-UADJ-LEIU), so a company given a page by hand is not due. Which check or search found the website does not matter. When the condition holds, the inputs are the fingerprint of the name, city and state, a `/`, and a hash of the website URL and the identity of that site text result, its prompts run id (recorded decision below). So such a company is due once for each empty site text result against its current website: a search recorded with those inputs settles it, whatever it answered, failed included; a newer empty site text result on the same URL, or a change of name, city or state, makes it due again; and `force` redoes it. When the condition does not hold, the inputs are empty when the subject carries a website the search did not find: one whose source is the check, or empty, as a row an earlier release projected holds it (`D04-store`). They are the inputs of the subject's newest search, its `SearchInputs`, when the subject carries a website the search found and those inputs begin with the fingerprint of its name, city and state now and a `/`: a search under the condition whose different site then became the company's website leaves the company not due, with no change of name, city or state, though its URL changed and the condition no longer holds (recorded decision below). Otherwise they are the fingerprint of the name, city and state, hashed as the link fingerprint is. So for every company that does not meet the condition and whose newest search was not recorded under it, the inputs are byte for byte what they were before the empty sites change. A company the check found a site for is thus never searched, `force` included, unless site text showed that site empty; one whose search answered no site, named a URL that did not load, or failed is not due again until its name, city or state changes or the condition newly holds; one whose search found a confirmed site keeps the same inputs and is not due; and `force` redoes each of these (website search decision 2; departure from S2 below). A website of `none` carries no URL and no source, since only an unfound search decides it (`D04-store`), so it leaves the inputs a fingerprint, and the company the search found nothing for is not due again until its name, city or state changes or `force` redoes it (website states outcome 4); a check that found nothing decides nothing, so its company reads `unknown` and, while no search with the inputs named now is recorded, is due for the search (website states outcome 2). The source is compared with the word `search`, the value of `store.WebsiteBySearch`, since the enrichers never import the store.

A run's input object holds what tells this company from a namesake (website search decision 3): the candidate's id, name, city, state, street, ZIP and phone; the domain of its email when the domain is its own (`website.Domain`, `D15-website-check`), and what the website check found there, its state, `unknown` when no check of that domain is recorded, and the error it failed with, these three empty when there is no own domain; each of these always present; the people named on its links not gone, as the brief input reads them; and the categories its records give, each with its source, leaving out every `brief` one, as the brief input does; and the site on record (empty sites outcome 3; empty sites decision 1): the website URL its facts row holds, which of the check and the search found it, `check` for a row whose source is empty, since only a release before the search projected one, and the status and pages count of the newest `site_text` result against that URL, null when there is none; the three empty, and null, unless the subject meets the empty-site condition, so one prompt serves both cases: a company with a website on record that is searched again, by the earlier rule for a site the search found or by `force`, is searched as one with none, its input naming no site (recorded decision below). Nothing else: no page or other fact.

The run answers on its standard output the object the prompt's schema describes: a URL, empty when no site belongs to the company, one sentence on what matched, and the sites set aside, each a URL and a sentence (website search decision 4). `Found` decodes it whatever status the run ended with, as the brief's does, and takes it whole or not at all: an object holding `url` and `why` as strings and `rejected` as an array of objects each holding `url` and `why` as strings, other members ignored; anything else gives no answer. Then, when the answer names a URL and the run exited with code 0, it confirms it: one `Checker.Get` of that URL under the pass's context, the website check's rules for one fetch (`D15-website-check`), recording what a check records, the state as the scheme the fetch landed on, `none` when it failed, the URL landed on, the title, the status, the size and the error (website search decision 5). The page's text is not kept; `site_text` keeps the pages. The found value nests the run (id, status, exit code, cost in nano-dollars), the answer (the URL, the reason and the sites set aside) and the confirmation, absent when no fetch was made (website search decision 6); the result's cost is the run's, in dollars. Beside them it records, from the subject `Found` is handed, the one the input was built from, why the search ran, `no_website` when no website was on record, `empty_site` when the subject met the empty-site condition and `again` when a website was on record and the search ran anyway, by the earlier rule for a site the search found or by `force`; and the website on record, its source and the newest site text result against it, taken from the subject, not from the input, which names no site for `again`; and, when the search is not failed, the **case** its answer is: `none` for an empty URL, `stands` when the URL names the website on record, the two compared by host (recorded decision below), and `different` for any other URL, any URL at all when no website was on record (empty sites outcomes 4, 6 and 7; empty sites decision 5). These five are top-level members beside `run`, `answer` and `confirmation`, so a result recorded before them is read as it was, with no reason and no case. No test reaches the web to confirm a site: a test hands the kind a transport checker or a stub checker (`D15-website-check`).

A website search is failed when the run did not exit with code 0, or exited 0 with no answer. An empty URL is not a failure, nor is a URL that did not load: each is an answer the result stands on, with no site, and `force` redoes it. Which result decides the facts row's website, and how, is the website decision (`D04-store`); a failed search never decides it. In outline (empty sites decision 3): a search that is not failed and whose confirmation's state is `https` or `http` decides the site when the newest check found no site, and, when its case is `different`, also over a check that found the URL the search ran against, the one it records as its website, whatever their order, until a check finds another URL; `site_text` is then due for the company at the owner's next ask, its inputs being the new URL, and the pages it keeps make the brief due, each by its own rule, unchanged (website search decisions 7 and 8; empty sites outcomes 5 and 9). A search whose case is `stands` changes nothing, nor does one whose case is `none` for a company that had a site, nor one that named a URL that did not load, so neither site text nor the brief becomes due by it (empty sites outcomes 6 to 9). A search that is not failed and answered no site, or named a URL whose confirmation's state is `none`, decides `none`, with the agent's reason, when neither the newest check nor a confirmed search found a site (website states outcomes 1 and 3). A check whose state is `none` is still recorded and counted, but decides nothing: with no search it leaves the website `unknown` (website states outcome 2). The search stays manual: as every `Prompter` kind, it runs only in a targeted pass the owner starts over a prospect list or one candidate, never in an untargeted walk or after a site text pass on its own, and `force` redoes it (empty sites outcome 2; `D06-passes`).

## drive_route

`drive_route` asks Google's routing service for the drive from the origin to a company's street address and records the minutes and the miles, so a prospect list is filtered and sorted on a real route instead of the ZIP estimate (drive route decisions 1 to 5). It is a `Router`, holding the routes client the `Config` carries: the registry holds `DriveRoute{Routes: cfg.Routes}`. `Ready` is whether that client is there with an HTTP client to request through, whatever its key, so a registry built without one holds the kind and never routes; an `enrich` of the kind is refused `invalid` when the service was started with no Maps key, since then no client is built, and `unavailable` when the client has no HTTP client (`D06-passes`). Its source word is `google_routes`.

Its inputs are a fingerprint of the street, city, state and ZIP and the origin, a tab between each, hashed as the website search's name, city and state are. So a company is due when it has no route yet or its address or the origin has changed since its newest one, and a company with no street is never due, `force` included, and keeps the ZIP estimate (drive route decision 3); `Destination` is therefore only ever asked for an address that has a street. An empty origin empties the inputs too, though the setting's default never leaves it empty (`D01-layout-and-run-seam`).

The destination is the address as one string (drive route decision 4), which `Destination` writes: the street, then the city, the state and the ZIP, each only when it is not empty. `Route` hands the origin and that destination to the routes client's `Drive`, under the pass's context, and records what it answers (`D17-drive-route`): the minutes, rounded to whole minutes; the miles, to one decimal; the address the service resolved the destination to and how precisely it placed it, to the building, the street or the town, one of the `routes.Precision` words; the service's own status; why nothing was routable, when nothing was; the error a request failed with, when one did; and the cost in nano-dollars, the result's cost the same in dollars (drive route decision 5). Beside the result it answers the tally of the requests: how many were made, how many failed and what those the service answered cost, which the pass adds into its runs, failed runs and cost (`D06-passes`).

Nothing routable is not a failure: an address placed no closer than the town, or one the service could not place or route, is a result with no minutes and a reason, and the ZIP estimate keeps deciding the facts row's minutes (the drive decision, `D04-store`). A request that did not complete, or whose answer was not the shape expected, is failed: the result holds the error, is still recorded with the cost spent, and is marked failed on the candidate page as a failed brief is. No test reaches Google: a test builds a routes client over an HTTP client whose transport it holds, answering by URL and recording each request, as the website check is faked.

## What a result holds

A result is the source word, the found value, JSON shaped by its kind, and the cost; the store adds the candidate, the kind, the inputs and the time (enrichers decision 4). `AddEnrichments` asks the registry whether a found value is valid for its kind instead of checking that the kind is `website_check` (`D04-store`). Every found value a `Derive` returns is valid for its kind, for every subject: `Derive` normalizes what it reads rather than copying a value its kind's `Valid` refuses. A negative minutes or count is absent; a time whose UTC year falls outside 0 to 9999, which `time.Parse` with `2006-01-02` cannot read back, is absent like a zero one; a category from a link whose scraper is empty is left out; and a fleet verdict other than `pass`, `fail` and `unknown` is `unknown`.

Release-43 decodes every row of a candidate's enrichments into its own `store.Enrichment`, whose fields are `Candidate`, `Kind`, `State`, `URL`, `Title`, `StatusCode`, `Size`, `Error`, `Source`, `Cost` and `At`, matching member names without regard to case. So the website found value keeps the Go member names release-43 reads, and the derived found values and those of the three `Prompter` kinds and of `drive_route` use snake_case top-level members none of which equals one of those names, the website search's nesting its URL, title, size and error one level down and the drive route's naming its error `request_error`, so a rolled-back release-43 decodes such a row into an empty website result and never fails on it.

## Known limits

- **A confirmation is one fetch.** A site that is down for a moment when the run ends is recorded `none`, as any URL that did not load, so the company's website reads `none` unless the newest check, or a confirmed search, found a site, and the company is not searched again on its own (website states outcome 4); `force` redoes it (website search decision 6). How a wrong site a search found is replaced is the website decision's (`D04-store`).
- **A changed origin makes every routed company due at once.** The origin is in every `drive_route` input, so once it changes no route matches, the ZIP estimate decides every facts row again (the drive decision, `D04-store`), and each company is routed anew only at the owner's next ask over it.
- **A tie reordered by last seen alone is not seen.** `store.Links` breaks filing-date ties by last seen, and the fingerprint leaves last seen out, so two FMCSA links filed the same day that swap places only because one was seen again do not make `link_facts` due, and the fleet verdict may stay the other link's until something else changes. A full ingest recomputes every derived kind and puts it right.
- **A rollback makes some empty-site companies due for the search again** (empty sites decision 4; empty sites outcome 13). The release before reads the new found members without fault, since every read of `found` decodes without refusing unknown members and only the write side calls `Valid`, and it decides due from the `inputs` column alone. It computes the earlier inputs: empty for a site the check found, so such a company is not due there; but the name, city and state fingerprint for a site the search found, which differs from the inputs a search under the empty-site condition, or one keeping them since (`SearchInputs`), recorded. So after a rollback such a company reads due again, and is searched again at the owner's next ask over it, a paid re-run and no error; back on this release, the result the release before recorded has the plain fingerprint, and the company is due once more only if it meets the condition.
- **The case reads the answer, not the landing.** An answer naming another host that redirects onto the site on record is `different`, though its confirmation lands where the check did; the website decision then takes the same URL from the search, and the pass counts a different site that is not one. An answer naming the site on record's host on another path is `stands` and changes nothing, though the agent may have meant a page the check never reached.

## Recorded decisions

- **The registry is fixed and built by `New` from a `Config`** (sheet 0.1), in the order `drive_minutes`, `link_facts`, `website_check`, `website_search`, `site_text`, `brief`, `drive_route`. Unlike `sources`, nothing forces an injected list: no import cycle, and the only runtime parts are the checker, the prompts client and the routes client, which the `Config` carries. `New` took the checker alone before; a struct now, so the signature does not change as kinds that bring a runtime part of their own are added. The site text document expected the brief to bring a client of its own; it does not: `site_text` and `brief` share the one prompts client, the registry holding `SiteText{Prompts: cfg.Prompts}` and `Brief{Prompts: cfg.Prompts}`, and `Config` is unchanged (brief decision 14). Website search brings none either: it holds the prompts client and the checker the `Config` already carries (S1). Exact drive time brings one, the routes client, `Config.Routes`, nil when the service has no Maps key (`D01-layout-and-run-seam`), and `drive_route` comes last.
- **Registry order is no longer kind name order.** `site_text` sorts before `website_check`, but site text decision 5 puts it after, and the fetched kinds come last because they are slow; `brief` sorts first of all, but brief decision 1 puts it after `site_text`, whose pages it reads. Nothing depended on the two orders being equal: the facts row's written times are sorted by kind themselves (`D04-store`).
- **A fetched kind that is not a `Fetcher`.** The enrichers document made every fetched kind a `Fetcher`, and the site text document asked for one whose `Fetch` runs prompts. `Fetch` is handed inputs strings and answers one result per distinct inputs; a prompts run needs the candidate's id, name, city and state as well, and runs once per candidate, not once per website. So `site_text` is a `Prompter`: it names the prompt, builds the input and records the outcome, and the pass makes the calls to prompts through the client the `Config` holds, the same one the registry's `SiteText` and `Brief` hold. `brief` is one for the same reasons. A `Fetch` that took subjects instead would have changed the website check, which the document leaves unchanged (site text decision 22).
- **A fetched kind that is neither a `Fetcher` nor a `Prompter`: the `Router`.** `drive_route` cannot be a `Fetcher`: `Fetch` is handed inputs strings, and its inputs are a fingerprint the address and origin cannot be read back from. Nor a `Prompter`: it runs no prompt, has no input object and no prompts outcome. So it gets an interface of its own, as `site_text` got `Prompter`: one call per subject, `Route`, under the pass's context. `Route` answers a `Tally` beside the result because one result may take more than one request (below), and the pass counts requests and their cost, not results (`D06-passes`). The pass knows a targeted-only kind by its type, as it knows a `Prompter`: an untargeted walk skips a `Prompter`, and walks a `Router` and writes nothing for it.
- **`Failer` names the one method the pass and the page ask for.** With two kinds of fetched result that can fail, the pass's failed count and the candidate page's mark would each need two type assertions, `Prompter` and `Router`, kept in step with every interface added; `Failer` is satisfied by both without either embedding it, so each asks one interface. `WebsiteCheck` and the derived kinds are not `Failer`s: none of their results is of a failed run.
- **The origin rides on the subject, not on the `Config`.** The inputs must change when the origin changes, and `Inputs` reads only the subject; the store computes inputs for its due read over a registry it builds with an empty `Config` (`D04-store`), so an origin in the `Config` would never reach that read. The store is configured with the origin and puts it on every subject it builds (`D04-store`), as it carries the facts row's website; a test of the kind builds subjects with an origin of its own.
- **Two requests per company** (departure from drive route decision 4's "one request per candidate", decided by the delivery). The result must hold the address the service resolved and how precisely it placed it (drive route decision 5), so the routes client first asks Google's geocoding service to place the destination and then asks the route to the place it answered; the origin goes as the address string, with no request of its own (`D17-drive-route`). Both requests count in the tally, so the pass's runs and cost show what was spent. A destination placed no closer than the town gets no route request: a route to a town's centre is the ZIP estimate's precision at a price.
- **`drive_route` uses the real client wherever it runs.** There is no loopback stub for it as there is for the website check: under `IKIGENBA_SANDBOX` the routes client requests through `Process.HTTP` as on a host (`D01-layout-and-run-seam`), since the kind runs only when the owner names it and the finish line's one sandbox check is meant to reach Google (drive route decision 6).
- **`RouteFound`'s error is `request_error`.** `error` would equal release-43's `Error` field name (R-K4OS-L1LH), so a rolled-back release-43 would decode a failed route's error into its own record; every other member is snake_case and none equals a release-43 name. Like the other fetched kinds' found values it refuses unknown members, since nothing old writes them.
- **`Destination` writes only what the address holds.** A missing city, state or ZIP is left out with its separator rather than sent as an empty part; a subject with no street never reaches it, since its inputs are empty.
- **`Found` copies the outcome rather than normalizing it.** Unlike `Derive`, `Found` reads prompts' answer, not the store: a run id or status that is empty, or a negative cost, is prompts misbehaving, and its result is not valid for the kind, so the store refuses to record it rather than record a guess. What the pass does then is `D06-passes`'.
- **`website_search` sits between `website_check` and `site_text`.** Kind name order would put it last; the enrich pass walks fetched kinds in dependency order, as `brief` sits after `site_text`: a site the check finds stops the search, unless the newest site text result against it kept no pages and the company holds none (empty sites outcome 1), and a site the search finds makes `site_text` due. Since the empty sites change `website_search` also reads `site_text` results, which come after it, so the order is no longer a pure dependency order. It matters only within one enrich walk, where it decides which kind's result a later kind's due read sees; a `Prompter` kind runs only in a targeted pass of one kind, so the order guarantees nothing across passes, and a site text pass that keeps no pages makes a company due for the search at the owner's next search pass over it, as a search that finds a site makes it due for site text at the next site text pass.
- **`Prompter.Found` gains a context** (departure from S1's "no new seam": it changes the one seam's signature, adding none). The confirmation is a network fetch, `Checker.Get(ctx, url)`; a fetch made without the pass's context could not be cancelled when the pass stops. `Found(ctx, out, pages)` was then the signature on the interface and on `SiteText`, `Brief` and `WebsiteSearch`, which the empty sites change extends with the subject (below); site text and brief ignore the context, so their behavior is unchanged and their requirements are re-minted only because their text names the signature.
- **The due rule empties the inputs only for a site the search did not find** (departure from S2, which empties them whenever the facts row carries a URL from either source). A targeted pass selects only targets whose inputs are not empty, `force` included (R-08ON-9XFA, `D06-passes`), so under S2 a site the search found could never be redone, against website search decision 2's "`force` redoes any of them"; the document wins. The cost: a company whose search found a site is searched again if its name, city or state changes, which a literal reading of decision 2 would not do; harmless, since a later search that finds nothing leaves the found site standing (the website decision, `D04-store`).
- **Landing off the named host is confirmed.** Website search decision 5 asks only that redirects be followed and the page load with content, so there is no `OnSite` guard: the URL landed on is recorded and becomes the website, though it may be on another host than the one the agent named, as a check's landed URL may.
- **A failed run is never confirmed.** No fetch is spent on an answer the result does not stand on; its decoded answer is still recorded and shown, as a failed brief's summary is.
- **The answer is taken whole or not at all.** An object missing `url`, `why` or `rejected`, or holding one of the wrong type, gives no answer, and the result is failed (website search decision 6: "whose answer is not the object"); a partial answer would let a URL through with no reason the owner could read.
- **The website search's found value nests its parts** (S3): `run`, `answer` and `confirmation`, so no top-level member equals a release-43 field name (R-85VH-Q371), although the confirmation's members are a check's. Like the other `Prompter` kinds' it refuses unknown members, since nothing old writes them.
- **`Subject` grows the street, the phone, the website source and the check** (S5). The input needs the street, the phone and what the check found at the own domain, and the due rule needs the website's source; the enrichers never read the store, so the store carries them (`D04-store`). The check rides as the decoded `website.Result` the store already validates; `domain_check` is `unknown` when no check of the own domain is recorded, the state's own word for not checked.
- **`Subject` grows the site text result and the search inputs** (empty sites decision 2). The empty-site condition reads the newest `site_text` result against the current website, and the due rule after a different site took over reads the newest search's inputs; the enrichers never read the store, so the store carries both (`D04-store`), read on the same terms as the pages, only for a `Prompter` kind. The site text result rides as the `SiteTextFound` the store already validates, its run id, status and pages among it, so no type is added. The fields are appended after `Origin`, so no field moves.
- **The identity of a site text result is its prompts run id** (empty sites decision 2: "that `site_text` result's identity"). `store.Enrichment` has no id of its own: its fields are the candidate, kind, inputs, source, found value, cost and time (R-GDOT-3LA9, `D04-store`). The found value's `run` is required non-empty by `Valid` and names one run of the prompt, so a newer site text run on the same URL gives another id. The time was rejected: it is the store's injected clock, which a test may repeat, and it is not on the found value the subject carries.
- **The empty-site inputs extend the earlier fingerprint, and a later search keeps them** (empty sites decision 2, extended; decided by this design). The inputs under the condition begin with the name, city and state fingerprint and a `/`, which no other inputs of the kind hold, then a hash of the website and the site text run. A pure rule over the subject alone would make a company due again the moment its search found a different site: the URL changes, the condition no longer holds, the earlier rule names the plain fingerprint, and the newest search's inputs differ from it, so the next pass would search every company whose site was replaced, against empty sites outcome 1 ("due once") and the finish line's second pass doing nothing. So a website the search found keeps the newest search's inputs while they begin with the fingerprint now: a change of name, city or state still makes the company due (empty sites outcome 1's existing rules), and a site text result against the new site that keeps no pages makes it due by the condition. Rejected: carrying the deciding search's found value and a site text run id in its `site_text` object, which would add a member to the settled vocabulary; and a site text result of any URL, which forgets the settlement once site text runs on the new site. Every company that does not meet the condition and whose newest search was not recorded under it keeps its inputs byte for byte.
- **`Found` is handed the subject** (empty sites decisions 1 and 5). The found value records why the search ran and the site on record from the subject the run's input was built from, and the case from the answer and that site; the outcome carries no input. So `Prompter.Found` takes the subject, as it took a context in the website search change: `Found(ctx, s, out, pages)` on the interface and on `SiteText`, `Brief` and `WebsiteSearch`, the pass handing the subject it built the input from (`D06-passes`). Site text and brief ignore it; their requirements are re-minted only because their text names the signature. Recomputing the subject in `Found` was not possible: the enrichers never read the store.
- **The case compares hosts** (empty sites decision 5's `stands`: "the answer names the recorded website"). The website on record is the URL a fetch landed on, often with `www.` and a path such as `/home`; the agent names a site in its own form, often the bare domain with another scheme or path. Byte equality would count most `stands` answers `different`, and a `different` confirmed search decides over the check (above), flipping the source of a site that did not change. So both URLs are reduced to their host, lowercased, one trailing dot and one leading `www.` removed, a URL with no scheme read as if it had `http://`, and equal hosts are the same site. Scheme, port, path and query are ignored. The rule is a pure function of two strings, so a test proves it without a fetch.
- **A confirmed `different` search wins over the check of the URL it ran against** (departure from website search decision 7, where the check's site always wins; empty sites outcome 5, empty sites decision 3). Site text showed that site empty, and the owner's rule is that such a company is searched as one with no site; a search that found another site and loaded it is the better answer. The rule is the website decision's (`D04-store`); here the found value records what it needs: the website the search ran against and the case. A check that later finds another URL takes over; a check of the same URL does not, whatever its order. A rebuild from the results gives the same answer, since both are recorded on the result.
- **`website_from` reads `check` for a website with an empty source.** A facts row an earlier release projected holds a URL and no source (`D04-store`); only the check decided a website then, so `check` is what found it, and the input and the found value never carry a URL with an empty `website_from`.
- **The five new members are optional, and `Valid` accepts a value without them** (empty sites decision 4). Every `website_search` result recorded before this change lacks them and is read as it was; but this release calls `Valid` only on what it writes, through `AddEnrichments` and its own pass, never on a result it reads, so it meets such a value only when one is written again, as a test, a hand-built record (`D10-tools`' examples) or a value the release before built would be. `Valid` still refuses unknown members, and with them accepts only the combinations `Found` writes: a `reason` always, a `website` and `website_from` together and never empty, exactly when the reason is not `no_website`, a `site_text` only beside a website, `empty_site` only beside a site text that kept no pages, and a `case` exactly when the search did not fail, agreeing with the answer's URL and the website by the site key, since the website decision trusts the recorded case and website (`D04-store`). The members are top-level, beside `run`, `answer` and `confirmation`, and none equals a release-43 field name (R-85VH-Q371).
- **A search over a site on record that site text did not show empty has reason `again`** (decided in the repair of this design; departure from empty sites outcome 3, whose input names the site on record whenever there is one). Such a company is due by the earlier rule, its site found by a search, or is forced; `no_website` keeps its meaning, no website on record, and `empty_site` its own. The input of an `again` run names no site, `website`, `website_from` and `site_text` empty, so the prompt performs its ordinary search: the appendix's prompt paragraph reads a website on record as one that gave nothing, which for this company is not so, and its "empty when none" is read as none for this search. The found value still records the site on record, its source and its site text result from the subject, and the case against that site, so an answer naming it back is `stands` and changes nothing and another confirmed site is `different`, as for `empty_site`. Rejected: `no_website` with the site on record in the input, which tells the agent a site with pages gave nothing and makes the card's reason wrong. What the card says for each reason is `D08-pages`'.
- **`Failed` is the found value's own rule**, not the pass's or the page's, so the count of failed runs and the mark on the candidate page cannot disagree. A status prompts may add later counts as failed unless it is an exit with code 0, for a brief an exit with code 0 that gave a summary, and for a website search an exit with code 0 that gave an answer.
- **`site_text`'s and `brief`'s found members are snake_case and avoid release-43's field names**, like the derived kinds', and each refuses unknown members, since nothing old writes them.
- **`Prompter` gains `Failed`** (departure: P4 leaves the interface unchanged unless design finds a cleaner seam, and records it). The pass counting `runs_failed` and the candidate page marking a failed result each need every prompts kind's failed rule over a stored found value. Without the method each would switch on the kind and decode into that kind's found type, two switches to keep in step with every kind added; with it, each asks the `Prompter` the registry holds. `Failed` of a value that does not decode into the kind's found type is false; the store holds only values valid for their kind (`AddEnrichments`), so it never meets one. `Found` kept its signature then, page count included, though `brief` ignores the count; it gains a context in the website search change (below).
- **A brief category's source is the kind word**, `enrichers.KindBrief`, with no source constant of its own (P4): as `fmcsa` and `ppp` name the scraper that gave a category and `name` the government match, `brief` names the kind whose result gave it, and the input's filter, the facts projection and the page compare against one constant.
- **The facts ride on the subject.** Brief decision 4 reads the input's facts from the facts row, but the enrichers never read the store; so the store carries them on the subject, as it already carries the facts row's website URL, in the `LinkFound` shape `link_facts` writes, dates as strings. Its categories are the facts' `Records`, what the newest `link_facts` result found (`D04-store`), not the row's `Categories`: once a good brief decides, the row holds only `brief` categories, and the input, which leaves them out, would carry none, where the previous release's input carried the records' categories; reading the records keeps the input's categories what the records say (category evidence decision 10). Nil means no facts row. Reusing `LinkFound` adds no type; the input still leaves out any `brief` category and every empty member.
- **`BriefFound` has no `pages` member** (departure from brief decision 6's "records the run as `site_text`'s does"): `site_text`'s `pages` is the count of page URLs the company held when its run ended; a brief keeps no page, and the decision lists the brief's members without one. So `Found` ignores the count it is handed and `Valid` refuses a `pages` member.
- **The brief's class comes from the links, not the facts row** (departure from category evidence decision 9's "as the facts row would carry it"): the facts row carries no class, and `LinkFound`, the shape the subject's facts ride in, does not change (category evidence decision 10). So `class` is read, like `people`, from the subject's links: the `Reading.Class` of the first FMCSA link not gone, the link whose verdict, power units and drivers `link_facts` copies (R-FDKI-IJBM). While the facts row is stale the class may describe another FMCSA link than its verdict, power units and drivers, accepted because a derived walk brings the row current and they are then one reading. `Subject` gains no field, and the inputs stay the pages alone, so no brief becomes due by it.
- **The page cut never splits a UTF-8 sequence.** Brief decision 4 cuts a page's text at `BriefPageBytes` bytes; a cut inside a multi-byte character would hand `json.Marshal` invalid UTF-8, which it turns into a replacement character. Moving the cut back to the start of that character keeps the bound and the text valid, at the price of at most three bytes.
- **`Found` decodes the standard output whatever the status.** A run that was killed may have written nothing and one that exited 1 may have written something; decoding always keeps `Found` one rule, and `Failed`, not the decode, says whether the result counts. A failed result's categories never reach the facts row (`D04-store`), so recording them costs nothing and shows on the result what the run said.
- **`BriefFacts` leaves out empty members, and its people come from the links** (departure from brief decision 4's "an object read from the company's facts row (empty when there is none)"): the facts row holds no people, so `people` is read from the links not gone, which the subject carries whether or not there is a facts row; `facts` is then `{}` only when there is no facts row and no person, and the model is never handed a member that says nothing.
- **Derived work runs inside the store** (sheet 0.3): the store calls `Derive` within its own writes, so a merge or split re-runs the derived kinds atomically with the ruling and the judge that follows. A pass's `store.Derive` writes its candidates in windows, one write transaction each, and a window the database answers busy is paused and tried again by the store before the call fails (decisions log P15, `D04-store`). `internal/store` imports `enrichers`; `enrichers` imports `sources`, `fmcsa` and `website` and never `store` or `passes`.
- **The website found value is `website.Result`'s JSON** (sheet 0.6), with Go's member names, because release-43 decodes it by those names. Rows release-43 wrote hold more members (`Candidate`, `Kind`, `Source`, `Cost`, `At`) and are still valid; a reader decodes them into `website.Result` and ignores the rest, so the website kind does not refuse unknown members.
- **Derived found members are snake_case and avoid release-43's field names** (sheet 0.7), so release-43 never fails on a derived row. The derived kinds refuse unknown members, since nothing old writes them.
- **`drive_minutes` writes `{}` for a ZIP the table lacks** (sheet 0.8), rather than writing nothing: otherwise a ZIP moving to an unknown one would keep stale minutes, and the candidate would be due on every pass forever.
- **The fingerprint hashes every link, gone ones included** (sheet 0.9; settled decision 2: each link's id, state and reading values, sorted), so a link going gone changes it; the inputs are empty only with no link at all.
- **The facts projection reads the newest results, not every result** (decisions log P15): the store re-makes a candidate's facts row from, for each kind, its newest result, reached newest first through the `enrichments_candidate` index, reading further back only as far as a fact needs (the newest brief that did not fail, the newest search with a confirmed site, the newest route whose inputs match). Earlier version: every result the candidate ever had was read for each projection. Why: a full ingest re-projects every candidate, a page of `FillFacts` at a time, and reading each one's whole history grows with every result recorded; on `us` that pass failed `store` under a fixed memory cap, and it must do its work with as little disk traffic as it can. The facts it projects are unchanged in content, so no requirement here changes; how the store reads, and the bound on what one projection allocates (R-Y053-JMWZ), are `D04-store`'s.
- **A candidate whose inputs become empty keeps its last result's facts** (sheet 0.10), departing from enrichers decision 3's "the facts projection shows unknown": the facts row is the projection of the newest result of each kind and must be re-made from the enrichments alone (enrichers decision 5), and a candidate that has none newer keeps its newest. This is `D13-judging`'s precedent for the website check: a candidate that stops being checked keeps its last result as the newest.
- **Derive is total; it normalizes rather than refuses.** A `Subject` is a plain value any caller can build, so `Derive` cannot assume what the store's readings guarantee (a scraper on every link, a verdict from `fmcsa.Read`, a date a scrape could hold). Rather than narrow the contract to an input domain, each value its kind's `Valid` would refuse is read as absent or unknown, the way an empty verdict already was: every found value it returns is then valid for any subject, and the store never fails on a derived write. For the store's own subjects nothing changes.
- **The rules moved, not changed** (enrichers decision 6): `link_facts` gives, field for field, what the judge wrote before for the same links, but for the category the government match adds (categories decision 3), and the rules version stayed 5 (the revenue bands change later brings 6, `D13-judging`).
- **The government match's category comes from the one match `link_facts` keeps**, the first in link order, not from every link's match: the fact and the category then never disagree, and two links matching `COUNTY` and `CITY OF` give `government` from `name` once. The fact stays, and so does the `government` prospect list filter. The fingerprint is unchanged: the match is already on each link's reading, which it hashes.
- **New reading fields change every fingerprint** (Form 5500 decisions 14, 21 and 22). The fingerprint hashes the whole reading's JSON, so when `sources.Reading` gains `NameKeys` and `EINKey` (`D01-layout-and-run-seam`), every link's line changes twice: first when a stored reading is encoded with the two new members empty, and again when the full ingest under rules 7 fills them. Every candidate is due for `link_facts` once per change, and each result it records finds what the one before found, since `LinkFound` carries neither key; the headcounts, a Form 5500 link's `participants` among them, flow through unchanged. A derived kind costs nothing, so this is accepted rather than leaving the keys out of the fingerprint, which would make the fingerprint a second list of reading fields to keep in step with the reading. While an older release runs alongside, or after a rollback and re-upgrade, each release's walk sees the other's fingerprint as changed and records a fresh `link_facts` result, which costs nothing and finds the same facts.
- **New reading fields change every fingerprint again** (OSHA ITA outcome 16). `sources.Reading` gains `Establishment`, `Year`, `Employees` and `Hours`, and `sources.Headcount` gains `Hours` (`D01-layout-and-run-seam`), so, as for Form 5500's keys, every link's line changes when a stored reading is first encoded with the new members, empty or null, and every candidate is due for `link_facts` once. The full ingest under rules 8 then leaves every reading of another source as it encodes now, its new members empty, and gives each OSHA ITA row its figures as it first links it. Each result recorded by the first walk finds what the one before found, since `LinkFound` carries none of the reading's new fields and a headcount of another source has no hours. Leaving the new fields out of the fingerprint was rejected for the Form 5500 change's reason: the fingerprint would become a second list of reading fields to keep in step with the reading. Beside release-63, or after a rollback and re-upgrade, each release's walk sees the other's fingerprint as changed and records a fresh result; what the previous release records lacks the hours (below).
- **Each link's hours are copied, never summed or checked** (OSHA ITA outcome 12, OSHA ITA P6.3). A business's several sites are one link each, and the facts row and the card show each site's count and hours, so `link_facts` keeps one entry per link and the judge sums a year's sites (`D13-judging`): a sum here would hide the sites and would be a second place deciding which entries are one submission entered twice. `Derive` copies the hours as the reading holds them and `Valid` checks them no more than it checks the count, so `Derive` stays total: a negative or zero hours no source gives is copied, and the judge reads what it reads. The rule is layered: R-F8OW-ZGCU is added beside R-FG0B-A2T0 and R-BT4J-M6FB beside R-JSLE-MUH6, which stay as they are, since the text of the `Headcounts` requirement was true of the new field's neighbours and re-minting it would then have re-minted the link facts requirement, which cites it, and R-JSLE-MUH6's decoding now knows `hours` without a word of it changing.
- **Every link's headcount is copied, raw** (revenue bands decisions 4 and 6). The judge, not `link_facts`, ranks the headcounts by kind and compares them, so the link facts keep each link's, in link order, not only the first FMCSA link's. `Derive` copies the kind and count as the reading holds them and `Valid` checks neither, so `Derive` stays total (above): a reading no source gives, with an unknown kind or a count below 1, is copied, and the judge's pick ignores it (`D13-judging`). A link with no scraper is copied too, unlike its category: a headcount without a scraper is still a count, and the judge's basis names it as it is.
- **The scraper is the link's**, not the reading's own `Headcount.Scraper`. The two agree for every reading a source gives (`D05-sources`), and the link's scraper is what the categories carry as their source, so the link facts never disagree with themselves.
- **A headcount's date is written as the other dates are**: `2006-01-02` in UTC, empty when the time is not datable (R-F7H0-LOM5), which for every reading a source gives means when it is zero. The contract sheet's "empty when zero" is extended to the years outside 0 to 9999 so that `Derive` never writes a date `Valid` refuses.
- **`Valid` accepts a result without `headcounts`, and with `null`** (revenue bands decisions 4, 19 and 20). Every `link_facts` result written before this change has no such member, and `FillFacts` and the facts projection read the newest of them until the full ingest recomputes them, so it is valid and decodes to none. `null` is what `json.Marshal` gives for a `LinkFound` whose `Headcounts` is nil, which tests and callers that build one by hand record through `AddEnrichments` (`D10-tools`' examples among them); refusing it would refuse values the rest of the design builds. Both read as no headcount. `categories` stays required. `Valid` refuses a `headcounts` that is not an array, an element that is not an object (`null` among them, which would otherwise decode to an empty `Headcount`) or not shaped like `Headcount`, and a date `2006-01-02` does not parse. `Derive` always writes the member, as an array.
- **Rollback across this change is safe for the found values.** The release before reads a `link_facts` result with `json.Unmarshal`, which ignores the `headcounts` member, and validates only what it writes itself; its own derived results carry no `headcounts` and stay valid here. `headcounts` differs from every release-43 field name, so R-K4OS-L1LH holds unchanged for the new member.
- **Rollback across the OSHA ITA change drops the hours until a fresh result** (OSHA ITA outcome 18; `D04-store` Known limits). Release-63 reads a `link_facts` result with `json.Unmarshal`, which ignores a `headcounts` element's `hours`, and its `Valid`, which refuses unknown members, runs only on the results it derives itself, so no stored result fails it. But its enrich walk sees every candidate's fingerprint as changed (above) and records on each a `link_facts` result without `hours`, so the facts rows show nil hours until this release's next enrich walk or a full ingest records a result with them. This release's `Valid` accepts release-63's results, `hours` being optional, and `hours` is a member of a `headcounts` element, not a top-level member, so R-K4OS-L1LH holds unchanged.
- **The fingerprint is unchanged, and now says so for the headcount** (revenue bands decision 4): the reading's JSON already holds `Headcount`, so R-INXX-S7U1 already covers it; R-ZAH5-J1V1 is added so a test proves that a changed headcount makes the kind due, since the example fields of R-INXX-S7U1 predate it.
- **The subject's facts carry no headcounts.** The store builds the subject's `Facts` from the facts row (R-UADJ-LEIU, unchanged), leaving `Headcounts` nil: no enricher reads them from the subject, and the brief's input stays as it was (revenue bands decision 5).
- **`brief_labels` is the store's, not an enricher's** (revenue bands decision 5; rejected there: the judge reading the newest brief result itself). The facts row carries the labels of the newest good brief, projected by `D04-store` from `BriefFound.Labels`, beside `brief` and `brief_at`; this document adds nothing for them.
