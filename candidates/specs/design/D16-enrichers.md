# D16-enrichers

The enrichers: the kinds of enrichment candidates keeps, each a kind word with a rule naming a candidate's inputs and a rule computing a result from them, held in one fixed registry the enrich pass walks without knowing any kind in particular (enrichers decisions document, decisions 1 and 2). This document owns the new package `internal/enrichers` whole: the kind words and source words, the **subject** an enricher reads of a candidate, the result and the found shapes, the `Enricher`, `Deriver` and `Fetcher` interfaces, the three enrichers `drive_minutes`, `link_facts` and `website_check`, what each names as its inputs and, for the two derived kinds, what each computes, which found values each accepts, and the registry. The records, the due read, the derived walk, the facts row and the reads of results are `D04-store`'s (`store.Enrichment`, `DueEnrichments`, `Derive`, `AddEnrichments`, `GetFacts`, `FillFacts`); the enrich pass, the rebuild, their counts and follow-ons are `D06-passes`'; what the website check fetches, how, and what its `Ready` and `Fetch` do are `D15-website-check`'s; merge and split re-running the derived kinds are `D12-resolution`'s; what a judge makes of the facts is `D13-scoring`'s; the `enrich` tool is `D10-tools`'.

## Fetched and derived

An enricher is one kind. A kind is **fetched** when it reaches outside the service and may cost money, and **derived** when it reads only what the service holds (decision 1). The distinction is the enricher's `Fetched`, and it decides two things only: a derived kind is a `Deriver`, whose `Derive` computes a result from the subject alone, so the store can run it inside its own writes; a fetched kind is a `Fetcher`, whose `Fetch` computes many inputs at a pace of its own and whose `Ready` says whether it has anything to fetch with. A rebuild treats every derived kind as due for every candidate and never a fetched one (`D06-passes`); a merge or split re-runs the derived kinds for what it touched (`D12-resolution`). No enricher is both.

Three kinds are registered in this release, in this order, which is both the order the enrich pass walks them and kind name order: `drive_minutes` and `link_facts`, derived, then `website_check`, fetched (decision 8: derived kinds first, since they finish in seconds and the fetched kind can take hours). `enrichers.Kinds()` lists them.

## Inputs and due

Every kind names a candidate's **inputs** as one string: the display ZIP for `drive_minutes`, the link fingerprint for `link_facts`, and the checked domain (`website.Domain` of the display email, `D15-website-check`) for `website_check`. Every result records the inputs it was computed from (`store.Enrichment.Inputs`, `D04-store`). A candidate is due for a kind when it has no result of that kind, or its newest result's inputs differ from the inputs named now; there are no refresh ages (decision 3). Empty inputs mean not due and no result: no ZIP, no own domain, no link at all.

The inputs are computed from the candidate's **subject**, the one value an enricher reads: the candidate's id, display email and display ZIP, the ZIP table's minutes for that ZIP, and every link with its scraper, source id, whether it is gone and the source's reading of it, in the order `store.Links` answers (`D12-resolution`: not gone first, then newest filing date, then newest last seen). The subject is built by the store, which holds all of it; the enrichers never read the store, so they are pure functions a test drives with subjects it builds.

## drive_minutes

The inputs are the display ZIP, and the result is the minutes the ZIP table holds for it (decision 2), as `{"minutes":84}`, with source word `zip_drive` and cost 0. When the table lacks the ZIP, or holds a negative value for it, the minutes are unknown and the result is still written, as `{}`: a candidate whose ZIP moves from a known one to an unknown one must lose its stale minutes, and a result keeps it from being due on every pass forever (recorded decision below).

## link_facts

The result is the facts a judge derived from links before this release (decision 2), computed now once, when the links change, over the links not gone: **known since**, the earliest date any shows the business existing; the **newest filing**; the **government match**, the first pattern a link's name matched, in link order; the **categories**, each a link's category with the scraper that gave it; and the **fleet verdict** with the **power units** and **drivers**, from the first FMCSA link, which in link order is the freshest. The rules are `D13-scoring`'s of before, moved here unchanged, and so are their examples; the source word is `links` and the cost 0. Link facts are one kind, not five, because they share one input and are always recomputed together.

## The fleet verdict

The fleet verdict is `pass`, `fail` or `unknown` (`sources.FleetPass`, `sources.FleetFail`, `sources.FleetUnknown`). Each FMCSA row already says which, on its reading (`D05-sources`). `link_facts` copies the verdict of the candidate's first FMCSA link not gone, and that link's power units and drivers beside it; with no such link the verdict is unknown and both counts are absent. Only FMCSA counts trucks, so a candidate known only from PPP is unknown, never fail, whatever its readings say; a link stored by an earlier release decodes with an empty verdict and gives `unknown`, never a guess from its old fleet boolean. A negative count on that link's reading is absent, as if the reading lacked it. Power units and drivers come from the same link as the verdict, so they never disagree.

## The link fingerprint

The inputs of `link_facts` are a fingerprint of every link of the candidate, gone ones included: the SHA-256, in lowercase hexadecimal, of one line per link holding its source key, whether it is gone and its reading as JSON, the lines sorted. Sorting makes the order of the links irrelevant; including the gone flag makes a link going gone change the inputs, so the facts drop it. The inputs are empty only when the candidate has no link at all.

## website_check

The website check is `D15-website-check`'s; here it is one enricher among three. Its inputs are the checked domain, its source word is that domain too, and its found value is the JSON of a `website.Result`. `Ready` is whether it holds a checker, so an enrich pass with no checker still runs the derived kinds; `Fetch` checks each domain once through the checker's `CheckAll` (`D15-website-check`).

## What a result holds

A result is the source word, the found value, JSON shaped by its kind, and the cost; the store adds the candidate, the kind, the inputs and the time (decision 4). `AddEnrichments` asks the registry whether a found value is valid for its kind instead of checking that the kind is `website_check` (`D04-store`). Every found value a `Derive` returns is valid for its kind, for every subject: `Derive` treats a negative minutes or count as absent rather than copying it.

Release-43 decodes every row of a candidate's enrichments into its own `store.Enrichment`, whose fields are `Candidate`, `Kind`, `State`, `URL`, `Title`, `StatusCode`, `Size`, `Error`, `Source`, `Cost` and `At`, matching member names without regard to case. So the website found value keeps the Go member names release-43 reads, and the derived found values use snake_case members none of which equals one of those names, so a rolled-back release-43 decodes a derived row into an empty website result and never fails on it.

## Known limits

- **A tie reordered by last seen alone is not seen.** `store.Links` breaks filing-date ties by last seen, and the fingerprint leaves last seen out, so two FMCSA links filed the same day that swap places only because one was seen again do not make `link_facts` due, and the fleet verdict may stay the other link's until something else changes. A rebuild recomputes every derived kind and puts it right.

## Recorded decisions

- **The registry is fixed and built by `New`** (sheet 0.1), in the order `drive_minutes`, `link_facts`, `website_check`. Unlike `sources`, nothing forces an injected list: no import cycle, and the only runtime part is the checker, which `New` takes.
- **Derived work runs inside the store** (sheet 0.3): the store calls `Derive` within its own writes, so a merge or split re-runs the derived kinds atomically with the ruling and the judge that follows. `internal/store` imports `enrichers`; `enrichers` imports `sources`, `fmcsa` and `website` and never `store` or `passes`.
- **The website found value is `website.Result`'s JSON** (sheet 0.6), with Go's member names, because release-43 decodes it by those names. Rows release-43 wrote hold more members (`Candidate`, `Kind`, `Source`, `Cost`, `At`) and are still valid; a reader decodes them into `website.Result` and ignores the rest, so the website kind does not refuse unknown members.
- **Derived found members are snake_case and avoid release-43's field names** (sheet 0.7), so release-43 never fails on a derived row. The derived kinds refuse unknown members, since nothing old writes them.
- **`drive_minutes` writes `{}` for a ZIP the table lacks** (sheet 0.8), rather than writing nothing: otherwise a ZIP moving to an unknown one would keep stale minutes, and the candidate would be due on every pass forever.
- **The fingerprint hashes every link, gone ones included** (sheet 0.9; settled decision 2: each link's id, state and reading values, sorted), so a link going gone changes it; the inputs are empty only with no link at all.
- **A candidate whose inputs become empty keeps its last result's facts** (sheet 0.10), departing from decision 3's "the facts projection shows unknown": the facts row is the projection of the newest result of each kind and must be rebuildable from the enrichments alone (decision 5), and a candidate that has none newer keeps its newest. This is `D13-scoring`'s precedent for the website check: a candidate that stops being checked keeps its last result as the newest.
- **The rules moved, not changed** (decision 6): `link_facts` gives, field for field, what the judge wrote before for the same links, and the rules version stays 5.

## Consumer tasks

The store builds a candidate's subject as `s := enrichers.Subject{ID: c.ID, Email: c.Email, Zip: c.Zip, Minutes: m, Links: ls}`, `m` from `ZipMinutes` and `ls` from `Links`, each `enrichers.Link{Scraper: l.Scraper, SourceID: l.SourceID, Gone: l.Gone, Reading: l.Reading}`; it keeps `reg := enrichers.New(nil)` and validates with `reg.Valid(e.Kind, e.Found)`; for a derived kind `k` it takes `en, ok := reg.Get(k)` and `d, ok := en.(enrichers.Deriver)`, `in := d.Inputs(s)`, skipping an empty `in`, and `r := d.Derive(s)` gives the `Source`, `Found` and `Cost` it records beside `in`.

The engine builds `reg := enrichers.New(cfg.Website)` and walks `reg.All()`: a value whose `Fetched()` is false is run through `st.Derive`; one that is a `enrichers.Fetcher` is skipped when `Ready()` is false and otherwise handed each batch's unchecked inputs through `Fetch(ctx, inputs, func(in string, r enrichers.Result) { … })`. The `enrich` tool refuses a `kind` not in `enrichers.Kinds()`.

A test of a derived kind builds a subject, calls `enrichers.LinkFacts{}.Inputs(s)` and `res := enrichers.LinkFacts{}.Derive(s)`, checks `res.Source == enrichers.SourceLinks`, decodes `var lf enrichers.LinkFound; json.Unmarshal(res.Found, &lf)` and compares it field by field; a test of the registry calls `` enrichers.New(nil).Valid(enrichers.KindDriveMinutes, json.RawMessage(`{"minutes":84}`)) ``.
