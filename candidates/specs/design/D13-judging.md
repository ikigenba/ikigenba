# D13-judging

Judging: what `candidates` judges about each candidate, as two separate **judgments**, under rules version 9. The first is **operating confidence**: how sure we are that it is a real business operating today, from 0 to 1, with the evidence behind it. The second is the **revenue estimate**: a low, a high and how much we trust the range, combined from the estimates the sources give and the one the judge forms from the candidate's **headcount** times a **revenue band** for its kind of business. The facts a prospect list's filters and order read beside them (when the business is first known to have existed, the newest filing, its categories, the government name pattern it matched, the **fleet verdict** with the power units and drivers behind it, the headcounts its links give, the labels of its brief, the **website** state and address with the source that decided it, and the drive minutes) are not judged: they are enrichments, which enrichers derive or fetch (`D16-enrichers`) and the store keeps as the candidate's **facts row** (`D04-store`). A judge reads the facts row and writes only the judgments (funnel rename decisions 12 and 13). This document owns the pure functions of `internal/judging` (the combined estimate, the freshness of a filing, the judged weight of a piece of evidence, the combining rule, the order key, the band a candidate's labels or categories choose, the headcount picked from its headcounts, the headcount estimate, and the headcount series with its trend) and what a judge writes (`store.Judge`, `store.JudgeAll`, and the judging `store.Decide` does of what it touches): the judgments, worked out from the candidate's links, rulings and facts row. What a source reads from one row (its estimate, evidence, dates, category, government match, its fleet verdict and counts, and its headcount) is `D05-sources`'; how the link facts and the drive minutes are derived from a candidate, the fleet verdict and the headcounts among them, is `D16-enrichers`'; which links a candidate holds and in which order, and which derived results a ruling records, is `D12-ingest`'s; how and when a website is checked is `D15-website-check`'s, and how and when one is searched for is `D16-enrichers`'; the records, the enrichments and the facts row among them, are `D04-store`'s; when a judge runs is `D06-passes`'; which candidates a prospect list answers, and in which order, is `D14-prospect-lists`'.

"Intent decision N" is entry N of the decisions document of the judgments change, *two judgments and the PPP reader*; "cohorts decision N" is entry N of the decisions document of the cohorts change, and "cohorts log N" entry N of its decisions log. "Website decision N" is entry N of the decisions document of the website-check change, and "enrichers decision N" entry N of the decisions document of the enrichers change. "Category evidence decision N" is entry N of the decisions document of the category-evidence change. "Website search decision N" is entry N of the decisions document of the website-search change, and "website states outcome N" outcome N of the decisions document of the website-states change, which made `none` mean that a search found nothing. "Funnel rename decision N" is decision N of the decisions document of the funnel rename, which gives what candidates stores, answers and shows the funnel's stage names, and "funnel rename log DN" entry DN of its delivery's decisions log. "Revenue bands decision N" is decision N of the decisions document of the revenue-bands change, *PPP jobs and the revenue bands*, which makes the revenue estimate a headcount times a band per employee and brings rules version 6. "Form 5500 decision N" is candidates decision N of the decisions document of the Form 5500 change, which adds the `form5500` source and brings rules version 7. "OSHA ITA outcome N" is outcome N of the decisions document that delivered the `osha_ita` source, which brings rules version 8, and "OSHA ITA P N" is entry P N of that delivery's decisions log. "Headcount trend decision N" is outcome N of the decisions document of the headcount-trend change, *the headcount series and trend*, which gives the judgments a headcount series and a trend and brings rules version 9.

## Why two judgments

Rules version 2 ranked every candidate in one list by one number, confidence times estimated revenue, and that number mixed two questions. Carrier type says how well a truck count predicts revenue; filing age says whether the business still exists. Blended, a well-documented trucking company always outranked a real manufacturer, and nobody could tell from the number why. Version 3 answers the two questions separately, and filing age touches only the first.

## Operating confidence

A source reads, from each row, the **row evidence** it gives: each piece a kind (`fmcsa_active`, `fmcsa_activity`, `ppp_loan`, `own_domain`, `form5500_filing`, `osha_ita_summary`), its source, its weight before freshness and a short description (`D05-sources`). At a judge, each piece of each link not gone is weighed: an `fmcsa_active` piece is multiplied by the **freshness** of its row's filing date, 1 within two years of the date (the MCS-150 cycle), falling linearly to 0 at five years, and 0 without a date; every other kind keeps its weight. A piece whose judged weight is not above 0 says nothing and is left out. Each kind counts once per candidate, from the link that gives it the greatest weight, and the kept pieces are listed by weight, highest first. An OSHA ITA row gives one `osha_ita_summary` piece, at the weight of a PPP loan or a Form 5500 filing, 0.2: a 300A summary shows the business operated in its filing year (OSHA ITA outcome 15). A business with OSHA links in many years, or many sites in one, still counts the kind once, so a candidate known only from OSHA judges 0.2 however many summaries it filed; the freshness of that evidence is deferred.

The **operating evidence** of a candidate is that list plus what enrichment adds, which today is the website. A judge reads the website state of the candidate's facts row and the source that decided it, check or search, which are the website decision over its results (`D04-store`): the newest `website_check` result's state when it found a site, otherwise the newest confirmed `website_search` result's, otherwise `none` when some search that is not failed found nothing, named no URL or named one that did not load, the newest such deciding, otherwise `unknown` (`D15-website-check`, `D16-enrichers`, website states outcomes 1 to 3). When that state is `https` or `http` and its source is not the search, the candidate gains one `website_live` piece, source `website_check`, weighing 0.5, described by `sources.DescribeWebsiteLive`. A newest check that found nothing adds nothing, even when an older check found a site, and leaves the website `unknown` or the search's answer standing; a `none`, a search that found nothing, adds nothing either; and neither touches `own_domain`: missing evidence is never counted against a company (website states outcome 6). No row, or a row whose website reads `unknown`, adds nothing. A site the search found adds nothing either (website search decision 12): the judgment is unchanged, and its website evidence still reads the website check alone, so a company the search found a site for judges as it did before the search. A row a release before the website search wrote has an empty source, and its site counts, since only the check could have set it. The piece joins the row evidence under the same rule, one per kind, listed by weight, so an FMCSA carrier filed within two years with its own domain judges 0.68, and 0.84 once its site answers. The judge reads neither the enrichments nor the ZIP table: a website result reaches the judgment only through the facts row it writes, its source included.

The combining rule closes part of the gap to certainty with each piece: `1 − (1 − w₁) × (1 − w₂) × …`. The result is rounded to six decimal places, so weights 0.6, 0.3 and 0.2 give 0.776 and one piece weighing 0.2 gives 0.2. **Missing evidence is never zero**: a candidate with no evidence has no operating confidence, not 0, and a prospect list ordered by operating confidence puts it after every candidate that has one (`D14-prospect-lists`).

## Revenue estimate

Each source gives each candidate at most one **estimate**: the estimate of its freshest link not gone. Since version 6 that is FMCSA's truck estimate alone, and only for a `for_hire` or `mixed` carrier (`D05-sources`, revenue bands decision 12): PPP gives none (revenue bands decision 13), so version 5's rule that took the PPP link with the largest estimate has nothing left to choose among and is gone, and every source keeps its freshest link's. Beside them the judge forms one estimate of its own, the **headcount estimate**, whose source word is `judging.HeadcountSource`, from the facts row alone (revenue bands decision 8): the headcount it picks times the band the labels or categories choose. A judge writes it into the estimates beside the sources' and combines it with them as any other. The **combined** estimate weights by confidence: `low` is the confidence-weighted mean of the estimates' lows, `high` the same over the estimates that give one and absent when none does, the confidence the highest of theirs (revenue bands decision 14). A candidate with no estimate has no revenue fields at all. Disagreement between estimates is still deferred.

## The headcount

A source can read a **headcount** from a row: a count, its kind word, the scraper that gave it and the date it is as of (`D05-sources`, revenue bands decisions 1 to 3). PPP reads `jobs_reported` as kind `jobs`, dated by the loan's approval; FMCSA reads its plausible driver count as kind `drivers`, dated by the MCS-150 filing; Form 5500 reads a single-employer plan's participants as kind `participants`, dated by the end or the beginning of its plan year (Form 5500 decisions 16 and 17); OSHA ITA reads a 300A summary's annual average employees as kind `employees`, dated the last day of its filing year, with the hours worked beside the count, and gives none when the hours disagree with the count (OSHA ITA outcomes 11 and 12). A headcount carries **hours** only from OSHA ITA, and none when its row gives none. The `link_facts` kind copies each link's headcount, its hours included, into the facts row's headcounts, in link order, one entry per link, so every site of a business stands in the facts row and on the card with its own count and hours (`D16-enrichers`, `D04-store`, revenue bands decision 4, OSHA ITA P6).

`judging.PickHeadcount` picks one from them (revenue bands decision 6). The kinds are ranked best first: `employees`, `participants`, `jobs`, `drivers`, `snippet`; a headcount of another kind, or of a count not above zero, is ignored. Before it compares, it folds the `employees` headcounts into **site sums**: those of one scraper on one date are one business's sites in one year, and they give one headcount whose count is the sum of theirs (OSHA ITA outcome 12). Two of them with an equal count and equal hours are one summary entered twice and count once, so Rosenbauer Minnesota's 2024 summary, entered under two establishment ids with 357 employees over 715,864 hours each, sums to 357, not 714; two with an equal count and no hours are two sites, since a missing figure is never an equal one. The sum's hours are the sum of the sites' hours when every site counted gives hours, and none otherwise. An undated headcount is never folded with another. Of each kind it then keeps one, the newest by its date, an undated one counting as the oldest, and of those on the newest date the greatest count (Form 5500 decision 18), so a newer year's sum beats an older, larger one. The best kind kept gives the headcount. When another kept headcount differs from the best by more than a third of the best, the headcount is a **range** from the least kept count to the greatest, and the band applies to each end. Confidence is `revenue-bands.md`'s table (revenue bands decision 11): 0.7 when two or more kinds are kept and agree; otherwise the best kind's own, 0.5 for `employees` and `participants`, 0.4 for `jobs`, 0.3 for `drivers` and `snippet`; a range takes its best kind's, never 0.7. The **basis** names each kind kept with the scraper that gave it, best first, so a Form 5500 count beside a PPP one gives `participants:form5500 jobs:ppp`, a site sum names its kind and scraper once (`employees:osha_ita`), and the pick is as of the best headcount's date. Every kind but `snippet` has a reader in this release. Design Ready Controls, five sites under its main EIN in 2025, so reads a headcount of 523; Hjort Excavating's 2025 count of 23 beside Form 5500's 20 participants agrees within a third and judges 23 at 0.7. A judge writes the pick into the judgments as the headcount low, high and basis (revenue bands decision 7), all empty when there is none.

## The bands

`judging.ChooseBand` chooses a band, a low and a high in dollars per employee per year, from the brief's labels and the candidate's categories (revenue bands decision 10). The **band table** and the **keyword rows** are `revenue-bands.md`'s, carried into the requirements as contract (revenue bands decision 9): fourteen bands, the thirteen per-employee rows of the document and `construction`, the union of construction's two sub-bands that a construction company without a sub-band takes; and eleven keyword rows in the document's order, the more specific first.

The labels come first: each label, in order, is read lower-cased against the rows top to bottom and each row's keywords in order, and the first keyword found anywhere in it decides; the first label that matches any row decides for the candidate, and the choice records that label and that keyword. With no label matching, the categories decide: construction takes `construction`, manufacturing `manufacturing_other`, trucking `trucking_private_mixed`, agriculture `agriculture_other`, wholesale, mining and waste their own bands, and every other category `other`. A candidate in several categories takes the widest band among theirs, the lowest low and the highest high, named by joining their names with `+` in table order, and the choice records the category names. With neither, the band is `other`, wide on purpose.

The document's two by-measure rows have no per-employee band. The for-hire trucking row's keywords take `trucking_private_mixed`, the per-employee fallback the document gives for it, since a for-hire carrier with a plausible truck count already has its truck estimate beside this one. The feedlot row's keywords take `agriculture_other`, as does the agriculture category: the band by animal unit waits for the MPCA source (revenue bands decision 10), and the row still matches first, so a `dairy farm` never reaches the food-manufacturing row's `dairy`.

## The headcount estimate

`judging.HeadcountEstimate` gives none when there is no headcount, and otherwise the estimate whose low is the headcount's low times the band's low and whose high is the headcount's high times the band's high, each an exact integer product that saturates at the largest `int64` (revenue bands decision 8). Its confidence is the pick's, its filed date the best headcount's and its formula `sources.Rules`. Its facts explain it to whoever reads the card or the `candidate` tool (revenue bands decision 16): the headcounts used, each with its scraper, kind, count and date, and its hours when it has them, a site sum giving the summed count and hours; the band's name, low and high; how the band was chosen; and, by label, the label and keyword, or, by categories, the category names.

## The headcount series

`judging.HeadcountSeries` turns the facts row's headcounts into the candidate's **headcount series**: for each yearly kind, one **figure** per calendar year (headcount trend decisions 1 to 4). The kinds are `employees` (OSHA ITA), `participants` (Form 5500) and `jobs` (PPP), in the pick's ranking, best first, and a kind with no figure has no series. Drivers are not in it: FMCSA's driver count is a floor dated by the carrier's last update, and it stays the pick's headcount of last resort; snippets, undated, stay out too (headcount trend decision 2). An undated headcount of any kind is out, since it has no year. The year of a figure is the calendar year of its headcount's date, read in UTC.

The figures come from the pick's own contenders, so each source's per-year rule is the one it already has: OSHA's sites of one year summed and a summary entered twice counted once, as `judging.PickHeadcount` folds them; Form 5500's largest active count on the newest plan year end, as its reader gives it; PPP's loan figure. Within one kind and one year the figure is the greatest count, the first given among equal counts, whatever their dates within the year, so two Form 5500 plan years ending in one calendar year, or two PPP loans of one year, give the larger, and the series never depends on the order rows were written (headcount trend decision 1). Nothing is invented: a year with no figure is a gap, an absent element, and a row that carries no headcount, a nonsense OSHA count or a multiemployer plan, gives none (headcount trend decision 3). Each series names the scraper behind its newest figure, the source its row on the card names. The series is a judgment, recomputed at every judge from the facts row and stored with the judgments (headcount trend decision 4), so the card and the `candidate` tool show it as the judge saw it (`D08-pages`, `D10-tools`).

## The trend

`judging.HeadcountTrend` reads the **trend** from the series: `growing`, `flat`, `shrinking` or `unknown`, with its **basis**, the kind, the two years compared, their figures and the change between them (headcount trend decisions 5 to 9).

It is read within one kind (headcount trend decision 5): Form 5500's participants run about a third high against OSHA's employees, so a series that changed source across years would read a change of source as a change of size. The series are tried in the order given, which the series' own order makes best kind first, and the first that has a comparison year decides; a better kind with no comparison year falls to the next, and with none the trend is unknown, its kind empty and its years, figures and change absent.

The **comparison year** (headcount trend decision 6) is chosen from the window of years before the kind's newest (`judging.TrendWindowYears`): the newest year of the kind at least `judging.TrendYearsBack` years older than the newest, when the window holds one; otherwise the oldest year in the window at least `judging.TrendMinYears` years older. With the owner's table that is, for a newest year N, N−3 when it has a figure, then N−4, then N−2, and N−1 never. One figure says nothing about direction, one year of change is noise for a small business, and a figure older than the window says nothing about today, so PPP's 2020 and 2021 alone read unknown, as do 2024 and 2025 alone.

The thresholds are the owner's table (headcount trend decisions 7 and 8), declared as the named constants of `judging` (`D01-layout-and-run-seam`), and the numbers in code equal the owner's document; a change there is a judge rule change, a new rules version and a judge over everything. Growing is the newest figure at least a fifth above the comparison figure (`judging.TrendDivisor`) and at least `judging.TrendFloor` people above it; shrinking is the same below; anything else is flat. The fifth is tested as the divisor times the difference against the comparison figure, with no division and no rounding, and computed without overflow, so exactly a fifth counts. The floor keeps a shop of five going to six, or a crew that loses one, from reading as a trend. The **change** is the newest figure less the comparison figure, signed.

Against the OSHA counts of the list members (the decisions document's Checked against): Hjort Excavating reads flat, 23 in 2025 against 20 in 2022, under the floor; Branch Manufacturing shrinking, 40 against 60; KTM Paving shrinking, 32 against 47; Heggies Pizza growing, 131 in 2025 against 100 in 2021, 2022 having no figure; Sheet Metal Connectors flat, 141 in 2025 against 140 in 2023, its only years; Molin Concrete flat, 243 against 261; Design Ready Controls growing, 534 against 393; Rosenbauer Minnesota growing, 402 against 327.

The trend is a prospect list filter and order field (`D14-prospect-lists`, headcount trend decision 10), and the card, the `candidate` tool and a prospect list member show it with its change (`D08-pages`, `D10-tools`, headcount trend decision 11). The brief's inputs are unchanged (headcount trend decision 12).

## The judgments

A judge writes, for each active candidate it is given, reading by its id the candidate's record, its own links, its rulings and its facts row, and nothing else, its estimates and its **judgments** (`store.Judgments`): the operating confidence with its order key and its operating evidence; the combined revenue low, high and confidence; the headcount low, high and basis; the headcount series, and the trend with its kind, years, figures and change, the series empty and the trend `unknown` with no basis when there is none; whether it is excluded; the rules version and when it was calculated. Only links not gone count, for every judgment the links give.

The judgments hold no fact (funnel rename decision 13). A judge reads the facts row for the website's operating evidence and for the headcounts the headcount estimate and the series are formed from, the brief labels and categories the band is chosen from, and nothing else of it, and writes no fact anywhere: the facts live in the facts row alone, which only enrichers write (`D04-store`). A prospect list's member carries its judgments and its facts side by side (`store.Ranked`), the `candidate` tool answers them as two members, `enrichments` and `judgments` (`D10-tools`), and the candidate page shows them apart (`D08-pages`), so a fact moves as soon as an enrich writes it, and only the judgments wait for a judge. A merged candidate keeps the judgments its last judge wrote, and its facts row is deleted at the merge, so it answers no facts.

`Judge` writes the candidates it is given in one transaction. `JudgeAll` judges one page (`D04-store`, `shape/`'s Bounded work): at most `n` active candidates with an id above its cursor, in id order, read and written as one query and one transaction, each stamped with the judge time `at` the caller passes as its `Calculated`, and answers the id of the last as the next cursor, empty once none remain; a page the database answers busy is paused and tried again by the store (`D04-store`). The judge pass (`D06-passes`) reads its judge time once and walks `JudgeAll` from an empty cursor with `passes.BatchSize` and that one `at` until the cursor comes back empty, each page its own query and transaction, never one open cursor across the pass, and sums each page's `Judged` and `Unranged`; so one judge's candidates share one `Calculated`. So a judge of the whole space is not atomic: a web page or tool read while it runs may find some candidates judged anew and others as the last judge left them, and a judge that fails keeps the pages it wrote.

## The facts

How each fact is worked out is `D16-enrichers`'. Known since, the newest filing, the government match, the categories the links give and the fleet verdict with its power units and drivers are the `link_facts` kind's, derived from the candidate's links not gone under the rules this document used to own and moved there unchanged: among them, the verdict and counts of the freshest FMCSA link not gone, `unknown` with no counts for a candidate known only from PPP, never `fail`. The categories are instead the `brief` kind's, read from the candidate's kept pages, when the good brief gives at least one; how the facts row chooses between the two kinds' categories is `D04-store`'s, and a judge reads the categories it chose for the band. The headcounts are the `link_facts` kind's, one per link not gone that carries one, and the brief labels the newest good brief's (revenue bands decisions 4 and 5). The drive minutes are the `drive_minutes` kind's, from the display ZIP. The website, its address and its source are the website decision over the `website_check` and `website_search` kinds' results (`D04-store`, `D15-website-check`, `D16-enrichers`): the check's site when it found one, otherwise the newest confirmed search's, so the `website` filter admits a company whose site the search found (website search decision 9), and a judge reads the state and source for the website's operating evidence. A judge neither derives nor fetches; a full ingest and a merge or split re-derive before they judge (`D06-passes`, `D12-ingest`).

**Nothing is removed automatically.** The government name patterns fill the government match and exclude nothing; the fleet verdict, an old filing, a missing estimate or missing minutes leave a candidate out of a prospect list only when that prospect list carries the filter that reads them, and each filter on a fact that can be unknown has its own switch for the unknowns (`D14-prospect-lists`). A candidate is excluded only when the owner's latest `exclude` or `include` on it is an `exclude`.

**Judgments are written only by a judge.** The judge pass (`D06-passes`, daily from the `judge_candidates` trigger, started by hand by the `judge` tool, and started as an enrich's follow-on under the rule stated there), a full ingest (the `ingest` tool naming no scraper, which ingests every scraper's current run, re-derives every derived kind for every candidate, then judges every candidate), and an owner's ruling (`merge`, `split`, `exclude`, `include`, which judges the candidates it touches in the same call, a merge or a split after re-deriving them (`D12-ingest`), so every prospect list answers a ruling at its next read; a `not_same` judges nothing) are the only writers. The rulings reach `Decide` through the `decide`, `exclude` and `include` tools (`D10-tools`); which tool records which kind changes nothing here. An ingest of one run or of one scraper stores rows and an enrich pass records results and their facts (`D06-passes`, `D16-enrichers`), and neither judges. There is no refresh: the pass-end statement of version 2 that re-applied the filing-age arithmetic in SQL to every stored ranking number, and its agreement test with Go, are gone, so the formulas live once, in Go. Between judges nothing decays: a stored judgment is what the last judge wrote, and the daily judge keeps it within a day of true, while the facts are always the newest results'.

**Why daily, not on change and not on read.** The candidates whose `fmcsa_active` weight is moving are the ones whose filing nobody updated, so their rows never change: 2,431 of the 16,706 active MN and WI carriers are in the two-to-five-year band, and 2,663 change weight within 30 days. Judging only changed candidates would never revisit them. Judging on read would make the first read after a quiet stretch wait for a full judge (18 seconds for the 16,580 candidates on `us`) and would add a staleness check to every read path, to save 18 seconds of CPU a day. A daily trigger is the pattern the suite already has. A prospect list read is the one place the service reads a clock at a read, for the filing-age filter, and it reads the stored newest filing against it; it judges nothing (`D14-prospect-lists`).

## No single ranking

Version 3's judgments were first read through one interim ranked list with one fixed order and the caller's filters applied per call. Prospect lists replace it (cohorts decision 15): which candidates a prospect list answers, its filters, unknown switches and order, is `D14-prospect-lists`', reading the judgments this document defines beside the facts. This document defines no list.

## Rules version 9

The rules version is `sources.Rules`, and its value is 9 (headcount trend decision 14); no requirement spells it, and tests read the value the source declares. As with the versions before it, the number lives in this prose alone: the requirement declaring `sources.Rules` (`D01-layout-and-run-seam`) states no value, so a new version opens no gap, and the delivery sets `sources.Rules` to 9 by direct instruction. Every estimate and judgments written carries it, the headcount estimate's formula included. Version 9 changes only what the judge writes: the judgments gain the headcount series and the trend with its basis. No reading, link fact, evidence, estimate or other judgment changes but for the version it carries, since the series reads the headcounts the facts row already holds. So it lands by a judge over everything, the `judge` tool or the daily `judge_candidates` trigger, with no full ingest. Until that judge runs, a candidate's judgments read the migration's defaults, no series and an unknown trend (`D04-store`), and carry version 8.

Version 8 (OSHA ITA P2) added a source, `osha_ita`, to the registry (`D05-sources`), with its `osha_ita_summary` evidence and its `employees` headcount with hours; of the judge it changed only the pick of `employees` headcounts, which since sums the sites of one scraper on one date before keeping the newest, and the headcount estimate's facts, which carry the hours. Every other kind was picked as under version 7, and no candidate without an OSHA ITA link judged differently. It landed by a full ingest, which re-read every row under one version before the first OSHA ITA run was ingested (OSHA ITA P2).

Version 7 (Form 5500 decision 21) changed a reading's keys for every source, its name keys and its EIN key (`D12-ingest`), and a new source, `form5500`, joined the registry (`D05-sources`); of the judge it changed only the pick between two headcounts of one kind on one date, which since keeps the greater count. Its full ingest re-read every row and rewrote every link's keys; until it ran on a space the new keys were empty on the links that existed, and matching by them found nothing extra, which was safe.

Version 6 (revenue bands decision 15) changed the revenue estimate: the headcount estimate joined, PPP's payroll estimate went, FMCSA's truck estimate was kept for `for_hire` and `mixed` carriers only, and the judgments gained the headcount. Operating confidence, its evidence and its order key were untouched, and `ppp_loan` evidence stayed.

Version 5 changed what a judge writes, the `website_live` evidence and the two website facts, and no formula: an estimate's arithmetic was version 3's, and its formula the same number stamped when a source reads the row (cohorts log 14). Version 4 brought the three-valued fleet verdict and its counts. The enrichers change kept version 5: from the same links, rulings and newest website result a judge writes the same opinions, and the facts are the same values, derived by enrichers instead of the judge. The website-search change kept version 5 too (website search decision 12): a new version marks the judge writing other opinions from the same inputs, and that one did not; a site the search found, which no earlier release could hold, is left out of the evidence and moves only the website facts, which are not judged.

A version that changes a reading lands by a full ingest (revenue bands decision 20): it re-reads every row, so each link's reading carries its headcount and no PPP or private-carrier estimate, re-derives `link_facts` so the facts row carries the headcounts, and judges everything. Until then a link keeps the reading an earlier release stored, so a judge reads no headcount from it and still counts the estimate it holds, a PPP payroll estimate included, as that scraper's freshest-link estimate; a release migration fills the new facts columns from the results already held (`D04-store`), which carry no headcounts until `link_facts` is derived again.

An active candidate's facts read unknown until it has a facts row (`D04-store`, `D03-serve`), and the first enrich after a release that adds a kind derives every candidate's link facts and minutes; its follow-on judge then rewrites the judgments. A full ingest re-ingests, re-derives and re-judges but never fetches or deletes a website result (`D04-store`), so the judge it ends with reads the website results already held, as it reads the owner's rulings; a merge moves the merged candidate's results to the survivor and re-derives the survivor, whose newest website result is then the one recorded last among them all.

## Recorded decisions

- **Ordering departure**: operating confidence is ordered by the **operating key**, the operating confidence, already rounded to six decimal places, scaled by a million and rounded to an integer, so the key is exact (0.776 keys 776000, 0.2 keys 200000), −1 when there is none. A prospect list ordered by operating confidence orders by the key, the candidate id breaking ties (`D14-prospect-lists`), and the stored key is indexed, so the order is one exact comparison in the query; two combined values less than a millionth apart may round to one confidence and one key and then fall to id order, a departure from ordering by the unrounded float (intent decision 18) that no person can see. An empty confidence is unknown and sorts after every judged value whatever the direction.
- **Operating confidence is rounded to six decimal places** (`math.Round(x*1e6)/1e6` of the combined value `x`, half away from zero), and the operating key is derived from the rounded value. The sandbox showed one 0.2 piece answering 0.19999999999999996, float residue no reader should see; the orchestrator ruled to store and answer the rounded value. A millionth is finer than any weight the sources give, so no ranking a person can see changes. Evidence weights and revenue confidence are not rounded. A candidate whose combined value is below half a millionth (only a piece weighing under 0.0000005 can do it) has an operating confidence of 0, keyed 0: it is still present, and still sorts before a candidate with none.
- **Rounding**: the combined low and high are the weighted means rounded half away from zero to whole dollars (`math.Round`), saturating at the `int64` bounds. With confidences summing to 0 the mean is unweighted; no rule of version 3 or 4 makes one.
- **Two and five years are calendar years** from the filing date (`AddDate(2, 0, 0)` and `AddDate(5, 0, 0)`), linear in elapsed time between them; a filing dated after the moment weighs 1. Freshness is measured at the judge's moment in UTC truncated to the second.
- **Evidence weighing nothing is left out**: a stale or undated `fmcsa_active` adds no piece, so a candidate with nothing else has no operating confidence rather than 0 (intent decision 2).
- **Evidence order** is weight, highest first, then kind name in byte order; one kind's best piece is the first in link order among equal weights.
- **Every source's estimate is its freshest link's** (revenue bands decision 13): version 5 took PPP's from the link with the greatest low, the largest amount; PPP now gives no estimate, so that rule is removed with the payroll estimate rather than kept for readings that no longer carry one. A PPP reading an earlier release stored still holds its payroll estimate until a full ingest re-reads it, and in that window it counts as PPP's freshest-link estimate (see Rules version 6).
- **The PPP payroll estimate is removed** (revenue bands decision 13, its Rejected entry): two formulas from one row would double-weight PPP in the mean and make the card harder to read; PPP contributes its headcount, and `amount` stays on the row.
- **The truck estimate is for for-hire and mixed carriers only** (revenue bands decision 12): FMCSA's reading gives none for a `private` or unknown class (`D05-sources`); the judge combines what the readings give, and a for-hire carrier with a plausible truck count and a headcount has both estimates, combined as any two.
- **The band table and the keyword rows are contract, equal to `revenue-bands.md`** (revenue bands decision 9): the numbers and keywords are in the requirements, so a change to the owner's document is a change to one requirement, a re-minted id and a release, and a disagreement between them is a bug in whichever moved. The table holds `construction`, the union of the two construction sub-bands, as a band of its own, since the document names the union without a row.
- **The feedlot row and the agriculture category take `agriculture_other`** (orchestrator decision, a departure that fills a gap the document leaves): the document's feedlot band is by animal unit, which waits for the MPCA source (revenue bands decision 10), and it names no per-employee band for a feedlot or for agriculture as a category. `agriculture_other` is the document's only per-employee agriculture band. The feedlot row is kept so its keywords still match before the food row's.
- **The for-hire keyword row takes `trucking_private_mixed`** (orchestrator decision): the document gives the private-or-mixed per-employee band as the for-hire row's fallback when FMCSA gives no plausible count, and this estimate is the per-employee one.
- **Newest per kind, then the kind ranking**: the document says the newest year of the best source wins and, within a source, the newest year; the pick keeps one headcount per kind, of `employees` one site sum, the latest `AsOf`, an undated one oldest, the greatest count among equal dates and the first given among equal dates and counts, before comparing kinds. Two links of one kind (two PPP loans) so give one headcount, and the range compares kinds, not loans.
- **Of one kind on one date, the greater count** (Form 5500 decision 18): a sponsor's pension plan and health plan on the same plan year give two `participants` counts of one date, and the larger is nearer the headcount, since more employees join a 401(k) than take the health plan. It replaces the first given, which made the pick depend on link order. A newer date still beats a greater count; the ranking, the range rule and the confidences are unchanged.
- **Several categories take the widest band, its name the bands' names joined by `+`** (revenue bands decision 10 and its Deferred entry on the brief's category order): the facts row sorts categories by name, so no category is first; the widest band is the honest one, and a joined name tells the reader which bands it spans. Names join in table order, so one set of categories always gives one name, and a category that maps to a band already taken adds nothing.
- **The range comparison is in exact integers**: "more than a third of the best" is three times the difference greater than the best count, with no division and no rounding, computed without overflow, so a difference of exactly a third agrees (30 and 40 give 30) and any count an `int64` holds compares correctly.
- **The headcount estimate saturates**: its low and high are exact products of a count and a band bound, and a product past the largest `int64` is the largest `int64`, as the combined mean saturates; no plausible count comes near it, but a reader's ceiling is far above what the bands can multiply.
- **The headcount estimate's facts keys** are `headcounts`, `band`, `band_low`, `band_high` and `chosen_by`, with `label` and `keyword` only when a label chose the band and `categories` only when the categories did: the card and the `candidate` tool show an estimate's facts as given (`D08-pages`, `D10-tools`), so these keys are what explain the band to the owner, and a key that does not apply is absent rather than empty.
- **The judgments' headcount is the pick, not the estimate**: the headcount low, high and basis are what `judging.PickHeadcount` gives, which is also what the estimate multiplies; both are absent together when there is no headcount.
- **The headcount estimate is the judge's** (revenue bands Rejected entry): it needs the band, which needs the labels and categories, which are the candidate's, not a row's; the judge reads them from the facts row alone, never from the newest brief result.
- **The link facts' rules moved to `D16-enrichers`** (enrichers decisions 2 and 6): the earliest known since, the newest filing, the first government match in link order, the categories with their sources, and the verdict and counts copied from the freshest FMCSA link's reading (an empty verdict read as unknown, a PPP reading's never consulted) are now what the `link_facts` kind derives, with the examples that proved them. Moving them changes no value; the judge reads their result from the facts row.
- **No fleet boolean**: the Go type of the facts holds the verdict, and the migration of the release that brought the verdict dropped the old `fleet` column with the unwritten `low` and `high` (`D04-store`, which records that departure from expand then contract).
- **The website piece weighs 0.5 whatever the result's age** (website decision 13, which sets no age): freshness is FMCSA's filing cycle, and `judging.Weight` leaves every other kind's weight alone. There are no refresh ages any more: a website result is renewed only when the candidate's domain changes (`D15-website-check`). A candidate that stops being checked keeps its last result as the newest: one whose display email turns free-provider or blank, or a survivor without its own domain that a merge gave an `https` result. That result then keeps 0.5 and its state indefinitely. This is accepted: the result is still what was last seen, and a later change may add an age if real results show it matters.
- **The newest check decides alone** (website decision 13): a site that answered once and has since stopped answering gives no evidence, and nothing averages over a candidate's results; the facts row holds a site only from the newest check, or one the search found, which gives no evidence, and otherwise the search's `none` or `unknown`, neither of which gives any.
- **The facts come from the facts row at the read** (enrichers decisions 5 and 7, replacing website decision 14's "the website facts come from the judge"): a prospect list's filters and rows, the `candidate` tool and the candidate page answer what the newest results say as soon as they are recorded, while the judgments say what the last judge saw. An enrich that counted a derived result or an `https`, `http` or `none` website result starts a judge unless a judge is running (`D06-passes`), so the website piece and the website fact seldom disagree for long. A `website_search` pass starts no judge (website search decision 1, `D06-passes`), and needs none: a site it finds, or the `none` it sets when it finds nothing, moves the website facts at the next read and no judgment.
- **A site the search found gives no evidence** (website search decision 12): the `website_live` piece reads the check alone, so a company whose only site is the search's judges as one with none, while its website facts read the found site and the `website` filter admits it (website search decision 9). Evidence from a searched site, with its own source word and weight, is deferred until the first prospect list's results show how often the agent is right; adding it is a new rules version.
- **The piece is withheld by source, not granted by it**: the condition is a source other than the search, not a source equal to the check, so a facts row a release before the website search wrote, whose source is empty, keeps its piece. The migration that adds the source sets it to the check on every row holding a site (`D04-store`), so only a row the previous release writes after a rollback is empty.
- **During a rollback the previous release's judge counts a search-found site** as live evidence until it re-projects that row from the check alone, which drops the site; the rollback's limits are `D04-store`'s known limits.
- **Rules version 5 was kept by the website search** (website search decision 12): no existing candidate's evidence changed, since only search-found sites, new in that release, are left out, and those give the evidence the candidate had without them (see Rules version 9, which records the history).
- **Rules version 6** (revenue bands decision 15): the judge writes other revenue opinions from the same inputs, so the version moves; operating confidence is unchanged under it.
- **The website states change kept the rules version** (website states outcome 6): a check that found nothing now leaves the website `unknown` where it read `none`, and a search that found nothing now reads `none` where it left the website as it was; neither ever gave the `website_live` piece, so every candidate's evidence is what it was and the judge writes the same opinions from the same inputs.
- **Rules version 7** (Form 5500 decision 21): a reading's keys changed for every source and a new source joined, so every row must be read again; the version moves so a full ingest under it rewrites every link's keys and the judgments it ends with carry the new number.
- **Rules version 8** (OSHA ITA P2): a new source adds evidence and a headcount the judge reads, and the judge sums sites, so the judge writes other opinions; the version moves so the full ingest that lands it re-reads every row under one version and the judgments it ends with carry the new number.
- **Rules version 9** (headcount trend decision 14): the judge writes new opinions, the series and the trend, from the same inputs, so the version moves; nothing a source reads or an enricher derives changes, so a judge over everything lands it and no full ingest is needed.
- **The series is the pick's contenders, per kind per year** (headcount trend decisions 1 to 3): the site sums, the repeat rule and the qualifying counts are the pick's, so a year's OSHA figure on the card equals what the pick would sum for that year, and a row that gives no headcount to the pick gives no figure. Within a year the greater count wins, then the first given, whatever the dates within the year (headcount trend decision 1: where two headcounts of one kind still share a year, the larger is the figure): a year is the series' unit, so two Form 5500 plan years ending in one calendar year, or two PPP loans of one year, give the larger, and the series reads the same whatever order the rows were written in. This departs from the pick, which keeps the newest date of a kind before the greater count; the pick answers what the business is today, the series what it was in each year.
- **An undated headcount is not in the series**: it has no year, and the pick's rule that an undated one is the oldest would invent one.
- **A figure's year is its date's year in UTC**: the readers date a headcount at UTC midnight, so the year is the one the source gives, whatever the server's zone.
- **The series names the newest figure's scraper**: each kind has one reader today, so a series has one scraper; were a second scraper to give a kind, the newest figure's names the row, as the pick's basis names the kept headcount's.
- **No series is an empty list, never absent**: a reader of the judgments, the store's column included, always finds the list, and an empty one means no yearly figure.
- **Drivers and snippets are not in the series** (headcount trend decision 2 and its Rejected entry): a driver count is a floor dated by the carrier, not a yearly count; it stays the pick's last resort.
- **The trend is within one kind, the first in the series' order that has a comparison year** (headcount trend decision 5 and its Rejected entry on comparing across kinds): the series' order is the ranking, so the best kind with a comparison decides; a cross-kind trend for a business with one year of each kind is deferred until a correction for the known bias between participants and employees is proven.
- **The comparison year is N−3, then N−4, then N−2** (headcount trend decision 6 and its Rejected entry on the year before only): three years is the span the owner reads; four admits a gap year such as Heggies Pizza's 2022; two is the least that is not noise; a figure older than the window says nothing about today. Fitting a line through the series is rejected as harder to explain than two figures and a change.
- **The thresholds are named constants equal to the owner's document** (headcount trend decisions 7 and 8): their values live in the declaration (`D01-layout-and-run-seam`) and nowhere else in the design, so a change to the owner's table is one re-minted declaration, a new rules version and a judge over everything. The ratio has an absolute floor (its Rejected entry): a shop of five going to six is not growing.
- **The trend's arithmetic is exact**: a fifth is the divisor times the difference compared with the comparison figure, so exactly a fifth counts and no rounding moves an edge, computed without overflow as the range comparison is; the change saturates at the `int64` bounds, which no count from a series can reach.
- **An unknown trend has no basis**: its kind is empty and its years, figures and change are absent, so a prospect list ordered by trend puts it last in either direction (`D14-prospect-lists`), and a PPP-only business, whose two years are one apart, reads unknown (the decisions document's Open entry).
- **During a rollback to the release before version 9** that release's judge writes its judgments without the series and trend columns: a candidate it judges keeps the series and trend this release last wrote, and one it inserts takes the defaults, no series and unknown, until the roll forward judges again; the rollback's limits are `D04-store`'s known limits.
- **Deferred from the series and trend** (the decisions document's Deferred): job postings as a growth source, hours worked as a size and trend signal, a trend per site of a multi-site business, and the trend as an input to the brief or on the dashboard.
- **Sites of one year are summed, per kind, not per scraper** (OSHA ITA outcome 12, OSHA ITA P6): `employees` is the people employed at one establishment, so two sites of one business count different people and their sum is the business's headcount; the largest site alone would read Design Ready Controls as 283 instead of 523 (534 with the Champlin site under its second EIN; the document's Rejected entry). The rule is written for the kind, since the judge's rules name no scraper, and only `osha_ita` gives `employees` today. `participants` keeps the greater count, since a sponsor's several plans count the same people, and `jobs` and `drivers` have no sites. The sum is the judge's: `link_facts` keeps one entry per link, so the facts row and the card show each site, and the estimate's facts show the sum.
- **One scraper and one date make a group**: an OSHA summary's headcount is dated the last day of its filing year, so the sites of one year share a date, and an older year's sites never join a newer year's. Sites under a second EIN are another candidate until the owner merges them (Design Ready Controls' Champlin site), and a merge brings their links, which then sum. An undated headcount, whose year did not read, is never grouped: no year means no evidence the two are of one year, so each stands alone and the greater counts.
- **A duplicate is an equal count with equal hours, both given** (OSHA ITA outcome 12): a company that enters one site twice gives two summaries with the same employees and the same hours, 184 to 350 such pairs a year, and counting both would double the business (Rosenbauer Minnesota 714 instead of 357). Two sites with the same count and different hours are two sites. Two with the same count and no hours are kept as two, since a missing figure is never equal to another, and hours are given on nearly every row. Dropping the later of a pair keeps the sum independent of which establishment id came first.
- **A sum's hours are given only when every site counted gives them**: a partial sum beside a full count would misstate the hours per employee. The hours reach the estimate's facts for the reader and move no number the judge writes; hours as a size signal of their own are deferred.
- **The sum saturates** at the largest `int64`, as the headcount estimate's products do; no plausible count comes near it.
- **During a rollback to the release before version 8** that release's judge keeps the greatest single site of a year, not the sum, until the roll forward judges again; the rollback's limits are `D04-store`'s known limits.
- **The judge no longer copies facts** (funnel rename decisions 12 and 13): until this release a judge copied the facts row into the stored judgments for the release before and for a merged candidate. The judgments now live in their own table holding the judgment fields alone (`D04-store`, migration `0017_prospect_lists.sql`), the old table stays for the previous release and goes stale after the upgrade (`D04-store`, Known limits), and a merged candidate answers its last judgments and no facts.
- **Unranged now means no revenue**: a judge counts the candidates it judged whose revenue low is absent; missing minutes no longer matter to any count.
- **A candidate not yet judged is in no prospect list**: with no judgments there is nothing to filter or order (`D14-prospect-lists`); a full ingest or the next judge gives it judgments, even when its facts row is already written.
- **Judge ignores ids it cannot judge**: an id naming no candidate or a merged one is skipped, not refused, since a ruling may merge a candidate between a pass's work and its judge.
- **A judge touches no prospect list, no reject, no enrichment and no facts row**: what a prospect list answers changes at its next read because the judgments or the facts changed, never because a judge wrote the prospect list, and only enrichers write the facts.
- **A merged candidate keeps its estimates and last judgments** as they stood at the merge and is never judged again; its facts row is deleted, so it answers no facts; the survivor's are recomputed; prospect lists answer active candidates only.
- **The judge is walked by its caller in pages, not written in one transaction** (decisions log P15, P17, `shape/`'s Bounded work): `JudgeAll(ctx, after, n, at)` judges one page, at most `n` active candidates above the cursor `after` in id order, in one query and one transaction, stamping `at`, and answers its cursor; the store holds no page size of its own. The judge pass reads its judge time once, passes `passes.BatchSize` and that `at` to every page, and walks by id cursor until the cursor comes back empty, each page its own query and transaction, so no statement, transaction or open cursor spans the space and no call holds more than its page; a busy page is retried by the store (`D04-store`). It is not atomic across the space (P15(a), accepted): a web page or tool read mid-judge may mix new judgments with the last judge's, and a judge that fails keeps the pages it wrote, which the next judge rewrites. Earlier versions: one write transaction over the whole space, then (P15) the store walking windows of 500 inside one `JudgeAll` with a judge time it read itself. Why: on `us`, the one transaction held the write lock for 48 to 67 seconds while litestream's checkpoint waited on it against appkit's 5 second busy timeout, and the full ingest failed `store` three times; and a store-side walk still held the space's work in one call, where only disk and wall time may grow with the data. Because the caller passes `at`, one judge's candidates share one `Calculated` whatever the store's clock answers between pages, and a test tells a page from the whole by the cursor and what is left alone (R-NMZK-A971, `D04-store`'s page requirements). `Judge` by ids stays one transaction.
- **`JudgeAll` no longer promises the `candidate_cargo` repair** (cohorts log 30): its one observable was the cargo filter of the interim ranked list, which is gone, and cargo is not a prospect list filter. Whether the table is still kept is `D04-store`'s; it is a finding to drop it in the next release.
- **The old ranking fields are gone**: confidence, base, effective, the combined number and its key, filed and exclusions, which version 3 left empty, were dropped by the cohorts release's migration (`D04-store`, cohorts decision 16).
