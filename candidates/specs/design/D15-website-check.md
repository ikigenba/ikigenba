# D15-website-check

The website check: the one fetched kind of enrichment in the registry `D16-enrichers` declares, which finds out, for every active candidate whose display email is on its own domain, whether that domain serves a website, and keeps each answer as a result on the candidate. This document owns the package `internal/website` whole (the fetcher seam, the four attempts, the result, the pace and bounds, the real fetcher and the in-process stub), which fetcher `cli.Run` checks through, what `enrichers.WebsiteCheck`'s `Ready` and `Fetch` do (its declaration, `Kind`, `Inputs` and `Valid` are `D16-enrichers`'), and the website part of an `enrich` pass: which candidates it checks, how it batches them, how it fetches each domain once, what it records and what the counts `due`, `fetched`, `https`, `http` and `none` mean. The records, the due query, the facts row and the reads and writes of results are `D04-store`'s (`store.Enrichment`, `store.Due`, `DueEnrichments`, `AddEnrichments`, `Enrichments`, `EnrichmentMark`, `EnrichmentAfter`); the pass's lifecycle, its refusals, which kinds it walks and in what order, the derived kinds' work and the `derived` count, what starts it and the judge it starts are `D06-passes`'; `cli.Process`, its `HTTP` and `After` fields and the keys `Run` looks up are `D01-layout-and-run-seam`'s; the `enrich` tool is `D10-tools`'; how a result reaches the facts row's `website` and `website_url` is `D04-store`'s, and what a judge makes of them (`website_live`) `D13-scoring`'s; how the sandbox shows that nothing outside was fetched is `D11-on-a-space`'s.

## Who is checked, and when

A candidate's **checked domain** is `website.Domain` of its display email: `sources.DomainKey`'s key, the one `D12-resolution` blanks for the free-provider list, with its trailing dots removed, and blank again when what is left is a free provider. A blank domain is never checked and never counted. Only the display email's domain is checked, even when a candidate's links carry other own-domain emails (decisions document, decision 1; log P12): the key is derived in Go at pass time, with no column of its own on the candidate, and kept on each result as its inputs.

The checked domain is the website check's **inputs** (`D16-enrichers`; enrichers decisions 2 and 3). A candidate is **due** for the check when its checked domain is not blank and it has no `website_check` result, or its newest result names other inputs. There are no refresh ages: a result is never fetched again because it is old, so a site checked 400 days ago on the domain the candidate still shows is not due, and a candidate whose display email moves to another domain is due at the next pass and gains a second result, the first kept. The `enrich` tool no longer takes `older_than_days`. The query that finds them is `store.DueEnrichments` (`D04-store`), which answers each due candidate with its inputs, in id order past a cursor.

Migration `0012` backfills `inputs` from `source` on every existing result, since the domain was and is the only input, so no result release-43 recorded is fetched again after the deploy; a candidate whose display email moved to another domain since its last check is the exception, and is fetched, which is the inputs rule working.

## The check

A check of a domain `d` makes up to four **attempts**, in order: `https://d`, `https://www.d`, `http://d`, `http://www.d` (decision 3). Each is a `GET` carrying `website.UserAgent`, a browser-like user agent whose words are the source's, and follows up to `website.Redirects` (5) redirects, a 301, 302, 303, 307 or 308 with a `Location`. The first attempt that ends in a 200 whose body is HTML and not empty wins, and no later attempt is made. Any other end fails the attempt: another status, an empty body, a body that is not HTML, a sixth redirect, a redirect to a scheme other than `http` or `https`, an address the fetcher refuses, a connection or DNS failure, or the attempt's timeout, `website.Timeout` (10 seconds), measured on the checker's `After`. Four failures give `none`.

A body is read up to `website.BodyCap` (262,144 bytes) and no further; what was read is what is judged and what `Size` reports, so a page over the cap still counts. A body is HTML when its `Content-Type`'s media type is `text/html` or `application/xhtml+xml`, or, when the header is absent, when `http.DetectContentType` of the bytes read says `text/html`. The title is the text of the document's first `title` element, its invalid UTF-8 replaced and its surrounding white space trimmed, parsed with `golang.org/x/net/html`: Go's standard library has no HTML parser and a hand-rolled scan mis-parses real pages (log P5). Adding `golang.org/x/net` to `go.mod` is decided by the delivery, so the build may require it.

The **state** is the scheme of the URL landed on after redirects, not of the URL tried, so an `http` site that redirects to `https` is `https` (decision 4). A redirect to another domain is accepted and the landed URL is what is kept, so the owner can see where it went.

## The result

`website.Result` is what a check found (decision 5): the state (`sources.WebsiteHTTPS`, `sources.WebsiteHTTP` or `sources.WebsiteNone`, `D01-layout-and-run-seam`); the landed URL, empty for `none`; the title, empty when there is none; the final status of the last attempt, 0 when no response came; the body bytes read from the last attempt's 200, 0 otherwise; and the last failed attempt's error, empty on success. A failure's error is one of six copy constants, `FailStatus`, `FailEmpty`, `FailType`, `FailTimeout`, `FailRedirects` and `FailRefused`, or, for a connection, DNS or protocol failure, the transport's own text.

The pass records it as a `store.Enrichment` (`D04-store`) with the kind `sources.WebsiteCheck`, the domain as both its inputs and its source, what `json.Marshal` makes of the `website.Result` as what it found, a cost of 0 and the store's time. A reader decodes the found bytes back into a `website.Result` with `json.Unmarshal`; rows release-43 recorded carry more members, which it ignores (`D16-enrichers`). The store projects the newest result into the facts row's `website` and `website_url` in the same write (`D04-store`), so a cohort sees a fresh result at once and the judge's opinions follow at the judge the pass starts (`D06-passes`).

## The fetcher seam

`website.Checker` is the seam (decision 8): its `Client` is the `*http.Client` every attempt goes through and its `After` the timer every wait of the check ends on, each attempt's timeout and the pace between checks. A test hands it a client over a `RoundTripper` of its own and an `After` whose channels it holds, so no test reaches the network or waits on the clock. `Check` checks one domain; `CheckAll` checks many, at most `website.Concurrency` (8) at once and their starts at least `website.Interval` (500 ms, about two a second) apart, one wait of `After(Interval)` between two starts, a domain's own attempts one after another (decision 7; log P1). That is polite per host, and `CheckAll` calls back once per finished check and never after its context is done.

`enrichers.WebsiteCheck` wraps a checker for the registry (`D16-enrichers`): it is ready when it holds a checker, and its `Fetch` checks the inputs it is handed through `CheckAll`, at `CheckAll`'s pace and bound, calling back for each finished check with the domain as the source, the marshalled result as what was found and a cost of 0. The engine hands it each batch's unchecked domains at once and leaves the pace to it.

`website.Client()` is the real fetcher, which the binary hands `cli.Run` as `Process.HTTP` (`D01-layout-and-run-seam`). It takes no proxy from the environment: a proxy read there would be an environment read outside `LookupEnv`. Its dialer refuses, after resolution, every address that is loopback, private, link-local or unspecified, each refusal a failed attempt with `FailRefused`, so a public name that resolves inward, a redirect to one, or a rebinding cannot make the service fetch from the host or its network (log P6). `Check` itself refuses a redirect to another scheme.

## The stub

`website.NewStub` starts the in-process loopback stand-in the sandbox fetches from (log P2, revised): two listeners on `127.0.0.1:0`, one plain and one TLS, whose client sends every connection for port 443 to the TLS listener and for port 80 to the plain one, whatever the host, resolves no name, and refuses a connection the table below refuses with a dial error before any traffic. For HTTPS the stub makes one self-signed certificate at start for a fixed name; its client trusts only that certificate and pins `ServerName` to it, which `net/http` keeps when it is set, so every host verifies without `InsecureSkipVerify` (gosec G402 is a gate and suppression is forbidden). There is no proxy. The stub's servers log nothing, through the `log` package or to standard error: `D03-serve` allows no line on standard error beyond those it states, and a handshake cut short by a cancelled attempt is ordinary here.

The stub answers by keyword. For a request host `h` (lower case, no port), let `b` be `h` with one leading `www.` removed; a keyword `k` applies when `b` ends in `.test` (RFC 2606 reserves it) and `b`'s first label is `k` or `k-` followed by anything, so `secure.test`, `secure-2.test` and `secure-a.b.test` are all `secure`. A page is a 200 with `Content-Type: text/html; charset=utf-8` whose title is `h`.

- `secure`: a page for every request, either scheme, `b` or `www.b`.
- `plain`: port 443 refused; `http://b` a page.
- `prefixed`: every connection to `b` refused; `www.b` a page on either scheme.
- `upgrade`: `https://b/` and `https://www.b/` a 404; `http://b/` a 301 to `https://b/home`, which is a page.
- `away`: every request a 302 to `https://secure.test/`.
- `missing`: every request a 404 with an HTML body; `empty`: a 200 `text/html` with no body; `binary`: a 200 `application/octet-stream` of 1,024 bytes.
- `refused`, and every host no keyword names: every connection refused.
- `slow`: accepts and answers nothing until the request is cancelled.
- `big`: a page padded to 300,000 bytes.
- `loop`: every request a 302 to its own URL.

The default is refused, so the sandbox's own scrape data gains no evidence, touches no network and keeps the confidences the existing sandbox checks expect. Two candidates on one domain use one keyword domain, such as `secure-shared.test`.

## Choosing the fetcher

`Run` checks through the stub when `LookupEnv` reports `IKIGENBA_SANDBOX` present, whatever its value: every sandbox sets it and no production host does (log P2, revised), so nothing a sandbox runs fetches an outside site, not its exercises and not a pass it starts on its own, and no sandbox or manifest change is needed. Otherwise it checks through `Process.HTTP` when that is not nil, and otherwise it has no checker: `enrichers.WebsiteCheck` is not ready, so `enrich` with `kind` `website_check` answers `unavailable`, and an enrich without a kind, the one an ingest starts included, runs the derived kinds and skips the website check (`D06-passes`). So every existing and future `Run`-level test stays offline unless it hands in a client, and `IKIGENBA_SANDBOX` wins over `HTTP`. The checker's waits end on `Process.After`, told apart from the sweep's by their durations. `Run` starts the stub before ready and closes it at the stop, after the drain and before the database (`D03-serve`). A stub that cannot start, which needs the loopback listen or the key generation to fail, refuses the start with `candidates: ` and the error, exit 1, before `service.started` is recorded, as a database that will not open does; no requirement states it, since no permitted test can make either fail.

When the drain gives up at its deadline and `Run` returns with an enrich's work still unwinding, that work's context is already done (`D06-passes` cancels it at once), so it records nothing more, and any fetch it still makes through a closed stub is a dial error, a failed attempt that reaches nothing; that is acceptable, since its result is never written.

## The enrich pass's work

The website part of an enrich pass is the same whatever started it and whatever else it runs: alone, for an `enrich` call with `kind` `website_check`, or after every derived kind has finished, for a call without a kind and for the enrich an ingest starts after it ends `done` (`D06-passes`). It reads `store.EnrichmentMark` when it begins, then takes the due set in keyset batches of `passes.BatchSize` by candidate id, each batch read with `DueEnrichments` for `sources.WebsiteCheck` after the last id of the batch before (log P8, revised; technical choice 4). One batch is held at a time. A candidate with a blank checked domain has empty inputs, so it is never due, selected or counted. The batch's candidates are grouped by inputs; a domain for which `EnrichmentAfter(sources.WebsiteCheck, domain, mark)` finds a result recorded with those inputs was already checked by this pass in an earlier batch, and that result's source, found and cost are written again for this batch's candidates without a fetch; the batch's other domains are handed once to `Fetch`, and each result is written with `AddEnrichments` to every candidate of its group as soon as its check ends. So each distinct domain is fetched once per pass, within a batch by the grouping and across batches by the store, with no in-memory set of domains and no time window (decision 6; technical choice 5). Because the cursor only moves forward, a candidate is never selected twice in one pass; and since a fresh result names the inputs it was fetched for, a candidate is not due again until its domain changes, whatever its state. A candidate the due query reaches above the cursor while the pass runs is selected like any other.

The pass's counts, recorded when it ends `done` (`D06-passes`): `due`, the candidates the pass selected for the website check; `fetched`, the distinct domains the pass checked, a domain whose result `EnrichmentAfter` found not counting; and `https`, `http` and `none`, the website results written by the state their found bytes decode to, which sum to the website results written, at most `due`, since `AddEnrichments` skips a candidate merged meanwhile (`D04-store`). Results a derived kind writes are counted in `derived`, which is `D06-passes`', and never in these; a pass that skips the website check has all five 0. A second pass at once finds nothing due and writes nothing, and so does one a year later while no domain has changed (decision 9; enrichers decision 3).

## Memory

The pass must hold no more than the fetches in flight and one batch of candidates, however many are due (decision 7; log 36, as `D04-store` records it). The bound is proved by the heap the process has in use while the pass runs: a test calls `runtime.GC()` before an `enrich` of the website kind alone and, from its own transport, calls `runtime.GC()` and reads `HeapInuse` with `runtime.ReadMemStats` while handling requests, at a due set of one batch and at one of twenty batches, and every reading stays under 6 MiB at both. The heap after the pass would prove nothing about the pass itself (root ruling R1). A probe over today's store, under `go test -race`, measured 2.0 to 2.3 MiB in use after a collection with 500 to 10,000 candidates stored, and 9.8 MiB once the 10,000 were held as `store.Candidate`s (about 780 bytes each, before any result), so 6 MiB fails a pass that holds the twenty batches' candidates and leaves one batch, eight fetches and the engine about 3.7 MiB of room. The transport's pages carry titles of about a kilobyte, made afresh per request, so a pass that held every result, about 10 MB of titles alone at 10,000, fails as well, while one batch's results are about half a megabyte. A pass that held only every domain's string, about a megabyte at 10,000, would pass; that limitation is accepted, and the design forbids it in prose here and by technical choice 5. The test seeds the store through `Attach` a batch at a time and keeps no reference to what it seeded, makes no other call while the pass runs, and does not call `t.Parallel`.

## Known limits

- **No outage guard, and no refresh** (log P9; enrichers decision 3). If the host's network is down during a pass, every check fails and every candidate due gets `none`; with no refresh age that `none` stands until the candidate's domain changes, since no later pass fetches it again and a rebuild never fetches. A site that comes up or goes down after its check is not noticed either. Re-running a kind for a cohort or one candidate is the manual enrichment change that follows this one.
- **Parked pages are not detected** (decisions document, decision 5; log, Exclusions). A parked or placeholder page answers 200 HTML and is `https` or `http`; its title and size are kept so it can be spotted from the card.

## Recorded decisions

- **One package owns the check** (technical choice 3): fetch, check, pace, the real fetcher and the stub are `internal/website`, declared whole here. The vocabulary (`sources.WebsiteCheck` and the four states) lives in `sources`, the records in `store` and the enricher that wraps the checker in `enrichers` (`D16-enrichers`), beside their siblings (technical choice 1), so `store` stays free of HTTP.
- **Due by inputs, with no refresh ages** (enrichers decision 3): `RefreshLive`, `RefreshNone` and `older_than_days` are gone, and a result is redone only when its inputs change. The website check is the expensive kind, and the 30- and 90-day refreshes would have refetched the corpus for nothing; the cost is the outage limit above.
- **The engine hands `Fetch` a batch's unchecked domains at once**: the pace and the bound on checks in flight are `CheckAll`'s, inside `Fetch`, so the engine keeps no chunking of its own and the memory bound is unchanged.
- **The checker reuses `Process.After`** (technical choice 10), told apart by duration (`cli.SweepInterval`, `website.Interval`, `website.Timeout`), instead of a new field; pace is per domain check start, a domain's attempts sequential (technical choice 11).
- **`Process.HTTP` nil means no checker** (technical choice 9), and the binary passes `website.Client()`: tests stay offline by default, and with no checker the website check alone is unavailable while the derived kinds still run.
- **The real fetcher's guard sits in the dialer, after DNS** (technical choice 14), so it covers redirects and rebinding, and it includes the unspecified addresses, which reach the host.
- **`Domain` re-applies the free-provider blank after removing the trailing dots.** `sources.DomainKey("x@gmail.com.")` is `gmail.com.`, which the list does not hold; removing the dot alone would check Gmail, which decision 1 forbids. `Domain` is therefore `DomainKey` of `@` and the key with every trailing dot removed, so `gmail.com..` is blank too. P7's lower-casing is `DomainKey`'s already. Its "identical attempt URLs dedupe" is met by the domain: the four attempt URLs of one domain always differ, and candidates whose emails differ only in case, surrounding space or trailing dots share one domain and so one fetch.
- **Departure: the stub writes markup of its own** (root `AGENTS.md`, Web assets, and candidates' `AGENTS.md`, Assets: "code never writes markup of its own"; technical choice 27). The stub's bodies are fixture bytes standing in for a foreign site, read by the checker and never shown to a person or served by candidates' routes; they must ship in the binary, since the sandbox runs the release's binary, and in `assets/` they would read as candidates' own pages. They are the source's, and no requirement spells them beyond the title the stub's table states.
- **Departure: candidates now fetches outside the suite and has a second dependency.** candidates' `AGENTS.md` says it calls no service outside the suite, needs no outbound network, and has appkit and what it pulls in as its only dependencies. This design supersedes those lines: on a host without `IKIGENBA_SANDBOX` the real fetcher reaches every checked domain on the public internet, and `golang.org/x/net` is required for the title parse (log P5). Live tests stay none: no test reaches outside, and the sandbox fetches only the stub. The root reports the `AGENTS.md` lines to change.
- **The stub's HTTPS pins `ServerName` over a self-signed root** (technical choice 15), not `InsecureSkipVerify`, and its default is refuse (technical choice 16).
- **The tuning numbers are taken as given** (log P1; technical choice 28): about an hour for 7,000 domains at two a second.
- **The memory bound is a reading during the pass** (root ruling R1), not the heap after it, and not `TotalAlloc`, which grows with the work done and so with the due set however little is held.

## Consumer tasks

`Run` builds the checker once at start: `s, err := website.NewStub()` and `&website.Checker{Client: s.Client(), After: p.After}` when `p.LookupEnv("IKIGENBA_SANDBOX")` reports present, closing `s.Close()` at the stop; `&website.Checker{Client: p.HTTP, After: p.After}` when `p.HTTP` is not nil; nil otherwise; and hands it to `passes.New(passes.Config{…, Website: k})`, whose engine builds `enrichers.New(k)`. `main` sets `HTTP: website.Client()`.

The website part of the enrich work: `en, _ := reg.Get(sources.WebsiteCheck)` and `f := en.(enrichers.Fetcher)`, skipped when `!f.Ready()`; `mark, err := st.EnrichmentMark(ctx)`; then, `after` starting empty, `due, err := st.DueEnrichments(ctx, sources.WebsiteCheck, after, passes.BatchSize)` until empty, each a `store.Due{Candidate, Inputs}`, grouped by `Inputs`; per inputs `e, err := st.EnrichmentAfter(ctx, sources.WebsiteCheck, in, mark)`, its `Source`, `Found` and `Cost` reused when found; the rest handed to `f.Fetch(ctx, unchecked, func(in string, r enrichers.Result) { st.AddEnrichments(ctx, es) })`, each `store.Enrichment{Candidate: id, Kind: sources.WebsiteCheck, Inputs: in, Source: r.Source, Found: r.Found, Cost: r.Cost}`; and the counts decode each result `AddEnrichments` answers with `var w website.Result; json.Unmarshal(x.Found, &w)` and count `w.State`.

A test of the check: `k := &website.Checker{Client: &http.Client{Transport: rt}, After: after}`, `rt` a `RoundTripper` of its own answering by URL and recording each request, `after` handing out channels it holds; `r := k.Check(ctx, "acme.test")`, compared field by field and `r.Error` with `website.FailStatus` and its siblings. A test of the stub: `s, err := website.NewStub()`, `defer s.Close()`, `k := &website.Checker{Client: s.Client(), After: after}`, `k.Check(ctx, "upgrade.test")`. A test of the enricher: `enrichers.WebsiteCheck{Checker: k}.Fetch(ctx, []string{"acme.test"}, func(in string, r enrichers.Result) { … })`, decoding `r.Found` into a `website.Result`, and `enrichers.WebsiteCheck{}.Ready()`. A test of the pass builds an engine with `Website: k`, seeds candidates with `Attach`, calls `eng.Enrich(ctx, c, sources.WebsiteCheck)`, polls `st.GetPass` and reads `st.Enrichments`, decoding each `Found` into a `website.Result`.
