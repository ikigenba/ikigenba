# D12-ingest

Ingest: how rows become candidates and how the owner's rulings move them (decisions 4, 5, 6 and 10, form5500 decisions 14, 15 and 21, OSHA ITA outcomes 6, 10 and 12 and OSHA ITA P2 and P11, and estimate inversion outcomes 1, 3 and 4 and estimate inversion log D1). It owns the normalized keys a reading carries (`sources.NameKey`, `sources.NameKeys`, `sources.EINKey`, `sources.PhoneKey`, `sources.DomainKey`, `sources.County`, `sources.SourceKey`), the **match key**, a candidate's **display** (the fields and tags it shows, its name, address, phone, email and people chosen from its records by the orders `D05-sources` declares, each with the source and record it came from, and the routed address that may lead the address), the **rebuild** of every candidate's display that a full ingest makes (`store.Rebuild`), the ingest side of an owner record (owner facts outcomes 6 and 7, owner facts decisions 4 and 5): which candidate `store.AddOwnerRecord` records one on when it names none, by the matching rules, and what the candidate shows and records after it, and the three store writes that change which candidate a row belongs to: `store.Attach` (the attach rule), `store.MarkGone` (a row absent from its scraper's latest run) and `store.Decide` (the five rulings, made through the `decide`, `exclude` and `include` tools), with what a `merge` or a `split` does to the enrichment results, facts rows, pages and campaign uses of the candidates it touches: it re-runs the derived kinds for them, and a `merge` moves the other's pages to the survivor while a `split` leaves them where they are (site text decision 1), and a `merge` moves the other's campaign uses to the survivor, combining two uses of one campaign into one, while a `split`, an `exclude` and an `include` leave them where they are (campaigns decisions 3 and 17, campaigns log D1). The records and their shapes, the enrichment results, the facts row, the pages and the campaign uses (`store.CampaignUse`, recorded from campaigns' events by `store.AddCampaignUse`, `store.RejectCampaignUse` and `store.DeliverCampaignUse` and read by `store.CampaignUses`) are `D04-store`'s; what each enricher names as its inputs and computes is `D16-enrichers`'; what a source reads from a row is `D05-sources`'; what a judge writes, and the effect of `exclude` and `include` on the judgments, are `D13-judging`'s; the engine that calls `Attach` and `MarkGone` is `D06-passes`'; the `decide`, `exclude` and `include` tools are `D10-tools`'; which candidates a prospect list holds, an excluded one included, is `D14-prospect-lists`'. "Enrichers decision N" is decision N of the decisions document that delivered the enrichers; "site text decision N" is decision N of the one that delivered the `site_text` kind and the pages; "brief decision N" is decision N of the one that delivered the `brief` kind; "category evidence decision N" is decision N of the one that made a company's good brief decide its categories; "website search decision N" is decision N of the one that delivered the `website_search` kind, and "S N" is entry DN of that delivery's decisions log; "website states outcome N" is outcome N of the one that made `none` mean that a search found nothing; "form5500 decision N" is candidates decision N of the decisions document that delivered the `form5500` source; "OSHA ITA outcome N" is outcome N of the decisions document that delivered the `osha_ita` source, and "OSHA ITA P N" is entry P N of that delivery's decisions log; "estimate inversion outcome N" is outcome N of the decisions document of the estimate-inversion change, which brings rules version 10, and "estimate inversion log DN" is entry DN of that change's decisions log; "empty sites decision N" is decision N of the delivery of the empty sites change, which runs the website search also for a company whose site on record gave site text no pages; "sources outcome N" is outcome N of the decisions document of the facts-from-the-candidate change, which takes every fact from the candidate's records by the owner's table of sources and precedence, and "sources P N" is entry P N of that delivery's decisions log; "owner facts outcome N" is outcome N of the decisions document of the owner-records change, by which the owner adds a business or sets a fact through an agent, "owner facts decision N" is decision N the user made for it, and "root ruling RN" is ruling RN of that change's design run.

## Candidates

A candidate is a record with an opaque, permanent id, `cand_` and 16 lowercase hex digits drawn from the random source when it is created, never derived from a name, an address or a DOT number (`D04-store` mints it). It is `active` or `merged`, with the id it was merged into; it is never deleted, its id never changes, and a merged candidate never becomes active again. The valid set is every candidate not merged; excluded is a flag on the judgments, so an excluded candidate is still valid (`D13-judging`).

An active candidate shows its **display**, which the orders choose (sources outcomes 1, 5 and 7, `D05-sources`). Its name, its address (street, city, state, ZIP and county, one source for all of them), its phone, its email and its people are what `sources.Pick` chooses by `sources.Orders()` over its **records**, one per link, and its **owner given records**, one per owner record (`D05-sources`), each with the source identifier and the record key it came from, and every other name, address, phone and email its records hold is kept beside them with its own source and record (R-FA1W-3DCK). The owner's value comes first in every order that ranks it (owner facts outcome 2), the newest owner record in force deciding, so an owner-founded candidate that holds no link still has a display, its owner records' alone; its EIN is the pick's too, which `D04-store` states for every candidate it answers. So a trade name is shown over a legal name, the walk's name first, then an FMCSA DBA, then a Form 5500 DBA or plan name, and the legal names newest filing first; a business location over a mailing address; the walk's phone over Form 5500's over FMCSA's; within one source the newest filing wins, and among records filed alike the one of least source id, never the one seen last, so re-reading a row that has not changed never moves what a candidate shows; and only links not gone count unless every one is gone, so a candidate whose every link is gone shows what its rows last said. A places link takes part like any other link, so a walk's name, address and phone lead their orders on a candidate it matched (sources P2). The address order's first slot after the owner's is the **routed address** (R-F8TZ-PLLV): when the candidate's newest `drive_route` result that did not fail and found a drive, found from its **route inputs**, those the drive is asked from to the **record-chosen address**, what the orders choose with the route slot skipped, an address the owner set and has not cleared included (`D05-sources`), so an owner address stops an older route standing until a `drive_route` pass is asked for again (owner facts decision 7), and the store's origin, placed the destination to the building and its resolved address reads as a street, a city, a two-letter state and a ZIP followed by `USA`, that address is the candidate's, its source `route`; otherwise the records decide. Since the route inputs are computed from the record-chosen address and never from the display, the route cannot feed itself. Its class tag and cargo tags are as before: the class from the first of its **giving links** that gives one, its cargo the union over its **tag links**, its links that are not places links when it has one; a places reading carries neither. Its `names` stay the union every earlier release wrote, which release-74 reads and `ListCandidates`' name filter matches: the display name first, then each tag link's name and other names. Every write that changes a candidate's links leaves its display current, and so does the rebuild; recording a `drive_route` result, and the facts walk after the origin changed, leave the display of the candidates they touch current too, which `D04-store` states; `updated` moves only when a field of the candidate changed. A merged candidate keeps the display fields and tags it had at the merge; only its links move.

## Keys

The **name key** is the name upper-cased, punctuation stripped, white space collapsed, and the legal suffixes `INC`, `LLC`, `CO`, `CORP`, `LTD` and `COMPANY` dropped from the end. A reading carries one name key per name it carries (form5500 decision 14): `sources.NameKeys` keys each of the reading's names in order, dropping an empty key and a repeat, so its first is usually the reading's `NameKey` (a link holds both its `NameKey` and its `NameKeys`, so matching tries either), and an FMCSA reading carries its DBA's key beside its legal name's, a PPP reading its one key, a Form 5500 reading the keys of its sponsor name, its DBA and its plan-derived name, and an OSHA ITA reading the key of its company name alone, or of its establishment name when the company name is empty; the establishment name is not a name key (OSHA ITA outcomes 6 and 10, OSHA ITA P11, `D05-sources`). The **EIN key** is the employer identification number when it is exactly nine ASCII digits and not all zeros, and none otherwise; `form5500` and `osha_ita` fill it. An OSHA ITA row that gives its EIN as eight digits, the leading zero dropped, is read as nine with a leading zero before it is keyed, so `10739420` keys as `010739420` (OSHA ITA outcome 10); that padding is the source's own (`D05-sources`), and `sources.EINKey` is the same for every source. The **match key** is the name key with the row's state and five-digit ZIP; a reading without all three has none, so a row with no name or no ZIP never joins another by name (under-merging is the intended failure). A row's **place** is its state and five-digit ZIP. The name keys a row **tries** are its `NameKeys`, or its `NameKey` alone when it has none; the name keys a link **holds** are its reading's `NameKey` together with its reading's `NameKeys`, so a link an earlier release wrote, whose reading has no `NameKeys`, still holds its `NameKey`; the EIN key a link holds is its reading's. City and street are evidence, not key. The phone key (digits, last ten), the domain key (the email's domain, lower-cased, none for a free provider) and the county (`MN-115`) are stored for later use and take no part in the attach rule; a walk's places attach rule matches by the phone and domain keys (`D19-walk`). The **source key** is `<scraper>:<source_id>`, how a split names the links it moves.

A candidate **holds** a key through its links and through its owner records (R-EXUW-9NXM): the EIN key of each link and of each owner given record, the name keys of each link at that link's place and each owner name at each owner place, and, for the owner's matching and a walk's alone, the phone and domain keys of each link and owner record. An owner value cleared is held no more, since only the owner values in force make the owner given records.

## The attach rule

`Attach` takes a batch of observations, rows as their source read them, and applies each in order, in one transaction (decision 5):

1. A link already holding the row's source key keeps the row on its candidate, whatever its keys now say. The row is **unchanged** when its digest is unchanged, or, when the attach is forced, as a full ingest forces it, when its new reading is byte for byte the reading the link stores (OSHA ITA P15): last seen moves, the run is recorded, a gone link returns, and the link's reading, digest and keys are not rewritten. Otherwise it is **changed**: the link takes the new reading and digest as well, and the keys that reading carries.
2. Otherwise the row is **new**, and it joins a candidate by one of its keys (form5500 decision 15):
   1. by EIN: when it has an EIN key and an active candidate holds the same EIN key, through one of its links, gone or not, or its owner records, the oldest such candidate takes it, by `ein` with confidence 0.9, whatever its name keys and place say;
   2. otherwise by name: when it has a place, for each name key it tries, in order, when an active candidate holding that name key at the same state and ZIP exists, through one of its links, gone or not, or its owner records, the oldest such candidate takes it, by `match_key` with confidence 0.8, the first name key that finds one deciding.

   A `not_same` ruling is honored at both: a row whose EIN key, or whose deciding name key, links of both of a ruled pair hold goes to the older, and the pair stays two candidates.
3. Otherwise a candidate is created for it, and the link that founds it attaches by `source_key` with confidence 1.0.

Each step reads by key, never the whole of the links (`D04-store`): the first step finds the row's link by its source key, the second the oldest active candidate holding the row's EIN key, through the links' index on EIN key, and otherwise, name key by name key, the oldest holding that name key at the row's place, through the links' name keys and their index on name key (`D04-store`); and the display of each candidate one of whose links the batch wrote is re-derived from that candidate's own links once, at the end of the batch, those links read in one query by the candidate's id and folded as read, so the read is bounded by the one candidate, the largest on the `us` space holding 3,224 links (`D04-store`, `shape/`'s Bounded work). Identical keys collapse at creation, since a later row of the same batch meets the candidate an earlier one created; so there are never more candidates than rows. A link moved by a ruling attaches by `decision` with confidence 1.0, and a link keeps how it came to its candidate until a ruling moves it. Matching reads only the links and the owner records: it never calls scrapes. So a scraped row that matches a candidate the owner founded links there with no confirmation, and the owner's values stay first in its display (owner facts decision 5, R-EZ2S-NFOB, R-F0AP-17F0). A batch the database answers busy, as when litestream's checkpoint holds the write lock, is tried again by the store after a pause, a few times, and a failed try changes nothing (`D04-store`); only when the tries are spent does `Attach` fail.

`MarkGone` marks gone every link the run a pass just read no longer holds, a page at a time (`D04-store`, `shape/`'s Bounded work): each call reads at most `n` of its scraper's links after its cursor, in source id order, marks those of them last seen in a run other than its run and not already gone, leaves the others of its page as they were, and answers the source id of the last link of its page as the next cursor, empty once none remain, all in one transaction; the caller walks it from an empty cursor with `passes.BatchSize` until the cursor comes back empty, so no statement, transaction or open cursor spans the scraper's links. A page may mark nothing, and its cursor may be a link it did not mark. A gone link is kept, with its last seen; it still counts for identity and no longer for the judgments (decision 6). In the same transaction `MarkGone` re-derives by key, from their own links, read in one query per candidate and folded as read, the display of the candidates holding a link the call marked, bounded by those candidates, since a link that is gone no longer leads the display or gives a field of it. It no longer answers those candidates' ids: they are the candidates holding a link it marked, which a caller that needs them reads by key. A row that returns is unmarked by `Attach`.

## Owner records

An owner record (`store.OwnerRecord`, `D04-store`) is written by `AddOwnerRecord`, through the `add_business` and `set_facts` tools (`D10-tools`). Its refusals, what it records and how the owner records are read are `D04-store`'s; the facts row after it is `D04-store`'s too (R-T59G-A7X3), and the judgments `D13-judging`'s. This document states the rest. A record naming a candidate is recorded on it. A record naming none runs the matching rules first (R-FHDA-DZSQ): by EIN, then by its name at its address's state and ZIP, then by its phone, then by its email's domain and its website's domain, each against what the active candidates hold through their links and their owner records, the oldest holder deciding at the first rule that finds one, as at `Attach`; and when none finds one, a candidate is created at once, holding no link, and the record founds it. Either way the write is synchronous, like `Decide` (root ruling R6): when it answers, the candidate's display is the pick over its links and its owner given records (R-FCHO-UWTY), the derived kinds whose inputs the record changed have been recorded again (R-0YH4-TCGZ), and no pass was started.

## The rebuild

The orders are data (`D05-sources`), and a candidate's display is a function of its links, its `drive_route` results, the store's origin and the orders; a new `drive_route` result and a changed origin reach the display at once (`D04-store`), and a change to the orders, and a member a release adds to the reading, reach every candidate through the **rebuild**: the full `ingest`, with no scraper named (sources outcome 7, sources P5). Its forced attach already rewrites every scraper link whose reading the rules change and re-derives the display of every candidate one of whose links its runs hold (R-WPDD-7660). That leaves the candidates no current run touches: a candidate the walk founded, whose links no scraper run carries, and one whose scraper has no current run. `store.Rebuild` reaches them all: it walks every active candidate a page at a time by id, as `FillFacts` does (`shape/`'s Bounded work), and for each candidate of its page rereads each places link's reading from its place record, the newest walk's record of that place, by `places.Reading`, as `RecordSweep` reads it, so a reading member a release adds reaches the walk's links too, and rewrites the candidate's display from its records and owner given records as they then are, a candidate the owner founded, holding no link, by its owner records alone, and one holding neither keeping its row as it is (R-2A55-HPMF). Candidates are never deleted, and a rebuild writes no owner record, so an owner-founded candidate keeps its id and its owner records through every rebuild (owner facts decision 5). Each candidate is read by key, its own links, its own `drive_route` results and its places links' place records, so a page's work is bounded by its candidates. A full ingest walks it after its runs' attaches and marking walks and before its forced derived walks, so `link_facts`, `drive_minutes` and every derived kind's inputs read the display the orders chose, and then judges (`D06-passes`). Nothing a rebuild writes depends on the time but `updated`, and the pick breaks every tie by the records' identity, never by when a link was last seen, the order links were attached in or any value a pass refreshes (`D05-sources`), so a candidate's display is a function of its links' readings and its owner records alone, the owner given records ranked as ordinary records by their owner order, and a second full ingest over the same runs changes nothing, whatever order it reads each run's rows in (R-SODA-05AS, R-1LR5-UASJ). For the same reason re-attaching an unchanged row of a link that is not gone, which refreshes only its link's last seen, leaves the candidate exactly as it was, `updated` included, so a reader during a pass never sees a value the pass later undoes (R-FG5E-0821); an unchanged row of a gone link clears its `Gone` and may change the display.

## Rulings

A ruling is made only by hand, through the `decide`, `exclude` and `include` tools (`D10-tools`), each of which calls `Decide`; nothing in the pipeline rules on its own. Each records its kind, the candidates involved, who, when and why, and is never changed or removed (decision 10).

- `merge` names the survivor and the other: the other's links, its enrichment results of every kind and its pages move to the survivor (`D04-store`), each page keeping its number and every other field, and its campaign uses move to the survivor in the same write, a campaign both hold becoming one use that keeps the earliest added time with its prospect list, the earliest rejection with its reason and the latest delivery; its facts row is dropped; the derived kinds are then run again for the survivor, from its links and display after the move, its facts row is rewritten from its newest results, and it is judged again; the other is marked `merged` into it and keeps everything else it had at the merge (display fields, tags, estimates and its last judgments).
- `split` names a candidate and the source keys to move out, a proper subset of its links: a new candidate is created for them, and the pair is recorded `not_same` in the same call, so matching never rejoins them. Its enrichment results, its pages and its campaign uses stay on it, so a fetched result such as the website check's, and every page an agent added, stays with the original and the new candidate has none until an enrich finds it due or an agent adds one; the derived kinds are then run again for both, from each one's own links and display, both facts rows are rewritten, and both are judged.
- `not_same` names two active candidates and changes nothing else.
- `exclude` and `include` name one candidate; the latest of the two on it stands (`D13-judging`). Each judges that candidate again, records no enrichment result and leaves its campaign uses as they were. An excluded candidate leaves every prospect list, unless a prospect list shows excluded candidates, and an `include` returns it (the exclusion rule, `D14-prospect-lists`).

A ruling on a merged candidate, a merge or `not_same` of a candidate with itself, a split naming keys the candidate does not hold or all of them, are refused `ErrInvalid`. Rulings are applied throughout and never overwritten: a pass or a full ingest moves no link a ruling moved, revives no merged candidate and changes no ruling, since the attach rule keeps every row on the candidate its link names. `Decide` judges the candidates it touches in the same call (`D13-judging`), so a ruling shows in every prospect list at once; nothing else in this document judges. The candidates it touches are those it answers: `r.Candidate`, `r.Other` where the kind takes one, and a split's new candidate. A `merge` or a `split` moves links, so it re-derives the display of the active candidates it answers, records one new result of each derived kind for each of them whose inputs are not empty (`D16-enrichers`), rewrites their facts rows and then judges them, all in the one write; an `exclude` or an `include` moves none, records no result, writes no facts row and judges `r.Candidate` alone; a `not_same` judges none and records nothing but its ruling (`D13-judging`). It reads and writes each candidate it touches by key (its record, its own links, its rulings, its enrichment results, its facts row, and the ZIP table's minutes for its display ZIP, which the drive-minutes kind reads), so its work is bounded by those candidates' links and results, each candidate's own links read in one query and folded as read, the largest candidate on the `us` space holding 3,224 links; it reads no table whole and leaves every other candidate as it was.

## Recorded decisions

- **Punctuation is removed, not spaced**: every rune that is not a letter, a digit or white space is dropped, so `J.B. HUNT` keys as `JB HUNT` and `SMITH-JONES` as `SMITHJONES`; a variant spelled with spaces then misses, which under-merging accepts. Suffixes drop repeatedly from the end while more than one word is left, so `ACME CO INC` keys as `ACME` and a business named `COMPANY` keeps its name.
- **A match key needs all three parts**, a non-empty name key, a state and a ZIP of five ASCII digits; the document's "name plus place" is read as all of the place. An empty name key never matches, as before: a row tries the non-empty elements of its `NameKeys` when it has one, or else its `NameKey` when that is not empty, which for every reading a source builds is the same as trying its `NameKeys`, or its `NameKey` when `NameKeys` is empty, since `sources.NameKeys` never answers an empty key.
- **The EIN step comes first and needs no place** (form5500 decision 15): one EIN is one employer, so two filings of one sponsor join after a move, a rename or across plans, whatever their ZIPs; 0.9 records that an EIN is stronger evidence than a name at a place (0.8) and weaker than the row's own source key (1.0). A row with an EIN key no link holds falls through to the name step, so a Form 5500 or OSHA ITA row still joins an FMCSA or PPP candidate, which hold no EIN key, by name, and an OSHA ITA row whose EIN a Form 5500 link holds joins that sponsor's candidate by EIN, whatever its site's name and ZIP.
- **A row tries its own keys; a link holds all of its keys.** The row's side is its `NameKeys` in order, so its first key, the legal or sponsor name, is tried before a DBA or a plan-derived name, and the first that finds a candidate decides even when a later one would find an older candidate; the link's side is every key its reading carries, its `NameKey` included, so a link written before a reading carried `NameKeys` still matches by its first key. Both sides keep "gone or not" at the name step as at the EIN step, as the match-key step had it: a gone link still counts for identity (decision 6).
- **Rules version 7 and the links that exist** (form5500 decision 21): every source's reading gained keys and a source joined, so `sources.Rules` becomes 7 and the next full ingest rereads every row and, attaching with force, rewrites every link's reading and keys, since every reading changed. Until that ingest runs on a space, a link written before carries an empty EIN key and no name key beyond its `NameKey`, so the new steps find nothing extra through it and the name step finds exactly what the match-key step did; that is safe, as under-merging is the intended failure. A link's stored keys are what `D04-store` keeps.
- **Several sites of one EIN are one candidate, with one link per row** (OSHA ITA outcomes 10 and 12): an OSHA ITA row is one establishment's summary for one year, with a source key of its own, so each row is its own link, and a site whose EIN key a link already holds joins that link's candidate at the EIN step, whatever its name and place say. So KTM Paving's rows across three establishment ids, with its EIN written in eight digits in some years and nine in others, are one candidate, its 2017 row, which carries no EIN since no row of 2016 to 2018 carries one, joining it by its name at its ZIP instead, and Design Ready Controls' sites under its main EIN, at different names and ZIPs, are one candidate with one link per site and per year; summing the sites' counts is the judge's (`D13-judging`), and the links stay one per row so the card shows each site. A site that files under another EIN meets by EIN only the links holding that EIN, and by name only those at its own place, so Design Ready Controls' Champlin site, under a second EIN, is its own candidate until the owner merges it (recorded, not fixed).
- **An EIN-less site matches by its company name at its place** (OSHA ITA outcome 10): a row without an EIN key, every row of 2016 to 2018 and about a tenth since, tries its name, the company name, at its state and ZIP as every row does, so a business that never gives an EIN joins across years only where its name and place stay the same. Molin Concrete is one candidate at its Lino Lakes ZIP, its rows joining by company name; its Ramsey site, at another ZIP through 2021, is a second candidate at that ZIP, which is accepted (recorded, not fixed): under-merging is the intended failure, and the owner can merge it.
- **The establishment id is not a key** (OSHA ITA outcome 10): it changes for a fifth to a quarter of employers each year and one business can hold three, so it is a fact the row keeps (`D05-sources`) and no step of the attach rule reads it. Joining EIN-less rows across years by establishment id, where their names and place did not join them, is deferred.
- **A site's name is not a name key** (OSHA ITA outcomes 6 and 10, OSHA ITA P11): an OSHA ITA reading's names hold only the row's name, the company name, so its company name alone keys it; the establishment name keys only when it is the row's name because the company name is empty, and otherwise it is shown, not matched (`Reading.Establishment`, `D05-sources`). scrapes publishes a row's names as its name then the establishment name when it differs, and this design first keyed both, accepting the risk as form5500 accepted plan-derived names. It diverges because the sandbox check found place-like site names keying unrelated businesses into one candidate at a ZIP: Molin Concrete's five `Ramsey` rows joined Forpak Automation's candidate with Multisource, Life Fitness, Cybex and others through names such as `Ramsey` and `Delavan`, and 795 candidates held OSHA ITA links under two or more EINs.
- **Rules version 8** (OSHA ITA P2): `osha_ita` joins the sources and adds evidence the judge reads, so `sources.Rules` becomes 8, and a full ingest rereads every row under one version and, attaching with force, rewrites every link whose reading the new rules change. No key rule of an existing source changes, and every row a link already holds keeps its candidate by its source key, so that ingest moves no link; on a space it runs before the first `osha_ita` run is ingested (`D11-on-a-space`).
- **Rules version 10** (estimate inversion outcomes 1, 3 and 4, estimate inversion log D1): FMCSA's reading stops giving a mixed carrier a truck estimate and the judge's combination changes (`D05-sources`, `D13-judging`), so `sources.Rules` becomes 10, and a full ingest rereads every row under one version and, attaching with force, rewrites every link whose reading the new rules change, so the mixed carriers' links lose their estimate, then records a fresh result of every derived kind and judges every candidate under the new combination. No key rule changes, and every row a link already holds keeps its candidate by its source key, so that ingest moves no link. A judge alone would not do: it reads the links as stored, so it would keep the mixed truck estimates on them.
- **The free-provider list** is exact domains, lower-cased: `aol.com`, `arvig.net`, `att.net`, `bellsouth.net`, `centurylink.net`, `centurytel.net`, `charter.net`, `comcast.net`, `earthlink.net`, `embarqmail.com`, `frontier.com`, `frontiernet.net`, `gmail.com`, `gmx.com`, `googlemail.com`, `hotmail.com`, `hughes.net`, `icloud.com`, `live.com`, `mac.com`, `mail.com`, `mchsi.com`, `me.com`, `midco.net`, `msn.com`, `outlook.com`, `protonmail.com`, `q.com`, `qwest.net`, `rocketmail.com`, `sbcglobal.net`, `tds.net`, `usfamily.net`, `verizon.net`, `windstream.net`, `yahoo.com` and `ymail.com`: the document's providers with their sister domains and the residential ISPs of Minnesota and Wisconsin.
- **The phone key keeps fewer than ten digits** when that is all there is: the document says digits only, last ten, and names no floor.
- **A link keeps its reason** when a later pass re-attaches it by source key: a founding link is `source_key`, a key match stays `match_key` at 0.8 and an EIN match `ein` at 0.9 until a ruling moves it, and a moved link stays `decision`. Rewriting a key match as `source_key` at the next pass would erase the uncertainty the confidence records and the trace of a ruling.
- **`linked` counts every changed row and every new row that joined a candidate**, so `created + linked = new + changed` on every batch; the first pass has `created + linked = rows`, a full ingest `linked` the rows whose readings it changed and `unchanged` the rest.
- **An unchanged row keeps its stored reading**: outside a full ingest its digest says the row is the same, and a change of rules is a full ingest, which rereads every row and rewrites each link whose reading the new rules change (decision 11).
- **A forced row whose reading did not change is unchanged** (OSHA ITA P15): a full ingest still reads every row and compares, but a row whose new reading encodes to exactly the bytes its link stores, the JSON the store keeps, member names included, touches only its link's run and last seen (and returns a gone link), rewrites neither the reading nor the keys, and counts `unchanged`; a row whose reading differs is `changed` and rewritten, as before. Earlier, a forced attach rewrote every link and its key rows whether or not its reading changed, and counted every row `changed`; on `us`, under a 256 MiB memory cap that stays, that was most of the some 23 GB a full ingest wrote, and the full ingest failed `store` after every row was read. The comparison is with the stored bytes, not the decoded reading, so a link an earlier release wrote, whose stored reading lacks members this release's reading has, is changed and rewritten once. The digest is left as it was: a row whose digest moved but whose reading did not keeps its old digest, so the next scraper ingest of it finds it changed and rewrites it once, which costs one row. The candidates a call founds, the touched ids it answers and the display re-derived at the end of the batch are as before, so a forced call still touches, and re-derives the display of, every candidate one of whose links it holds; R-7DIN-CZDO, the unforced unchanged row, keeps its text, and the forced one is a requirement of its own.
- **Every candidate a call writes a link of is touched**, the unchanged ones included; `Attach` answers their ids, and `MarkGone`'s are the candidates holding a link it marked, which it no longer answers (`D04-store`). An ingest pass judges nothing (`D06-passes`): the daily judge, the judge an enrich that wrote anything starts, a full ingest or a ruling brings their judgments up to date.
- **Every active candidate has records to pick from**: one is created with a link, a merge leaves the survivor all of both, and a split moves a proper subset, so the pick always has at least one record. When every link is gone, every link counts, so the candidate shows what its rows last said. A candidate whose records give no name or no address shows it empty, with no source and no record.
- **A link that gives no value does not blank one** (root ruling, intent decision 20): a PPP row "adds no phone, email or people", yet a PPP link approved after a carrier's last filing comes first among the links. The orders already keep it from blanking a fact, since each fact is the first value a slot gives and a record that gives none is passed over (`D05-sources`). Cargo stays the union over every tag link, gone ones included, as decision 4 says "its links". Neither is in the owner's table, so neither changes (sources outcome 10).

## Consumer tasks

The engine (`D06-passes`) attaches each batch with `n, _, err := st.Attach(ctx, run, force, batch)`, summing its counts and keeping no id beyond the batch in hand, since a pass holds no set that grows with the space (`shape/`'s Bounded work), and, once the stream has ended, marks the absent rows by walking `after, g, err = st.MarkGone(ctx, "fmcsa", run, after, passes.BatchSize)` from an empty `after` until it answers an empty one, summing each `g.Gone`; an ingest pass judges nothing. A judge pass reads its judge time `at` once and walks `after, c, err = st.JudgeAll(ctx, after, passes.BatchSize, at)` the same way, summing `c.Judged` and `c.Unranged`, and a full ingest does the same after its ingests (`D13-judging`). The `decide` tool (`D10-tools`) calls `r, cs, err := st.Decide(ctx, store.Ruling{Kind: store.KindSplit, Candidate: a, Keys: []string{sources.SourceKey("fmcsa", id)}, Why: why, UserID: c.UserID, UserEmail: c.Email})` and answers `r` with `cs[1]`, the new candidate; `store.ErrNotFound` is `not_found` and `store.ErrInvalid` `invalid`. The `exclude` tool calls `r, cs, err := st.Decide(ctx, store.Ruling{Kind: store.KindExclude, Candidate: a, Why: why, UserID: c.UserID, UserEmail: c.Email})`, and `include` the same with `store.KindInclude`, each answering `r` with `cs` and mapping the errors as `decide` does. A page names a link's source key with `sources.SourceKey(l.Scraper, l.SourceID)`. A test of a forced attach attaches `o`, then calls `st.Attach(ctx, run2, true, []store.Observation{o})` and checks that the answer counts `Unchanged` 1 and that the link's `Run` is `run2`; it then attaches, forced, an observation of the same source key whose `Reading` differs, and checks `Changed` and `Linked` 1 and the new `Reading`. A test builds `store.Observation{Scraper: "fmcsa", SourceID: "1023916", Digest: "d1", Reading: sources.Reading{NameKey: sources.NameKey("Acme Trucking, Inc."), State: "MN", Zip: "55072", Name: "ACME TRUCKING INC"}}`, attaches it and a second with the same keys and another source id, and checks that `st.Links(ctx, id)` answers both on one candidate, the second with `store.LinkMatchKey`.
