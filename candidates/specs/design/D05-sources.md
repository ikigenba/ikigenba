# D05-sources

The sources: the fixed registry that knows sources by name (`sources.Registry`), and the one source this pass registers, `fmcsa`, which reads a row of scrapes' FMCSA scraper (decisions 3 and 7). A **source** is Go code, not configuration: it knows how one row of its scraper becomes a **reading** (`sources.Reading`), the row's match keys, its common fields as candidates keeps them, its class and cargo tags, its filing date, whether it passes the fleet test, the size estimate it gives with the facts it used, and its exclusion signals. A source reads one row at a time and needs nothing else: no other row, no candidate, no clock, no read of scrapes. Adding a source is adding one to the registry; the candidate, the links, the pass, the tools and the pages hold every source without change. The types a source makes, the `Source` interface and the registry's shape are `D01-layout-and-run-seam`'s; the normalized keys a reading carries (`sources.NameKey` and its siblings) and the rules of matching are `D12-resolution`'s; what a re-score does with the readings, and the rules version, are `D13-scoring`'s. The engine (`D06-passes`) hands each streamed row to the source the registry holds for its scraper and skips a run of a scraper the registry does not hold.

## The registry

The registry is fixed: `Run` builds it as `sources.New(fmcsa.New())`, so the one source is `fmcsa` and a `scrape.finished` for any other scraper is skipped (`D06-passes`). A test builds a registry of its own sources with `sources.New`. A source's name is the name of the scraper whose rows it reads, so a link's scraper, an estimate's source and the registry's name are one word.

## FMCSA

The FMCSA source reads the row envelope appkit's `scrapes.Row` decodes, as scrapes' FMCSA scraper fills it: the common fields as the scraper normalized them, and `facts`, the source's strings as received, null when absent, with `cargo` a sorted list of the cargo codes the record marks. A **string fact** is a fact whose value is a JSON string; a fact that is absent, null, a number or anything else gives no value, and no rule reads it.

- **Identity.** The reading's name, names, street, city, state and ZIP are the row's as streamed; its county is `<state>-<fips>` built from the row's state and FIPS code (`MN-115`); its phone and email are the string facts `phone` and `email`; its people are the row's. The keys are the normalized forms of the name, the phone and the email's domain (`D12-resolution`).
- **Power units** is the string fact `power_units` read as a whole number: one or more ASCII digits and nothing else. Any other value gives no fleet pass and no estimate. A value so large that the for-hire high would not fit a signed 64-bit integer is not read either.
- **Driver count** is the string fact `total_drivers` and **mileage** the string fact `mcs150_mileage`, each read exactly as power units are, the same ceiling included. A row whose driver count does not read has drivers not reported; a mileage that reads as 0 or does not read, garbage included, is zero or absent.
- **Plausibility.** A row is **implausible** when its drivers are reported, its power units are more than fifty times its driver count (a count of zero taken as one) and its mileage is zero or absent: a one-person carrier with 199,999 units and no miles. A row is **suspicious** when it is not implausible, its drivers are reported and its power units are more than ten times its driver count (zero taken as one): 1,828 units, one driver and 19,390,412 miles. A row whose driver count does not read is judged neither way, so 510 units with no driver count and no miles keeps its full estimate.
- **Filing date** is the string fact `mcs150_date` read as `YYYYMMDD`, a real calendar date, midnight UTC. An unreadable or absent value means no filing date, which the rules treat as past the cutoff (`D13-scoring`).
- **Class tag** from the string fact `class`, split on `;`, each part trimmed and compared without regard to case: `AUTHORIZED FOR HIRE` or `EXEMPT FOR HIRE` is for hire, `PRIVATE PROPERTY` is private; both kinds is `mixed`, neither (no class, or passenger and government classes alone) `unknown`.
- **Fleet pass**: the string fact `status` is `A`, power units is 2 or more and the row is not implausible. Owner-operators with one truck drop out; the floor stays low because FMCSA sees registered road vehicles only.
- **Cargo tags** are the fact `cargo` as given: its string elements in order (`genfreight`, `bldgmat` and the like), none when it is not a list.
- **Estimate**, `n` the power units: for hire `150,000 × n` to `250,000 × n` at confidence 0.7; private `150,000 × n` and no high at 0.3; mixed `150,000 × n` and no high at 0.5; unknown as private at 0.2. A suspicious row's confidence is half its class's (0.35, 0.15, 0.25, 0.1) and its estimate's facts carry `plausibility` as `"suspicious"`. It carries the facts it used, copied as the row held them (`power_units`, `class`, `mcs150_date`, `total_drivers`, `mcs150_mileage`), the filing date, and the rules version (`sources.Rules`, `D13-scoring`). A row whose power units do not read, or an implausible row, gives no estimate.
- **Exclusion signals**, each recorded with its reason word and what matched: government by class, one `government_class` per government class word among the class parts (`STATE GOVERNMENT`, `LOCAL GOVERNMENT`, `FEDERAL GOVERNMENT`, `INDIAN NATION`); government by name, one `government_name` per pattern found as whole words in the normalized name (`COUNTY`, `CITY OF`, `TOWN OF`, `VILLAGE OF`, `TOWNSHIP`, `SCHOOL DISTRICT`, `STATE OF`, `DEPT`, `DEPARTMENT`, `UNIVERSITY`, `PUBLIC WORKS`), exactly the document's list. Public companies and franchises are left to `decide`.

These numbers and lists are rules version 2: contract, not copy. The plausibility thresholds, fifty and ten, are among them, and the owner expects to revise them. A revision is a new rules version and a rebuild (decision 8), which re-reads every row.

## Recorded decisions

- **The facts an estimate copies** are `power_units`, `class`, `mcs150_date`, `total_drivers` and `mcs150_mileage`, the five the formula, its confidence and its plausibility read, each present under the row's own key only when the row's facts hold it; `status` decides the fleet pass, not the estimate, and is not copied. `plausibility` is the one key of an estimate's facts that is a judgment rather than a copy, present only on a suspicious row; it cannot collide with a copied key, since it is not an FMCSA column.
- **An estimate does not need the fleet pass.** A row whose power units read and that is not implausible gives an estimate whatever its status or count (decision 7 ties an unreadable count to "no estimate", and version 2 adds an unbelievable one); the shortlist's fleet filter keeps the one-truck and inactive rows out (`D13-scoring`).
- **The source stops believing counts that cannot be true.** Under version 1 a one-person carrier reporting 199,999 power units and no miles led the shortlist. Only the obviously implausible is rejected: drivers reported, units more than fifty times the drivers, no miles. Such a row gives no estimate and no fleet pass, exactly as a row with no power units, and records no reason anywhere: it is of unknown size, not out of scope. The band between ten and fifty times the drivers, or past fifty with miles driven, holds real leasing fleets whose drivers are not on the carrier's books, so its estimate stands at half confidence and says why in its facts. Blank drivers are not judged, since a missing count is no evidence against the units. The rule lives in the FMCSA source; scoring, the shortlist and the store keep their shape, and scrapes is untouched.
- **Rejected alternatives.** An absolute cap on power units: Schneider has 9,866 real units and a junk row has 10,000, so no cap separates them. Miles per unit: real fleets that are not trucking companies run a few hundred miles a unit. Rejecting the suspicious band too: it holds real leasing fleets. Fixing scrapes: it is a faithful copy of what FMCSA publishes. A ruling per junk row: exclusion means out of scope, and these are of unknown size.
- **Unknown stays unknown.** A row that gives no estimate leaves its candidate at confidence 0 for that source, and a candidate with no estimate stays off the shortlist (`D13-scoring`).
- **Class parts are compared trimmed and case-folded**, and the government class words the same way, so a stray space or a lower-cased export cannot turn a carrier `unknown`.
- **Government name patterns match whole words** of the normalized name (`D12-resolution`'s `NameKey`, punctuation already stripped): `COUNTY` matches `PINE COUNTY HIGHWAY` and not `COUNTYLINE`, and `DEPT` not `DEPTFORD`. The list is exactly the document's, which closes it; a private business a pattern catches (a `COUNTY MATERIALS`) is returned with `include` (`D12-resolution`).
- **Power units has a ceiling** of 36,893,488,147,419 (the largest `n` with `250,000 × n` in an `int64`); a larger value reads as no whole number rather than overflowing. The driver count and mileage feed no formula, but they share the ceiling so every FMCSA count is read one way: each fits an `int64`, fifty times a driver count stays far inside one, and a mileage past it is garbage and counts as absent.
- **A filing date must be a real date**: `20250230` is no filing date, so a corrupt field counts as past the cutoff rather than as a guess.
- **A source shares nothing with its row**: the reading's slices and maps are its own, so the link the store writes cannot change when the streamed row is reused.

## Consumer tasks

`cli.Run` builds `reg := sources.New(fmcsa.New())` and hands it to `passes.New`. The engine (`D06-passes`) skips a run with `_, ok := reg.Get(scraper); !ok`, lists `reg.Names()` for a rebuild, and for each streamed row `r` builds `store.Observation{Scraper: r.Scraper, SourceID: r.SourceID, Digest: r.Digest, Reading: src.Read(r)}`. A test of `internal/fmcsa` builds `scrapes.Row` values with `Facts` of its own, calls `fmcsa.New().Read(r)` and compares the `sources.Reading` field by field: `rd.Class == sources.ClassForHire`, `*rd.Estimate.High == 250000*14`, `rd.Estimate.Facts["power_units"]`, `rd.Exclusions[0].Reason == sources.ExcludeGovernmentName`; for an implausible row `rd.Estimate == nil` and `!rd.Fleet`, and for a suspicious for-hire row `rd.Estimate.Confidence == 0.35` and `rd.Estimate.Facts["plausibility"]` holding `"suspicious"`. A test of the registry builds `sources.New(a, b)` from fakes and calls `Names` and `Get`.
