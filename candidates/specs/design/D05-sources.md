# D05-sources

The sources: the fixed registry that knows sources by name (`sources.Registry`), and the two sources it holds, `fmcsa`, which reads a row of scrapes' FMCSA scraper, and `ppp`, which reads a row of scrapes' PPP scraper. A **source** is Go code, not configuration: it knows how one row of its scraper becomes a **reading** (`sources.Reading`): the row's match keys, its common fields as candidates keeps them, its class and cargo tags, its filing date, whether it passes the fleet test and its three-valued fleet verdict, the power units and drivers it reports, the size estimate it gives with the facts it used, the operating evidence it gives, the date it shows the business existing (its known-since date), the category it gives, and the government name pattern its name matches. A source reads one row at a time and needs nothing else: no other row, no candidate, no clock, no read of scrapes. Adding a source is adding one to the registry; the candidate, the links, the passes, the tools and the pages hold every source without change. The types a source makes (`sources.Reading`, `sources.Estimate`, `sources.Evidence`), the `Source` interface, the registry's shape, the signature of `sources.GovernmentMatch`, the class, category, fleet verdict and evidence constants, the rules version `sources.Rules`, and the exports of `internal/fmcsa` and `internal/ppp` are `D01-layout-and-run-seam`'s; the normalized keys a reading carries (`sources.NameKey` and its siblings) and the rules of matching are `D12-resolution`'s; what a judge makes of the readings (freshness, the best evidence of each kind, the combining rule) and the rules version's history are `D13-scoring`'s; the facts a candidate's links give (its earliest known-since date, its newest filing, its categories and government match, and its fleet verdict, power units and drivers, read from its freshest FMCSA link not gone) are the `link_facts` enricher's, `D16-enrichers`', which records them as an enrichment that the candidate's facts row projects (`D04-store`), and the judge copies them from that row rather than reading the links; what a cohort makes of the verdict and the class tag (its `fleet` and `class` filters and their unknown switches) is `D14-cohorts`'. The engine (`D06-passes`) hands each streamed row to the source the registry holds for its scraper and skips a run of a scraper the registry does not hold.

Decisions cited by number are the decisions document that delivered candidates; "cohorts decision N" is entry N of the decisions document of the cohorts change, and "decisions log N" entry N of its decisions log. "Categories decision N" is entry N of the decisions document of the categories change, and "category evidence decision N" entry N of the decisions document of the category-evidence change, which makes the brief decide a company's categories when it has one and FMCSA give trucking and logistics only to carriers with for-hire authority.

## The registry

The registry is fixed: `Run` builds it as `sources.New(fmcsa.New(), ppp.New())`, so its names are `fmcsa` and `ppp`, a `rebuild` reads the current run of each in that order, and a `scrape.finished` for any other scraper is skipped (`D06-passes`). A test builds a registry of its own sources with `sources.New`. A source's name is the name of the scraper whose rows it reads, so a link's scraper, an estimate's source, an evidence's source and the registry's name are one word.

## What every source gives

A reading carries, beside its keys, tags and estimate:

- **Evidence**: the operating evidence the row gives, each piece a kind word (`sources.EvidenceFMCSAActive` and its siblings), the source's name, a weight, and a description, the kind's copy constant (`sources.DescribeFMCSAActive` and its siblings). The weight is the kind's base weight. A source has no clock, so it never applies freshness: the judge weighs `fmcsa_active` by the freshness of the row's filing date at the moment it runs (`D13-scoring`). The source's name is the evidence's `Source`, so a later enrichment can give evidence of its own under its own name without a row.
- **Known since**: the date the row shows the business existing, zero when it shows none.
- **Category**: one of the 23 category words (`sources.CategoryConstruction` and its siblings), empty when the row gives none. Seven are the target categories: construction, trucking and logistics, agriculture, manufacturing, wholesale and distribution, mining and aggregates, and waste and environmental. Fifteen are the NAICS sectors outside them, broadly cut: utilities, retail, other transportation, information, finance and insurance, real estate, professional services, administrative services, education, health care, arts and recreation, food and lodging, repair and personal services, religious and civic, and government. The last is other, for a business placed outside every one of them. Other is a category; empty is unknown. A category is a filter the owner chooses, never an exclusion: nothing is removed for it.
- **Government match**: `sources.GovernmentMatch` of the row's normalized name, the first of the document's government name patterns found as whole words in it, or empty. It is a fact, not an exclusion: nothing is removed for it, and the owner's `exclude` and `include` rulings are the only exclusions (`D13-scoring`).
- **Fleet verdict**: `sources.FleetPass`, `sources.FleetFail` or `sources.FleetUnknown`, what the row says about the business running a fleet of trucks. Only FMCSA says anything; every other source answers unknown. Beside it the reading still carries `Fleet`, true exactly when the verdict is pass, which the release before this one reads.
- **Power units and drivers**: the counts the row reports, each absent when the row reports none or the count cannot be trusted. Only FMCSA reports them.

## FMCSA

The FMCSA source reads the row envelope appkit's `scrapes.Row` decodes, as scrapes' FMCSA scraper fills it: the common fields as the scraper normalized them, and `facts`, the source's strings as received, null when absent, with `cargo` a sorted list of the cargo codes the record marks. A **string fact** is a fact whose value is a JSON string; a fact that is absent, null, a number or anything else gives no value, and no rule reads it.

- **Identity.** The reading's name, names, street, city, state and ZIP are the row's as streamed; its county is `<state>-<fips>` built from the row's state and FIPS code (`MN-115`); its phone and email are the string facts `phone` and `email`; its people are the row's. The keys are the normalized forms of the name, the phone and the email's domain (`D12-resolution`).
- **Power units** is the string fact `power_units` read as a whole number: one or more ASCII digits and nothing else. Any other value gives no fleet pass and no estimate. The reading carries the power units it read, none when they do not read. A value so large that the for-hire high would not fit a signed 64-bit integer is not read either.
- **Driver count** is the string fact `total_drivers` and **mileage** the string fact `mileage`, scrapes' name for FMCSA's `mcs150_mileage` column, each read exactly as power units are, the same ceiling included. A row whose driver count does not read has drivers not reported, and the reading carries no drivers; one whose count reads carries it; a mileage that reads as 0 or does not read, garbage included, is zero or absent.
- **Plausibility.** A row is **implausible** when its drivers are reported, its power units are more than fifty times its driver count (a count of zero taken as one) and its mileage is zero or absent: a one-person carrier with 199,999 units and no miles. A row is **suspicious** when it is not implausible, its drivers are reported and its power units are more than ten times its driver count (zero taken as one): 1,828 units, one driver and 19,390,412 miles. A row whose driver count does not read is judged neither way, so 510 units with no driver count and no miles keeps its full estimate.
- **Filing date** is the string fact `mcs150_date` read as `YYYYMMDD`, a real calendar date, midnight UTC. An unreadable or absent value means no filing date, which gives `fmcsa_active` no weight when the judge weighs it (`D13-scoring`).
- **Added date** is the string fact `added`, scrapes' name for FMCSA's `add_date` column, the carrier's registration date, read as the filing date is. It is the reading's known-since date; an unreadable or absent value gives none.
- **Class tag** from the string fact `class`, split on `;`, each part trimmed and compared without regard to case: `AUTHORIZED FOR HIRE` or `EXEMPT FOR HIRE` is for hire, `PRIVATE PROPERTY` is private; both kinds is `mixed`, neither (no class, or passenger and government classes alone) `unknown`.
- **Fleet verdict**: unknown when the row is implausible, since its count cannot be trusted; otherwise unknown when the string fact `status` is `A` and power units do not read; otherwise pass when `status` is `A` and power units is 2 or more; otherwise fail. Owner-operators with one truck fail; the floor stays low because FMCSA sees registered road vehicles only. The **fleet pass** (`Fleet`) is true exactly when the verdict is pass. The verdict hides nothing by itself: only a cohort whose `fleet` filter is carried leaves a candidate out by it (`D14-cohorts`).
- **Counts on an implausible row.** An implausible row carries neither power units nor drivers: the counts are what the plausibility rule refuses to believe.
- **Cargo tags** are the fact `cargo` as given: its string elements in order (`genfreight`, `bldgmat` and the like), none when it is not a list.
- **Estimate**, `n` the power units: for hire `150,000 × n` to `250,000 × n` at confidence 0.7; private `150,000 × n` and no high at 0.3; mixed `150,000 × n` and no high at 0.5; unknown as private at 0.2. A suspicious row's confidence is half its class's (0.35, 0.15, 0.25, 0.1) and its estimate's facts carry `plausibility` as `"suspicious"`. It carries the facts it used, copied as the row held them (`power_units`, `class`, `mcs150_date`, `total_drivers`, `mileage`), the filing date, and the rules version (`sources.Rules`). A row whose power units do not read, or an implausible row, gives no estimate. Filing age does not touch the estimate or its confidence.
- **Evidence**, in this order: `fmcsa_active`, weight 0.6, when `status` is `A`; `fmcsa_activity`, weight 0.3, when the driver count reads and is above zero and the mileage is not zero or absent, both read as the plausibility rule reads them; `own_domain`, weight 0.2, when the email's domain key is not empty, that is, the email is on a domain that is not a free provider's. An implausible row gives no evidence of any kind.
- **Category** is trucking and logistics when the class tag is for hire or mixed, and none when it is private or unknown (category evidence decision 7). Registration says a company runs trucks; only for-hire authority says hauling is its business.
- **Government match** is `sources.GovernmentMatch` of the normalized name. The class's government words (`STATE GOVERNMENT`, `LOCAL GOVERNMENT` and the like) feed nothing: they leave the class tag `unknown` and match nothing.

These numbers and lists are part of the rules: contract, not copy. The plausibility thresholds, fifty and ten, are among them, and the owner expects to revise them. A revision is a new rules version and a rebuild (decision 8), which re-reads every row.

## PPP

The PPP source reads a row of scrapes' PPP scraper: one SBA Paycheck Protection Program loan. scrapes publishes what such a row holds: the name is `BorrowerName`; the address is the borrower's (the project's city, state or ZIP when the borrower's is blank), with `ProjectCountyName` as the county, a name such as `HENNEPIN`; no people; and facts that are every one a JSON string, never null, an empty cell the empty string. The facts this source reads are `amount` (the column `CurrentApprovalAmount`, dollars that may carry cents, `152162.5`), `date_approved` (`DateApproved`), `business_age` (`BusinessAgeDescription`), `naics` (`NAICSCode`, its spaces kept, ` 238220 `) and `jobs_reported` (`JobsReported`), which it copies but no formula reads.

- **Identity.** The reading's name, names, street, city, state and ZIP are the row's as streamed; its county is the row's state and county name joined by `-` (`MN-HENNEPIN`). It has no phone, email or people, so it adds none, and its phone and domain keys are empty. Matching is unchanged: a PPP row matches by normalized name with state and five-digit ZIP, like every source (`D12-resolution`).
- **Tags.** No class tag (empty, not `unknown`), no cargo tags, no fleet pass, the fleet verdict unknown, and no power units or drivers.
- **PPP amount** is the string fact `amount` read as a decimal number: digits, optionally a point and more digits, nothing else; greater than zero and at most one billion dollars. Anything else gives no amount.
- **Approval date** is the string fact `date_approved` read as `MM/DD/YYYY`, a real calendar date with two-digit month and day, midnight UTC. It is the reading's filing date, so it feeds a candidate's newest filing.
- **Established** means the string fact `business_age` is exactly `Existing or more than 2 years old`.
- **Known since** is the approval date moved two years earlier when the row is established, the approval date when it is not, and none when there is no approval date.
- **NAICS category** is read from the string fact `naics` trimmed of white space, by leading digits, the longest matching prefix first: 23 construction; 484, 488, 492 and 493 trucking and logistics; 11 agriculture; 31, 32 and 33 manufacturing; 42 wholesale and distribution; 21 mining and aggregates; 562 waste and environmental; 22 utilities; 44 and 45 retail; 481, 482, 483, 485, 486, 487 and 491 other transportation; 51 information; 52 finance and insurance; 53 real estate; 54 and 55 professional services; 561 administrative services; 61 education; 62 health care; 71 arts and recreation; 72 food and lodging; 811 and 812 repair and personal services; 813 religious and civic; 92 government; any other code (814, 99 and anything unlisted) other. An empty code, or one that is not all digits, gives no category.
- **Estimate**, `a` the amount: yearly payroll is `a ÷ 2.5 × 12`; the low is payroll ÷ 0.4 and the high payroll ÷ 0.25, each rounded to whole dollars; confidence 0.4. It carries the facts `amount`, `naics`, `date_approved`, `business_age` and `jobs_reported`, each copied as the row held it when the row holds it, so a card can explain the estimate without going back to scrapes; the approval date; and the rules version. A row with no amount gives no estimate.
- **Evidence** is `ppp_loan`, weight 0.2, for every row: a loan shows the business existed.
- **Government match** is `sources.GovernmentMatch` of the normalized name, as for FMCSA.

## Recorded decisions

- **The facts an estimate copies** are `power_units`, `class`, `mcs150_date`, `total_drivers` and `mileage`, the five the formula, its confidence and its plausibility read, each present under the row's own key only when the row's facts hold it; `status` decides the fleet pass and `fmcsa_active`, not the estimate, and is not copied. `plausibility` is the one key of an estimate's facts that is a judgment rather than a copy, present only on a suspicious row; it cannot collide with a copied key, since scrapes' FMCSA rows carry no fact of that name.
- **The facts are scrapes' names, not FMCSA's columns.** scrapes renames some columns as it copies them into a row's facts: the raw `mcs150_mileage` is the fact `mileage`, with `mileage_year` beside it, and `add_date` is `added`, while `power_units`, `total_drivers` and `mcs150_date` keep theirs. The source reads and copies `mileage`; `mileage_year` feeds no rule and is not copied. Version 2 as first built read `mcs150_mileage`, which no row carries, so every row read as mileage-absent and real leasing fleets such as 1,828 units, one driver and 19,390,412 miles were dropped as implausible.
- **An estimate does not need the fleet pass.** A row whose power units read and that is not implausible gives an estimate whatever its status or count (decision 7 ties an unreadable count to "no estimate", and version 2 adds an unbelievable one). The fleet verdict is a stored fact; it leaves a candidate out of a cohort only when that cohort's `fleet` filter is carried (`D14-cohorts`).
- **The source stops believing counts that cannot be true.** Under version 1 a one-person carrier reporting 199,999 power units and no miles led the ranked list. Only the obviously implausible is rejected: drivers reported, units more than fifty times the drivers, no miles. Such a row gives no estimate, no fleet pass, an unknown fleet verdict, no counts and no evidence, and records no reason anywhere: it is of unknown size, not out of scope. The band between ten and fifty times the drivers, or past fifty with miles driven, holds real leasing fleets whose drivers are not on the carrier's books, so its estimate stands at half confidence and says why in its facts. Blank drivers are not judged, since a missing count is no evidence against the units. The rule lives in the FMCSA source; scoring, the cohorts and the store keep their shape, and scrapes is untouched.
- **Rejected alternatives.** An absolute cap on power units: Schneider has 9,866 real units and a junk row has 10,000, so no cap separates them. Miles per unit: real fleets that are not trucking companies run a few hundred miles a unit. Rejecting the suspicious band too: it holds real leasing fleets. Fixing scrapes: it is a faithful copy of what FMCSA publishes. A ruling per junk row: exclusion means out of scope, and these are of unknown size.
- **Missing is never zero.** A row that gives no estimate adds no estimate, and one that gives no evidence adds no evidence; a candidate with neither has empty judgments, not zeros, and stays in every cohort whose filters do not need them, sorted after the candidates whose values are known (`D14-cohorts`).
- **An implausible row still has a date, its class's category and a government match.** The plausibility rule rejects its counts, not its existence on FMCSA's books: its added date, the category its class tag gives and its name's match are facts like its address. Only evidence, the estimate, the counts and a definite fleet verdict, which rest on the counts or vouch for the business operating, are withheld.
- **FMCSA gives trucking and logistics only with for-hire authority** (category evidence decisions 7 and 8). In the owner's cohort of thirty, every for-hire carrier was a trucking business and no private one was: private registration means a company runs its own trucks for its own goods, as a school district, a contractor or a feed mill does, so it says what the company owns and nothing about its business. A mixed carrier holds for-hire authority beside its private operation and keeps the category; a brief corrects the ones that are something else (`D04-store`). A private or unknown carrier is classified by its other links, its name's government match or its brief, or is unknown. The class tag, cargo, fleet test and verdict, counts, estimate, evidence, known-since date and government match are unchanged. FMCSA cargo classes are deliberately not read for a category: they are a second heuristic the brief does better, which a later reading rule may add if the unknown pool proves too big.
- **The category change moves no rules version and needs a rebuild** (category evidence decisions 10 and 12). Categories feed no judgment (`D13-scoring`), so the version stays where `D13-scoring` states it. The category lives in the reading stored on the link, so a link stored before this change keeps trucking and logistics until a rebuild re-reads its row; `link_facts` reads the stored link (`D16-enrichers`). The release before this one, after a rollback, writes trucking and logistics again for every row it reads; accepted, as category evidence decision 11 accepts its re-projection.
- **Evidence carries base weights.** `fmcsa_active` is 0.6 on the reading whatever the filing date; the judge applies freshness at its own moment, so the formula lives once and a stored link never goes stale. `fmcsa_activity` needs both a driver count above zero and miles: a carrier reporting no drivers, or no miles, shows no activity. `own_domain` comes from FMCSA only in this change, since a PPP row carries no email.
- **Class parts are compared trimmed and case-folded**, so a stray space or a lower-cased export cannot turn a carrier `unknown`. The government class words now feed nothing: the document names only name patterns for the government match, and a government carrier's name almost always carries one.
- **Government name patterns match whole words** of the normalized name (`D12-resolution`'s `NameKey`, punctuation already stripped): `COUNTY` matches `PINE COUNTY HIGHWAY` and not `COUNTYLINE`, and `DEPT` not `DEPTFORD`. The list is exactly the document's, which closes it, and `sources.GovernmentMatch` answers the first pattern in the list's order, one word for a candidate's card. It applies to every source's name, PPP's included. A private business a pattern catches (a `COUNTY MATERIALS`) is now merely marked; nothing hides it.
- **Power units has a ceiling** of 36,893,488,147,419 (the largest `n` with `250,000 × n` in an `int64`); a larger value reads as no whole number rather than overflowing. The driver count and mileage feed no formula, but they share the ceiling so every FMCSA count is read one way: each fits an `int64`, fifty times a driver count stays far inside one, and a mileage past it is garbage and counts as absent.
- **A date must be a real date**: `20250230` is no filing or added date, and `02/30/2020` no approval date, so a corrupt field gives nothing rather than a guess.
- **A PPP amount is plain decimal, at most a billion.** scrapes keeps the SBA's string, which carries cents (`152162.5`); the source accepts digits with an optional fraction and nothing else, so a sign, an exponent, a comma or a space gives no amount. No PPP loan exceeds ten million dollars; the ceiling of one billion keeps the high, 19.2 times the amount, far inside an `int64` while refusing garbage. A positive amount small enough to round to a zero estimate still gives one.
- **The PPP estimate always has a high**, since the formula gives a range, and the largest amount among a candidate's PPP rows gives the candidate's estimate (`D13-scoring`).
- **The approval date is strict `MM/DD/YYYY`**, the SBA's form: `4/9/2020` gives none. Moving it two years earlier uses calendar arithmetic, so 02/29/2020 established is 2018-03-01.
- **Established is the SBA's exact phrase.** The phrase is data a program reads, not copy; any other value, a different case or a trailing space included, leaves the approval date as is.
- **NAICS is trimmed, then read by prefix.** scrapes keeps the code's spaces (` 238220 `), so the source trims them; a code of one digit, or a two-digit code such as `48` or `81` that only a longer prefix names, is read like any other and falls to other. The longest matching prefix wins. No prefix in the table begins another today, so the rule decides nothing yet; it is stated so that a later, narrower prefix inside a listed sector (a three-digit code inside a two-digit one) takes its rows without reordering the table.
- **The table follows the NAICS sectors** (categories decision 2), so the PPP codes and the brief share one list and the mapping falls out of the codes rather than anyone's judgment. Agriculture takes the whole of sector 11, adding forestry and logging (113) and fishing (114), and mining the whole of sector 21, adding oil and gas extraction (211): both move physical goods and are rare. Repair shops (811) do not move goods, so they stay non-target, in a category of their own a cohort can pick out. The SIC codes a later source may carry are not mapped. The list is 23 words; the categories document's "twenty-four" miscounts the same list. A rebuild re-reads every PPP row under the table; the rules version does not move, since categories feed no judgment (`D13-scoring`).
- **A PPP row has an empty class, not `unknown`**, no fleet pass, an unknown fleet verdict and no counts: PPP says nothing about carriage. A cohort's `class` filter leaves a candidate with no class out unless its unknown switch is set, which admits an empty class and `unknown` alike (`D14-cohorts`). A PPP row's county is the state and the county's name (`MN-HENNEPIN`), where FMCSA's is the FIPS code (`MN-115`); the county is shown, not matched, so the two need not agree.
- **Every PPP row gives `ppp_loan`**, an unreadable amount or date included: the loan exists, so the business did.
- **The fleet verdict has three values, and the reading holds it** (cohorts decision 13, decisions log 11). A company with no FMCSA record used to read "no fleet" as if FMCSA had counted its trucks; it now reads unknown. The `link_facts` enricher reads the stored link, not the scraped row, and an implausible row is stored just like one whose units do not read, so the verdict is decided here, per row, and `link_facts` takes the freshest FMCSA link's into the candidate's facts (`D16-enrichers`), which the judge copies (`D13-scoring`). A rebuild re-reads every row and then re-derives every candidate's link facts, which is what makes the stored verdicts right after a change to the rule.
- **An active row whose power units do not read is unknown** (decisions log 12): it says nothing about fleet size either way, which is the case the third value exists for. An inactive row is fail whatever its units, readable or not, since the document's rule is pass only when active with two or more units and fail otherwise; only an implausible row and an active row with unreadable units are excepted.
- **`Fleet` is still written**, true exactly when the verdict is pass (decisions log 11): the release before this one reads `Fleet` from links this release writes, and a rollback must keep working. The next release can stop writing it.
- **The reading carries power units and drivers** (decisions log 20), so `link_facts` (`D16-enrichers`) can give a candidate its trucks and drivers from the same freshest FMCSA link its verdict comes from, without going back to the estimate's copied facts, which an implausible row does not have. An implausible row carries neither (decisions log 28): its counts are not trusted, as its verdict says. They are the counts the plausibility rule reads, so a count past the ceiling is none here too.
- **A link stored before this change has no verdict.** It decodes with an empty `FleetVerdict` and no counts; whoever reads it treats empty as unknown (`D16-enrichers`, `D10-tools`), and a rebuild rewrites it.
- **The rules version is `D13-scoring`'s.** It moves when what a judge writes changes, and `D13-scoring` states its current value; every estimate a source gives carries `sources.Rules` as before, so after a rebuild every estimate carries the version `D13-scoring` states. No source formula changes with it.
- **A source shares nothing with its row**: the reading's slices and maps are its own, so the link the store writes cannot change when the streamed row is reused.

## Consumer tasks

Current: `cli.Run` builds `reg := sources.New(fmcsa.New(), ppp.New())` and hands it to `passes.New`; the engine (`D06-passes`) builds `store.Observation{Scraper: r.Scraper, SourceID: r.SourceID, Digest: r.Digest, Reading: src.Read(r)}` for each streamed row. A test of `internal/fmcsa` builds `scrapes.Row` values with `Facts` of its own, calls `fmcsa.New().Read(r)` and compares the `sources.Reading` field by field: `rd.Class == sources.ClassForHire`, `*rd.Estimate.High == 250000*14`, `rd.Evidence[0] == sources.Evidence{Name: sources.EvidenceFMCSAActive, Source: fmcsa.Name, Weight: 0.6, Description: sources.DescribeFMCSAActive}`, `rd.FleetVerdict == sources.FleetPass`, `*rd.PowerUnits == 3`, `*rd.Drivers == 4`, `rd.KnownSince`, `rd.GovernmentMatch == "COUNTY"`, and `rd.Category == sources.CategoryTrucking` for every row whatever its class, `Pine County Highway Dept.` with class `LOCAL GOVERNMENT` and a `PRIVATE PROPERTY` carrier included; for an implausible row `rd.Estimate == nil`, `!rd.Fleet`, `len(rd.Evidence) == 0`, `rd.FleetVerdict == sources.FleetUnknown`, `rd.PowerUnits == nil` and `rd.Category == sources.CategoryTrucking`. A test of `internal/ppp` does the same with `ppp.New().Read(r)`: `rd.Estimate.Low == 2400000`, `rd.Class == ""`, `rd.FleetVerdict == sources.FleetUnknown`, and `rd.Category == sources.CategoryConstruction` for `naics` `"236220"`. The `link_facts` enricher (`D16-enrichers`) reads `FleetVerdict`, `PowerUnits` and `Drivers` of a candidate's freshest FMCSA link not gone, together with every live link's `KnownSince`, `Filed`, `Category` and `GovernmentMatch`, so every candidate with an FMCSA link has trucking and logistics from `fmcsa`. Registry, copy-constant and `sources.GovernmentMatch` tests are as before and unchanged.

Proposed: the registry, the engine and every comparison but the category stay. A test of `internal/fmcsa` compares `rd.Category` beside `rd.Class`: class `AUTHORIZED FOR HIRE` gives `rd.Class == sources.ClassForHire` and `rd.Category == sources.CategoryTrucking`; `PRIVATE PROPERTY;AUTHORIZED FOR HIRE` gives `sources.ClassMixed` and the same category; `PRIVATE PROPERTY` gives `sources.ClassPrivate` and `rd.Category == ""`; an empty class gives `sources.ClassUnknown` and `""`; `Pine County Highway Dept.` with class `LOCAL GOVERNMENT` and `added` `"20040115"` gives `sources.ClassUnknown`, `rd.GovernmentMatch == "COUNTY"`, `rd.KnownSince` 2004-01-15 and `rd.Category == ""`; an implausible row keeps the category its class gives. `internal/ppp` and `link_facts` are unchanged, so a private carrier's FMCSA link gives its candidate no category, and the candidate's categories come from its other links and its name's government match (`D16-enrichers`), or from its brief (`D04-store`).
