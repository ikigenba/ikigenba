# D05-sources

The sources: the fixed registry that knows sources by name (`sources.Registry`), and the four sources it holds, `fmcsa`, which reads a row of scrapes' FMCSA scraper, `ppp`, which reads a row of scrapes' PPP scraper, `form5500`, which reads a row of scrapes' Form 5500 scraper, and `osha_ita`, which reads a row of scrapes' OSHA ITA scraper. A **source** is Go code, not configuration: it knows how one row of its scraper becomes a **reading** (`sources.Reading`): the row's match keys, its common fields as candidates keeps them, its class and cargo tags, its filing date, whether it passes the fleet test and its three-valued fleet verdict, the power units and drivers it reports, the headcount it reports, the size estimate it gives with the facts it used, the operating evidence it gives, the date it shows the business existing (its known-since date), the category it gives, the government name pattern its name matches, and, for an OSHA ITA row, the establishment's name and the figures it gives as read. A source reads one row at a time and needs nothing else: no other row, no candidate, no clock, no read of scrapes. Adding a source is adding one to the registry; the candidate, the links, the passes, the tools and the pages hold every source without change. The types a source makes (`sources.Reading`, `sources.Headcount`, `sources.Estimate`, `sources.Evidence`), the `Source` interface, the registry's shape, the signatures of `sources.GovernmentMatch` and `sources.NAICSCategory`, the class, category, fleet verdict, headcount kind and evidence constants, the rules version `sources.Rules`, and the exports of `internal/fmcsa`, `internal/ppp`, `internal/form5500` and `internal/oshaita` are `D01-layout-and-run-seam`'s; the normalized keys a reading carries (`sources.NameKey` and its siblings, `sources.NameKeys` and `sources.EINKey` among them) and the rules of matching are `D12-ingest`'s; what a judge makes of the readings (freshness, the best evidence of each kind, the headcount it uses, the sum of a business's sites among it, the band per employee and the `headcount` estimate it forms from them, the combining rule) and the rules version's history are `D13-judging`'s; the facts a candidate's links give (its earliest known-since date, its newest filing, its categories and government match, its fleet verdict, power units and drivers, read from its freshest FMCSA link not gone, and the headcount each link not gone gives, with its hours worked) are the `link_facts` enricher's, `D16-enrichers`', which records them as an enrichment that the candidate's facts row projects (`D04-store`), and the judge reads that row, its headcounts, categories and brief labels among it (`D13-judging`); what a prospect list makes of the verdict and the class tag (its `fleet` and `class` filters and their unknown switches) is `D14-prospect-lists`'. The engine (`D06-passes`) hands each streamed row to the source the registry holds for its scraper and skips a run of a scraper the registry does not hold.

Decisions cited by number are the decisions document that delivered candidates; "cohorts decision N" is entry N of the decisions document of the cohorts change, and "decisions log N" entry N of its decisions log. "Categories decision N" is entry N of the decisions document of the categories change, and "category evidence decision N" entry N of the decisions document of the category-evidence change, which makes the brief decide a company's categories when it has one and FMCSA give trucking and logistics only to carriers with for-hire authority. "Revenue bands decision N" is entry N of the decisions document of the revenue-bands change, which makes a candidate's revenue estimate its headcount times a band of revenue per employee, has PPP give its reported jobs and FMCSA its drivers as headcounts, ends PPP's payroll estimate, and keeps FMCSA's truck estimate only for carriers with for-hire authority. "Form 5500 decision N" is entry N of the decisions document of the Form 5500 change, whose candidates decisions are 12 to 24 and whose scrapes decision 6 names the facts a Form 5500 row holds; it adds the `form5500` source, gives every reading the name keys of all its names and an EIN key, and moves the NAICS table PPP used into `sources.NAICSCategory`, which any source reading a NAICS code shares. "OSHA ITA outcome N" is outcome N of the document of the OSHA ITA change, which adds the `osha_ita` source, reading a Form 300A summary's annual average employees as an `employees` headcount with the hours worked beside it, and "OSHA ITA P N" is entry P N of its delivery log. "Estimate inversion outcome N" is entry N of the Outcomes of the decisions document of the estimate-inversion change, which brings rules version 10, and "estimate inversion log DN" entry DN of its decisions log.

## The registry

The registry is fixed: `Run` builds it as `sources.New(fmcsa.New(), ppp.New(), form5500.New(), oshaita.New())` (Form 5500 decision 12, OSHA ITA outcome 9), so its names are `fmcsa`, `ppp`, `form5500` and `osha_ita`, in the order the call lists them, which is the order in which an `ingest` reads the current run of each, and a `scrape.finished` for any other scraper is skipped (`D06-passes`). A test builds a registry of its own sources with `sources.New`. A source's name is the name of the scraper whose rows it reads, so a link's scraper, an estimate's source, an evidence's source and the registry's name are one word. A place a walk found is no scrapes row and no source reads it: `places.Reading` (`D19-walk`) reads it, outside the registry, as the parcel scraper is outside it (`D18-parks`).

## What every source gives

A reading carries, beside its keys, tags and estimate:

- **Keys**: its name key (`NameKey`, the key of its name), its **name keys** (`NameKeys`, `sources.NameKeys` of its names: the key of each name it carries, in order, the empty and the repeated left out), its phone and domain keys, and its **EIN key** (`EINKey`, `sources.EINKey` of the employer identification number the row gives, empty when it gives none). Every source fills its name keys the same way; only `form5500` and `osha_ita` fill the EIN key today (Form 5500 decision 14, OSHA ITA outcome 10). What the keys are and how matching uses them is `D12-ingest`'s.
- **Evidence**: the operating evidence the row gives, each piece a kind word (`sources.EvidenceFMCSAActive` and its siblings), the source's name, a weight, and a description, the kind's copy constant (`sources.DescribeFMCSAActive` and its siblings). The weight is the kind's base weight. A source has no clock, so it never applies freshness: the judge weighs `fmcsa_active` by the freshness of the row's filing date at the moment it runs (`D13-judging`). The source's name is the evidence's `Source`, so a later enrichment can give evidence of its own under its own name without a row.
- **Known since**: the date the row shows the business existing, zero when it shows none.
- **Category**: one of the 23 category words (`sources.CategoryConstruction` and its siblings), empty when the row gives none. Seven are the target categories: construction, trucking and logistics, agriculture, manufacturing, wholesale and distribution, mining and aggregates, and waste and environmental. Fifteen are the NAICS sectors outside them, broadly cut: utilities, retail, other transportation, information, finance and insurance, real estate, professional services, administrative services, education, health care, arts and recreation, food and lodging, repair and personal services, religious and civic, and government. The last is other, for a business placed outside every one of them. Other is a category; empty is unknown. A category is a filter the owner chooses, never an exclusion: nothing is removed for it. A source that reads a NAICS code turns it into a category with `sources.NAICSCategory`, one table for every such source (PPP, Form 5500 and OSHA ITA today).
- **Government match**: `sources.GovernmentMatch` of the row's normalized name, the first of the document's government name patterns found as whole words in it, or empty. It is a fact, not an exclusion: nothing is removed for it, and the owner's `exclude` and `include` rulings are the only exclusions (`D13-judging`).
- **Fleet verdict**: `sources.FleetPass`, `sources.FleetFail` or `sources.FleetUnknown`, what the row says about the business running a fleet of trucks. Only FMCSA says anything; every other source answers unknown. Beside it the reading still carries `Fleet`, true exactly when the verdict is pass, which the release before this one reads.
- **Power units and drivers**: the counts the row reports, each absent when the row reports none or the count cannot be trusted. Only FMCSA reports them.
- **Headcount**: one count of the people the business employs as the row gives it, with the source's name, its kind word (`sources.HeadcountJobs` and its siblings) and the date it is as of, the zero date when the row gives none; absent when the row gives no count or a count of zero. PPP gives `jobs`, FMCSA `drivers`, Form 5500 `participants` and OSHA ITA `employees`, the last with the hours worked beside the count; `snippet` is for a later source, declared now so the judge's ranking of the kinds (`D13-judging`) is written once (revenue bands decisions 1 and 6). A source only reports the count: the estimate made from it needs a band per employee, which the candidate's categories and brief labels choose, so the judge forms it (`D13-judging`, revenue bands decision 8).
- **Establishment figures**: the establishment's name, the filing year, the annual average employees and the hours worked, as the row gives them, each empty or absent when it gives none or the figure does not read. Only OSHA ITA gives them, and it gives them whether or not it gives a headcount, so a site whose figures fall outside the band still shows them (OSHA ITA P6.2); the card and the `candidate` tool's link answers show them (`D08-pages`, `D10-tools`).

## FMCSA

The FMCSA source reads the row envelope appkit's `scrapes.Row` decodes, as scrapes' FMCSA scraper fills it: the common fields as the scraper normalized them, and `facts`, the source's strings as received, null when absent, with `cargo` a sorted list of the cargo codes the record marks. A **string fact** is a fact whose value is a JSON string; a fact that is absent, null, a number or anything else gives no value, and no rule reads it.

- **Identity.** The reading's name, names, street, city, state and ZIP are the row's as streamed; its county is `<state>-<fips>` built from the row's state and FIPS code (`MN-115`); its phone and email are the string facts `phone` and `email`; its people are the row's. The keys are the normalized forms of the name, the phone and the email's domain (`D12-ingest`); its name keys are those of each of its names, so its DBA's key stands beside its legal name's, and its EIN key is empty, since FMCSA's rows give no EIN (Form 5500 decision 14).
- **Power units** is the string fact `power_units` read as a whole number: one or more ASCII digits and nothing else. Any other value gives no fleet pass and no estimate. The reading carries the power units it read, none when they do not read. A value so large that the for-hire high would not fit a signed 64-bit integer is not read either.
- **Driver count** is the string fact `total_drivers` and **mileage** the string fact `mileage`, scrapes' name for FMCSA's `mcs150_mileage` column, each read exactly as power units are, the same ceiling included. A row whose driver count does not read has drivers not reported, and the reading carries no drivers; one whose count reads carries it; a mileage that reads as 0 or does not read, garbage included, is zero or absent.
- **Plausibility.** A row is **implausible** when its drivers are reported, its power units are more than fifty times its driver count (a count of zero taken as one) and its mileage is zero or absent: a one-person carrier with 199,999 units and no miles. A row is **suspicious** when it is not implausible, its drivers are reported and its power units are more than ten times its driver count (zero taken as one): 1,828 units, one driver and 19,390,412 miles. A row whose driver count does not read is judged neither way, so 510 units with no driver count and no miles keeps its full estimate.
- **Filing date** is the string fact `mcs150_date` read as `YYYYMMDD`, a real calendar date, midnight UTC. An unreadable or absent value means no filing date, which gives `fmcsa_active` no weight when the judge weighs it (`D13-judging`).
- **Added date** is the string fact `added`, scrapes' name for FMCSA's `add_date` column, the carrier's registration date, read as the filing date is. It is the reading's known-since date; an unreadable or absent value gives none.
- **Class tag** from the string fact `class`, split on `;`, each part trimmed and compared without regard to case: `AUTHORIZED FOR HIRE` or `EXEMPT FOR HIRE` is for hire, `PRIVATE PROPERTY` is private; both kinds is `mixed`, neither (no class, or passenger and government classes alone) `unknown`.
- **Fleet verdict**: unknown when the row is implausible, since its count cannot be trusted; otherwise unknown when the string fact `status` is `A` and power units do not read; otherwise pass when `status` is `A` and power units is 2 or more; otherwise fail. Owner-operators with one truck fail; the floor stays low because FMCSA sees registered road vehicles only. The **fleet pass** (`Fleet`) is true exactly when the verdict is pass. The verdict hides nothing by itself: only a prospect list whose `fleet` filter is carried leaves a candidate out by it (`D14-prospect-lists`).
- **Counts on an implausible row.** An implausible row carries neither power units nor drivers: the counts are what the plausibility rule refuses to believe.
- **Headcount** is the driver count, kind `drivers`, as of the filing date, when the row is not implausible and the count reads and is above zero; otherwise there is none (revenue bands decision 3). The class tag does not touch it: a private carrier's drivers are its headcount though its trucks give no estimate.
- **Cargo tags** are the fact `cargo` as given: its string elements in order (`genfreight`, `bldgmat` and the like), none when it is not a list.
- **Estimate**, `n` the power units, only for a carrier with for-hire authority (revenue bands decision 12): for hire `150,000 × n` to `250,000 × n` at confidence 0.7. A mixed, private or unknown-class row gives no estimate (estimate inversion outcome 1). A suspicious row's confidence is halved (0.35) and its estimate's facts carry `plausibility` as `"suspicious"`. It carries the facts it used, copied as the row held them (`power_units`, `class`, `mcs150_date`, `total_drivers`, `mileage`), the filing date, and the rules version (`sources.Rules`). A row whose power units do not read, or an implausible row, gives no estimate. Filing age does not touch the estimate or its confidence.
- **Evidence**, in this order: `fmcsa_active`, weight 0.6, when `status` is `A`; `fmcsa_activity`, weight 0.3, when the driver count reads and is above zero and the mileage is not zero or absent, both read as the plausibility rule reads them; `own_domain`, weight 0.2, when the email's domain key is not empty, that is, the email is on a domain that is not a free provider's. An implausible row gives no evidence of any kind.
- **Category** is trucking and logistics when the class tag is for hire or mixed, and none when it is private or unknown (category evidence decision 7). Registration says a company runs trucks; only for-hire authority says hauling is its business.
- **Government match** is `sources.GovernmentMatch` of the normalized name. The class's government words (`STATE GOVERNMENT`, `LOCAL GOVERNMENT` and the like) feed nothing: they leave the class tag `unknown` and match nothing.

These numbers and lists are part of the rules: contract, not copy. The plausibility thresholds, fifty and ten, are among them, and the owner expects to revise them. A revision is a new rules version and a full ingest (decision 8), which re-reads every row.

## PPP

The PPP source reads a row of scrapes' PPP scraper: one SBA Paycheck Protection Program loan. scrapes publishes what such a row holds: the name is `BorrowerName`; the address is the borrower's (the project's city, state or ZIP when the borrower's is blank), with `ProjectCountyName` as the county, a name such as `HENNEPIN`; no people; and facts that are every one a JSON string, never null, an empty cell the empty string. The facts this source reads are `date_approved` (`DateApproved`), `business_age` (`BusinessAgeDescription`), `naics` (`NAICSCode`, its spaces kept, ` 238220 `) and `jobs_reported` (`JobsReported`, whole jobs as text, `26`). `amount` (`CurrentApprovalAmount`) stays on the row, which the candidate's link points to, and no rule reads it any more (revenue bands decision 13).

- **Identity.** The reading's name, names, street, city, state and ZIP are the row's as streamed; its county is the row's state and county name joined by `-` (`MN-HENNEPIN`). It has no phone, email or people, so it adds none, and its phone and domain keys are empty. Its name keys are those of its names, the one key it had before, and its EIN key is empty; a PPP row matches by normalized name with state and five-digit ZIP, like every source (`D12-ingest`).
- **Tags.** No class tag (empty, not `unknown`), no cargo tags, no fleet pass, the fleet verdict unknown, and no power units or drivers.
- **Jobs reported** is the string fact `jobs_reported` read as FMCSA reads power units: one or more ASCII digits and nothing else, the same ceiling; anything else, `12.0` and a blank included, gives none (revenue bands decision 2).
- **Approval date** is the string fact `date_approved` read as `MM/DD/YYYY`, a real calendar date with two-digit month and day, midnight UTC. It is the reading's filing date, so it feeds a candidate's newest filing.
- **Established** means the string fact `business_age` is exactly `Existing or more than 2 years old`.
- **Known since** is the approval date moved two years earlier when the row is established, the approval date when it is not, and none when there is no approval date.
- **NAICS category** is `sources.NAICSCategory` of the string fact `naics`: the code trimmed of white space, by leading digits, the longest matching prefix first: 23 construction; 484, 488, 492 and 493 trucking and logistics; 11 agriculture; 31, 32 and 33 manufacturing; 42 wholesale and distribution; 21 mining and aggregates; 562 waste and environmental; 22 utilities; 44 and 45 retail; 481, 482, 483, 485, 486, 487 and 491 other transportation; 51 information; 52 finance and insurance; 53 real estate; 54 and 55 professional services; 561 administrative services; 61 education; 62 health care; 71 arts and recreation; 72 food and lodging; 811 and 812 repair and personal services; 813 religious and civic; 92 government; any other code (814, 99 and anything unlisted) other. An empty code, or one that is not all digits, gives no category, and so does a `naics` that is not a string fact.
- **Headcount** is the jobs reported, kind `jobs`, as of the approval date, when they read and are above zero; otherwise there is none.
- **No estimate.** A PPP row gives none (revenue bands decision 13): its headcount is what it contributes to a candidate's size, and the judge's `headcount` estimate turns it into revenue (`D13-judging`).
- **Evidence** is `ppp_loan`, weight 0.2, for every row: a loan shows the business existed.
- **Government match** is `sources.GovernmentMatch` of the normalized name, as for FMCSA.

## Form 5500

The Form 5500 source reads a row of scrapes' Form 5500 scraper: one annual return of an employee benefit plan, a Form 5500 or a Form 5500-SF, filed with the Department of Labor. scrapes publishes what such a row holds (Form 5500 scrapes decision 6): the name is the plan sponsor's and the names are the sponsor's then its DBA when the filing gives one; the address is the sponsor's location address when its state is given and its mailing address otherwise; no county; the people are the sponsor's signer and the administrator's; and facts that are every one a JSON string, never null, an empty cell the empty string, under one set of keys for both forms. The facts this source reads are `form` (`5500` or `5500-SF`), `entity` (the plan entity code), `sponsor_name`, `plan_name`, `phone`, `ein`, `business_code` (the NAICS code), `received` (the date the department received the filing), `plan_effective` (the plan's effective date), `plan_year_begin`, `plan_year_end`, `active_participants_eoy` (active participants at the end of the plan year) and `participants_boy` (all participants at its beginning), the dates as `YYYY-MM-DD` and the counts as whole numbers in text (`178`). The others stay on the row, which the candidate's link points to; no rule reads them.

- **Identity** (Form 5500 decision 13). The reading's name, street, city, state and ZIP are the row's as streamed, so the scraper's choice of the location address over the mailing one stands; its county is empty; its phone is the string fact `phone`, so its phone key fills; it has no email; its people are the row's. Its EIN key is `sources.EINKey` of the string fact `ein` (Form 5500 decision 14): a nine-digit number, not all zeros, or nothing.
- **Names.** The reading's names are the row's, followed by two kinds of derived name, in this order. The **DBA names**: when the string fact `sponsor_name` holds ` DBA `, ` D/B/A ` or ` D.B.A. `, compared without regard to case, the part before the first such separator and the part after it, each trimmed. The **plan-derived name**: the string fact `plan_name` cut before the first **plan word** found in it as a whole word, a word of the source's table such as `401(K)`, `PROFIT SHARING`, `HEALTH` or `PLAN`, trimmed, with a trailing `AND`, `&`, `-` or `,` dropped; a plan name holding no plan word is kept whole. A derived name whose name key is empty, or is the key of a name already carried, is left out. So `BROWN HAWK LLC DBA NORTHERN LIGHTS STEEL FAB`, whose plan is `NORTHERN LIGHTS STEEL FABRICATION 401(K) PROFIT SHARING PLAN`, carries `BROWN HAWK LLC`, `NORTHERN LIGHTS STEEL FAB` and `NORTHERN LIGHTS STEEL FABRICATION` after its own name, and `HEGGIES PIZZA, LLC`, whose plan is `HEGGIES SLICE OF THE PIE`, carries the plan's name too, which is harmless because a name key is only ever tried with the row's state and ZIP (`D12-ingest`). Its name keys are those of all its names.
- **Tags.** No class tag (empty, not `unknown`), no cargo tags, no fleet pass, the fleet verdict unknown, no power units or drivers, and no estimate (Form 5500 decision 20): the headcount is the row's contribution to the candidate's size, and the judge's `headcount` estimate turns it into revenue (`D13-judging`).
- **Single-employer.** A row is single-employer when its `form` is `5500` and its `entity` `2`, or its `form` is `5500-SF` and its `entity` `1` (Form 5500 decision 16). Only such a row gives a headcount: a multiemployer plan's sponsor is a board of trustees, a multiple-employer plan's a PEO or an association, and a DFE is a trust, so their participants are not the sponsor's employees. Those rows still give a reading, evidence and a category.
- **Headcount** (Form 5500 decision 17), only of a single-employer row: kind `participants`, the string fact `active_participants_eoy` as of `plan_year_end` when it reads and is above zero; otherwise `participants_boy` as of `plan_year_begin` when it reads and is above zero; otherwise none. A count reads as PPP's jobs do: ASCII digits only, the same ceiling. A count whose as-of date does not read is undated, which the judge takes as oldest (`D13-judging`).
- **Dates** (Form 5500 decision 19). The filing date is the string fact `received` read as `YYYY-MM-DD`, so it feeds the candidate's newest filing; the known-since date is `plan_effective` read the same way, none when it does not read, since a plan cannot predate its sponsor.
- **Category** is `sources.NAICSCategory` of the string fact `business_code`, as PPP's is of `naics`; an empty or non-digit code gives none.
- **Evidence** is `form5500_filing`, weight 0.2, for every row, a final filing and a multiemployer plan included (Form 5500 decision 20): a filing shows the business existed in its plan year.
- **Government match** is `sources.GovernmentMatch` of the normalized name, as for the others.

## OSHA ITA

The OSHA ITA source reads a row of scrapes' OSHA ITA scraper: one establishment's Form 300A injury and illness summary for one year, sent to OSHA through its Injury Tracking Application (OSHA ITA outcome 9). scrapes publishes what such a row holds: the name is the company name, or the establishment name when the company name is empty, and the names are that name then the establishment name when it differs, though this source takes the name alone (OSHA ITA P11); the address is the establishment's street, city, state and ZIP; no county and no people; and facts that are every one a JSON string, as received, a missing column the empty string. The facts this source reads are `establishment_name`, `ein`, `year_filing_for`, `naics_code`, `annual_average_employees`, `total_hours_worked` and `date_submitted` (the submission timestamp, in its year's spelling). The others, `company_name`, `id`, `establishment_id`, `naics_year`, `industry_description`, `establishment_type`, `size`, `no_injuries_illnesses` and `change_reason`, stay on the row, which the candidate's link points to; no rule reads them.

- **Identity** (OSHA ITA outcome 10). The reading's name, street, city, state and ZIP are the row's as streamed, and its names are its name alone, so a site carries its company's name, or its own when the company name is empty, and its establishment name only for display; its county is empty; it has no phone, email or people, so its phone and domain keys are empty. Its name keys are those of its name. Its EIN key is the string fact `ein` read as nine digits, eight digits read as nine with a leading zero, so KTM Paving's `10739420` is `010739420` and its years join; anything else, one to seven digits, ten, a separator or a space, gives none, and so do nine zeros. A row with no EIN key matches by its names with state and ZIP, as every row does (`D12-ingest`), so Molin Concrete, which never gives one, joins at its ZIP. The establishment id is not a key.
- **Figures** (OSHA ITA P6.1). The annual average employees, the hours worked and the filing year are read as whole numbers in either form the files use: ASCII digits, optionally followed by a point and zeros, the `.00` the 2019 file appends (`23` and `23.00` are both 23). Anything else, a sign, a separator, a fraction or a space, gives none, and so does a number past the ceiling every source shares. The filing year is the year read from `year_filing_for` when it is 1 to 9999.
- **Establishment figures** (OSHA ITA outcome 16, P6.2). The reading carries the string fact `establishment_name`, the filing year, the employees and the hours as read, whether or not it gives a headcount: 43,694 employees over 25 hours stays 43,694 and 25 on the link, and only the headcount is withheld.
- **Tags.** No class tag (empty, not `unknown`), no cargo tags, no fleet pass, the fleet verdict unknown, no power units or drivers, and no estimate (OSHA ITA outcome 15): the headcount is the row's whole contribution to the candidate's size, and the judge's `headcount` estimate turns it into revenue (`D13-judging`).
- **Headcount** (OSHA ITA outcome 11): kind `employees`, the annual average employees as of December 31 of the filing year, undated when the year does not read, with the hours worked beside it, when the employees read and are above zero and the hours agree with them. The hours agree when they are **absent**, the fact empty once spaces are trimmed, or when they read, are above zero and lie between 500 and 4,000 hours per employee, both ends included. Hours that are present and zero, or present and unreadable (`n/a`, `47,840`), do not agree: the row gives no headcount, and its figures stay on the reading. So Hjort Excavating's 2025 row of 23 employees over 47,840 hours gives 23 with 47,840 beside it, and 152,574 employees over 92 hours gives none.
- **Several sites** (OSHA ITA outcome 12). A business with several sites files a row per site; each row is a reading and a link of its own, so the card shows each site. The source reads one row and knows no other: the sum of a business's sites in one year, and counting once two rows of the same count and the same hours, such as Rosenbauer Minnesota's 2024 summary entered twice, are the judge's (`D13-judging`, OSHA ITA P6.3), and `link_facts` gives one headcount per link with its own hours (`D16-enrichers`).
- **Dates** (OSHA ITA outcome 13). The filing date is the calendar date of the string fact `date_submitted`, read in any of its three spellings (`1/2/2018 8:16:46`, `01JAN22:11:14:00`, `1/1/2023`), at midnight UTC like every source's filing date; one that does not read leaves the row with no filing date. The known-since date is January 1 of the filing year, none when the year does not read.
- **Category** (OSHA ITA outcome 14, P6.4) is `sources.NAICSCategory` of the string fact `naics_code`, trimmed and read with the figures' tail dropped, so the 2019 file's `238910.00` is construction; its vintage (`naics_year`) is not read. An empty or non-digit code gives none.
- **Evidence** is `osha_ita_summary`, weight 0.2, for every row, one with nonsense figures or no headcount included (OSHA ITA outcome 15): a summary for a year shows the business operated that year, at the weight of a PPP loan or a Form 5500 filing.
- **Government match** is `sources.GovernmentMatch` of the normalized name, as for the others, so a school district's row matches `SCHOOL DISTRICT` from its name and gives its `employees` headcount like any row; the source's own establishment type is not read (OSHA ITA Deferred).

The band of 500 to 4,000 hours per employee is part of the rules: contract, not copy, the owner's number (OSHA ITA outcome 11). A revision is a new rules version and a full ingest.

## Recorded decisions

- **The facts an estimate copies** are `power_units`, `class`, `mcs150_date`, `total_drivers` and `mileage`, the five the formula, its confidence and its plausibility read, each present under the row's own key only when the row's facts hold it; `status` decides the fleet pass and `fmcsa_active`, not the estimate, and is not copied. `plausibility` is the one key of an estimate's facts that is a judgment rather than a copy, present only on a suspicious row; it cannot collide with a copied key, since scrapes' FMCSA rows carry no fact of that name.
- **The facts are scrapes' names, not FMCSA's columns.** scrapes renames some columns as it copies them into a row's facts: the raw `mcs150_mileage` is the fact `mileage`, with `mileage_year` beside it, and `add_date` is `added`, while `power_units`, `total_drivers` and `mcs150_date` keep theirs. The source reads and copies `mileage`; `mileage_year` feeds no rule and is not copied. Version 2 as first built read `mcs150_mileage`, which no row carries, so every row read as mileage-absent and real leasing fleets such as 1,828 units, one driver and 19,390,412 miles were dropped as implausible.
- **An estimate does not need the fleet pass.** A row whose power units read, that is not implausible and whose class tag is for hire gives an estimate whatever its status or count (decision 7 ties an unreadable count to "no estimate", version 2 adds an unbelievable one, version 6 a class without for-hire authority, and version 10 a mixed class). The fleet verdict is a stored fact; it leaves a candidate out of a prospect list only when that prospect list's `fleet` filter is carried (`D14-prospect-lists`).
- **The source stops believing counts that cannot be true.** Under version 1 a one-person carrier reporting 199,999 power units and no miles led the ranked list. Only the obviously implausible is rejected: drivers reported, units more than fifty times the drivers, no miles. Such a row gives no estimate, no fleet pass, an unknown fleet verdict, no counts, no headcount and no evidence, and records no reason anywhere: it is of unknown size, not out of scope. The band between ten and fifty times the drivers, or past fifty with miles driven, holds real leasing fleets whose drivers are not on the carrier's books, so its estimate stands at half confidence and says why in its facts. Blank drivers are not judged, since a missing count is no evidence against the units. The rule lives in the FMCSA source; judging, the prospect lists and the store keep their shape, and scrapes is untouched.
- **Rejected alternatives.** An absolute cap on power units: Schneider has 9,866 real units and a junk row has 10,000, so no cap separates them. Miles per unit: real fleets that are not trucking companies run a few hundred miles a unit. Rejecting the suspicious band too: it holds real leasing fleets. Fixing scrapes: it is a faithful copy of what FMCSA publishes. A ruling per junk row: exclusion means out of scope, and these are of unknown size.
- **Missing is never zero.** A row that gives no estimate adds no estimate, and one that gives no evidence adds no evidence; a candidate with neither has empty judgments, not zeros, and stays in every prospect list whose filters do not need them, sorted after the candidates whose values are known (`D14-prospect-lists`).
- **An implausible row still has a date, its class's category and a government match.** The plausibility rule rejects its counts, not its existence on FMCSA's books: its added date, the category its class tag gives and its name's match are facts like its address. Only evidence, the estimate, the counts, the headcount and a definite fleet verdict, which rest on the counts or vouch for the business operating, are withheld.
- **FMCSA gives trucking and logistics only with for-hire authority** (category evidence decisions 7 and 8). In the owner's prospect list of thirty, every for-hire carrier was a trucking business and no private one was: private registration means a company runs its own trucks for its own goods, as a school district, a contractor or a feed mill does, so it says what the company owns and nothing about its business. A mixed carrier holds for-hire authority beside its private operation and keeps the category; a brief corrects the ones that are something else (`D04-store`). A private or unknown carrier is classified by its other links, its name's government match or its brief, or is unknown. The class tag, cargo, fleet test and verdict, counts, estimate, evidence, known-since date and government match are unchanged. FMCSA cargo classes are deliberately not read for a category: they are a second heuristic the brief does better, which a later reading rule may add if the unknown pool proves too big.
- **The category change moves no rules version and needs a full ingest** (category evidence decisions 10 and 12). Categories feed no judgment (`D13-judging`), so the version stays where `D13-judging` states it. The category lives in the reading stored on the link, so a link stored before this change keeps trucking and logistics until a full ingest re-reads its row; `link_facts` reads the stored link (`D16-enrichers`). The release before this one, after a rollback, writes trucking and logistics again for every row it reads; accepted, as category evidence decision 11 accepts its re-projection.
- **Evidence carries base weights.** `fmcsa_active` is 0.6 on the reading whatever the filing date; the judge applies freshness at its own moment, so the formula lives once and a stored link never goes stale. `fmcsa_activity` needs both a driver count above zero and miles: a carrier reporting no drivers, or no miles, shows no activity. `own_domain` comes from FMCSA only in this change, since a PPP row carries no email.
- **Class parts are compared trimmed and case-folded**, so a stray space or a lower-cased export cannot turn a carrier `unknown`. The government class words now feed nothing: the document names only name patterns for the government match, and a government carrier's name almost always carries one.
- **Government name patterns match whole words** of the normalized name (`D12-ingest`'s `NameKey`, punctuation already stripped): `COUNTY` matches `PINE COUNTY HIGHWAY` and not `COUNTYLINE`, and `DEPT` not `DEPTFORD`. The list is exactly the document's, which closes it, and `sources.GovernmentMatch` answers the first pattern in the list's order, one word for a candidate's card. It applies to every source's name, PPP's included. A private business a pattern catches (a `COUNTY MATERIALS`) is now merely marked; nothing hides it.
- **Power units has a ceiling** of 36,893,488,147,419 (the largest `n` with `250,000 × n` in an `int64`); a larger value reads as no whole number rather than overflowing. The mileage feeds no formula and the driver count only the headcount, which the judge multiplies by a band with saturation (`D13-judging`), but they share the ceiling so every FMCSA count is read one way: each fits an `int64`, fifty times a driver count stays far inside one, and a mileage past it is garbage and counts as absent. PPP's jobs reported share it too, so every count a source reads is read one way.
- **A date must be a real date**: `20250230` is no filing or added date, and `02/30/2020` no approval date, so a corrupt field gives nothing rather than a guess.
- **A reading carries one headcount, not a list** (revenue bands decision 1): a row describes one business at one date and gives at most one count. The judge chooses among the headcounts of a candidate's links, which `link_facts` gathers into the facts row (`D16-enrichers`, `D04-store`); each headcount names its scraper and kind, and carries its date, so the facts row, the card and the judge's ranking need nothing else of the link.
- **PPP's jobs are read as FMCSA's counts are** (revenue bands decision 2): digits only, the same ceiling, so `12.0`, a blank or a sign gives none rather than a guess. Zero is none, since a loan reporting no jobs says nothing about size. The headcount is dated by the approval date, when the borrower reported it.
- **FMCSA's headcount is its drivers, dated by its filing** (revenue bands decision 3): the driver count is already read under the plausibility rule, so an implausible row gives none, as it gives no counts, and a suspicious row keeps its drivers, since the rule doubts its units, not its drivers. The filing date is when the carrier last reported the count.
- **The truck estimate survives only where trucks are the business** (revenue bands decision 12). A private carrier's trucks say what it owns, not what it earns: a utility contractor with five trucks and twenty drivers scored $750,000 against a third-party $4M. Unknown is better than wrong, so a private or unknown-class row gives no estimate at any confidence, and its drivers reach its size through the headcount. A mixed carrier held for-hire authority and kept its estimate, as it keeps trucking and logistics, until estimate inversion outcome 1 (below). The halving of a suspicious row is unchanged. A for-hire carrier with a plausible count and a headcount has both estimates, combined as before (`D13-judging`).
- **A mixed carrier's trucks give no estimate** (estimate inversion outcome 1). The owner's band table says that for a private or mixed carrier the trucks say nothing about its size and the headcount does, so a mixed row gives no estimate, as a private or unknown one does, and its drivers reach its size through the headcount. Its low-only estimate was the one estimate with no high, and the judge's combination let it pull a candidate's combined low above its combined high (`D13-judging`). Rejected: giving mixed a high of 250,000 per unit, which would keep a size the owner says the trucks do not show. The class tag, the category, the fleet verdict, the drivers headcount and the for-hire estimate and its halving are unchanged. The change moves the rules version `D13-judging` states and lands by a full ingest (estimate inversion log D1), which re-reads every row, so no mixed truck estimate stays stored on a link.
- **PPP gives no estimate** (revenue bands decision 13): its payroll formula beside the `headcount` estimate would count one row twice in the combined mean and make the card harder to read. The source no longer reads the amount, so the PPP amount's reading, its ceiling and the facts the estimate copied go with the estimate, and so does the judge's rule that a candidate's largest PPP amount gives its PPP estimate (`D13-judging`). `ppp_loan` evidence is unchanged.
- **No source forms a headcount estimate.** The band it needs is chosen from the candidate's categories and brief labels, which no single row holds, so the estimate is a judgment and the judge forms it (`D13-judging`); a source reports the count and nothing more. Rejected: computing it in each source.
- **The approval date is strict `MM/DD/YYYY`**, the SBA's form: `4/9/2020` gives none. Moving it two years earlier uses calendar arithmetic, so 02/29/2020 established is 2018-03-01.
- **Established is the SBA's exact phrase.** The phrase is data a program reads, not copy; any other value, a different case or a trailing space included, leaves the approval date as is.
- **NAICS is trimmed, then read by prefix.** scrapes keeps the code's spaces (` 238220 `), so the source trims them; a code of one digit, or a two-digit code such as `48` or `81` that only a longer prefix names, is read like any other and falls to other. The longest matching prefix wins. No prefix in the table begins another today, so the rule decides nothing yet; it is stated so that a later, narrower prefix inside a listed sector (a three-digit code inside a two-digit one) takes its rows without reordering the table.
- **The table follows the NAICS sectors** (categories decision 2), so the PPP codes and the brief share one list and the mapping falls out of the codes rather than anyone's judgment. Agriculture takes the whole of sector 11, adding forestry and logging (113) and fishing (114), and mining the whole of sector 21, adding oil and gas extraction (211): both move physical goods and are rare. Repair shops (811) do not move goods, so they stay non-target, in a category of their own a prospect list can pick out. The SIC codes a later source may carry are not mapped. The list is 23 words; the categories document's "twenty-four" miscounts the same list. A full ingest re-reads every PPP row under the table; the rules version does not move, since categories feed no judgment (`D13-judging`).
- **A PPP row has an empty class, not `unknown`**, no fleet pass, an unknown fleet verdict and no counts: PPP says nothing about carriage. A prospect list's `class` filter leaves a candidate with no class out unless its unknown switch is set, which admits an empty class and `unknown` alike (`D14-prospect-lists`). A PPP row's county is the state and the county's name (`MN-HENNEPIN`), where FMCSA's is the FIPS code (`MN-115`); the county is shown, not matched, so the two need not agree.
- **Every PPP row gives `ppp_loan`**, an unreadable date or jobs count included: the loan exists, so the business did.
- **The fleet verdict has three values, and the reading holds it** (cohorts decision 13, decisions log 11). A company with no FMCSA record used to read "no fleet" as if FMCSA had counted its trucks; it now reads unknown. The `link_facts` enricher reads the stored link, not the scraped row, and an implausible row is stored just like one whose units do not read, so the verdict is decided here, per row, and `link_facts` takes the freshest FMCSA link's into the candidate's facts (`D16-enrichers`), which the judge copies (`D13-judging`). A full ingest re-reads every row and then re-derives every candidate's link facts, which is what makes the stored verdicts right after a change to the rule.
- **An active row whose power units do not read is unknown** (decisions log 12): it says nothing about fleet size either way, which is the case the third value exists for. An inactive row is fail whatever its units, readable or not, since the document's rule is pass only when active with two or more units and fail otherwise; only an implausible row and an active row with unreadable units are excepted.
- **`Fleet` is still written**, true exactly when the verdict is pass (decisions log 11): the release before this one reads `Fleet` from links this release writes, and a rollback must keep working. The next release can stop writing it.
- **The reading carries power units and drivers** (decisions log 20), so `link_facts` (`D16-enrichers`) can give a candidate its trucks and drivers from the same freshest FMCSA link its verdict comes from, without going back to the estimate's copied facts, which an implausible row does not have. An implausible row carries neither (decisions log 28): its counts are not trusted, as its verdict says. They are the counts the plausibility rule reads, so a count past the ceiling is none here too.
- **A link stored before this change has no verdict.** It decodes with an empty `FleetVerdict` and no counts; whoever reads it treats empty as unknown (`D16-enrichers`, `D10-tools`), and a full ingest rewrites it.
- **The rules version is `D13-judging`'s.** It moves when what a judge writes changes, and `D13-judging` states its current value; every estimate a source gives carries `sources.Rules` as before, so after a full ingest every estimate carries the version `D13-judging` states. Version 6 (revenue bands decision 15) changes the sources' formulas, the first version since 2 to do so: FMCSA's private and unknown-class estimates and PPP's estimate are gone, and the readings carry headcounts.
- **A link stored before this change has no headcount.** It decodes with a nil `Headcount`, and a PPP link stored before it keeps its payroll estimate, until a full ingest re-reads every row under version 6 (revenue bands decision 20). The release before this one, after a rollback, reads a link this release wrote without its headcount, so a candidate known only from PPP has no revenue under it until it re-reads that row, when it writes the payroll estimate again and no headcount; accepted, as category evidence decision 11 accepts its re-projection.
- **The DBA is split from the fact `sponsor_name`, not from the row's name.** Form 5500 decision 13 names "the sponsor name", and the plan-derived name is read from the fact `plan_name`; reading both from the facts as received keeps the derived names independent of how scrapes' envelope normalizes the row's name, and the row's name is the same sponsor name in every row scrapes writes today. A row whose facts give no `sponsor_name` has no DBA names.
- **A separator is a whole word by its spaces.** Each separator carries one space on each side, so `DBA HOLDINGS LLC`, `ADBA SERVICES` and `SMITH-DBA-JONES` hold none; only the first separator splits, so a second stays inside the part after it. Each part is trimmed of white space and nothing else, its punctuation left for the name key to drop.
- **The plan name is cut at the leftmost plan word**, whichever word of the table it is: `ACME HEALTH AND 401K PLAN` gives `ACME`, not `ACME HEALTH`. A plan word is compared without regard to ASCII case, as FMCSA's class parts are, so a lower-cased filing cuts like an upper-cased one, and is a whole word when the bytes on either side of it are not ASCII letters or digits, so `PLANET`, `LIFEWAY` and `401KPLUS` hold none. The trailing `AND`, `&`, `-` and `,` are dropped for as long as one ends the name, `AND` only as a word of its own, so `ACME & - PENSION PLAN` gives `ACME` while `BRAND 401K PLAN` and `HIGHLAND PLAN` keep their names. A plan name with no plan word is a name already (`HEGGIES SLICE OF THE PIE`) and is kept whole; one that begins with a plan word gives an empty name, which is left out.
- **The plan words are a table in the reading**, contract and not copy, unexported (`D01-layout-and-run-seam`): the requirement that defines the plan-derived name lists them, and a revision is a new requirement and a full ingest.
- **Brown Hawk carries four names**: the row's own name, `BROWN HAWK LLC DBA NORTHERN LIGHTS STEEL FAB`, then the three derived names. Form 5500 decision 13's rule puts the derived names beside the row's names, and the rule wins over its example and the Verification bullet, which list only the three derived; those are read as naming what is among its names.
- **Derived names never repeat a key.** A derived name whose key is that of a name already carried, the row's own or an earlier derived one, adds nothing to matching and would only crowd the card, so it is left out; the row's own names are kept as the row gives them.
- **Only a single-employer plan counts its sponsor's people** (Form 5500 decision 16), and the codes are compared exactly as received: the forms number their entity codes differently, `2` single-employer on a Form 5500 and `1` on a 5500-SF, and anything else, a space included, is not single-employer.
- **Active participants at year end first** (Form 5500 decision 17): it is the count nearest the people employed; the total at the beginning of the year is the fallback because every form has always carried it. Zero is none, as for PPP's jobs, and a count past the ceiling is none, so every count a source reads is read one way. Several plans of one sponsor give several headcounts; the judge keeps the greatest of the newest (`D13-judging`).
- **Form 5500 dates are strict `YYYY-MM-DD`**, real calendar dates at midnight UTC, the form the scraper passes through: `2023-02-29` and `12/31/2024` give none.
- **The NAICS table is one function** (Form 5500 decision 19): `sources.NAICSCategory` holds the table PPP's reading held, unchanged, so a PPP row reads the category it read before and a Form 5500 row reads its code the same way, and the OSHA source the plan names next will too. The requirement that defined the PPP NAICS category is replaced by the one that defines the function; what a PPP reading gives does not change.
- **Every Form 5500 row gives `form5500_filing`**, a final filing included: the filing exists, so the business did in its plan year. Its weight is applied as given; freshness for evidence other than `fmcsa_active` is a later judge change. Its description is a copy constant declared beside `ppp_loan`'s, in the one requirement that declares the sources' evidence descriptions.
- **The keys need a full ingest.** A link stored before this change holds no name keys beyond its name key and no EIN key, until a full ingest re-reads its row under the rules version `D13-judging` states (7, Form 5500 decision 21); `D12-ingest` says what such a link holds for matching.
- **A source shares nothing with its row**: the reading's slices and maps are its own, so the link the store writes cannot change when the streamed row is reused.
- **The source pads an eight-digit EIN** (OSHA ITA outcome 10): OSHA's file drops the leading zero on hundreds of rows a year (481 in 2022, 807 in 2023), and KTM Paving's nine years join only if `10739420` is read as `010739420`. The padding is the OSHA ITA source's own; `sources.EINKey` keeps its nine-digit rule (`D12-ingest`), since Form 5500's EINs are never short. Only exactly eight digits are padded: a shorter number would need a guess of more than one zero and could join strangers, so it gives no key.
- **A figure takes the 2019 file's tail and nothing else.** Digits with a point and zeros after them are the same whole number, since the 2019 file appends `.00` to every numeric column; `23.5` is a fraction no annual average or hours total should hold, so it gives none rather than a rounded guess. The figures share the ceiling every count a source reads shares. The NAICS code drops the same tail before the table reads it, or the 2019 file's codes would give no category for a whole year.
- **Only an empty hours fact is absent.** The document lets a count stand with its hours absent; an empty cell, or one of spaces, is absent. A value that is there but does not read (`n/a`, `47,840`, a number past the ceiling) is a typo, not a missing value, and since the hours are the check on the count, the count is withheld rather than trusted unchecked. Hours of zero are present and fail the band: zero hours worked by a positive count is the kind of entry the band exists to catch.
- **The band is exact.** The hours agree when 500 times the employees is at most the hours and the hours are at most 4,000 times the employees, compared as whole numbers; no division and no rounding decides a boundary.
- **A row outside the band keeps its figures** (OSHA ITA P6.2). The reading carries the establishment's name, the year, the employees and the hours as read, whatever the band says, so the owner sees why a site gave no headcount; the link still gives its evidence and its category. Figures that do not read at all are absent, not zero.
- **The headcount is as of the end of its filing year.** An annual average describes the year, so it is dated December 31, and the newest year wins among a candidate's `employees` headcounts (`D13-judging`). The filing date is when the summary was sent and the known-since date the year's first day, since the business employed people through that year.
- **The filing date is a date.** The submission timestamp is read in its three spellings and its time of day dropped, so an OSHA ITA filing date is midnight UTC like every other source's and sorts with them; the 2021 spelling's two-digit year reads as Go's layout reads it, and its month is matched without regard to case.
- **The establishment name is always carried**, also when it equals the company name: it is the site's name as read, and the card shows it beside the year and figures; it is a display name, never a name key.
- **The reading's names are the row's name alone, not the row's names** (OSHA ITA P11). scrapes publishes the establishment name as a second name when it differs, and candidates diverges and does not take it as a name: the sandbox check found place-like site names, such as `Ramsey` and `Delavan`, keying unrelated businesses together at a ZIP, Molin Concrete's Ramsey rows joining Forpak Automation's candidate with Multisource, Life Fitness and Cybex. The company name alone keys a row, or the establishment name when the company name is empty, since that is then the row's name; the establishment name stays in `Establishment` for display. KTM Paving's EIN-less 2017 row still joins by its company name at its ZIP, and Molin's Lino Lakes rows still join by theirs; Molin's Ramsey site becomes a candidate of its own at its own ZIP, recorded, not fixed. The rules version stays 8: this is the same unreleased change.
- **One reading per site; the sum is the judge's** (OSHA ITA P6.3). A source reads one row and nothing else, so it cannot sum. Each site is a link with its own headcount and hours, which `link_facts` keeps one per link; the judge sums the sites of a business's newest year and counts an identical pair once (`D13-judging`).
- **The government match comes from the name only.** A school district, a county or a city files like an employer; the name patterns mark it, as for every source, and the establishment type code that would flag it directly is deferred (OSHA ITA Deferred).
- **The OSHA ITA source moves the rules version to 8** (OSHA ITA P2): it adds evidence and headcounts the judge reads, so the first ingest after deploy is a full ingest that re-reads every row under one version; `D13-judging` states the value. A link stored before this change has an empty establishment name and no figures or hours, and a rollback's release reads a link this one wrote without them.
