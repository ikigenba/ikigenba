# D09-trail

The trail as a whole: the closed set of event names candidates records, the request pair the telemetry middleware records for every request, deliveries from the bus as appkit records them, `sibling.called` for every read of scrapes and every request to prompts, the two pass events as the assembled run shows them, the clock and the random source every event is stamped and named from, and decision 18's secrecy, stated once for the whole app. It is scrapes' trail document with the pass events in place of the run events and appkit's `sibling.called` in place of `source.called`.

Who tells each event is another document's. The request pair is appkit's `telemetry.Middleware`'s, which `D03-serve` mounts on every request; `tool.called` is appkit's MCP server's; `service.started` and `service.stopping` are `D03-serve`'s; `event.lost` is appkit's emitter's, since the emitter is built with the writer; `sibling.called` is appkit's `scrapes` client's and appkit's `prompts` client's, each of which records one for every request it sends, as every sibling client of appkit does. `pass.started` and `pass.finished`, their attributes, their identity and their cause are `D07-events`'; when each is told is `D06-passes`'. This document states what the assembled run shows of them.

Decisions cited by number are the decisions document that delivered candidates; "cohorts decision N" is entry N of the decisions document of the cohorts change, and "cohorts log N" entry N of its decisions log. "Site text decision N" is entry N of the decisions document of the site-text change, and "brief decision N" entry N of the decisions document of the brief change. "Website search decision N" is outcome N of the decisions document of the website-search change, and "S N" entry DN of its decisions log. "Campaigns decision N" is entry N of the decisions document of the campaigns change, and "campaigns log DN" entry DN of its delivery log.

## The request pair and deliveries

Every request, whatever its path and whether or not it carries an identity, is recorded by the telemetry middleware as one `request.started` (method, path) and one `request.finished` (status, duration, bytes), under its request id and its caller's user (`D03-serve`). A delivery from the bus is a request like any other, `POST /events` with the events app's `X-Request-Id` and no user: appkit's delivery handler records nothing of its own, so a delivery is recorded as that request pair, its record read as a `sibling.called`, and the pass it starts tells its events, and its reads their `sibling.called`, under the same request id (decision 18). A judge fire, a delivery of `cron.judge_candidates.fired`, is a delivery like any other: its request pair and, under its request id, the pass events of the pass it starts, if any (a skipped or busy judge delivery starts no pass and leaves only the request pair), and no `sibling.called`, since a judge pass reads nothing from scrapes and calls no sibling (`D06-passes`). A campaign delivery, of `campaign.member.added`, `campaign.member.rejected` or `campaign.piece.delivered`, is recorded by its request pair alone, whatever it answers: it starts no pass, calls no sibling and tells no event of its own, its one write to the store being seen only in the uses the `candidate` tool and page show (`D06-passes`, `D07-events`, R-XKCL-21UF).

## sibling.called

One `sibling.called` per request to scrapes (decision 18): `target` `scrapes`, `method` `GET`, `path` the read's path (`/scrapers/<name>/current`, `/runs/<id>/record` or `/runs/<id>/rows`), the status answered (0 when none came), and `duration_us`, as appkit's client records it. Its request id and user are those of the identity the read is made as (`D06-passes`): a full ingest's reads name its caller and the tool call's request id; a delivery's reads name `candidates`, the service identity, and the delivery's request id (decision 12), so the trail shows which reads no person made. Paths carry run ids and scraper names only; no row, query or body travels in it.

One `sibling.called` per request to prompts (site text decision 21), recorded by appkit's `prompts` client, the installed interface D27 of appkit describes: it sends every call, `show`, `run` or `result`, as one `tools/call` POST to `/mcp` on the socket the services file's `prompts` entry names, so the event has `target` `prompts`, the entry's name, `method` `POST`, `path` `/mcp`, the status answered and `duration_us`. candidates records nothing of its own for a prompts request. Every one is made as the caller of the `enrich` that needs it, forwarded on the request (site text decision 7, `D06-passes`): the `show` the call makes before it starts a pass, and the `run` and `result` calls of the pass, which runs on after the call is answered, all name that caller's user and the tool call's request id. The path names no prompt, run or input; what candidates sends prompts, a company's id, name, city and state, and, for `site_text` and `brief`, its website, for `brief` also its facts, the categories and its people's names and roles among them, and the titles and text of the pages kept on it, and, for `website_search`, no website and no page but its street, ZIP and phone, its email's domain and what the website check found there, its people's names and roles and its categories (website search decision 3, `D16-enrichers`), travels in the request body, which no event carries; so does what prompts answers, a brief's or a website search's standard output among it. The fetch that confirms the site a website search names is the website checker's, as the check's own fetches are, and records no event (website search decision 5). A delivery's pass and an ingest's follow-on never call prompts, since a kindless enrich leaves out every kind that runs prompts (`D06-passes`). candidates calls no other sibling, so no `sibling.called` names a target but `scrapes` and `prompts`.

## The clock and the random source

Every event's time is read from `Process.Now`, and every request id `Run` mints, every pass id and every bus event id are drawn from `Process.Rand` (`D01-layout-and-run-seam`). Both are seams `cmd/candidates` fills with `time.Now` and `crypto/rand.Reader`; a test fills them with a fixed clock and a constant reader and finds the stamps and ids those give.

## Secrecy

Events, the trail, refusals, standard error and the logs carry ids, names of scrapers and tools, words and counts only (decision 18). No business name, address, phone, email, person, ruling or reject reason, prospect list name, kept page's title or text, brief's summary, reasons or labels, website search's named or rejected sites, reasons, or confirming page's URL or title, or campaign reject's reason reaches the trail, the bus, standard output or standard error, on success or failure; candidates writes no log of its own, and a serving run writes standard error only as `D03-serve` says. Rows and what candidates makes of them reach the callers' tool answers and the signed-in pages, which is what they are for, and nowhere else. A refusal's sentence, a delivery's failure message and an error page are fixed copy constants and templates, but for three refusals that carry a detail (`D10-tools`): `enrich`'s missing prompt names the prompt and quotes prompts' refusal, `add_page`'s failed fetch quotes the failure, which may hold the URL the caller gave, and `add_page`'s cap names the cap. None of them holds a value a row, scrapes' answer or the store supplied, so no row's contents, no `name` or `phone` filter, no prospect list's name and no ruling's or reject's why can reach them. The paging refusals are appkit's sentences, `paging.BadPage` and `paging.Window`'s, which hold the bounds and the offending `limit` and never the `page` (`D10-tools`).

Paging adds nothing to the trail: reading a page of `prospect_lists`, `candidates` or `passes` is a tool call recorded by appkit's `tool.called`, which carries no arguments, so neither a `page` a caller sent nor a `next` candidates answered reaches an event, the bus, a stream or a log line. A `candidates` or `passes` token holds only a number or a time and an id, but a `prospect_lists` token holds a prospect list's name, which a person wrote, and the landing's prospect list window takes the same token in its `prospect_lists_page` parameter (`D08-pages`); the request pair records the path and never the query, so the token stays out of the trail there too. A token is the caller's handle on a position and the rule is kept whole, so the run that proves secrecy also searches its captures for every token, and pages the landing with one.

The rule is proved at the run seam, by use, as scrapes proves its own: a fake scrapes answers rows whose names, street, city, people, phone and email are random **markers**; some of its answers are failures whose bodies echo every marker; a fake prompts answers `show`, `run` and `result` with no marker, refuses some, ends some runs failed, and fails some requests with bodies that echo every marker; the website check and `add_page` fetch through a transport the test holds, whose pages hold markers in their titles and text, so a company's website, found from its email's domain, holds that domain's marker; the test's `candidates` calls filter by a marker, its `create_prospect_list` and `edit_prospect_list` calls name prospect lists with markers, and its `decide`, `exclude`, `include`, `reject` and `unreject` calls give a marker as why; every tool, page and delivery is exercised, `add_page` with pages kept and fetches failed and `enrich` with `site_text` over a prospect list of members holding markers, with passes ending `done`, `failed` with `scrapes`, `failed` with `prompts` and `failed` with `interrupted`; and every capture of the run is searched, letters compared without regard to case, since candidates upper-cases names into keys and lower-cases domains: both sinks, in every field and as JSON, both streams, every refused tool result and every answer to `/events`. The brief is proved on the same run (R-WYEE-66HX): after `add_page` has kept pages on members of a prospect list holding markers, an `enrich` of `brief` over that prospect list runs the prompt for them, and the fake prompts answers that pass's `result` calls with standard output whose summary, labels and every category's reason hold every marker and phone marker, one category named by a category word and one by a marker, exited 0 and exited 1, and with output that is not JSON and holds every marker, so the brief's summary, a category's reason and the labels reach the store and the facts row; once the pass ends the test reads them back through `candidate`, `prospect_list` and the members' and prospect list's pages, and every capture is searched the same way. The website search is proved on the same run too (R-NY14-F797): an `enrich` of `website_search` over a prospect list holding markers, some of whose members have no website on record, runs the prompt for those, and the fake prompts answers that pass's `result` calls with standard output whose named site and every rejected site hold a marker and whose reason and every rejected site's reason hold every marker and phone marker, exited 0 and exited 1, with one naming no site, and with output that is not JSON and holds every marker; the transport answers one named site with a page whose title and text hold every marker and phone marker, so the site is confirmed, recorded on the facts row and its page's title kept, and another with a 404 whose title holds a marker, so the confirmation records the failure; once the pass ends the test reads them back through `candidate`, `prospect_list` and the members' and prospect list's pages, and every capture is searched the same way. The database failing is proved where the test hands the store in, as candidates' test discipline requires: a handler built over the test's own store, with the same fakes, the same transport and the same markers, has its handle failed with `SetFailing(true)` while a pass reads rows holding markers, and is then called on every tool, page and delivery; both captures, every refused tool result and every answer to `/events` are searched the same way. The handler writes neither stream, so the streams are searched at the run seam alone.

## Recorded decisions

- The closed set is scrapes' with `source.called` replaced by `sibling.called` and the two run names by the two pass names: candidates calls no source of its own, and scrapes and prompts are siblings reached through appkit's clients.
- **prompts adds a target, not a name** (site text decision 21): its requests are recorded by appkit's `prompts` client as `sibling.called`, with the entry's name `prompts` as target, `POST` and `/mcp`, so the closed set is unchanged, and candidates records no event of its own around a run, since appkit's client already records each request and the pass events carry the runs' counts (`D07-events`).
- **A delivery's reads are recorded as `candidates`** (decision 12): the user of their `sibling.called` is the service identity, while the pass events keep the delivery's starter, which has no user (`D07-events`). The two share the delivery's request id, so a delivery's whole trail is found under it.
- A delivery is recorded by the request pair alone, as appkit's delivery handler records it: the handler takes no writer and records nothing; its outcome is the answer's status and body, which the middleware's `request.finished` carries as the status.
- Markers are compared without regard to letter case and phone markers as digits, because candidates' normalized keys upper-case names, lower-case email domains and keep a phone's last ten digits (`D12-ingest`); a leak of a key is a leak of the value.
- A `name` or `phone` filter given to `candidates` and a ruling's why are treated as record text: each may be a business's name, number or a person's words, so each is a marker in the secrecy conditions and never reaches the trail, a refusal or a delivery's answer.
- **Every person-written text is a marker** (cohorts log 31): a prospect list's `name`, given to `create_prospect_list` and `edit_prospect_list`, and the `why` given to `reject`, `unreject`, `exclude` and `include`, as to `decide`, are words the owner wrote and may name a business or a person, so the secrecy conditions give a marker in each, both on the run and on the failing handler. The cohorts change adds no event (cohorts decision 26): a prospect list tool call is appkit's `tool.called`, which carries no arguments, so the closed set and the request pair are unchanged. Filter values, order entries and argument names hold no marker: they are enumerated words and numbers, and appkit's schema refusal names an unknown argument and echoes a number out of range before the handler runs (`D10-tools`), so a marker there would test appkit, not candidates.
- **A kept page is record text**: its title and text are what a company says about itself, so the transport's pages hold markers in both, and the trail, the bus and the streams must hold neither. They reach the `add_page` and `candidate` answers and the candidate and text pages, which is what they are for.
- **Departure: refusals that carry variable text** (`D10-tools`' rule that a refusal is a word and a fixed sentence, and decision 18, which keeps refusals to ids, names and words). Three refusals carry a detail: `enrich`'s missing prompt names the prompt and quotes prompts' refusal (site text decision 7), `add_page`'s failed fetch quotes the failure (site text decision 2), and `add_page`'s cap names the cap (site text decision 3). The prompt's name is the kind's and the cap a constant, so neither can hold a marker. prompts' refusal is prompts' sentence about a prompt candidates named, so the secrecy conditions make no prompts answer a marker source: the fake's refusals and successes hold none, but for the brief's standard output, which R-WYEE-66HX gives markers, and the website search's, which R-NY14-F797 gives markers (below). A failed fetch's text may hold the URL the caller gave, which is on the company's website and so holds its domain's marker; the caller sent it and gets it back, and it reaches no event, so the refusal requirements (R-OW6B-4S0N, R-QCLZ-KWWI) let a refused `add_page` result hold the markers its own `url` holds and no other, while the trail and stream requirements make no exception. A leak of the company's email domain into that one refusal cannot be told from the URL there, which is accepted: it is the host the caller named.
- **prompts' failure bodies echo markers**, as scrapes' do: candidates sends prompts a company's name, city and state, for `site_text` and `brief` its website, for `brief` its people and its kept pages' titles and text, and for `website_search` its street, phone, people and email's domain, so a failing prompts may answer with them, and nothing of a failed request's body may reach a refusal, the trail or a stream. Under R-21BB-JRQ3's conditions only the answers candidates may relay, its refusals and the run's members, hold no marker; R-WYEE-66HX and R-NY14-F797 add the successes that hold them.
- **A brief's output is record text** (brief decisions 5 and 6): release-47 is the first release that stores what a prompts run wrote, a brief's summary, the reason for each category and its labels, decoded from the run's standard output. The model wrote them from the company's name, people and pages, so they may repeat any of them, and they reach the store, the facts row, the `candidate` and `prospect_list` answers and the pages, and nowhere else. R-WYEE-66HX proves it in the "and, besides" form of R-YTGV-7KA4: the fake prompts' brief outputs hold every marker in the summary, the labels and every category's reason, with one category named by a category word, so that its reason is kept, and one named by a marker, so that it moves to the labels, in a decoded one exited 0 and exited 1 and in one that is not JSON; and once the pass ends the test reads the stored brief back through `candidate`, `prospect_list` and the pages, so a leak met only when the brief is answered or rendered is provoked. - Secrecy is required of the whole run, because the leak it guards against is a wiring one only the assembled process shows. What the tools and the pages answer is `D10-tools`' and `D08-pages`'.
