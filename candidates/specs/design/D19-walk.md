# D19-walk

The walk: the owner names industrial parks, and candidates searches each with Google Places in circles, so the businesses that sit in a park and that no registry lists enter the pool as candidates (walk outcomes 1 to 13). A **walk** is one pass of kind `walk`, started only by the `walk` tool naming parks; it searches each named park in turn, keeps every place Google answered as a row under the walk and the park, matches each place to a candidate or creates one, records on each park what the walk found there and what its requests cost, records the walk's parks, circles, requests, places, new candidates and cost on the pass row, and stops at the walk ceiling. Nothing else walks: no event, no start, no rules edit, no schedule and no other pass reaches Places (walk outcome 10).

This document owns the package `internal/places` whole, as `D17-drive-route` owns `internal/routes` and `D18-parks` owns `internal/parks`: the Nearby Search client and its seam, the circle, the cover of a full circle, the sweep of one park, the reading of a place (`places.Reading`), the address, domain and category rules, the walk ceiling's bounds, and the in-process fake the sandbox searches. It also owns, as `D12-ingest` owns the behavior of `store.Attach`, the behavior of the store's walk methods (`store.RecordSweep`, `store.FinishWalk`, `store.FailWalk`, `store.PassWalks`, `store.ParkWalks`, `store.ParkPlaces`, `store.ParkCandidates`, `store.CandidatePark` and `store.WalkCeiling`) and the **places attach rule**; the behavior of `Engine.Walk` and of the walk's work; the `walk` tool's behavior; `cli.Run`'s choice of the Places client; and the walk data the pages and the tools show. The declarations live where their packages' declarations live: `D04-store` declares the walk's records, constants and methods, the walk counts on the pass, the `walk_ceiling` of the park rules and migration `0024_walk.sql`, and states what `GetPark` answers of a park's walk; `D01-layout-and-run-seam` the package's place, `passes.Config.Places`, `Engine.Walk` and the pass record's walk members; `D06-passes` the walk's copy constants and bound, its counts, how a walk ends and its diagnostic; `D07-events` the walk counts on `pass.finished`; `D08-pages` the page data types, the pass view's walk fields and who started a walk; `D10-tools` the tool's argument and answer types, its schema, its description and the catalog; `D12-ingest` a candidate's display, which a places link joins only when the candidate has no other link; `D16-enrichers` the link facts' categories, where a places category is a weak hint.

"Walk outcome N" is outcome N of the decisions document of the walk change, and "walk decision N" is decision N of its delivery log. "Parks decision N" and "parks outcome N" are as `D18-parks` cites them; "drive route decision N" as `D17-drive-route` cites it.

## The client

`places.Client` holds the `*http.Client` every request goes through, the API key and the `After` each request's timeout waits on, as `routes.Client` does. `Nearby(ctx, c)` asks Google's Places API (New) Nearby Search for the places in one circle and answers one `places.Answer`; it never returns an error, a failed request being part of the answer. The request is a `POST` to `places.NearbyURL` carrying the key in `X-Goog-Api-Key`, the field mask `places.FieldMask` in `X-Goog-FieldMask` and a JSON body restricting the search to the circle, centre and radius in metres, with `maxResultCount` `places.MaxResults`, 20, the most one request returns. The mask asks for the place's id, name, address, primary type, website and phone, and never for reviews, photos, hours or ratings (walk outcome 2); the walk never narrows a search by type. Nearby Search does not page, so a circle that comes back with twenty places may hold more, and is searched again in smaller circles.

An answer is **full** when it holds `MaxResults` places. A request is charged `places.PriceNanos` only when Google answered it 200 in the shape expected; a request that got no answer, was refused or came back misshapen costs nothing, as for the routes client (walk decision 4). The fail words and the timeout copy the routes client's, and so does the rule that the key and the request's URL never reach an answer's words; a refusal's status word is kept only when it reads as one, upper-case letters and underscores.

The endpoint, the headers, the mask, `maxResultCount`, the cap of 20, the absence of paging and the Enterprise price are the plan's proven facts. The body's `center.latitude` and `center.longitude`, the answer's member names, the empty answer `{}`, the error body's `status`, the US address format, the primary type words and the 50 km radius bound are taken from Google's published Places API (New) documentation, not observed; the first walk on `us` proves them (decided: no live request before it).

## Circles and the sweep

A park is searched first in one circle at its centre and radius (walk outcome 2), the radius held to `MinRadius` to `MaxRadius` (`places.First`). A full circle is covered by four circles (`places.Cover`) whose centres are the centres of its quadrants, half its radius north or south and east or west of its centre, each of radius `ChildRatio`, three quarters, of its own (walk decision 8, refined). Each quadrant of a disc of radius R lies inside the square of side R whose corner is the disc's centre, and that square's circumscribed circle, centred on the quadrant's centre, has radius R/√2, about 0.7071 R; three quarters of R leaves a margin of about 0.043 R for the flat approximation the centres are placed by, whose error over 50 km at Minnesota's latitudes is under one per cent of R. So every point of a full circle lies in one of its four.

`Client.Sweep` searches one park: it asks the first circle, then, breadth first, the four of every full circle, in the order north-west, north-east, south-west, south-east, until no circle is left or it has made as many requests as its budget allows. It keeps each place the first time it sees its Place ID, with the circle it came from. It stops at the first failed request, keeping what it found before. It reports the circles answered, the requests made and their cost, and whether it stopped at its budget with a circle left: **ceiling reached**.

## The walk

The `walk` tool names one park or several, at most `passes.MaxWalkParks`, and `again`, false unless the owner says to walk an already walked park again (walk decision 7). `Engine.Walk` refuses, in order: `stopping` while draining; `invalid` with the text `passes.NoPlacesKey` when the space holds no Maps key, so the engine has no Places client (walk outcome 11); `invalid` for an empty list, too many parks or a park named twice; `not_found` for an id that is not a park id or a park the store does not hold; `invalid` with the text `passes.ParkGone` and the ids for parks marked gone; `invalid` with the text `passes.AlreadyWalked` and the ids for walked parks named without `again`; and `busy` while another walk runs. Otherwise it records the pass, kind, origin and trigger `walk`, the caller as its starter, reads the walk ceiling once, and works in the background. A key Google rejects is not known before a request: the call answers `running`, the first request is refused and not charged, and the pass fails `places`, which the pass page shows (decided).

The work walks the parks in the order named. Before each park its budget is the ceiling less the requests the walk has made; a park reached with no budget left is never searched, and the walk stops with the ceiling reached. Each park's sweep is handed to the store in one write, `RecordSweep`, which keeps the places, matches or creates their candidates and, when at least one circle was answered, records the park's walk: its date, the walk, the places found and new candidates, the circles, requests and cost, and whether the ceiling cut it (walk outcomes 3 and 7). A park reached and cut off by the ceiling is walked, with the ceiling reached, so walking it again needs `again`; a park never reached is neither among the walk's parks nor walked (walk decision 9). A failed request stops the walk: no further park is swept and no further request made; the walk ends `failed` with the reason `places`; what it found stays, and the park in flight is walked when it had an answered circle, so its places show on its page (decided), and is not walked otherwise. A walk records its totals on the pass row whether it ends `done` or `failed`, parks walked, circles, requests, places found, new candidates and cost, with whether the ceiling stopped it (walk decision 1), and starts an enrich, as an ingest does, so its new candidates are enriched and then judged like any others (walk outcome 6).

## The places attach rule

A place found is matched in this order (walk decision 5): its **Place ID**, the source key within `places`; then its **phone key**, the ten digits of its phone; then its **domain key**, its website's host as an email domain is keyed, a shared host (a social network, a link page, a site builder's shared domain, a directory) giving none; then its **name key at its place**, its name with the state and ZIP its address gives, as `D12-ingest`'s name step reads it; otherwise it founds a candidate. Each finds the oldest active candidate holding the key, through its links, gone or not, or through its owner records, as `D12-ingest` defines what a candidate holds (R-EXUW-9NXM), so a business the owner added with no link is found rather than founded again; the Place ID alone is read from links, since an owner record holds none; a found link attaches by `phone` or `domain` at 0.85, by `match_key` at 0.8, and a founding link by `source_key` at 1.0. A place already linked keeps its candidate, its link moving its last seen, its walk and its park to the newest walk that found it. Within one walk a Place ID is kept once, under the park and the circle that first found it. A places link has no scrapes run and no row page; nothing marks it gone, and a walk removes no link, candidate or fact (walk outcome 4).

The **reading** of a place is a `sources.Reading` with its name, its address split into street, city, state and ZIP, its phone and keys, and a **category hint** from its primary type through one table onto the 23 category words, none where no word fits; it carries no government match. A place matched to a candidate that has another link adds the new link and the park, and the new link takes part in the candidate's display like any other (`D12-ingest`): the orders rank a place's name as a trade name ahead of every other, and its address and phone ahead of every registry's (sources outcomes 1, 5 and 7, `D05-sources`), so the matched candidate shows the place's name, address and phone, every other value kept beside them with its source; its class and cargo are still read from its links that are not places links, and its link facts take a places link's category only while every link it holds, gone or not, is a places link (`D16-enrichers`), the hint the owner settled as weak, so a matched candidate never gains a category and no groomed prospect list gains a matched candidate by it.

## Recorded decisions

- **Walk decision 1, a pass kind with its totals on the row.** A walk is a pass of kind `walk`, origin `walk`, trigger `walk`, with the caller as its starter. Its parks walked, circles, requests, places found, new candidates and cost are pass counts, as the parks pass's are and as `drive_route` records its `cost`, ending in the pass record and `pass.finished`, and, but for the parks walked, which the page shows as the parks covered, on the pass page, with whether the ceiling stopped it on the pass and its record (`D04-store`, `D06-passes`, `D07-events`); the pass counts become thirty-two. Each park's requests and cost are kept on its walk as well.
- **A failed walk keeps what it spent (departure from the failed-pass precedent; walk outcome 8, decided).** Every other failed pass's counts are 0; a walk that fails, by Places, the store or the drain, ends through `store.FailWalk` with the parks walked, circles, requests, places, new candidates and cost it had swept, answered and charged up to the failure, so the spend is on the pass. Only a walk the next start settles, its process having died, is failed by `Recover` with its counts at 0.
- **Walk decision 2, the ceiling.** `park_rules.walk_ceiling`, seed `places.SeedCeiling` (100), edited through `edit_park_rules`, bounded `places.CeilingMin` (1) to `places.CeilingMax` (5,000). The upper bound lets one walk cover every one of the 433 parks, about 1,600 requests by the plan, three times over, and caps one walk's list price at $175. Editing it, alone or with other rules, records a parks pass like any rules edit (walk decision 9). The validator is `places.ValidCeiling`, beside the client, not in `internal/parks`, whose rules are the derivation's. The ceiling is read once, when the walk is recorded.
- **The parks one call names.** `passes.MaxWalkParks` is 50: the owner names parks a handful at a time from the list sorted nearest first, and fifty bounds the refusal texts that name parks and the reads a call makes before it answers, well inside the gateway's fifty seconds, while the ceiling, not the list, bounds the spend.
- **Walk decision 3, the fake (departure from `D17-drive-route`).** Under `IKIGENBA_SANDBOX` the Places client is built over `places.Fake`, an `http.RoundTripper` that answers in-process and dials nothing, so no request to `places.NearbyURL` leaves a sandbox, where a `drive_route` pass in a sandbox reaches Google. The fake answers a circle of radius at least `FakeFullRadius` with a full answer, so a sandbox walk of a park of that radius goes on splitting and stops at the ceiling, and a smaller circle with `FakeFew` places. Its places carry no phone, website or ZIP, so they found new candidates and match none. The decision says a loopback fake, after the website stub; the stub listens because the website checker's real fetcher dials, while the Places client only needs a transport, so an in-process transport is the stronger form of the same rule (confirmed). Tests fake Google through their own `http.RoundTripper`. With no Maps key the client is nil in a sandbox as elsewhere, so the refusal is the same.
- **Walk decision 4, the price.** `places.PriceNanos` is 35,000,000 nano-dollars, $0.035, the list price of a Nearby Search request whose mask holds a website or phone field: Enterprise, $35 per 1,000, the highest SKU of any field in the mask deciding (the plan's Sources, Google Places API (New)); $35 per 1,000 is 35,000,000 nano-dollars per request at `routes.RoutePriceNanos`' scale, where $5 per 1,000 is 5,000,000. The decision log names the mask Pro; the evidence puts website and phone in Enterprise, and Enterprise is used. The free monthly allowance is not subtracted, so a cost is an upper bound. Only answered requests are charged; whether Google bills a refused request is not documented, and the routes client's rule is followed.
- **Walk decision 5, matching (departure from `D12-ingest`).** The attach rule matches by source key, EIN and name at a place, and keeps the phone and domain keys for later use; a place matches by Place ID, phone key, domain key and name key at its place, with no EIN step, since Places gives none. The phone key must be ten digits, so a seven-digit number never joins two businesses. The domain key is the website's host, one leading `www.` removed, read through `sources.DomainKey` as an email domain, empty for a free provider and for a host under one of the shared hosts `places.DomainKey` lists, so two shops whose site is a Facebook page, a link page, a site builder's subdomain or a directory listing never join by it. The phone, domain and name steps read the keys a candidate holds through its owner records as well as its links (the owner's decision after owner facts), with the oldest holder deciding as before, which is also how `D12-ingest` resolves a key held by several. Phone and domain confidences are 0.85, between the EIN's 0.9 and a name at a place's 0.8. The decision also says a places source "per the registry": the walk keeps `places` out of the source registry (departure from that wording), below.
- **A places link is a source outside the registry**, as `mn_parcels` is (`D18-parks`): `places.Name` is this package's constant, not a registry entry, so an ingest never reads it, a full ingest's attach never forces it, though its rebuild re-reads a places link from its place record (`D12-ingest`), and `MarkGone`, which walks one scraper's links per run, never meets it; the registry holds scrapes' sources, and a place is no scrapes row.
- **Outcome 6, a matched candidate gains no category** (decided; its display revised by the facts-from-the-candidate change). A places reading carries no government match, and its category counts only for a candidate every link of which, gone or not, is a places link (`D16-enrichers`), so a matched candidate gains no category and the finish line's groomed lists change only by new candidates. Earlier version: a matched candidate changed in nothing but the link and the park, its display read from its other links whenever it had one, and a founded candidate showed its place's name, address and phone only until another source's row joined it. Why: the owner's table of sources ranks Places' name, address and phone first (sources outcomes 1, 5 and 7, `D05-sources`), so the walk's values lead a candidate it matched and keep leading one it founded, every other source's kept beside them (`D12-ingest`).
- **The category table departs from D05's NAICS map in three places.** `car_rental` is other transportation, where NAICS 532 is real estate (rental and leasing): a Places car rental is a vehicle counter at an airport or a dealer, a transport business to the owner; `storage` is trucking and logistics, as NAICS 493 warehousing is in D05's map, since Places gives `storage` to warehouses and self-storage alike and warehousing is the segment's; `airport` is other transportation, as NAICS 481 air transportation is, where D05 maps 488 support activities to trucking, since the place is the airfield, not a freight forwarder. A hint, weak as above, and changed with the table when a walk shows otherwise.
- **What bounds a sweep's write and a park's places (departure from `shape/`'s Bounded work).** A sweep is held in memory and written by `RecordSweep` in one transaction, and `ParkPlaces` answers a park's places unpaged. Neither grows with the space: a sweep holds at most its budget times `MaxResults` places, 2,000 at the seed ceiling and 100,000 at `CeilingMax`, and a park's places are its walks' sweeps, a few hundred for the parks the plan expects (about 1,600 requests for all 433 parks). Paging them would split a sweep's places across transactions, so a failure could keep a park's places without its walk; the ceiling the owner sets is the bound, and a ceiling raised toward `CeilingMax` for one park is the owner's choice of a larger write.
- **Walk decision 6, the park on the card.** The candidate's card and the `candidate` tool show the park of its places link whose Place ID sorts first, a stable choice no walk or re-read moves (earlier: its newest places link, by last seen), and each places link shows its walk and park; the park page and the `park` tool show the park's walks, its places and the candidates found in it.
- **Walk decision 7, the tool.** One tool, `walk`, taking `parks` and `again`. A walked park named without `again` is refused naming it, and a park marked gone too (departure from `D10-tools`' rule that a refusal names no id): the refusal's text is the copy constant and the parks' ids, which are candidates' own ids and no business's data. The page offers no walk.
- **Walk decision 8, the circles.** Breadth first, so a walk cut by the ceiling has searched each level of a park whole before the next; four circles of three quarters, not half, the radius, since four half-radius circles cannot cover a circle; the first circle's radius held to `MinRadius` (50 m), since a park of parcels at one point has radius 0, and to `MaxRadius` (50 km), the most Nearby Search accepts by Google's documentation.
- **Walk decision 9, the assets' answers.** As recorded in the log: the card shows the newest places link's park (since revised to the places link whose Place ID sorts first, walk decision 6); a ceiling-cut park is walked with ceiling reached; phone and domain matches are the link reasons `phone` and `domain`, Place ID and name matches `source_key` and `match_key`; a places link has no run and no row page; editing only the ceiling re-derives the parks; the circle is stored, not shown; `.Park.WalkRun` became `.Park.Walk` on the page.
- **A park with answered requests is walked even when the walk then failed** (decided): its walk row holds what it found, so the park page, which shows places only beside walks, shows them; a park whose first request failed holds no place and no walk.
- **Every rule the walk makes false is re-minted** (decided): the display, the link facts' categories, `GetPark`'s answer, a pass's ending, its counts, its record and events, the pass view and who started a pass, with every requirement citing them; nothing of another document is stated here in place of its own text.
- **The store field keeps its name.** `store.Park.WalkRun` holds the pass id of the park's newest walk, and the `park` and `parks` tools answer it as `walk_run`, as before; the pages call it `Walk`, as the asset does. A tool member a dashboard may read is never renamed (walk outcome 12).
- **The secrecy rule quantifies over keys that are no copy** (decided). The client's fixed copy, `places.FailStatus` and every other exported string constant of `places`, is produced independently of the key, so a key equal to one of them would show in a failed answer's `Error` without the key having leaked; such a key is no credential. R-Y3PK-VAFC therefore holds for every non-empty `Key` that equals no exported string constant of `places`, and forbids the key in every string field of a returned value, its status and its error alike.
- **A walk is accepted without waiting for Places, not answered before it (departure from strict chronology)** (decided). appkit's MCP server gives a tool handler no signal that its answer has been written, so no registered server can order the walk's first request after the answer; publishing such a seam was rejected as a library release for a chronology nothing observes. R-Y65D-MTWQ instead requires that a `walk` call is answered `running` while every Places request is held open, and that a refused call makes no request and no write.
- **An unusable key is found by Google** (decided): the walk's first request is refused, not charged, the walk fails `places`, and the engine writes the client's fail word and Google's status word, never the key, to its diagnostics.
- **`GrownFrom` is not reset by a walk.** Re-walking a grown park is rejected (walk outcomes and Rejected), so grown stays a fact of the derivation.
- **A sweep is one write.** `RecordSweep` is not among the write units the store retries when the database is busy (R-DS8O-A9S0, `D04-store`); a busy sweep fails the walk `store`, keeping the parks recorded before it. Making it one would re-mint the definition and every requirement that lists the write units.
- **The rows a rolled-back release reads.** `RecordSweep` keeps the `walked`, `walk_run` and `found` columns `0023_parks.sql` gave a park equal to its newest walk, and `ApplyParks` leaves them, so release-70, run after a rollback, shows walked parks walked.

## Known limits

- **A cut sweep's places are lost on a drain.** A drain cancels the work, and the store refuses a write on a done context, so the places the park in flight had found are not kept and that park is not walked; what its requests cost is still on the pass. The next walk of that park needs no `again`.
- **Walk passes are pruned** (decided). A walk pass is pruned like any pass, so a park's walk row links to a pass page that is gone; the park's walk record keeps its date, places, new candidates, requests and cost.
- **A walk settled at start keeps no totals.** `Recover` fails a walk a dead process left running as it fails every pass, counts 0; its parks' walks keep what it had recorded.
- **The address is parsed, not given in parts.** The state and ZIP come from the formatted address's last part, a format taken from Google's documentation; one that does not read so gives a place no state or ZIP, which matches by name at no place (under-merging, the intended failure).
- **The category table is a first cut.** Its type words are Google's documented types; one no word fits gives no category, and the table is extended when a walk shows types worth placing.
- **The first circle is the parcels' circle.** A park's centre and radius are its parcels' centroids', so a building at the edge of an edge parcel may lie just outside it; its business is found only if a circle reaches it.
- **Two walks of one park in one second** order their places by walk id, not by which came later.

## Consumer tasks

`cli.Run` builds `&places.Client{HTTP: h, Key: s.MapsAPIKey, After: p.After}` when the key is not empty, `h` being `&http.Client{Transport: places.Fake{}}` under `IKIGENBA_SANDBOX` and `p.HTTP` otherwise, and hands it to `passes.Config.Places`. The `walk` tool calls `p, err := e.Walk(ctx, c, a.Parks, a.Again)` and answers `passes.RecordOf(p)`. The work reads `n, err := st.WalkCeiling(ctx)` once, in the call, then for each park `sw := cl.Sweep(ctx, places.First(k.Lat, k.Lon, k.Radius), n-made)` and `w, err := st.RecordSweep(ctx, store.Sweep{Pass: p.ID, Park: k.ID, Found: sw.Found, Circles: sw.Circles, Requests: sw.Requests, Cost: sw.CostNanos, CeilingReached: sw.CeilingReached})`, sums `w`'s counts, and at the end `st.FinishWalk(ctx, p.ID, store.Counts{ParksWalked: parks, Circles: w1, Requests: r, Found: f, NewCandidates: nc, Cost: cost}, cut)`, or, on a failure, `st.FailWalk(ctx, p.ID, store.ReasonPlaces, counts)`. A test of the client builds `&places.Client{HTTP: &http.Client{Transport: rt}, Key: "test-key", After: after}` and calls `a := cl.Nearby(ctx, places.Circle{Lat: 45.5, Lon: -92.97, Radius: 600})`.
