# D13-scoring

Judgments: what `candidates` says about each candidate, as two separate answers, under rules version 5. The first is **operating confidence**: how sure we are that it is a real business operating today, from 0 to 1, with the evidence behind it. The second is the **revenue estimate**: a low, a high and how much we trust the range. Beside them an assessment carries the facts a cohort's filters and order read (`D14-cohorts`): when the business is first known to have existed, the newest filing, its categories, the government name pattern it matched, the **fleet verdict** with the power units and drivers behind it, the **website** state and address from the website check, and the drive minutes. This document owns the pure functions of `internal/scoring` (the combined estimate, the freshness of a filing, the judged weight of a piece of evidence, the combining rule and the order key) and what a judge writes (`store.Judge`, `store.JudgeAll`, and the judging `store.Decide` does of what it touches). What a source reads from one row (its estimate, evidence, dates, category, government match, and its fleet verdict and counts) is `D05-sources`'; which links a candidate holds and in which order is `D12-resolution`'s; how and when a website is checked is `D15-website-check`'s; the records, the website check's results among them, are `D04-store`'s; when a judge runs is `D06-passes`'; which candidates a cohort answers, and in which order, is `D14-cohorts`'.

## Why two judgments

Rules version 2 ranked the shortlist by one number, confidence times estimated revenue, and that number mixed two questions. Carrier type says how well a truck count predicts revenue; filing age says whether the business still exists. Blended, a well-documented trucking company always outranked a real manufacturer, and nobody could tell from the number why. Version 3 answers the two questions separately, and filing age touches only the first.

## Operating confidence

A source reads, from each row, the **row evidence** it gives: each piece a kind (`fmcsa_active`, `fmcsa_activity`, `ppp_loan`, `own_domain`), its source, its weight before freshness and a short description (`D05-sources`). At a judge, each piece of each link not gone is weighed: an `fmcsa_active` piece is multiplied by the **freshness** of its row's filing date, 1 within two years of the date (the MCS-150 cycle), falling linearly to 0 at five years, and 0 without a date; every other kind keeps its weight. A piece whose judged weight is not above 0 says nothing and is left out. Each kind counts once per candidate, from the link that gives it the greatest weight, and the kept pieces are listed by weight, highest first.

The **operating evidence** of a candidate is that list plus what enrichment adds, which today is the website check (`D15-website-check`). A judge reads the candidate's **newest website result**, the way it reads links: of its `website_check` results, the one `Enrichments` answers first, which is the one recorded last (`D04-store`). When that result's state is `https` or `http`, the candidate gains one `website_live` piece, source `website_check`, weighing 0.5, described by `sources.DescribeWebsiteLive`. A newest result of `none` adds nothing, even when an older result found a site, and it leaves `own_domain` alone: missing evidence is never counted against a company. No result adds nothing. The piece joins the row evidence under the same rule, one per kind, listed by weight, so an FMCSA carrier filed within two years with its own domain judges 0.68, and 0.84 once its site answers.

The combining rule closes part of the gap to certainty with each piece: `1 − (1 − w₁) × (1 − w₂) × …`. The result is rounded to six decimal places, so weights 0.6, 0.3 and 0.2 give 0.776 and one piece weighing 0.2 gives 0.2. **Missing evidence is never zero**: a candidate with no evidence has no operating confidence, not 0, and a cohort ordered by operating confidence puts it after every candidate that has one (`D14-cohorts`).

## Revenue estimate

Each source gives each candidate one **estimate**: for FMCSA the estimate of its freshest link not gone, unchanged from version 2 except that it is no longer multiplied by freshness; for PPP the estimate of the link with the largest amount, which is the largest low since PPP's low is proportional to the amount. The **combined** estimate weights by confidence: `low` is the confidence-weighted mean of the sources' lows, `high` the same over the sources that give one and absent when none does, the confidence the highest of theirs. A candidate with no estimate has no revenue fields at all. Disagreement between sources is still deferred.

## The assessment

A judge writes, for each active candidate it is given, reading by its id the candidate's record, its own links, rulings and website results, and the ZIP table's minutes for its display ZIP, its estimates and one **assessment**: the operating confidence with its order key and its operating evidence; the combined revenue low, high and confidence; **known since**, the earliest date any link not gone shows the business existing; the **newest filing** among its links not gone; its **categories**, each category a link not gone gives with the scraper that gave it, `other` a real category and an empty list unknown; the **government match**, the first pattern a link's name matched in link order; the **fleet verdict**, with the **power units** and **drivers**, from its freshest FMCSA link not gone; the **website**, the newest website result's state (`https`, `http` or `none`), `unknown` when it has none, and the **website address**, that result's landed URL when the state is `https` or `http` and empty otherwise; the drive minutes from its display ZIP (absent when the ZIP table lacks it); whether it is excluded; the rules version and when it was calculated. Only links not gone count, for every field the links give.

## The fleet verdict

The fleet verdict is `pass`, `fail` or `unknown` (`sources.FleetPass`, `sources.FleetFail`, `sources.FleetUnknown`). Each FMCSA row already says which, on its reading (`D05-sources`): pass when the carrier is active with two or more power units, unknown when the row is implausible or an active row's units do not read, fail otherwise. A judge copies the verdict of the candidate's freshest FMCSA link not gone, and that link's power units and drivers beside it; with no such link the verdict is unknown and both counts are absent. Only FMCSA counts trucks, so a candidate known only from PPP is unknown, never fail: before this change it read as failing the fleet test, as if FMCSA had counted its trucks, and that is the gap the third value closes.

**Nothing is removed automatically.** The government name patterns fill the government match and exclude nothing; the fleet verdict, an old filing, a missing estimate or missing minutes leave a candidate out of a cohort only when that cohort carries the filter that reads them, and each filter on a fact that can be unknown has its own switch for the unknowns (`D14-cohorts`). A candidate is excluded only when the owner's latest `exclude` or `include` on it is an `exclude`.

**Judgments are written only by a judge.** The judge pass (`D06-passes`, daily from the `judge_candidates` trigger, and started by an enrich recorded `done` with at least one result unless an ingest, judge or rebuild is running), a rebuild (which ingests every source's current run, then judges every candidate), and an owner's ruling (`merge`, `split`, `exclude`, `include`, which judges the candidates it touches in the same call, so every cohort answers a ruling at its next read; a `not_same` judges nothing) are the only writers. The rulings reach `Decide` through the `decide`, `exclude` and `include` tools (`D10-tools`); which tool records which kind changes nothing here. An ingest pass stores rows and an enrich pass records website results (`D15-website-check`), and neither judges. There is no refresh: the pass-end statement of version 2 that re-applied the filing-age arithmetic in SQL to every stored score, and its agreement test with Go, are gone, so the formulas live once, in Go. Between judges nothing decays: a stored judgment is what the last judge wrote, and the daily judge keeps it within a day of true.

**Why daily, not on change and not on read.** The candidates whose `fmcsa_active` weight is moving are the ones whose filing nobody updated, so their rows never change: 2,431 of the 16,706 active MN and WI carriers are in the two-to-five-year band, and 2,663 change weight within 30 days. Judging only changed candidates would never revisit them. Judging on read would make the first read after a quiet stretch wait for a full judge (18 seconds for the 16,580 candidates on `us`) and would add a staleness check to every read path, to save 18 seconds of CPU a day. A daily trigger is the pattern the suite already has. A cohort read is the one place the service reads a clock at a read, for the filing-age filter, and it reads the stored newest filing against it; it judges nothing (`D14-cohorts`).

## The shortlist is gone

The shortlist was an interim view of the version 3 judgments with one fixed order and the caller's filters applied per call. Cohorts replace it (cohorts decision 15): which candidates a cohort answers, its filters, unknown switches and order, is `D14-cohorts`', reading the assessment this document defines. This document defines no list.

## Rules version 5

The rules version is `sources.Rules`, and its value is 5; no requirement spells it, and tests read the value the source declares. As with versions 3 and 4, the number lives in this prose alone: the requirement declaring `sources.Rules` (`D01-layout-and-run-seam`) states no value, so a new version opens no gap, and the delivery sets `sources.Rules` to 5 by direct instruction. Every estimate and assessment written carries it. Version 5 changes what a judge writes, the `website_live` evidence and the two website facts, and no formula: an estimate's arithmetic is version 3's, and its formula is the same number stamped when a source reads the row (decisions log 14), so estimates say 5 only once a rebuild has read every row again (`D11-on-a-space`), while assessments say 5 from the first judge after the release. Version 4 brought the three-valued fleet verdict and its counts.

An assessment written before this release reads `unknown` with an empty address (the migration's defaults, `D04-store`) until a judge rewrites it. A rebuild re-ingests and re-judges but never deletes or redoes a website result (`D04-store`), so the judge it ends with reads the results already held, as it reads the owner's rulings; a merge moves the merged candidate's results to the survivor, whose newest result is then the one recorded last among them all.

## Recorded decisions

- **Ordering departure**: operating confidence is ordered by the **operating key**, the operating confidence, already rounded to six decimal places, scaled by a million and rounded to an integer, so the key is exact (0.776 keys 776000, 0.2 keys 200000), −1 when there is none. A cohort ordered by operating confidence orders by the key, the candidate id breaking ties (`D14-cohorts`), and the stored key is indexed, so the order is one exact comparison in the query; two combined values less than a millionth apart may round to one confidence and one key and then fall to id order, a departure from ordering by the unrounded float (intent decision 18) that no person can see. An empty confidence is unknown and sorts after every judged value whatever the direction.
- **Operating confidence is rounded to six decimal places** (`math.Round(x*1e6)/1e6` of the combined value `x`, half away from zero), and the operating key is derived from the rounded value. The sandbox showed one 0.2 piece answering 0.19999999999999996, float residue no reader should see; the orchestrator ruled to store and answer the rounded value. A millionth is finer than any weight the sources give, so no ranking a person can see changes. Evidence weights and revenue confidence are not rounded. A candidate whose combined value is below half a millionth (only a piece weighing under 0.0000005 can do it) has an operating confidence of 0, keyed 0: it is still present, and still sorts before a candidate with none.
- **Rounding**: the combined low and high are the weighted means rounded half away from zero to whole dollars (`math.Round`), saturating at the `int64` bounds. With confidences summing to 0 the mean is unweighted; no rule of version 3 or 4 makes one.
- **Two and five years are calendar years** from the filing date (`AddDate(2, 0, 0)` and `AddDate(5, 0, 0)`), linear in elapsed time between them; a filing dated after the moment weighs 1. Freshness is measured at the judge's moment in UTC truncated to the second.
- **Evidence weighing nothing is left out**: a stale or undated `fmcsa_active` adds no piece, so a candidate with nothing else has no operating confidence rather than 0 (intent decision 2).
- **Evidence order** is weight, highest first, then kind name in byte order; one kind's best piece is the first in link order among equal weights.
- **PPP's estimate is its largest**: the PPP link whose estimate has the greatest low (the largest amount) gives PPP's estimate, the first in link order on ties; every other source keeps its freshest link's.
- **The government match is the first in link order** among links not gone, from any source's name.
- **The verdict is the reading's, copied** (decisions log 11): a judge reads stored links, not scraped rows, and an implausible row is stored like one whose units do not read, so the verdict is decided per row by the source and the judge takes the freshest FMCSA link's. A plausible active row whose units do not read is unknown, the source's rule (decisions log 12).
- **Only FMCSA's freshest link counts** for the verdict and the counts (cohorts decision 13): a PPP reading's verdict is always unknown, and the judge does not consult it, so no other source can make a candidate pass or fail. The freshest link is the first FMCSA link not gone in the order `store.Links` answers (`D12-resolution`), so a newer filing that fails outranks an older one that passed.
- **A link without a verdict is unknown**: a link stored by an earlier release decodes with an empty verdict and gives the assessment `unknown`, never a guess from its old fleet boolean; the rebuild rewrites every link.
- **Power units and drivers come from the same link** as the verdict (decisions log 20), so a candidate's trucks and drivers and its verdict never disagree; an implausible row carries neither (decisions log 28), so its candidate's counts are absent beside an unknown verdict.
- **The assessment no longer carries the fleet boolean**: the Go type holds the verdict in its place, and this release's migration drops the old `fleet` column with the unwritten `low` and `high` (`D04-store`, which records that departure from expand then contract).
- **The website piece weighs 0.5 whatever the result's age** (website-check decision 13, which sets no age): freshness is FMCSA's filing cycle, and `scoring.Weight` leaves every other kind's weight alone. The check's refresh ages (`D15-website-check`) renew a result only while its candidate stays due. A candidate that stops being checked keeps its last result as the newest: one whose display email turns free-provider or blank, or a survivor without its own domain that a merge gave an `https` result. That result then keeps 0.5 and its state indefinitely. This is accepted: the result is still what was last seen, and a later change may add an age if real results show it matters.
- **The newest result decides alone** (website-check decision 13): a site that answered once and has since stopped answering gives no evidence, and nothing averages over a candidate's results.
- **The website facts come from the judge, not the read** (website-check decision 14): a cohort's filter and its rows read the assessment's `Website` and `WebsiteURL`, so they say what the last judge saw, while the candidate page's card reads the newest result itself (`D08-pages`). An enrich recorded `done` with at least one result starts a judge unless an ingest, judge or rebuild is running (`D06-passes`), so the two seldom differ for long.
- **Unranged now means no revenue**: a judge counts the candidates it judged whose revenue low is absent; missing minutes no longer matter to any count.
- **A candidate not yet judged is in no cohort**: with no assessment there is nothing to filter or order (`D14-cohorts`); a rebuild or the next judge gives it one.
- **Judge ignores ids it cannot judge**: an id naming no candidate or a merged one is skipped, not refused, since a ruling may merge a candidate between a pass's work and its judge.
- **A judge touches no cohort, no reject and no website result**: what a cohort answers changes at its next read because the assessments changed, never because a judge wrote the cohort.
- **A merged candidate keeps its estimates and last assessment** as they stood at the merge and is never judged again; the survivor's are recomputed; cohorts answer active candidates only.
- **`JudgeAll` no longer promises the `candidate_cargo` repair** (decisions log 30): its one observable was the shortlist's cargo filter, which is gone, and cargo is not a cohort filter. Whether the table is still kept is `D04-store`'s; it is a finding to drop it in the next release.
- **The old score fields are gone**: confidence, base, effective, score, score key, filed and exclusions, which version 3 left empty, were dropped by the cohorts release's migration (`D04-store`, cohorts decision 16).

## Consumer tasks

Current: the judge pass and a rebuild call `c, err := st.JudgeAll(ctx)`; `Decide` judges what it touches; the `cohort` tool and the cohort page call `cr, err := st.ReadCohort(ctx, id)` and show each `m` of `cr.Members` from its assessment's operating confidence, revenue fields, `Categories`, `FleetVerdict`, `Minutes`, `GovernmentMatch`, `KnownSince`, `NewestFiling` and `Excluded`; `candidate` and the candidate page show `GetAssessment`'s fields. A test of a judge attaches observations whose readings it builds, sets its clock, calls `st.Judge(ctx, ids)` and compares `GetAssessment` with the assessment it expects.

Proposed: the passes and `Decide` are unchanged, and an enrich pass that records a result may start a judge (`D06-passes`). Cohort members and `GetAssessment` also carry `a.Website` and `a.WebsiteURL`, and `a.Evidence` may hold a piece named `sources.EvidenceWebsiteLive`. A test of a judge attaches an FMCSA reading whose `Filed` is within two years of its clock and whose `Evidence` holds `sources.EvidenceFMCSAActive` weighing 0.6 and `sources.EvidenceOwnDomain` weighing 0.2, calls `st.Judge(ctx, ids)` and finds `*a.Operating` 0.68 and `a.Website == sources.WebsiteUnknown`; it calls `st.AddEnrichments(ctx, []store.Enrichment{{Candidate: id, Kind: sources.WebsiteCheck, State: sources.WebsiteHTTPS, URL: u}})`, judges again and finds 0.84, `a.Website == sources.WebsiteHTTPS`, `a.WebsiteURL == u` and the `website_live` piece carrying `sources.DescribeWebsiteLive`; it records a later result with `State: sources.WebsiteNone`, judges again and finds 0.68, `a.Website == sources.WebsiteNone` and `own_domain` still weighing 0.2.
