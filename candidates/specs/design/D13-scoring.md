# D13-scoring

Judgments: what `candidates` says about each candidate, as two separate answers, under rules version 5. The first is **operating confidence**: how sure we are that it is a real business operating today, from 0 to 1, with the evidence behind it. The second is the **revenue estimate**: a low, a high and how much we trust the range. Beside them an assessment carries the facts a cohort's filters and order read (`D14-cohorts`): when the business is first known to have existed, the newest filing, its categories, the government name pattern it matched, the **fleet verdict** with the power units and drivers behind it, the **website** state and address, and the drive minutes. Those facts are not judged. Enrichers derive or fetch them (`D16-enrichers`), the store keeps the newest result of each kind as the candidate's **facts row** (`D04-store`), and every read of an active candidate's assessment answers its facts from that row. This document owns the pure functions of `internal/scoring` (the combined estimate, the freshness of a filing, the judged weight of a piece of evidence, the combining rule and the order key) and what a judge writes (`store.Judge`, `store.JudgeAll`, and the judging `store.Decide` does of what it touches): the opinions, worked out from the candidate's links, rulings and facts row, and a copy of the facts row beside them. What a source reads from one row (its estimate, evidence, dates, category, government match, and its fleet verdict and counts) is `D05-sources`'; how the link facts and the drive minutes are derived from a candidate, the fleet verdict among them, is `D16-enrichers`'; which links a candidate holds and in which order, and which derived results a ruling records, is `D12-resolution`'s; how and when a website is checked is `D15-website-check`'s; the records, the enrichments and the facts row among them, are `D04-store`'s; when a judge runs is `D06-passes`'; which candidates a cohort answers, and in which order, is `D14-cohorts`'.

"Intent decision N" is entry N of the decisions document of the judgments change, *two judgments and the PPP reader*; "cohorts decision N" is entry N of the decisions document of the cohorts change, and "cohorts log N" entry N of its decisions log. "Website decision N" is entry N of the decisions document of the website-check change, and "enrichers decision N" entry N of the decisions document of the enrichers change. "Category evidence decision N" is entry N of the decisions document of the category-evidence change.

## Why two judgments

Rules version 2 ranked the shortlist by one number, confidence times estimated revenue, and that number mixed two questions. Carrier type says how well a truck count predicts revenue; filing age says whether the business still exists. Blended, a well-documented trucking company always outranked a real manufacturer, and nobody could tell from the number why. Version 3 answers the two questions separately, and filing age touches only the first.

## Operating confidence

A source reads, from each row, the **row evidence** it gives: each piece a kind (`fmcsa_active`, `fmcsa_activity`, `ppp_loan`, `own_domain`), its source, its weight before freshness and a short description (`D05-sources`). At a judge, each piece of each link not gone is weighed: an `fmcsa_active` piece is multiplied by the **freshness** of its row's filing date, 1 within two years of the date (the MCS-150 cycle), falling linearly to 0 at five years, and 0 without a date; every other kind keeps its weight. A piece whose judged weight is not above 0 says nothing and is left out. Each kind counts once per candidate, from the link that gives it the greatest weight, and the kept pieces are listed by weight, highest first.

The **operating evidence** of a candidate is that list plus what enrichment adds, which today is the website. A judge reads the website state of the candidate's facts row, which is the state of its newest `website_check` result (`D04-store`, `D15-website-check`). When that state is `https` or `http`, the candidate gains one `website_live` piece, source `website_check`, weighing 0.5, described by `sources.DescribeWebsiteLive`. A newest result of `none` adds nothing, even when an older result found a site, and it leaves `own_domain` alone: missing evidence is never counted against a company. No result, so no row or a row whose website reads `unknown`, adds nothing. The piece joins the row evidence under the same rule, one per kind, listed by weight, so an FMCSA carrier filed within two years with its own domain judges 0.68, and 0.84 once its site answers. The judge reads neither the enrichments nor the ZIP table: a website result reaches the judgment only through the facts row it writes.

The combining rule closes part of the gap to certainty with each piece: `1 − (1 − w₁) × (1 − w₂) × …`. The result is rounded to six decimal places, so weights 0.6, 0.3 and 0.2 give 0.776 and one piece weighing 0.2 gives 0.2. **Missing evidence is never zero**: a candidate with no evidence has no operating confidence, not 0, and a cohort ordered by operating confidence puts it after every candidate that has one (`D14-cohorts`).

## Revenue estimate

Each source gives each candidate one **estimate**: for FMCSA the estimate of its freshest link not gone, unchanged from version 2 except that it is no longer multiplied by freshness; for PPP the estimate of the link with the largest amount, which is the largest low since PPP's low is proportional to the amount. The **combined** estimate weights by confidence: `low` is the confidence-weighted mean of the sources' lows, `high` the same over the sources that give one and absent when none does, the confidence the highest of theirs. A candidate with no estimate has no revenue fields at all. Disagreement between sources is still deferred.

## The assessment

A judge writes, for each active candidate it is given, reading by its id the candidate's record, its own links, its rulings and its facts row, and nothing else, its estimates and one **assessment**: the operating confidence with its order key and its operating evidence; the combined revenue low, high and confidence; whether it is excluded; the rules version and when it was calculated. Only links not gone count, for every opinion the links give.

Beside the opinions the assessment holds the ten facts: known since, the government match, the newest filing, the categories, the fleet verdict with its power units and drivers, the website and its address, and the drive minutes. A judge copies them from the facts row as it reads it, or writes the unknown values (`unknown` for the verdict and the website, nothing for the rest) when the candidate has no row. The categories it copies are the facts row's whole list: the good brief's, each with source `brief`, when that brief gives at least one, and otherwise the categories the links give, never the two mixed (category evidence decision 1); the facts row's `brief` text is not one of the ten facts, and the assessment neither holds nor copies it. No reader of an active candidate takes them from that copy: `GetAssessment` and a cohort's members answer an active candidate's facts from its facts row at the read (`D04-store`), so a fact moves as soon as an enrich writes it, and only the opinions wait for a judge. The copy is kept for two readers: the release before this one, which reads the assessment's own columns, and a merged candidate, whose facts row the merge deletes and whose assessment then answers the copy its last judge wrote.

## The facts

How each fact is worked out is `D16-enrichers`'. Known since, the newest filing, the government match, the categories the links give and the fleet verdict with its power units and drivers are the `link_facts` kind's, derived from the candidate's links not gone under the rules this document used to own and moved there unchanged: among them, the verdict and counts of the freshest FMCSA link not gone, `unknown` with no counts for a candidate known only from PPP, never `fail`. The categories are instead the `brief` kind's, read from the candidate's kept pages, when the good brief gives at least one; how the facts row chooses between the two kinds' categories is `D04-store`'s, and a judge copies the chosen list unchanged. The drive minutes are the `drive_minutes` kind's, from the display ZIP. The website and its address are the `website_check` kind's (`D15-website-check`). A judge neither derives nor fetches; a rebuild and a merge or split re-derive before they judge (`D06-passes`, `D12-resolution`).

**Nothing is removed automatically.** The government name patterns fill the government match and exclude nothing; the fleet verdict, an old filing, a missing estimate or missing minutes leave a candidate out of a cohort only when that cohort carries the filter that reads them, and each filter on a fact that can be unknown has its own switch for the unknowns (`D14-cohorts`). A candidate is excluded only when the owner's latest `exclude` or `include` on it is an `exclude`.

**Opinions are written only by a judge.** The judge pass (`D06-passes`, daily from the `judge_candidates` trigger, and started as an enrich's follow-on under the rule stated there), a rebuild (which ingests every source's current run, re-derives every derived kind for every candidate, then judges every candidate), and an owner's ruling (`merge`, `split`, `exclude`, `include`, which judges the candidates it touches in the same call, a merge or a split after re-deriving them (`D12-resolution`), so every cohort answers a ruling at its next read; a `not_same` judges nothing) are the only writers. The rulings reach `Decide` through the `decide`, `exclude` and `include` tools (`D10-tools`); which tool records which kind changes nothing here. An ingest pass stores rows and an enrich pass records results and their facts (`D06-passes`, `D16-enrichers`), and neither judges. There is no refresh: the pass-end statement of version 2 that re-applied the filing-age arithmetic in SQL to every stored score, and its agreement test with Go, are gone, so the formulas live once, in Go. Between judges nothing decays: a stored opinion is what the last judge wrote, and the daily judge keeps it within a day of true, while the facts are always the newest results'.

**Why daily, not on change and not on read.** The candidates whose `fmcsa_active` weight is moving are the ones whose filing nobody updated, so their rows never change: 2,431 of the 16,706 active MN and WI carriers are in the two-to-five-year band, and 2,663 change weight within 30 days. Judging only changed candidates would never revisit them. Judging on read would make the first read after a quiet stretch wait for a full judge (18 seconds for the 16,580 candidates on `us`) and would add a staleness check to every read path, to save 18 seconds of CPU a day. A daily trigger is the pattern the suite already has. A cohort read is the one place the service reads a clock at a read, for the filing-age filter, and it reads the stored newest filing against it; it judges nothing (`D14-cohorts`).

## The shortlist is gone

The shortlist was an interim view of the version 3 judgments with one fixed order and the caller's filters applied per call. Cohorts replace it (cohorts decision 15): which candidates a cohort answers, its filters, unknown switches and order, is `D14-cohorts`', reading the assessment this document defines. This document defines no list.

## Rules version 5

The rules version is `sources.Rules`, and its value is 5; no requirement spells it, and tests read the value the source declares. As with versions 3 and 4, the number lives in this prose alone: the requirement declaring `sources.Rules` (`D01-layout-and-run-seam`) states no value, so a new version opens no gap, and the delivery sets `sources.Rules` to 5 by direct instruction. Every estimate and assessment written carries it. Version 5 changed what a judge writes, the `website_live` evidence and the two website facts, and no formula: an estimate's arithmetic is version 3's, and its formula is the same number stamped when a source reads the row (cohorts log 14). Version 4 brought the three-valued fleet verdict and its counts. The enrichers change keeps version 5: from the same links, rulings and newest website result a judge writes the same opinions, and the facts are the same values, derived by enrichers instead of the judge.

An active candidate's facts read unknown until it has a facts row. The release's migration creates the table empty, the start fills a row for each candidate already holding a website result (`D04-store`, `D03-serve`), and the first enrich after the release derives every candidate's link facts and minutes; its follow-on judge then rewrites the opinions and the copy. A rebuild re-ingests, re-derives and re-judges but never fetches or deletes a website result (`D04-store`), so the judge it ends with reads the website results already held, as it reads the owner's rulings; a merge moves the merged candidate's results to the survivor and re-derives the survivor, whose newest website result is then the one recorded last among them all.

## Recorded decisions

- **Ordering departure**: operating confidence is ordered by the **operating key**, the operating confidence, already rounded to six decimal places, scaled by a million and rounded to an integer, so the key is exact (0.776 keys 776000, 0.2 keys 200000), −1 when there is none. A cohort ordered by operating confidence orders by the key, the candidate id breaking ties (`D14-cohorts`), and the stored key is indexed, so the order is one exact comparison in the query; two combined values less than a millionth apart may round to one confidence and one key and then fall to id order, a departure from ordering by the unrounded float (intent decision 18) that no person can see. An empty confidence is unknown and sorts after every judged value whatever the direction.
- **Operating confidence is rounded to six decimal places** (`math.Round(x*1e6)/1e6` of the combined value `x`, half away from zero), and the operating key is derived from the rounded value. The sandbox showed one 0.2 piece answering 0.19999999999999996, float residue no reader should see; the orchestrator ruled to store and answer the rounded value. A millionth is finer than any weight the sources give, so no ranking a person can see changes. Evidence weights and revenue confidence are not rounded. A candidate whose combined value is below half a millionth (only a piece weighing under 0.0000005 can do it) has an operating confidence of 0, keyed 0: it is still present, and still sorts before a candidate with none.
- **Rounding**: the combined low and high are the weighted means rounded half away from zero to whole dollars (`math.Round`), saturating at the `int64` bounds. With confidences summing to 0 the mean is unweighted; no rule of version 3 or 4 makes one.
- **Two and five years are calendar years** from the filing date (`AddDate(2, 0, 0)` and `AddDate(5, 0, 0)`), linear in elapsed time between them; a filing dated after the moment weighs 1. Freshness is measured at the judge's moment in UTC truncated to the second.
- **Evidence weighing nothing is left out**: a stale or undated `fmcsa_active` adds no piece, so a candidate with nothing else has no operating confidence rather than 0 (intent decision 2).
- **Evidence order** is weight, highest first, then kind name in byte order; one kind's best piece is the first in link order among equal weights.
- **PPP's estimate is its largest**: the PPP link whose estimate has the greatest low (the largest amount) gives PPP's estimate, the first in link order on ties; every other source keeps its freshest link's.
- **The link facts' rules moved to `D16-enrichers`** (enrichers decisions 2 and 6): the earliest known since, the newest filing, the first government match in link order, the categories with their sources, and the verdict and counts copied from the freshest FMCSA link's reading (an empty verdict read as unknown, a PPP reading's never consulted) are now what the `link_facts` kind derives, with the examples that proved them. Moving them changes no value; the judge reads their result from the facts row.
- **The assessment no longer carries the fleet boolean**: the Go type holds the verdict in its place, and the migration of the release that brought the verdict dropped the old `fleet` column with the unwritten `low` and `high` (`D04-store`, which records that departure from expand then contract).
- **The website piece weighs 0.5 whatever the result's age** (website decision 13, which sets no age): freshness is FMCSA's filing cycle, and `scoring.Weight` leaves every other kind's weight alone. There are no refresh ages any more: a website result is renewed only when the candidate's domain changes (`D15-website-check`). A candidate that stops being checked keeps its last result as the newest: one whose display email turns free-provider or blank, or a survivor without its own domain that a merge gave an `https` result. That result then keeps 0.5 and its state indefinitely. This is accepted: the result is still what was last seen, and a later change may add an age if real results show it matters.
- **The newest result decides alone** (website decision 13): a site that answered once and has since stopped answering gives no evidence, and nothing averages over a candidate's results; the facts row holds only the newest.
- **The facts come from the facts row at the read** (enrichers decisions 5 and 7, replacing website decision 14's "the website facts come from the judge"): a cohort's filters and rows, `GetAssessment`, the `candidate` tool and the candidate page answer what the newest results say as soon as they are recorded, while the opinions say what the last judge saw. An enrich that counted a derived result or an `https`, `http` or `none` website result starts a judge unless an ingest, judge or rebuild is running (`D06-passes`), so the website piece and the website fact seldom disagree for long.
- **The judge still writes the fact columns** (enrichers decision 7): the release before this one reads the assessment's columns, so a judge copies the facts row into them, and a merged candidate keeps answering that copy. Dropping the columns is the next release's contract step.
- **Unranged now means no revenue**: a judge counts the candidates it judged whose revenue low is absent; missing minutes no longer matter to any count.
- **A candidate not yet judged is in no cohort**: with no assessment there is nothing to filter or order (`D14-cohorts`); a rebuild or the next judge gives it one, even when its facts row is already written.
- **Judge ignores ids it cannot judge**: an id naming no candidate or a merged one is skipped, not refused, since a ruling may merge a candidate between a pass's work and its judge.
- **A judge touches no cohort, no reject, no enrichment and no facts row**: what a cohort answers changes at its next read because the assessments or the facts changed, never because a judge wrote the cohort, and only enrichers write the facts.
- **A merged candidate keeps its estimates and last assessment** as they stood at the merge and is never judged again; its facts row is deleted, so its assessment answers the facts its last judge copied; the survivor's are recomputed; cohorts answer active candidates only.
- **`JudgeAll` no longer promises the `candidate_cargo` repair** (cohorts log 30): its one observable was the shortlist's cargo filter, which is gone, and cargo is not a cohort filter. Whether the table is still kept is `D04-store`'s; it is a finding to drop it in the next release.
- **The old score fields are gone**: confidence, base, effective, score, score key, filed and exclusions, which version 3 left empty, were dropped by the cohorts release's migration (`D04-store`, cohorts decision 16).

## Consumer tasks

Current: the judge pass and a rebuild call `c, err := st.JudgeAll(ctx)`; `Decide` judges what it touches; a judge reads each candidate's links for every fact, `ZipMinutes` for its minutes and `Enrichments` for its newest website result; the `cohort` tool and the cohort page show each `m` of `cr.Members` from its assessment, and `candidate` and the candidate page show `GetAssessment`'s fields, every fact as the last judge wrote it. A test of the website piece records a result with `st.AddEnrichments(ctx, []store.Enrichment{{Candidate: id, Kind: sources.WebsiteCheck, State: sources.WebsiteHTTPS, URL: u}})` and judges.

Proposed: the passes call `JudgeAll` as before; a judge reads the candidate, its links, its rulings and its facts row, and nothing else. The readers are unchanged in code: `a, err := st.GetAssessment(ctx, id)` and the members of `st.ReadCohort(ctx, id)` answer `a.KnownSince`, `a.NewestFiling`, `a.Categories`, `a.GovernmentMatch`, `a.FleetVerdict`, `a.PowerUnits`, `a.Drivers`, `a.Website`, `a.WebsiteURL` and `a.Minutes` from the facts row at the read. A test of the website piece builds `found, _ := json.Marshal(website.Result{State: sources.WebsiteHTTPS, URL: u})`, calls `st.AddEnrichments(ctx, []store.Enrichment{{Candidate: id, Kind: sources.WebsiteCheck, Inputs: d, Source: d, Found: found}})`, calls `st.Judge(ctx, []string{id})` and finds `*a.Operating` 0.84 and `a.Website == sources.WebsiteHTTPS`. A test of the round trip derives with `st.Derive(ctx, enrichers.KindLinkFacts, "", false, 10)`, reads `f, err := st.GetFacts(ctx, id)`, judges, and finds `a.FleetVerdict == f.FleetVerdict` and the other facts equal; before the derive, with no row, it finds `a.FleetVerdict == sources.FleetUnknown` whatever the links say. A test of the copy judges `a`, merges `a` into `b` with `st.Decide`, and finds `st.GetAssessment(ctx, a.ID)` still answering `a`'s facts.
