# D10-tools

The thirteen MCP tools agents reach a user's Gmail by through the MCP gateway: `internal/tools`. Mail text, headers, snippets and body text, is answered to the caller; no tool accepts or answers an attachment's bytes, a raw message, a token or a credential (decision 2). An agent finds mail with `search`, `thread`, `show` and `labels`; moves bytes with `pull` (Gmail to artifact) and `push` (artifact onto a draft), each of which records a transfer and answers it at once (`D06-transfers`); makes, sends and discards drafts with `compose`, `send` and `discard` (`D07-drafts`); and reads its own records with `drafts`, `draft`, `transfers` and `transfer`. Every tool works for its caller alone, the `identity.Caller` appkit hands the handler.

## The tools

- `search` takes `query`, handed to Gmail verbatim as its search language (empty meaning all mail, newest first), optional `label`, one label by name, and optional `page` and `limit`; it answers one page of message summaries under `messages`, newest first, with `next` when more remain. Spam and trash are excluded, as Gmail's own search excludes them (`D05-gmail`).
- `thread` takes `thread` and optional `page` and answers one page of the thread's summaries under `messages`, oldest first, with `next` when more remain.
- `show` takes `message` and answers the full message.
- `labels` takes nothing and answers the mailbox's labels under `labels`, each `name` and `system`.
- `pull` takes `message`, `path`, optional `attachment` (a part id `show` answered) and optional `expires`, and answers the transfer it recorded.
- `compose` takes optional `to`, `cc`, `bcc` (lists of addresses), `subject`, `body` and `reply_to` (a message id) and answers the draft it made.
- `push` takes `path` and `draft` (a `drf_` id) and answers the transfer it recorded.
- `send` and `discard` take `draft` and answer the draft, `sent` or `discarded`.
- `drafts` takes optional `page` and `limit` and answers one page of the caller's drafts newest first under `drafts`, with `next` when more remain; `draft` takes `draft` and answers that draft with its body.
- `transfers` takes optional `page` and `limit` and answers one page of the caller's transfers newest first under `transfers`, with `next` when more remain; `transfer` takes `transfer` and answers that transfer.

## Paging

`search`, `drafts` and `transfers` page as the suite's listing rule asks (`shape/`, Listing), on the contract of appkit's `paging` package (appkit's D25), which this document cites and never restates; `dummy` is the precedent. Each input struct declares its own `page` (`*string`) and `limit` (`*int`) with the shared descriptions `paging.PageDescription` and `paging.LimitDescription`, and each answer its `next` (`*string`, absent when nil) with `paging.NextDescription`. `limit` is read with `paging.Window`: absent is `paging.DefaultLimit`, and one outside 1 to `paging.MaxLimit` is refused with the text of the error `Window` returns, before the store or Gmail is read. The agent hands `next` back as `page` unchanged.

`drafts` and `transfers` page from the store. Their keys, within the caller, are the store's (`D04-store`): drafts by `(created DESC, id DESC)`, transfers by `(started DESC, id DESC)`. The tokens are `paging.Token{Tool: "drafts"}` and `paging.Token{Tool: "transfers"}`, each over two fields, both strings: the record's time as the store keeps it (RFC 3339 UTC to the second) and its id. The tool reads one window, `ListDraftsAfter` or `ListTransfersAfter` with the page size plus one, answers the page, and sets `next` from the key of the last record answered only when the extra record came back. A non-empty `page` the token's `Decode` refuses is refused with `paging.BadPage`. Both refusals come before the store is read, so they are answered alike when the store is unreachable. The drafts or transfers and the `next` a call answers for a `page` and no `limit` are exactly the window and the token the landing page shows for that same `page` as its `drafts_page` or `transfers_page`, the first window when it has none (`D08-pages`).

No page token, Gmail's or the store's, reaches any event or log line: a tool call is recorded only by appkit's `tool.called`, which carries no arguments, and by `gmail.called`, whose attributes are the endpoint, status and duration alone (`D05-gmail`, `D09-trail`); the trail is unchanged.

`search` is provider-backed, as `shape/` names it: `page` and `next` carry Gmail's own page token through untouched (`pageToken` in, `nextPageToken` out, `next` present exactly when Gmail answers one), and `limit` is sent as `maxResults`, whose documented cap of 500 equals `paging.MaxLimit` (`D05-gmail`). A bad `limit` is refused with `Window`'s text after the connection check and before any Gmail request. A `page` Gmail refuses is answered as Gmail's error is, `provider` (below); `paging.BadPage` is not used for `search`, since gmail does not issue its tokens and cannot tell one Gmail would refuse.

`thread` keeps its own paging: it is a departure from `shape/` recorded here. Gmail answers a thread whole, so a thread is bounded by itself and the client pages it at `gmail.PageSize` (25) with a decimal offset as `page` and the next offset as `next` (`D05-gmail`), both opaque to the agent; it takes no `limit`, and its `page` description is its own. `labels` is bounded by the account and is listed whole, unpaged.

## The answers

A **summary** (`search`, `thread`) is `id`, `thread`, `date`, `from`, `to`, `cc`, `subject`, `snippet`, `labels`, `unread`, `attachments` (a count) and `link`. A **full message** (`show`) is the summary's members but the count, then `message_id`, `in_reply_to`, `body`, `truncated` and `attachments`, a list of parts each `id` (Gmail's MIME part id), `name`, `mime_type` and `bytes` (decision 5). The reduction of Gmail's answers to these values, the body's plain-first choice and its 64 KiB cut are `D05-gmail`'s; the tools encode what the client answers.

A **draft record** is `id`, `draft` (Gmail's draft id, only while `open`), `message`, `thread`, `status`, `to`, `cc`, `bcc`, `subject`, `reply_to` (only for a reply), `attachments` (each `path`, `name`, `content_type`, `bytes`), `link` (absent once `discarded`), `created`, `modified`, `sent` (only once `sent`) and, answered by `draft` alone, `body` (decision 10). A **transfer record** is gdrive's with mail's references: `id`, `direction`, `status`, `reason` (only when failed), `message`, `attachment` (only for a pulled part), `draft` (only for a push), `name`, `path`, `content_type`, `bytes`, `started`, `finished` (only once ended), and, when done, `url` and `link` (decision 11). Times are RFC 3339, UTC, to the second. The caller, the request id and the artifact id stay in the store.

## Refusals

A refusal is an MCP error result whose text is `<word>: <sentence>`, the sentence a copy constant per word (`tools.Refuse*`), and for `not_connected` and `reconnect` a space and the URL the person opens (decision 8, decision 14). The words are gdrive's that apply, `not_connected`, `reconnect`, `not_found`, `too_large`, `provider`, `path`, `expires`, `unavailable`, `stopping` and `queue_full`, and gmail's own four, `not_open`, `busy`, `to` and `address`. The two paging refusals are the suite's own and carry no word: their text is the shared sentence alone, `Window`'s error text or `paging.BadPage`, as every app's listing tool answers them. A sentence is a constant, so a refusal names no subject, address or filename the call or Gmail supplied (decision 8, decision 16); the id the agent passed is already the agent's.

Each tool that reaches Gmail checks the caller's connection first, through `transfers.Token`, so a missing connection is `not_connected` with the connect page, a broken one `reconnect` with connections' URL, and no `connections` entry `unavailable`, before any other refusal. The tools relay every other refusal from the layer that judges it, and its order is that layer's:

- `pull` and `push`: `D06-transfers` judges the order: for `pull`, `expires`, `path`, the message, the part, the size (a part's size, or a whole message's size estimate, root ruling A9), `stopping` and `queue_full`; for `push`, the artifact (`path`, `not_found`, `unavailable`), its size over `TRANSFER_MAX_BYTES`, then what the drafts service judges for the draft (`D07-drafts`: Gmail's 35 MiB upload cap, the draft's absence, its stored status, the probe and reconciliation), then `stopping` and `queue_full`; each before any record. This follows decision 7's order after the connection (root ruling A8), except that the upload cap is judged only once the caller's draft is found, since the cap needs the draft's composition.
- `compose`, `send`, `discard` and `draft`: `D07-drafts` judges the order: for `compose`, `to` with no recipient and no reply, `address`, the original of a reply (`not_found`, or `reconnect` and `provider` for Gmail's other answers), `to` for a reply that finds no recipient, then Gmail's answer to the draft's creation (`too_large` for a message past Gmail's upload cap, `reconnect`, `provider`); for `send` and `discard`, the draft's absence (`not_found`), its stored status (`not_open`), the probe and reconciliation, `busy` while a push onto it is queued or running, then Gmail's own answer (`provider`, or `reconnect`), which leaves the draft `open`.

A Gmail answer in a read tool is refused as its kind: unknown is `not_found` (an unknown message, thread or label, root ruling A8), Gmail refusing the token is `reconnect` with the connect page, and anything else is `provider`, a page token the client refuses (`gmail.ErrBadPage`) included, as gdrive answers a page token Drive refuses (root ruling A8, coordinator R5). gmail's database failing is `unavailable`. Malformed arguments are appkit's schema refusal.

## Reconciliation

A draft sent or deleted by the person in Gmail is learned when a tool touches an `open` record and Gmail answers its draft id unknown (decision 10, root rulings A5, A6, A13, A18 and A19). `draft`, `push`, `send` and `discard` reconcile, through the drafts service's `Get`, `Attachable`, `Send` and `Discard`, which `D07-drafts` declares with the probe, the marks, the events and the answers; the tools relay what those methods answer. In short: `draft` answers the record as it now stands; `push` and `send` are refused `not_open` whichever way it ended; `discard` answers a draft reconciled to `discarded` as its success and is refused `not_open` for one reconciled to `sent`. `drafts` makes no Gmail call and answers what is stored.

## Descriptions

Every tool's description is its first line, a copy constant `tools.Describe<Tool>`, an empty line, and `tools.MailGuide`, the one shared line of decision 2. `search`'s first line says the query is Gmail's search language passed verbatim; `show`'s says an HTML-only body is reduced to text and the reduction is lossy (decisions 6, 7). `tools.Catalog()` answers each name with its first line for the tools page (`D08-pages`).

## Recorded decisions

- **Typed registration throughout.** Every answer is a type of `internal/tools` whose fields appkit's typed encoder accepts: plain JSON names, absent members as nil pointers, times as strings, lists never `null` (appkit encodes a nil slice as `[]`). This departs from gdrive, which registered its Drive tools raw to encode Drive's types as they were; the tools here convert `D05-gmail`'s types (`SummaryOf`, `MessageOf`, `LabelOf`), so the JSON names are this document's contract and the client's types stay free of JSON.
- **Optional inputs are plain fields**, not pointers, since appkit's schema at the required release takes required-ness from the `mcp:"required"` tag; an empty optional argument means what its absence means. Four kinds are pointers: `expires`, because given empty it is refused `expires` (gdrive's precedent); `subject`, because given empty it is a reply's chosen empty subject rather than the default, as `drafts.ComposeRequest` takes it; and `page` and `limit` on `search`, `drafts` and `transfers`, which appkit's D25 declares as pointers. An empty `to` list is an absent one.
- **Effects.** `search`, `thread`, `show`, `labels`, `drafts`, `draft`, `transfers` and `transfer` are `mcp.Read` (decision 7, though `draft` may record a reconciliation, which learns a fact rather than making one). `compose` is `mcp.Additive`: it only adds a draft. `pull` (may replace the artifact at `path`), `push` (replaces the draft Gmail holds, a person's edits included, decision 9), `send` and `discard` (each irreversible) are `mcp.Destructive`.
- **`draft` asks for a token only for an `open` record** (root ruling A14), as the drafts service's `Get` does (`D07-drafts`): an ended record is final and needs no Gmail call, so it works without a connection; an `open` one must be probed, so the connection is checked then.
- **Behaviour is cited, not restated.** The refusal orders, the compose rules, the reconciliation and the send and discard outcomes are stated once, in `D06-transfers` and `D07-drafts`; this document maps each tool to the method that judges it and states only what the tools add: the arguments, the JSON, the descriptions, the refusal text, the connection check of the read tools, the read tools' answers and the record tools.
- **Gmail refusing the token in a read tool is `reconnect` with the connect page**: Gmail's 401 carries no connections address, and the connect page is where a person reconnects.
- **Unspecified refusals follow gdrive** (root ruling A8): an unknown label, thread or message is `not_found`; a page token the client refuses, for `search` or `thread`, is `provider`, the word gdrive gives a page token Drive refuses; a bad `expires` is `expires`; a path artifacts refuses is `path`, for `push` as for `pull`.
- **An empty id is not found**: `show` and `thread` given the empty string are refused `not_found` with no Gmail request, after the connection check; `transfer` judges a malformed `xfr_` id `not_found` without reading the store; `draft`, `send` and `discard` relay the drafts service's `not_found` for an id that names no draft of the caller's.
- **Reconciliation's events** (root ruling A7) are recorded by the drafts service as the tool's caller (`D07-drafts`, `D09-trail`).
- **A first line is one sentence** of at most 120 characters ending with a full stop, because appkit's `mcp` refuses to register a tool whose description's first line is not.
- **Refusal sentences are constants** naming no value, so the secrecy of decision 8 holds by construction; a test compares the whole text.
- **The tools hold no token code**: the token is the engine's (`D06-transfers`), and `tools.Config` carries no connections client.
- **A search with a large `limit` is costly**: Gmail's list answers ids only, so each summary is one `messages.get`; a `search` with `limit` 500 sends one `labels.list`, one `messages.list` and up to 500 `messages.get`. That is the price of answering summaries, and the agent chooses it.
- **Paging refusals carry no word**, departing from the `<word>: <sentence>` form of every other refusal, so that a model meets the same sentence in every app's listing tools (appkit's D25).
- **Departures from gdrive**: thirteen tools in place of eight; the read tools answer mail text, not only references (decision 2); the four new words; the draft record and its reconciliation; typed answers in place of raw ones; `MailGuide` in place of `TransferGuide`; first lines as named constants rather than catalog literals.

## Consumer tasks

`D03-serve`'s handler calls `tools.Register(srv, tools.Config{Transfers: eng, Drafts: dr, Gmail: gc, Store: st})`; the tools page reads `tools.Catalog()`. A model calls `search` with `{"query":"from:alice has:attachment newer_than:7d"}` and gets `{"messages":[{"id":"…","thread":"…","subject":"Invoice",…,"attachments":1,"link":"https://mail.google.com/mail/u/0/#all/…"}],"next":"…"}`; `show` with `{"message":"…"}` and gets the body and `attachments":[{"id":"2","name":"invoice.pdf","mime_type":"application/pdf","bytes":48213}]`; `pull` with `{"message":"…","attachment":"2","path":"finance/invoice.pdf"}` and gets `{"id":"xfr_…","direction":"pull","status":"running",…}`; `compose` with `{"reply_to":"…","body":"Received, thanks."}` and gets `{"id":"drf_…","draft":"…","status":"open",…}`; `push` with `{"path":"finance/receipt.pdf","draft":"drf_…"}`; `transfer` until `done`; `send` with `{"draft":"drf_…"}` and gets `"status":"sent"`. Or it is refused `not_connected: <sentence> https://connections.<space>/google/connect` and hands the link to the person. A model reads its drafts with `drafts` and `{}`, gets `{"drafts":[…],"next":"…"}`, and calls `drafts` with `{"page":"…"}` until an answer has no `next`; `search` with `{"query":"","limit":100}` and then `{"query":"","limit":100,"page":<next>}`. A test builds a store over its own database, a Gmail client over a loopback fake Gmail, fake artifacts and connections sockets, the engine and the drafts service over them, an `mcp.Server` from `mcp.NewServer`, registers the tools, serves the server inside `identity.Require`, calls `mcp.Client.CallTool` with JSON arguments, compares a paging refusal's text with `paging.BadPage` or with the `Error()` of `paging.Window`'s error, decodes a success's structured content into `tools.MessageList`, `tools.Message`, `tools.LabelList`, `tools.Record`, `tools.TransferList`, `tools.Draft` or `tools.DraftList`, compares a refusal's text with the word, `": "`, the `tools.Refuse*` constant and, for the connection refusals, `" "` and the URL, and inspects the requests the fakes recorded and the store.
