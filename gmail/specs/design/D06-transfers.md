# D06-transfers

The engine: `internal/transfers`, the one place a transfer is validated, recorded, queued, started, run, finished, settled at start and abandoned at the stop. A `pull` or a `push` (`D10-tools`) hands the engine the caller and the arguments; the engine asks connections for the caller's Google token, checks everything it can check before anything is written, records the transfer `running` or `queued` (`D04-store`), answers it at once, and moves the bytes in the background as the caller: Gmail to artifacts for a pull, artifacts onto a draft for a push. It is gdrive's engine with mail's references: the same record-then-run shape, the same bounded queue shared by every user, the same settling at start and drain at the stop, with Gmail's client (`D05-gmail`) in place of Drive's and one rule gdrive did not need, that pushes onto one draft run one at a time.

`D01-layout-and-run-seam` declares the run seam, the addresses and the default Gmail client; `D04-store` the records, their words and their transitions; `D05-gmail` the Gmail client, its message, attachment, draft and composition types and its errors; `D07-drafts` the drafts service, the composition rules, the reconciliation and the upload cap; `D09-trail` the events and the trail's secrecy; `D10-tools` the tools and the sentences a refusal word is shown with; `D03-serve` builds the engine and calls its `Recover` and `Drain`, and prunes through the store directly.

## Asking for the token

Every tool that reaches Gmail begins by asking connections, over its socket and as the caller, for the caller's Google token. The lookup is a package function, `transfers.Token`, so that the drafts service (`D07-drafts`), which the engine cannot be handed at its own construction without a cycle, maps a missing or broken connection exactly as the engine does; `Engine.Token` is the same lookup over the engine's own client and connect page, answering the access token alone, as gdrive's does, for the read tools; `Engine.ConnectURL` answers that connect page for the tools. The package function answers appkit's `connections.Token` whole, because the sender of every message gmail builds is the connected account, `Token.Account` (decision 7), which a push's work and `compose` need. connections' `not_connected` becomes the refusal `not_connected` carrying the connect page; `reconnect` becomes `reconnect` carrying the address connections named; `unknown_provider` and `provider` become `provider`; connections answering `unavailable`, an answer the client cannot read, an unexpected status, an unreachable socket or no `connections` entry at all become `unavailable`. These are gdrive's mappings unchanged.

## Refusals

A refusal is a `*transfers.Refusal`: a word and, for `not_connected` and `reconnect`, the URL the person opens. All of gmail's refusal words are declared here, the engine's and the drafts service's alike, so that the tools tell every refusal apart with one `errors.As` (`D10-tools`): gdrive's `not_connected`, `reconnect`, `expires`, `too_large`, `not_found`, `provider`, `path`, `unavailable`, `stopping` and `queue_full`, and decision 8's four new words `not_open`, `busy`, `to` and `address`. The engine and the drafts service never word a refusal; the sentences are `D10-tools`' copy constants, and a sentence names an id, never a subject, an address or a filename. gmail's own database failing is not a refusal: the call answers an error matching `store.ErrUnreachable`.

Gmail answering 401 for the token connections handed out means the grant no longer works: the client answers `gmail.ErrUnauthorized` (`D05-gmail`), and a call refuses `reconnect` with the connect page, the same page a person reconnects at; a transfer's work fails with reason `reconnect`.

## Pull

`Pull` checks, in order, and refuses at the first that fails: the token; `expires`, when given, as gdrive judges it (RFC 3339, later than the clock to the second); the path, by artifacts' `Head` of it as the caller (a path artifacts refuses is `path`, any other failure but not found `unavailable`, an artifact already there or none passing); the message, by the client's `Show` (unknown is `not_found`, Gmail's 401 `reconnect`, any other failure `provider`); the attachment, when given, which must be the `ID` of one of the message's listed attachments, the part ids `show` answers (decision 5), or `not_found`; the size, the part's `Bytes` for an attachment and, for a whole message, the `SizeEstimate` the client's `Stat` answers (root ruling A9), over `TRANSFER_MAX_BYTES` being `too_large`; the drain, `stopping`; and the queue, `queue_full`. The transfer recorded names the message, the part, the path, the content type the artifact will carry (the part's MIME type, or `message/rfc822` for a whole message) and a name: the part's filename, or the message's subject for a whole message (decision 11, root ruling A1).

## Push

`Push` checks, in order (decision 7's order, after the connection; root ruling A8): the token; artifacts' `Head` of the path as the caller (a path artifacts refuses is `path`, an artifact that does not exist `not_found`, any other failure `unavailable`); the artifact's size over `TRANSFER_MAX_BYTES`, `too_large`; then the draft, which the engine hands to the drafts service through the `transfers.Drafts` interface it is configured with: the upload cap (`too_large`), a draft unknown or another user's (`not_found`), a draft not `open` (`not_open`), and the reconciliation an `open` draft gets there (root ruling A5), all as `D07-drafts` states for `Attachable`; then the drain and the queue. The engine is handed the drafts service as an interface rather than the concrete type because `internal/drafts` imports this package for its refusals and its token lookup; the interface is the one method the engine needs. The transfer recorded names the draft, the draft's message as it stood, the path, the artifact's content type as `Head` answered it, and the path's last segment as its name.

## The queue

`TRANSFER_MAX_ACTIVE` transfers run at once, across every user; a transfer holds a slot from the moment it is recorded or started `running` until its `transfer.finished` is emitted. Waiting transfers wait in one queue in the order recorded, shared by every user, with one exception decision 9 requires: a push onto a draft waits for every earlier push onto the same draft, so pushes onto one draft run one at a time in the order recorded. A queued transfer is **eligible** unless it is a push and an earlier push onto the same draft is running or queued. When a slot is released, the eligible transfer queued longest is started and its `transfer.started` emitted, after the `transfer.finished` of the transfer whose slot was released; a queued push that is not eligible lets later eligible transfers pass it, so a slot never stands idle behind a draft's earlier push. A transfer is recorded `running` when a slot is free and it would be eligible, and `queued` otherwise. The queue-full check counts every waiting transfer, eligible or not, once, at the call, and applies only to a transfer that would wait: one that would start at once, a slot being free and no earlier push holding its draft, is never refused `queue_full` (decision 11: more than `TRANSFER_MAX_QUEUED` waiting refuses), however many blocked pushes wait.

## The work

A transfer's work runs in a goroutine, as the caller: under a context carrying the `identity.Caller` rebuilt from the record's user id, email and request id (`identity.NewContext`), and the event cause the call's context carried, so every request it sends to connections and artifacts carries the caller's identity headers and every event it records and emits names the caller and the originating request id. It is cancelled by the drain and by nothing else. It asks connections for a fresh token first, since a transfer may wait longer than the call's token lives.

A **pull** asks the client for the bytes, with `TRANSFER_MAX_BYTES` as the most it will take: `Attachment` of the message and part, which resolves the part to its current `attachmentId` itself, since Gmail does not keep that id stable (decision 5), or `Raw` of the message for a whole message. Gmail answers no media stream, so the client reads Gmail's JSON answer into one buffer and hands back a `gmail.Content`, a reader that base64url-decodes from it and the decoded length; about four thirds of the bytes are held and nothing touches disk (decision 12). Bytes past the maximum are the client's `ErrTooLarge`, which fails the transfer `too_large` before any `Put` (root ruling A9). Otherwise the reader goes to one artifacts `Put` of the path with the recorded content type and the expiry. A done pull carries the artifact's `url`, its id, and as `link` the source message in Gmail (`urls.MessageLink`).

A **push** rebuilds the whole message from the composition the connector keeps (decision 9): it reads the draft, opens an artifacts `Get` of every attachment the draft already holds, in order, and of the pushed path, fresh each time, and hands the client's `UpdateDraft` the draft's Gmail id and thread and the message `gmail.Build` makes of the composition: `transfers.Composition` of the draft and the connected account, each part sized by what its `Get` answered now (an artifact replaced since its push is uploaded at its current size, since a part whose stream does not match its declared size fails the build), and the pushed artifact appended. The builder streams each body through a base64 encoder into the resumable upload, `gmail.PushChunk` at a time; only headers, text and the piece in flight are held (decision 12). Every `Get` is opened before any byte goes to Gmail, so a missing artifact fails the push with nothing sent; a push overwrites any edit a person made to that draft in Gmail (decision 9). Gmail replaces the draft only when the upload completes, and only then is the attachment added to the composition (`store.AttachToDraft`), with the new message id and thread Gmail answered, since `drafts.update` gives the draft a new message, and a draft that is not a reply a new thread (root rulings A6, A19). A done push carries the pushed artifact's `url`, no artifact id, and as `link` the draft in Gmail by its new message id (`urls.DraftLink`), a snapshot that goes stale if the draft changes later. A push whose draft is no longer `open` when its work begins, or ends while its upload runs, fails `not_found`: there is no draft to attach to.

Bytes moved are counted as the destination reads them (artifacts' `Put` for a pull, the upload for the pushed artifact's body), and the record's `bytes` follows the count at every whole multiple of `ProgressStep`. A progress write the store refuses is ignored, as in gdrive: progress is a view, and the ending writes the final count.

A transfer ends exactly once, `done` or `failed` with one of the store's seven reasons: `reconnect` for a work token connections refuses with `reconnect` or `not_connected`, or for Gmail's 401; `provider` for any other connections or Gmail failure; `not_found` for a message, part, draft or artifact gone; `forbidden` for a `Put` artifacts refuses with 403; `too_large` for bytes over the maximum, a message past Gmail's cap or a `Put` refused with 413; `artifacts` for any other artifacts failure, a body that breaks mid-upload or does not match its size included; `interrupted` for the drain, the start after a dead process, and gmail's own database failing under the work.

## Starting and stopping

`Recover` runs at start, before ready (`D03-serve`): every transfer still `queued` or `running` is finished `failed` with `interrupted`, its `transfer.finished` emitted as its caller. `Drain` is the stop: from its call `Pull` and `Push` refuse `stopping`; every queued transfer is finished `failed` with `interrupted` at once; running transfers go on while `Drain`'s context lives and are cancelled when it ends, finished `failed` with `interrupted`. An artifacts `Put` cut off leaves nothing, and a resumable upload cut off before its end replaces nothing at Gmail (decision 9). `Drain` returns once no transfer of the engine is queued or running. Pruning is not the engine's in gmail: `D03-serve` calls the store's two prunes directly.

## Recorded decisions

- **Departures from gdrive.** The engine has no `Prune` and no `KeepDays` or `KeepCount` (`D03-serve` prunes transfers and drafts through the store). The lookup is also a package function, `transfers.Token`, answering the whole `connections.Token` because the sender is the connected account; the drafts service and the engine's own `Pull`, `Push` and work use it, while `Engine.Token` answers the access token alone as gdrive's does. `Engine.ConnectURL` is new, for the tools. The refusal words of the drafts service live here beside the engine's, one type for the tools. There is no `not_a_file`, `not_exportable` or `format`, which were Drive's. The queue gains the per-draft eligibility rule of decision 9. The read side holds one encoded copy in memory where gdrive streamed (decision 12). A pull's size check uses the message's `SizeEstimate` for a whole message (root ruling A9) and the decoded length again at run time; gdrive did not count a stream against the maximum. A push's link is the draft's, taken from the message id the upload answered.
- **Gmail's 401 is `reconnect`** with the connect page (brief, `D05-gmail`'s error mapping): connections handed out a token Google no longer honours, and the person's remedy is to reconnect. gdrive treated Drive's 401 as `provider`.
- **The push's draft checks belong to the drafts service.** The cap needs the composition, and the reconciliation of an `open` draft (root ruling A5) is the drafts service's; the engine calls `Attachable` at the place decision 7's order puts the draft, and is refused with what it answers. A reconciliation there may end the draft (`sent` or `discarded`) and emit its event even though the push is then refused `not_open`: the draft's change is a fact learned, not a write of the push.
- **Every `Get` before any byte to Gmail.** A push opens the artifacts `Get` of every attachment, in order, before it calls `UpdateDraft`, so a missing artifact fails the push `not_found` with no request sent to Gmail. The bodies are held open, not read: their bytes still stream through the encoder one part at a time.
- **The pushed part's content type is the transfer's**, the one `Head` answered at the call, so the record, the composition and the rebuilt part agree.
- **A push whose draft ended meanwhile fails `not_found`.** A `send` or `discard` that ran between a push's call and its work (the `busy` refusal covers only pushes already recorded) leaves the push nothing to attach to; the work sends no upload, or, when `AttachToDraft` answers `ErrNotOpen` after the upload, fails the transfer all the same.
- **The store failing under the work.** A progress write that fails is ignored and the work carries on, its ending counting every byte, as gdrive's does. A draft read or an `AttachToDraft` the store cannot answer is met by trying to finish the transfer `failed` with `interrupted`, the closest of the seven reasons; with the store failing that ending is not recorded either, so no requirement states it: an ending the store cannot record emits no event and releases the slot, as in gdrive, and the next start settles it `interrupted`.
- **The decoded length is checked before the `Put`** (root ruling A9's run-time check): the client is given the maximum and refuses past it, so a message whose raw form outgrew its `SizeEstimate` never touches the artifact. A whole-message pull costs one `Stat` beside the `Show`, because the full message the client answers carries no size estimate and the minimal one no subject.
- **The work's token is fresh**, as in gdrive: a queued transfer may start minutes after the call.
- **The busy check reads the store.** Whether a push is queued or running on a draft is what the store records for the caller's push transfers naming that draft; the engine records every push `queued` or `running` before `Push` returns and the store holds its ending before its slot is released, so the store and the engine's queue agree (`D07-drafts`).
- **One mapping from a draft to a message.** `transfers.Composition` turns a stored draft and the sender into the client's `gmail.Composition`; `compose`, the cap check and the push's work all use it, so the message Gmail receives, the size the cap judges and the rebuild cannot drift apart. It lives here, not in `internal/drafts`, because the engine cannot import that package.
- **Deferred** (decisions document): a short wait inside `pull` and `push`, `cancel`, retries, resuming an interrupted transfer, pulling every attachment of a message in one call.

## Consumer tasks

`D03-serve` builds `dr := drafts.New(drafts.Config{...})` first, then `eng := transfers.New(transfers.Config{Store: st, Gmail: gc, Artifacts: ac, Connections: cc, Drafts: dr, Telemetry: w, Events: em, ConnectURL: urls.Connect(urls.Base(connectionsURL)), MaxBytes: s.TransferMaxBytes, MaxActive: s.TransferMaxActive, MaxQueued: s.TransferMaxQueued, Now: p.Now})`, calls `eng.Recover(bg)` before ready and `eng.Drain(drainCtx)` at the signal. The tools (`D10-tools`), `c` the call's caller: `tok, err := eng.Token(ctx, c)` before `search`, `thread`, `show` and `labels`; `t, err := eng.Pull(ctx, c, transfers.PullRequest{Message: m, Attachment: part, Path: p, Expires: expires})` and `t, err := eng.Push(ctx, c, transfers.PushRequest{Path: p, Draft: id})`; a refusal told with `var r *transfers.Refusal; errors.As(err, &r)`, reading `r.Word` and `r.URL`, and any other error, which matches `store.ErrUnreachable`, answered `unavailable`. A test builds a store over its own database, appkit's `artifacts` and `connections` clients over entries naming Unix sockets on which loopback fakes listen, a `gmail.Client` whose `BaseURL` is an `httptest.Server` faking Gmail, a `drafts.Service` over the same, a writer and an emitter over captures, and an engine with small limits and a fixed clock; it calls `Pull` and `Push` with an `identity.Caller`, holds a fake's answer open to keep a transfer running, polls `store.GetTransfer`, calls `Drain` with a context it cancels, and compares the store, the fakes' requests and the captured events.
