# D07-drafts

The drafts service: `internal/drafts`, the one place a draft is composed, sent, discarded and reconciled with what Gmail holds, and where the rule that keeps a draft sendable, Gmail's 35 MiB upload cap, is computed. `compose`, `send`, `discard` and `draft` (`D10-tools`) hand the service the caller and the arguments; the engine (`D06-transfers`) hands it a `push`'s draft to check. Gmail has no call that adds a part to a draft, because `drafts.update` replaces the whole message, so the connector keeps what `compose` was given, the **composition**, in its own `drafts` table (`D04-store`) and rebuilds the whole message from it on every `push` (decision 9). The connector never parses a draft. `push` therefore works only on a draft the connector made, and overwrites any edit a person made to it in Gmail; `send` sends what Gmail holds, so a person can still correct wording in Gmail before the agent sends.

`D04-store` declares the draft record, its words, its writes and how its `link` and `modified` follow its status; `D05-gmail` the client, its message, stat, draft and composition types, the builder, the cap and its errors; `D06-transfers` the token lookup, the refusal type and its words, and the engine that calls `Attachable`; `D09-trail` the draft events and the trail's secrecy; `D10-tools` the tools and the sentences.

## Compose

`Compose` checks, in order (decision 7): the token, by `transfers.Token`; a recipient, since without `reply_to` `to` is required (`to`); every address given in `to`, `cc` and `bcc`, each of which must be one address the standard library's `net/mail.ParseAddress` accepts (`address`); and the original, when `reply_to` names one, by the client's `Show` (unknown is `not_found`, Gmail's 401 `reconnect`, any other failure `provider`). A reply then takes its defaults from the original: it joins the original's thread; it carries `In-Reply-To`, the original's `Message-ID`, and `References`, the original's `References` followed by its `Message-ID`; with no `subject` given, its subject is the original's with `Re: ` prefixed once, not again when the original's already begins `Re:` in any case; with no `to` given, its recipients are the addresses of the original's `Reply-To`, or of its `From` when it has none, and a reply that finds none is refused `to`. The sender is always the connected account, the `Account` connections answers with the token; gmail never takes a sender from the caller. The service builds the message with `transfers.Composition` and `gmail.Build` and creates the draft at Gmail in the original's thread, records it `open` with Gmail's draft id, message id and thread and the composition (the body included, which only `draft` answers and no page shows), and emits `draft.created`.

Addresses are kept as given: an address the caller wrote with a display name keeps it. Defaulted recipients are the bare addresses (`addr-spec`) of the original's header, so a reply's `to` reads as the mailbox it goes to.

## Send and discard

`Send` checks: the token; the draft, which must be the caller's (`not_found` otherwise, another user's indistinguishable from none); that it is `open` as stored (`not_open`); that it is still a draft at Gmail, by the reconciliation below, which may end it (`not_open`); and that no push onto it is queued or running (`busy`), which the service learns from the caller's transfers in the store (`D06-transfers`). Then one synchronous `drafts.send`: the record is marked `sent` with the sent message's id and answered. A Gmail failure is the `provider` refusal (`reconnect` for Gmail's 401) and leaves the draft `open`. `Discard` checks the same and deletes the draft at Gmail; the record is marked `discarded`, keeping its message. Each emits its event, `draft.sent` or `draft.discarded`.

## Reconciliation

A draft sent or deleted in Gmail by the person is learned when a tool next touches its record (decision 10): `draft`, `push` (at its validation, through `Attachable`), `send` and `discard` reconcile an `open` draft (root ruling A5); `drafts` and the pages make no Gmail call and show what is stored. The probe is `drafts.get` of the draft's Gmail id. When it answers, the draft is still there, and when the message id it answers differs from the stored one, because a person edited the draft in Gmail, the service stores the new id (root ruling A18; `store.SetDraftMessage`, no event, `modified` unchanged, the `link` following it). When Gmail answers the draft id unknown, the service looks up the draft's stored message with the client's `Stat`: found with the label `SENT`, the record becomes `sent`; otherwise, the message unknown included, `discarded`.

This rests on an assumption not yet proven: that Gmail keeps a draft's message id when a person sends the draft in Gmail, so the stored id finds the sent message (root ruling A15, pending the orchestrator; no live probe was made). The requirements that depend on it are the reconciliation's lookup and its outcomes, R-K912-4V82, R-KBGU-WEPG and R-KCOR-A6G5, the reconciliation path of R-K7T5-R3HD after a send or delete answered unknown, and `D09-trail`'s R-XFL1-EK3L. `send` does not depend on it: it records the id `drafts.send` answers. A remaining limitation (root ruling A18): a draft a person edits in Gmail and then sends in Gmail, with no tool touching the record in between, is reconciled `discarded`, since the stored id names the message before the edit. The reconciliation emits `draft.sent` or `draft.discarded` as the tool's caller, exactly as an explicit `send` or `discard` does (root ruling A7). A `drafts.send` or `drafts.delete` that Gmail answers with the draft id unknown is not taken alone as the draft gone, since a 404 from those calls is weaker evidence than the probe's own (a send Gmail refuses for a bad header answers 400, which the client reads as `provider`, needing no probe): the service probes with `drafts.get`, reconciles only when that also answers the draft unknown, and otherwise refuses `provider`, leaving the draft `open`. What the tool then answers follows root ruling A13: `discard` of a draft the reconciliation finds `discarded` succeeds with that record (the one `draft.discarded` is the reconciliation's), and is refused `not_open` when it finds it `sent`; `send` is refused `not_open` whenever the reconciliation ends the draft, since the agent did not send it; `push` is refused `not_open`; `draft` answers the reconciled record. There is no polling.

## The upload cap

Gmail takes a draft's message up to 36,700,160 bytes (35 MiB), `gmail.UploadCap` (`D05-gmail`). A `push` is refused `too_large` before anything is recorded when the message rebuilt with its artifact would pass that cap (decision 7), so a draft that can never send is never built. The size is not estimated but computed exactly: `gmail.Build` of the composition the push would upload, the stored attachments at their recorded sizes and the new artifact at the size `Head` answered, reports its `Size()` without opening any part, so no byte is read to judge the cap. This makes decision 7's "sizes times four thirds plus the headers and text" exact, base64 line breaks and part headers included, and the same builder makes the message the push uploads.

## Recorded decisions

- **Departures from gdrive.** gdrive has no drafts; this service is new. The cap is Gmail's own constant and the builder's exact size (`D05-gmail`), declared there rather than here. Its refusals are the engine's `*transfers.Refusal`, and its token lookup is `transfers.Token`, so the tools tell every refusal apart with one type and one mapping of connections' answers. It holds appkit's `connections` client itself, beside the engine, because the engine is built with the service (`Attachable`) and cannot also be handed to it.
- **The busy check reads the store**, not the engine's memory: a push queued or running on a draft is a transfer of the caller recorded `queued` or `running` with that draft (`D06-transfers`). The engine records each push before `Push` returns and the store holds its ending before its slot is released, so the two agree, and the service needs nothing of the engine. A push recorded after the busy check, while `drafts.send` runs, fails at its work with `not_found` (`D06-transfers`).
- **Check order for `send` and `discard`**: the token, then the record, then the stored status, then the reconciliation, then `busy`, then the Gmail call. Decision 7 lists no connection, not found, not open and busy in that order; the reconciliation is part of learning whether the draft is open.
- **`Get` asks for the token only for an `open` draft** (root ruling A14): a draft already `sent` or `discarded` needs no Gmail call, so reading it needs no connection. An `open` draft's reconciliation that Gmail fails is refused `provider` (or `reconnect`), not answered from the store, so the caller never takes a stale `open` for the truth.
- **The sent time of a reconciled draft is the clock's**, when the reconciliation learned it (root ruling A6 lets the author choose): it needs nothing of the message's own date from the client, and `sent` is then when gmail learned of the send.
- **The probe is `drafts.get`, the follow-up `Stat` of the stored message** (`messages.get` with format `minimal`, whose `Sent` reads the `SENT` label). A successful probe refreshes the stored message id (root ruling A18).
- **`compose` past Gmail's cap is `too_large`**: a body so long that the message passes `gmail.UploadCap` is refused by the client before any request, and the service answers gdrive's word for it, though decision 7 lists no such refusal for `compose`.
- **Subject `Re: ` once**: a case-insensitive `re:` at the start of the original's subject, after leading spaces, counts as already prefixed. An explicit `subject`, even empty, is never changed.
- **References**: the original's `References`, trimmed, then a space and its `Message-ID`; only the `Message-ID` when it has no `References`; only its `References` when it has no `Message-ID`, in which case `In-Reply-To` is empty.
- **A reply's recipients come from the header**: the original's `Reply-To` when it is not empty, else its `From`, each parsed with `net/mail.ParseAddressList`; a header that does not parse, or names no address, leaves the reply with no recipient (`to`), since the caller can name `to` explicitly.
- **A draft created at Gmail and not recorded is deleted.** When the store cannot record a draft Gmail has just created, the service asks Gmail to delete it before answering the database's failure, so no draft the connector does not know is left behind.
- **Address parsing is the standard library's** (`net/mail.ParseAddress`): one address per element; a list in one element, a bare name, and the empty string are refused `address`.

## Consumer tasks

`D03-serve` builds `dr := drafts.New(drafts.Config{Store: st, Gmail: gc, Connections: cc, Telemetry: w, Events: em, ConnectURL: urls.Connect(urls.Base(connectionsURL))})` before the engine and hands it to `transfers.Config.Drafts`. The tools (`D10-tools`), `c` the call's caller: `d, err := dr.Compose(ctx, c, drafts.ComposeRequest{To: to, Cc: cc, Bcc: bcc, Subject: subject, Body: body, ReplyTo: replyTo})`, `dr.Send(ctx, c, id)`, `dr.Discard(ctx, c, id)` and `dr.Get(ctx, c, id)`, each answering the `store.Draft`, `draft` (through `Get`) with its `Body` and the others too, the tools choosing what to show; `drafts` calls `st.ListDrafts(ctx, c.UserID)` and makes no Gmail call. A refusal is told with `var r *transfers.Refusal; errors.As(err, &r)`; any other error matches `store.ErrUnreachable`. The engine calls `dr.Attachable(ctx, c, tok, id, a)` through `transfers.Drafts`. A test builds a store over its own database, a `connections.Client` over a loopback fake, a `gmail.Client` whose `BaseURL` is an `httptest.Server` faking Gmail, captures for the writer and the emitter, and the service; it records push transfers through the store to make a draft busy, has the fake answer `drafts.get` 404 and `messages.get` with or without `SENT`, and compares the answers, the store, the fake's requests and the captured events.
