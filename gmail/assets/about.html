How it works

gmail lets your agents work with your Gmail. An agent finds mail with search, reads it with thread and show, and sees your labels with labels; the text of a message reaches it in the tools' answers.

pull copies an attachment, or the whole message as an .eml file, into an artifact. compose writes a draft, a reply when asked, push attaches an artifact to it, and send sends it or discard deletes it. drafts and draft show the drafts it wrote.

pull and push record a transfer and answer at once. The bytes move on this host in the background, streamed between Gmail and artifacts as you, and transfer shows the outcome. When a pull is done, the artifact's url is where a shell fetches the result.

No attachment byte, raw message or credential passes through the agent, and gmail never marks, labels, archives or trashes your mail: it reads mail and writes drafts, and gets a short-lived Google token from connections on the host.

Connect Google at connections first. Until you do, or while the connection needs reconnecting, every tool refuses with the link to connect, for the agent to hand you.
