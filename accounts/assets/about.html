How it works

accounts keeps the business groups and the SSH users on this space, and makes each change on the host at once. A group is a business, named by a slug that is valid as a Linux account name. The slug names the group, a service account of the same name, and the folder /srv/<slug>, which the service account owns. The folder is writable by the group's members and unreadable by anyone else, every file in it is writable by the whole group, and it starts as an empty git repository, so members commit to it as themselves. Nothing in it refers to the host, so a business moves to its own host by copying the folder. A group with members cannot be removed, and removing an empty group leaves its folder in place.

A user is a person, with a login name, one or more ed25519 public keys, membership in one or more groups, and a state, active or locked. Each signs in over SSH with a key, never a password, and gets an ordinary home readable by them alone, with no other access to the host. Locking a user ends their access and their sessions and keeps their home and memberships; unlocking restores them. Only a locked user can be deleted, which removes the account and its home.

Every change is made by an agent with the tools, and each is recorded in telemetry with its caller and result. A change that could not be made on the host is reported as failed and not recorded as done. This page only lists the groups and users.
