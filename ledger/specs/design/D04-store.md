# D04-store

Decisions cited as seam N are /home/mgreenly/projects/ikigenba-ledger.md, the decisions document that delivers ledger; the orchestrator's rulings on its open questions are cited by their letter and number (B1 and the rest).

The records: `internal/store`, which owns ledger's tables in its SQLite database, `state/ledger.db`, and every read and write of them: `book`, the one row holding the currency, the lock date and the fiscal year start (seam 2); `commodities`, seeded with `USD` at precision 2 and nothing else; `accounts`, one row per declared account, keyed by its name; `transactions`, one row per posted transaction, reversals included, never changed but for its reversal pointer and its current version; `postings`, the money of each transaction, never changed; `annotations`, every version of a transaction's words; `tags` and `links`, the current words in rows a filter can search; and `assertions`, `notes` and `documents`, each kept with its retraction. This document declares the store's whole public surface: its records and the words they are spelled with, amounts and dates and how they are read and written, every method with its signature, the rules every record obeys and how each changes, how ids are minted, the reports and their arithmetic, the windows and the statements that serve them, the errors every consumer tells apart, the one line every consumer answers when the database cannot be reached, and the migration. Which tool calls which method, how an amount and a time ride the wire and which refusal word an error becomes are the tools' (`D07-tools`); which bus and trail events a change makes is `D05-events`' and `D08-trail`'s; the landing page reads `Status` (`D06-pages`). Opening, migrating, transactions and the failure seam are appkit's `db`; `D01-layout-and-run-seam` resolves the path and declares the root package's `Migrations()`.

## One database, one writer

ledger is the only writer of its database, and the database is the only record of its books. `store.New` builds a store over a handle and opens or creates nothing. What a store holds when its handle is closed is what a store over the next `db.Open` of the same path holds; a test proves the records that way, never by reading tables. A store whose handle is failing (`SetFailing(true)`), or closed, answers every call with an error matching `ErrUnreachable` and changes nothing. `Config.Now` is the clock every stamp is read from, read once per call, so every time one call writes is the same second, and it is also where the store's today comes from: the UTC calendar date of that one read (B1). Every default of "today" and every "as of today" is the store's, so the tools and the pages carry no clock. `Config.Rand` is the source the four kinds of id are drawn from. `Run` hands in `Process.Now` and `Process.Rand`, and the store has no default for either.

## The records

The `Book` is the one set of books (seam 2): its currency, `USD`, and that commodity's precision, its lock date (empty when none), its fiscal year start as `MM-DD` (`01-01` until changed), and when and by whom it last changed. Nothing creates or removes it; the migration seeds it. `Status` is the book with its counts and the latest transaction's date: the `book` tool answers the book, who last changed it and when, and the counts of open accounts, transactions and failing assertions, and the landing page relays those counts with the latest transaction's date (Part C, B15).

An `Account` is a declared account: its name, which is its key, its open date and its close date (empty until closed), and when and by whom it was opened and closed. Only declared accounts are rows; a parent such as `Assets:Bank` is implied by `Assets:Bank:Checking` and has a balance in every report, but it is not an account until it is opened. An `AccountRead` is one account with its balance as of a date, rolled up through its subtree, the count of postings to it alone, and its assertions, notes and documents, retracted ones included.

A `Transaction` is a posted transaction: its id, `txn_` and 16 lowercase hexadecimal digits, a sequence number that orders transactions in the order they were posted, its date, its key (empty when the caller gave none), the id of the transaction it reverses and the id of the transaction that reversed it (each empty when none), when and by whom it was posted, its postings and its current `Annotation`. A `Posting` is a position, counting from 1, an account and an `Amount`. The date, the postings, the key and the reversal pointers are the money and never change, but for the one time `reversed_by` is set. An `Annotation` is one version of the transaction's words: the flag, `pending` or `cleared`, the payee, the narration, the tags, the links and the metadata, with when and by whom it was written. The highest version is current; every read but `ListVersions` answers it.

An `Assertion` (`bal_`) says an account's balance, rolled up through its subtree, at the end of a date; its `AssertionRow` adds whether it holds, the actual balance and the difference, computed at every read and never stored. A `Note` (`note_`) is text on a declared account at a date. A `Document` (`doc_`) is a reference to an artifact on a declared account at a date, optionally naming a transaction, with the artifact id artifacts answered for its path. Each of the three keeps its retraction, who and when, empty until retracted; a retracted record is kept and answered with its retraction.

The reports are `Line`s, each an account name and an amount: a `Trial`, the trial balance with its total; a `BalanceSheet`; and an `IncomeStatement` with its net.

## Amounts and dates

An amount is an integer count of minor units with its commodity, summed in SQL and in Go integers, never in floating point. `ParseAmount` reads the wire's decimal text, an optional minus, digits, an optional point and at most the commodity's precision of fractional digits, so `"42."` is 42; it refuses anything else, and any amount past 999999999999999 minor units in magnitude (B2), which keeps every sum of a realistic book far inside `int64`. `Amount.String` writes the text back at the commodity's precision, `"0.50"`, never `"-0.00"`. Every amount the store answers carries `USD` and precision 2, read from `commodities`, so a second commodity is an added row later, never a schema change.

A date is `YYYY-MM-DD` text, a calendar date with no time and no zone, stored as text so that byte order is date order; `ValidDate` refuses one that does not exist. The fiscal year start is `MM-DD` and names a day that exists in every year, so `02-29` is refused (B5).

## Ids

A transaction id is minted by `Post` and `Reverse`, an assertion id by `AssertBalance`, a note id by `AddNote` and a document id by `AddDocument`, each from `Config.Rand`: 8 bytes per attempt written as 16 lowercase hexadecimal digits after the prefix, drawn again when the store already holds that id among records of its kind, at most 8 attempts. A `Rand` yielding `0x5a` for every byte gives `txn_5a5a5a5a5a5a5a5a`, `bal_5a5a5a5a5a5a5a5a`, `note_5a5a5a5a5a5a5a5a` and `doc_5a5a5a5a5a5a5a5a`. Nothing else reads `Rand`, and a call that records nothing reads none of it.

## The book and the accounts

`SetBook` takes a pointer for the lock date and one for the fiscal year start, nil leaving each; the lock date may move either way, lie in the future, and is cleared by the empty string (seam 2). A call that changes neither writes nothing. `OpenAccount` declares an account by a name of at least two segments, the first a root (B7a); an existing name is answered as it stands. `CloseAccount` closes an account once, on a date not before its open date and not before any posting to it, whatever its balance; a second close is answered as it stands. Accounts are never renamed, reopened or removed.

## Transactions

`Post` writes a transaction and its first annotation in one write. Its postings sum to exactly zero; one of them may leave its amount out and takes the remainder, which the store writes in full. A posting names a declared account and falls within its open and close dates, and a transaction dated on or before the lock date is refused. A key the store already holds answers that transaction and false before anything else is looked at, so a caller retrying a post never posts twice.

`Annotate` writes the next version of the words: each field it names replaces that field whole, the rest carry forward, and a call that names nothing or only what already holds writes nothing. The flag of a post defaults to `pending`. Annotations are not dated changes, so the lock date does not stop them.

`Reverse` writes a new transaction whose postings are the original's negated in the same order, dated the date given or today, not before the original's date and after the lock date. Its words are the ones given, defaulting to the original's current ones, and its flag is `cleared`. The original's `reversed_by` is set; its words are not touched. A transaction is reversed once: a second reverse answers the existing reversal and false. A reversal is itself a transaction, so it can be annotated and reversed.

## Assertions, notes and documents

`AssertBalance` records an assertion on any account prefix, declared or not, since a parent is a valid thing to assert, at any date after the lock date, whatever the account's open and close dates (B6). Its status is computed at every read: it holds when the balance at the end of its date equals what it expects, and its difference is actual minus expected (B12). `ListFailing` lists the unretracted assertions dated on or before today that fail. `AddNote` and `AddDocument` need a declared account, a date within its open and close dates (B6) and after the lock date; a document's transaction, when named, must be one the store holds, whatever it posts to and whenever (B14). `CheckDocument` answers every refusal `AddDocument` would, reading only, so the tools can refuse before they ask artifacts anything. The three retractions mark a record and change nothing when it is already marked.

## Reports

A balance as of a date is the sum of the postings dated on or before it whose account is the prefix or in its subtree (seam 2). `Balances` lists every declared account and every implied parent that has a balance, in name order, optionally under a prefix, with the total of the lines it answers, counting each line once with no line under another (B8b): under a prefix it is that prefix's balance, the filtered total, not the whole book's, and with no prefix it is the sum of the five roots, zero in a sound book. `BalanceSheetAt` lists the `Assets`, `Liabilities` and `Equity` subtrees with the two computed earnings lines under `Equity`, rolled up into `Equity:Earnings` and `Equity` so the three roots sum to zero (B11); a declared `Equity:Earnings:Current` carries its own postings plus the computed amount (B11c). `IncomeStatementFor` lists the `Income` and `Expenses` subtrees over a date range and their sum negated as the net, so profit is positive. A line with a zero amount and no declared account is left out. Signs are beancount's.

## Windows

The journal, the register, an annotation's versions and the failing assertions are windowed by the database on an index (shape's Storage). The journal walks `transactions_journal` on date and sequence, newest first, from the `to` date or the position down to the `from` date, its account, tag, link, payee and flag filters each a test guarded by a bound value and answered by a primary key search; the register walks `postings_register` on date, transaction sequence and position, oldest first, its subtree a filter; the versions walk `annotations`' primary key down from the position; and the failing assertions walk `assertions_check` on the retraction mark, date, account and id, each assertion's actual a sum over `postings_account`. Each window is two fixed statements, the first window and the window after a position, every value bound, exported so a test can ask SQLite for its plan; no list a page shows is windowed, so there are no reversed statements. A position is the sort key of the last entry: a transaction's date and sequence, a register row's date, sequence and position, a version number, an assertion's date, account and id; an empty date, a version of 0 or less or an empty id asks for the first window. The store knows nothing of page tokens; the tools encode and decode positions with appkit `paging`, asking for one more entry than they show. The chart, the trial balance, the statements and an account's own assertions, notes and documents are answered whole: each is bounded by configuration or by one account.

## Errors

`ErrNotFound`, `ErrInvalid`, `ErrLocked`, `ErrUnbalanced` and `ErrUnreachable` are the store's errors. `ErrNotFound` answers an id the store does not hold or that is malformed, an account a posting, note, document, close or account read names that is not declared, and a document's transaction the store does not hold. `ErrLocked` answers a dated change on or before the lock date, and `ErrUnbalanced` postings that do not balance; seam 2 refuses both `invalid`, and the tools tell them apart only to choose a sentence. `ErrInvalid` answers every other value or state the store refuses. Every other error matches `ErrUnreachable`, and every consumer answers it with `Unreachable` (a tool's `unavailable`), never quoting the cause: a failing or closed handle, a done context, a random source that fails or keeps colliding, and a call given a value no consumer sends (an empty user id, an empty artifact id, a window of fewer than one entry). Before anything else, a done context and a failing or closed handle answer `ErrUnreachable`, whatever the arguments. After that, within one call the checks run in a fixed order, which each method's requirement states: a value no consumer sends, then the idempotent answer, then the values, then the ids and declared accounts, then the state; `OpenAccount` and `CloseAccount` check the name's shape before their idempotent answer, since the name is the key the answer is looked up by.

## The migration

The schema is one migration, `0001_ledger.sql`: the seeded `book` and `commodities`; `accounts` keyed by name; `transactions` keyed by sequence, unique on id, on a non-empty key and on a non-empty reversed id, with the journal index on date and sequence; `postings` keyed by transaction sequence and position, each carrying its transaction's date so the register and the sums need no join, with the register index on date, sequence and position and the account index on account and date; `annotations` keyed by transaction sequence and version; `tags` and `links` keyed by the word and the transaction; and `assertions`, `notes` and `documents` keyed by id, each with its account index on account, date and id, assertions with the check index on the retraction mark, date, account and id. Times are RFC 3339 UTC text to the second, the empty text where none. The postings leave room for a cost and a price, nullable columns a later expand migration adds; the store names every column it reads and writes, so such a migration changes nothing it answers.

## Known limits

The journal's account, tag, link, payee and flag filters and the register's subtree are predicates tested along the date order, not index bounds: a window under a rare filter walks every transaction or posting in its date range until it has its rows. The book of one small business keeps that walk short; an index per filter is added when a book outgrows it.

## Recorded decisions

- **A store fails only through its handle**, as campaigns': a failing (`SetFailing(true)`) or closed handle is how a test provokes a database failure, and both answer `ErrUnreachable` whatever the arguments.
- Departure: **who is a user id only** (seam 3: "who did something is a user id"). campaigns keeps a user's email beside the id; ledger stores and answers neither an email nor anything but the id.
- Departure: **dates are `YYYY-MM-DD` strings, not `time.Time`** (seam 2: "calendar dates, no time, no zone"); byte order is date order, so every date comparison and order is on text. Stamps stay `time.Time`, UTC to the second, as campaigns'.
- Departure: **the store resolves today** (campaigns' store has no today): `reverse`, `account`, `balances`, `balance_sheet` and `check` default to or measure against today, so one clock, the store's, decides it, and the tools and pages carry none. Today is the UTC date of `Now` (B1, the orchestrator's ruling; a US business sees tomorrow from the evening).
- Departure: **the store checks every value and has five errors** (campaigns' store has three and its tools check values first): the rules are seam 2's and the store is their one owner, so the tools pre-check only what `D07-tools` lists as its own checks before the store. `ErrLocked` and `ErrUnbalanced` are separate sentinels so the tools can choose a sentence for each, though both become `invalid`.
- **The order of checks is fixed per method**: first a done context or a failing or closed handle, which answers `ErrUnreachable` before any other check, then a value no consumer sends, the idempotent answer, values, ids and declared accounts, then state; `OpenAccount` and `CloseAccount` check the name's shape before their idempotent answer; `Post` checks its key before anything else and `Reverse` whether the transaction is reversed, so a retried call answers what it made whatever else it carries (the brief).
- **The change is a result, not an inference**: each changing method answers whether it wrote, a `bool` before the error, so the tools record a trail event and emit on the bus exactly when told a record changed. `Post`, `Reverse`, `AssertBalance`, `AddNote` and `AddDocument` change records whenever they succeed, but for `Post`'s and `Reverse`'s idempotent answers.
- Departure: **the chart, the trial balance, an account's own records and the statements' lines are answered whole** (shape's Listing pages a list a person can add to): the chart and its balances are configuration-sized, an account's assertions, notes and documents belong to one account, and a statement is read whole. `D07-tools` records the tools' side.
- **Every declared account and only declared accounts are rows**; parents are implied, so reports roll up by name prefix in SQL. A name to open has at least two segments (B7a); a bare root is a valid prefix for the journal, the register, the trial balance and an assertion. `GetAccount` answers only declared accounts and refuses an undeclared implied parent of two or more segments `ErrNotFound` (B7b); a bare root is not an account name and is `ErrInvalid`, while `ListRegister` and `AssertBalance` take any account prefix, declared or not, deliberately: a subtree's postings and a parent's balance are meaningful without the parent being opened. Account names are ASCII (B16).
- **An open account is one whose close date is empty** (the orchestrator's ruling): a close is an act done once, and the count reads the act, not today's relation to its date. An account's `Postings` count is every posting to it alone, whatever its date, not bounded by the date asked (the orchestrator's ruling); its balance is the one figure measured at a date.
- **Positions count from 1** in the order the caller gave the postings, and a reversal's postings keep the original's positions.
- **Same account twice and a zero amount are allowed** in one transaction, since the document forbids neither; an elided remainder of zero is stored as 0, and an elided remainder past the amount bound is refused like an amount given (B2).
- **At most 100 postings per transaction** (B4, the orchestrator's ruling): `transaction.posted` carries every posting on the bus and appkit caps an event at 65536 bytes, so an unbounded transaction would lose its event.
- **Tags and links count distinct values** (decided for this delivery): they are kept sorted and deduplicated, so the limit is on what is kept. Payee, narration and note text are kept trimmed; metadata values are kept as given and their length is counted as given, since seam 2 trims only the payee and the narration (and the note text).
- **A reversal has no key** and its flag is `cleared`; its words default to the original's current ones and the store never composes a narration (seam 2). The original's annotations are not versioned by a reversal.
- **Retracted assertions: `account` answers them marked, `check` ignores them** (the orchestrator's ruling). The decisions document reads two ways: seam 2 says "Retracted assertions are kept and ignored by `check` and `account`", while seam 3 has `account` answer "its assertions with their status, ... retracted ones marked". `account` answers every assertion on the account with its retraction, and `ListFailing` leaves retracted ones out.
- **The book's failing count is the `check` set counted**, so the `book` tool, the landing page and `check` agree.
- **Transactions are a table without rowid** (a change from the sheet's outline, which made `seq` the rowid): SQLite takes a row value on `(date, seq)` as an index bound only when `seq` is a stored column of the index, and with `seq` as the rowid it bounded the window after a position on the date alone, walking every earlier transaction of that date. The store assigns `seq` as one more than the greatest held.
- **Windows by position, each two fixed statements** (shape's Storage and Listing), every value bound once with numbered parameters. The journal's and register's `to` date is an index bound in the first window through `iif(? = '', '9999-12-31', ?)`, the greatest date `ValidDate` accepts, and the position is the bound after it; every filter is a test guarded by its bound value (`? = '' OR ...`), answered through a correlated primary key search, so `transactions` stays the outer loop. Observed while designing with the `sqlite3` CLI and through appkit's `db` driver (`Read` on a handle `db.Open` returned) over this document's `0001_ledger.sql` with no `ANALYZE`, with every parameter empty and with every parameter set: `TransactionsFirst` `SEARCH t USING INDEX transactions_journal (date>? AND date<?)`, `TransactionsAfter` `SEARCH t USING INDEX transactions_journal (date>? AND (date,seq)<(?,?))`, each followed by four correlated subqueries, `SEARCH p USING PRIMARY KEY (txn=?)`, `SEARCH g USING PRIMARY KEY (tag=? AND txn=?)`, `SEARCH l USING PRIMARY KEY (link=? AND txn=?)` and `SEARCH a USING PRIMARY KEY (txn=? AND version=?)`; `RegisterFirst` `SEARCH p USING INDEX postings_register (date>? AND date<?)` and `RegisterAfter` `SEARCH p USING INDEX postings_register ((date,txn,position)>(?,?,?) AND date<?)`; `VersionsFirst` `SEARCH a USING PRIMARY KEY (txn=?)` and `VersionsAfter` `SEARCH a USING PRIMARY KEY (txn=? AND version<?)`; `FailingFirst` `SEARCH a USING INDEX assertions_check (retracted=? AND date<?)` and `FailingAfter` `SEARCH a USING INDEX assertions_check (retracted=? AND (date,account,id)>(?,?,?) AND date<?)`, each with a correlated subquery whose `MULTI-INDEX OR` searches `postings_account` on `(account=? AND date<?)` and `(account>? AND account<?)`; no `SCAN` and no `USE TEMP B-TREE` in any. That a method runs its statement rather than another giving the same rows cannot be observed by use; it is accepted unobserved, and a sandbox check's plans and timings on the real binary cover it.
- **The register's running balance is historical** (B8a, settled): a row's balance counts every posting of the subtree before it, before `from` included, so a window stands alone and the balance is the account's balance at that row.
- **Counts, balances and statuses are read, not kept**: computed from the postings at each read, so no write keeps a total in step and an assertion's status follows every later post.
- **Room for cost and price** (seam 2): no column now; a later expand migration adds nullable columns to `postings`, and the store names every column, so the release before it runs on that schema and inserts leave them null.
- A write given a value no consumer sends (an empty user id, an empty artifact id) answers `ErrUnreachable` and changes nothing, as campaigns'; `ErrInvalid` is kept for what a caller chose.

## Consumer tasks

The tools (`D07-tools`), `c` the caller and `st` the store, call: `book` `s, err := st.Status(ctx)`; `set_book` `s, changed, err := st.SetBook(ctx, store.BookEdit{LockDate: a.LockDate, FiscalYearStart: a.FiscalYearStart, UserID: c.UserID})`, recording `book.changed` only when `changed`; `accounts` `as, err := st.Accounts(ctx)`; `account` `r, err := st.GetAccount(ctx, a.Name, date)` with `date` `""` when absent, each `r.Assertions[i].Status`, `.Actual` and `.Difference` rendered with `Amount.String`; `open_account` and `close_account` `acct, changed, err := st.OpenAccount(ctx, store.AccountInput{Name: a.Name, Date: a.Date, UserID: c.UserID})` and `st.CloseAccount(...)`; `post` `t, posted, err := st.Post(ctx, store.PostInput{Date: a.Date, Postings: ps, Flag: flag, Payee: payee, Narration: narration, Tags: a.Tags, Links: a.Links, Metadata: md, Key: key, UserID: c.UserID})`, each `ps[i]` `store.PostingInput{Account: p.Account, Amount: p.Amount}`, recording and emitting `transaction.posted` from `t.ID`, `t.Date`, `t.Annotation.Flag`, `t.Key` and each `t.Postings[i].Account`, `.Amount.String()` and `.Amount.Commodity` only when `posted`; `transaction` `st.GetTransaction(ctx, a.Transaction)`; `annotate` `t, changed, err := st.Annotate(ctx, store.AnnotationEdit{Transaction: a.Transaction, Flag: a.Flag, Payee: a.Payee, Narration: a.Narration, Tags: a.Tags, Links: a.Links, Metadata: md, UserID: c.UserID})`, recording `transaction.annotated` with `t.Annotation.Version`; `reverse` `r, made, err := st.Reverse(ctx, store.ReversalInput{Transaction: a.Transaction, Date: date, Payee: a.Payee, Narration: a.Narration, Tags: a.Tags, Links: a.Links, Metadata: md, UserID: c.UserID})`, emitting from `r` with `r.Reverses` the original and recording `transaction.reversed` only when `made`; `history` `vs, err := st.ListVersions(ctx, a.Transaction, version, n+1)` after `paging.Token{Tool: "history"}.Decode(page, &version)`; `assert_balance` `row, err := st.AssertBalance(ctx, store.AssertionInput{Account: a.Account, Date: a.Date, Amount: a.Amount, UserID: c.UserID})`; `retract_assertion` `row, changed, err := st.RetractAssertion(ctx, store.Retraction{ID: a.Assertion, UserID: c.UserID})`; `check` `rows, err := st.ListFailing(ctx, date, account, id, n+1)`; `add_note` `nt, err := st.AddNote(ctx, store.NoteInput{Account: a.Account, Date: a.Date, Text: a.Text, UserID: c.UserID})`; `retract_note` `st.RetractNote(ctx, store.Retraction{ID: a.Note, UserID: c.UserID})`; `add_document`, with `in := store.DocumentInput{Account: a.Account, Transaction: txn, Date: a.Date, Path: a.Path, UserID: c.UserID}`, `err := st.CheckDocument(ctx, in)` first, then the artifacts client's `Head` and `Show`, then `in.Artifact = md.ID` and `d, err := st.AddDocument(ctx, in)`; `retract_document` `st.RetractDocument(ctx, store.Retraction{ID: a.Document, UserID: c.UserID})`; `journal` `ts, err := st.ListTransactions(ctx, store.JournalFilter{From: from, To: to, Account: account, Tag: tag, Link: link, Payee: payee, Flag: flag}, date, seq, n+1)`, its token `(t.Date, t.Seq)` of the last shown; `register` `rows, err := st.ListRegister(ctx, a.Account, from, to, date, seq, position, n+1)`, its token `(row.Date, row.Seq, row.Position)`; `balances` `tr, err := st.Balances(ctx, date, prefix)`; `balance_sheet` `bs, err := st.BalanceSheetAt(ctx, date)`; `income_statement` `is, err := st.IncomeStatementFor(ctx, a.From, a.To)`. `store.ErrNotFound` answers `not_found`; `store.ErrInvalid`, `store.ErrUnbalanced` and `store.ErrLocked` answer `invalid`, each with its own sentence; every other error answers `unavailable` with `store.Unreachable`. An amount rides the wire as `x.String()` beside `x.Commodity`; a time as `t.UTC().Format(time.RFC3339)`, `""` when `t.IsZero()`.

The pages (`D06-pages`) call `s, err := cfg.Store.Status(ctx)` for `/` and fill the landing data from `s.Book.Currency`, `s.Book.LockDate`, `s.Book.FiscalYearStart`, `s.OpenAccounts`, `s.Transactions`, `s.LatestDate` and `s.FailingAssertions`, answering `store.Unreachable` on an error.

A test opens its own database with `db.Open` and the root package's `Migrations()` in a temporary directory, builds `store.New(d, store.Config{Now: clock, Rand: rnd})` with a clock it moves and a reader yielding the bytes it chooses, opens accounts, posts, annotates, reverses, asserts and pages each window, flips `d.SetFailing(true)` or closes `d`, and reopens the path to prove what persists. It parses and renders amounts with `store.ParseAmount` and `store.Amount{Units: u, Commodity: "USD", Precision: 2}.String()`. A test of a window's plan runs `d.Read(ctx, func(tx *sql.Tx) error { rows, err := tx.QueryContext(ctx, "EXPLAIN QUERY PLAN "+store.TransactionsAfter, "", "", "", "", "", "", "", "2026-01-01", int64(1), 26); ... })` and reads each row's `id`, `parent`, `notused` and `detail`; a test of a statement's rows runs it the same way and compares its first columns with the window method's answer. A test of the migration reads `fs.ReadFile(ledger.Migrations(), "0001_ledger.sql")`; a test of the room for cost and price builds `fstest.MapFS` holding those bytes and a second file of its own that adds nullable columns to `postings`, opens a database with it and drives a store over it.
