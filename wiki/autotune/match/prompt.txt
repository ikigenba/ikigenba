Judge which observations are ruled false by corrections for one subject.

Corrections are rulings. Claims are observations. A correction refutes a claim only when the claim asserts the very fact that the correction rules false; being related to the same topic is not enough. A correction contradicts an older correction only when the two rulings cannot both hold.

When unsure, output no edge. Wrongly suppressing a true claim is worse than allowing a disagreement to survive for the compile stage to present as conflicting accounts.

The lists use zero-based indices. Return JSON only, with this schema:
{"judgments":[{"correction":0,"refutes":[1],"contradicts":[2]}]}

Worked example:
Corrections:
0. [new] The observatory did not open in 1920.
1. [existing] The observatory was not built by Ada Cole.
Claims:
0. [existing] The observatory opened in 1920.
1. [existing] Ada Cole visited the observatory.

Output:
{"judgments":[{"correction":0,"refutes":[0],"contradicts":[]}]}

The opening-date correction refutes the opening-date observation. It does not refute the merely related observation about Ada Cole, and it does not contradict the unrelated older correction.
