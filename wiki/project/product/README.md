# wiki — Product

**Authority: intent.** This document owns *why* the wiki exists, *for whom*, what is in and out of scope, and what we **promise** the user — in outcome terms only. Mechanism, data formats, schema, exit codes, and test assertions live in `project/design/README.md`. Where the two touch observable behavior, product states the *promise* and design states the *exact, checkable proof*; that boundary keeps product, design, and plan from overlapping.

## Problem

Knowledge worked out through an agent evaporates when the session ends, and the next conversation starts cold. Retrieval over raw chunks re-derives understanding every time — nothing accumulates, cross-references go undrawn, contradictions unreconciled — while a hand-maintained wiki collapses under its own upkeep. The user is left with no durable, compounding, inspectable store of what they've learned.

Running such a store surfaces more pains. The ingest is LLM-backed, and LLMs fail invisibly — most often by not returning the structured content the ingest needs — so failed and aborted work must be siftable, stoppable, and cleanly redoable, with each call's footprint attributed clearly enough (which stage, which job, which question) that a fix is not a guess; the suite keeps that record in one place, and wiki must label its calls so the record is usable. Each ingest is minutes of work, so if it holds the store while it runs, status checks, questions, and new ingests all stall — and one hung job wedges the whole service. And every LLM call site is a quality-and-cost choice (which model, thinking level, generation settings), so each must be tunable without moving the others. The extract prompt in particular is the quality ceiling of everything downstream — every subject, claim, and page inherits its errors — yet without measurement a prompt edit's effect is invisible, and improving it by eyeballing outputs is guesswork.

## Purpose

The wiki is ikigenba's knowledge base: an MCP service that *compiles* ingested text into durable per-subject pages and answers questions from them. Its primary surface is **MCP** (agents work through tools, not screens), but it also serves a small **human web surface** at its mount root: a logged-in person who opens `…/srv/wiki/` in a browser gets a search box to **ask** the wiki and read a cited answer, and can **follow links** from there into per-subject pages and onward through the subject graph. The web is **read-only navigation** — ask and browse, nothing that changes content — a styled human window onto the same `ask` and pages the MCP surface already exposes, with page and answer prose rendered as **formatted, sanitized markdown** rather than raw text; all mutating and operational work stays on MCP. Each ingest breaks text into the **subjects** it concerns and the **claims** it makes, then writes or updates each subject's page, so knowledge **compounds** — the LLM does the upkeep humans abandon; the user curates what goes in and asks. The owner can **merge** two names for the same thing into one page. All content lives in named **scopes** — independent partitions of the wiki (a team picks a scope name and lives in its own wiki space) — with a built-in `default` scope; every interaction names its scope explicitly, and nothing ever bleeds between scopes. A scope is private by default, readable only by signed-in users, but can be made **public**, letting anyone on the internet browse its pages and ask it questions (but never change anything). Around that core the wiki lets the user steer the ingest queue (list, abort, re-run); reads are always served and never wait on the slow, strictly-serial ingest work. Every call site is configured **independently**. The wiki executes **no model calls itself**: all inference runs through the suite's central inference service (prompts), which also keeps the durable record of every call and its spend — wiki attributes each call to its stage, its job or question, and the person who caused it, so that record is inspectable and meaningful.

## Users

- **The owner, through an agent with the wiki's MCP.** Ingests topics mid-conversation, later asks questions answered from everything ingested, and merges two names for the same thing on spotting a duplicate. Never edits pages by hand.
- **The developer/operator, on the same MCP surface.** Sees inside — subjects, claims, pages, and job state — and acts: aborts a wedged job, re-runs one after fixing code or a prompt **off-line** (applied by restarting the service). For any inference call's exact request, response, and spend, they turn to the suite's central inference service, where wiki's calls arrive clearly attributed. First-class, not an afterthought.

## Scope

The wiki does this and only this:

- **Partition content into scopes** — every subject, claim, page, merge, and job lives in exactly one named scope, and no read or answer ever draws on another scope's content: the same name in two scopes is two independent subjects with two independent pages. Scopes are **created explicitly** before use (naming an unknown scope is an error, never a silent new partition); the built-in `default` scope always exists and cannot be deleted; a scope can be deleted along with everything in it (`default` excepted); scope names are simple lowercase slugs. Every content interaction — MCP or web — names its scope: there is **no implicit scope**, because content silently landing in the wrong partition is the one failure this feature exists to prevent. Scopes are namespaces, not permissions: any signed-in user may use any scope.
- **Per-scope visibility** — each scope is **private** (its web pages readable only by signed-in users) or **public** (its web pages browsable and its questions askable by anyone, read-only); scopes start private, including `default`, and the owner can flip a scope's visibility at any time. The owner accepts that a public scope's question box spends inference on anonymous visitors' behalf. Visibility governs only the human web pages — the agent/MCP surface always requires authentication, for every scope.
- **Ingest text** — accept text, return a job handle immediately, integrate in the background. Each ingest infers the **subjects** and **claims**; raw text and claims are kept **permanently**.
- **Maintain per-subject pages** — write or update one coherent, deliberately **lossy** page per subject from its claims; a later mention updates the page rather than creating a duplicate. A page is **plain article prose**: it describes what its subject is in natural language (a king, a company, a battle), never in the wiki's internal record vocabulary, and it never contains an internal identifier or citation marker of any kind — the only thing woven into the prose for a reader is the subject links.
- **Merge two subjects** — the owner names the **survivor** and the one to **fold in**; claims move to the survivor, its page is rewritten, the folded subject and page are removed, and the folded name thereafter **routes to the survivor** for ingest, `ask`, links, and direct `type/slug` lookup. Background (returns a handle), owner-approved only, no claim lost, **irreversible**. The owner can list merges performed, newest-first, paged.
- **Track and control jobs** — report each job as `pending`/`working`/`done`/`failed`/`aborted`; list newest-first filtered by **any combination of states** and/or a **time window**, paged; **count** matches without retrieving them; **abort** a pending/working job (nothing partial committed) into a distinct `aborted` state; **re-run** a terminal job from its original text, **replacing** earlier claims. Filterable states are discoverable; an invalid state is **refused with a clear error** naming the valid set; re-running a pending/working job is refused.
- **Attribute every inference call** — for extract, compile, ask, and the **embedding** calls that power search, hand execution to the suite's central inference service with the call labeled by its stage, correlated to its ingest job or its question, and attributed to the person whose action caused it (falling back to the service itself when no person did); each retry is its own labeled call. The durable record — requests, responses, tokens, spend — lives with that central service, filterable by those labels.
- **See ingest progress** — by handle, report a job's state and, when finished, the subjects it produced.
- **Ask** — find the wiki pages relevant to the question and synthesize a **cited** answer from **wiki content only** (the compiled pages, never raw claims or source text); best-effort and partial; when the wiki holds nothing on the question, answer that it holds nothing. The answer is **formatted markdown**, and the **first mention of each subject it names** is woven in as an **inline link** to that subject's page (the link text reads as the sentence's own words; the target is a fully-qualified front-door link), alongside the existing list of cited pages.
- **Inspect** — list subjects (filter by type and name, paged), view a subject's claims (paged), view its page.
- **Link pages** — each page shows the subjects it points to and those that point to it, both ways; a link exists when a subject's name — or a name **merged into it** — appears on another page, matched by exact normalized name (nothing fuzzy), shown by the survivor's name. In the page's own prose, the **first occurrence of each subject it points to** is also rendered as an **inline link** to that subject (its own name is never self-linked); the point-to/pointed-by summary stays in the footers.
- **Readable names** — every subject is named by its `type/slug` path (e.g. `entity/acme-corp`), accepted wherever a subject is requested; internal ids are never shown.
- **Page large results** — jobs, subjects, and claims return a bounded page plus a cursor; any filter narrows the set **before** paging.
- **Report liveness** — standard health, and a reflection that is **empty** (no event plane in this release).
- **Serve a read-only web surface, per scope** — present a small styled web surface where **every address names its scope and its visibility tier**, so any page's link is shareable on its own and always shows the same content. A signed-in dashboard human gets the full surface for any scope; an anonymous visitor gets **only public scopes** — the same browse-and-ask experience, still read-only — and a private scope looks to them exactly like one that doesn't exist. Opening the wiki's bare address lands a signed-in user on the scope they last chose (remembered in a browser cookie, starting at `default`), arriving on that scope's **public address when the scope is public** and its signed-in address when private — picking a scope in the selector follows the same rule — so the address bar always holds the scope's shareable form, and a **header bar on every page** carries the **suite brand mark** at the far left (following it lands on the dashboard's own landing page), a **Home control that returns to that landing**, a **scope selector** to switch, and the **question box** — switching is the one act that updates the remembered choice; merely following someone's link never does. On public pages the selector shows only public scopes. The surface has three states per scope — identical on the public and signed-in tiers — all consistent with the suite's shared visual style. Wherever compiled-page or answer prose is shown, it is rendered as **formatted markdown** — headings, emphasis, ordered and unordered lists, inline code and code blocks, blockquotes, GFM tables, and any inline links the prose itself contains — so a page reads like an article rather than showing raw `##`/`**` characters; the rendered output is styled with the suite's **shared design tokens** (the same `tokens.css` the rest of the suite uses — no new, ad-hoc styling system) and is **sanitized** so untrusted LLM-authored prose can never inject active HTML or scripts. The question box's button reads **Ask** everywhere — public and signed-in alike, it runs the wiki's question-answering — and after submitting, the page **visibly shows it is working** (dimmed under a spinner, the button reporting progress) until the result arrives; nothing about the page leaves the user guessing whether anything happened. **Home / reset**: a **Suggested pages** list of the scope's **seven most recently added pages** as a place to jump in (anything older stays reachable by search and links) — the question box sits in the header, as on every page; a small footer names the service and version. **Answer** (both tiers): submitting the ask replaces the page with the cited answer — the same `ask` capability, drawn from compiled pages only — while the header's question box keeps the just-asked question in place, ready to edit and resubmit; below the answer sit the **same quiet side-by-side lists a subject page carries** — visually subordinate to the prose and presented identically to the subject page's footer — of the **cited pages** (the pages the answer drew on) and the **outbound** subjects (those whose name or alias appears in the answer prose), each navigable to its page. **Subject page**: reached by following a link, and addressable on its own, it shows the subject's compiled page prose; the next question starts right in the header's question box, and below the prose sit **quiet side-by-side lists** — visually subordinate to the prose — of the **outbound** subjects (named in this page's prose) and the **inbound** subjects (whose pages name this one). Linking follows the existing rule — a subject is linked when its name, or an alias merged into it, appears in the text by exact normalized match (nothing fuzzy), aliases routing to the survivor. Every referenced subject surfaces in the footers **and** the **first time it is named in the prose** it is an inline link to its page (on a subject page the page's own name is never self-linked); the prose also renders any markdown links it literally contains. **Every subject link — inline links and both footers, on the answer page and subject pages — is a fully-qualified front-door URL** (the same shareable, copy-paste-able form the `ask` MCP citations use), so a link lifted from any page resolves on its own; only the in-app navigation controls (the brand mark, the Home link, the question box) stay app-relative.
- **Stay responsive** — serve every read without waiting on an in-progress ingest, accept a new ingest immediately, integrate ingests **one at a time** (each visible all-at-once on completion), and let no slow or wedged job hold anything hostage (it can still be aborted).
- **Configure each call site independently** — `extract`, `compile`, and `ask` each get their own model, thinking level, and generation settings, with no two forced to match and no global; the **embedding** call site gets its own model and dimensions (one embedding model serves both page and query embedding, since the two must share a vector space); the configuration is inspectable.
- **Keep every pipeline prompt tunable, as committed data** — every prompt the service submits for inference — **extract** (source text in, subjects and claims out), **ask question analysis** (a question in, its retrieval decomposition out), **page compile** (a subject's claims in, its page out), and **answer synthesis** (a question plus pages in, a cited answer out) — has a committed per-step **tune folder** in the repo: the step's current prompt as a plain copy, a curated, committed, operator-reviewed gold case set split into tuning and held-out halves, the step's scoring program, and the pinned tuning configuration. The folders are ready-to-run inputs for the **standalone, external tuning tool** the suite uses (the wiki repo carries **no tuning executables of its own** — only the data and each step's scorer); the four case sets all draw on one shared fictional universe with the tuning/held-out boundary aligned across steps, so no step's held-out material ever leaks into another step's tuning half. The folder's prompt copy has **no mechanical tie** to the code — tuning a winner changes nothing in the service, and adopting one is always a deliberate, manual edit of the prompt the code carries. Scoring is deterministic wherever the step's contract is mechanical (extract and analysis entirely so — the same candidate against the same cases always scores the same); for the two prose-quality steps (compile and synthesis) the scorer combines deterministic contract checks with a **model judge** under a fixed rubric, identical across tuning and held-out cases.

It deliberately does **nothing else** — in particular it does not: enforce per-scope access control among signed-in users (scopes are namespaces, not permissions); default or guess a scope when one is omitted; move or copy content between scopes, or answer any question from more than one scope; let an anonymous visitor reach any private scope, or change anything; process more than one ingest at a time; touch the event plane (reflection is empty); **detect or propose** duplicates, or **merge or link** subjects by fuzzy/semantic name matching (merge, links, and direct `type/slug` lookup stay exact — an un-merged variant like "Gygax" for "Gary Gygax" resolves to nothing for those); **un-merge**; run background lint/dedup/contradiction/staleness maintenance; answer `ask` from raw claims or source text (it answers only from the compiled pages); structure pages differently per kind; draw a link across differently-named subjects by guessing; keep its own record of inference requests, responses, or spend (that record lives with the suite's central inference service); hold any model-provider credential or call any model provider directly (the running service — the tune folders are development-time data plus scoring programs, not part of the service, and their presence changes no deployed behavior); ship or run any tuning executable of its own (the tuning loop is an external standalone tool; the repo carries only tune-folder data and scorers); adopt an improved prompt automatically (adoption is always a deliberate manual change); allow interactive editing of a job, prompt, or page (changes are off-line, applied by restart); ingest anything but text; let `ask` write anything back; expose any mutating or operational action through the web surface (ingest, merge, and job control remain MCP-only); or introduce a bespoke styling system for the web surface (rendered markdown reuses the suite's shared design tokens, nothing new).

## Contractual constants

Promised values the design must honor verbatim and never re-declare:

- **The built-in scope is named `default`.** It always exists, starts private, can be made public like any scope, and can never be deleted.
- **A scope name is a lowercase slug: `a-z`, `0-9`, and hyphen, 1–64 characters, no leading or trailing hyphen.** Names are validated, never coerced — the name you type is the name everywhere.
- **Scope visibility is the closed set `private`, `public`.** Every scope is in exactly one; new scopes start `private`. Visibility governs only the human web pages.
- **Page size cap: 12,000 characters.** No subject page exceeds it — the cap forces a page to stay a compressed summary rather than an append-log.
- **Subject types are the closed set `entity`, `event`, `concept`.** Every subject carries exactly one.
- **A subject's public name is its `type/slug` path** (e.g. `entity/acme-corp`) — the only subject identifier ever shown or accepted; internal ids are never exposed.
- **The job lifecycle is the closed set `pending`, `working`, `done`, `failed`, `aborted`.** Every job is in exactly one; `aborted` (a deliberate cancellation) is distinct from `failed`; `done`/`failed`/`aborted` are terminal.
- **`ask` is strictly read-only — permanently.** It never creates or modifies wiki content (the accounting of its calls happens elsewhere and is bookkeeping, not a change).
- **The production LLM call sites are `extract`, `compile`, `ask`, and `embed`** — each named, each configured independently (the single `embed` model serves both page and query embedding, which must share a vector space).
- **The default production chat model is `gpt-5.6-luna`** — every chat call site (extract, compile, and both ask stages) serves on it unless deliberately reconfigured, and each step's prompt is tuned under the same model it serves on.
- **The tuning improver and judge model is `gpt-5.6-sol`** — the model that proposes prompt revisions in every step's tune folder, and the judge inside the compile and synthesis scorers.
- **Subjects match by normalized `type`-and-`name`** — the exact-match rule behind links, merges, and lookups.

## What we promise (user-facing behavior)

- **Scopes are walls.** Everything you ingest lands in the scope you named and nowhere else; everything you list, read, search, or ask answers only from that scope. The same name in two scopes is two subjects that never mix. Naming a scope that doesn't exist is a clear error, never a quiet new partition — and forgetting to name one is an error too, never a guess.
- **A team can have its own wiki space in one step.** Create a scope with the team's name; from then on every interaction naming that scope lives entirely inside it. Delete the scope and all of it is gone (the built-in `default` refuses deletion); listing scopes shows every space and its visibility.
- **A public scope is fully public.** Flip a scope public and anyone with the link can browse its pages and ask it questions — the same cited answers a signed-in user gets, with the owner accepting the model spend those questions cause — but they cannot change anything, cannot reach any other scope, and cannot even learn which private scopes exist. Flip it back and the public doors close.
- **Ingest is fire-and-return, and the wiki stays responsive.** You get a handle immediately; throughout an ingest's multi-minute run every read returns promptly and new ingests are accepted. A stuck job holds nothing hostage — reads and submissions keep working and you can still abort it.
- **Ingests never corrupt each other.** Integrated one at a time, each completing before the next; a finished ingest's pages and claims appear **all together**, never half-applied.
- **Knowledge compounds.** Each subject gets one evolving page; feeding more text about it updates that page instead of piling up duplicates.
- **You can merge, but not un-merge.** Name which of two duplicate subjects survives and which folds in; afterward asking either name, ingesting under the old name, following a link to it, or looking it up by its old path all land on the one surviving page, with no claim lost. Merging is **irreversible** this release. You can list the merges you've made.
- **Jobs are siftable and steerable.** Watch one by handle; list newest-first filtered by any combination of states and/or a time window, paged; get a **count** cheaply; abort a queued or running job; re-run a finished/failed/aborted job as a clean do-over. A bad filter state fails with a clear error; re-running an unfinished job is refused.
- **Failures are legible.** A too-large or otherwise unusable ingest fails with a **clear, recorded reason** (not a cryptic parse error) and leaves nothing half-built.
- **Every inference call is on the suite's record, legibly.** Extract, compile, ask, and the embeddings that power search all run through the suite's central inference service, and each arrives labeled with its stage, tied to its ingest job or its question, and attributed to whoever caused it — so from that one place you can see what a job or a question asked, got back, retried, and spent, across re-runs. Wiki itself holds no provider keys and keeps no shadow copy.
- **The raw record is permanent;** pages are lossy, the source is not.
- **A page reads like it was written for you.** Its opening line says what the subject is in plain words — "a dwarven king", never "an entity" — no internal id, citation marker, or database term ever appears in the prose, and every subject it names is a link you can follow at its first mention.
- **Submitting a question tells you what it does and shows you it's working.** The button reads **Ask** on every page — public or signed-in, it asks the wiki. The moment you click, the page visibly goes to work — dimmed under a spinner, the button reporting "Asking…" — until the answer replaces it, so you always know to wait rather than wonder.
- **You can look inside and navigate.** List subjects, view any subject's claims and page, and follow a page's links (what it points to and what points to it) to related subjects — everything by readable `type/slug` name, never an internal id, a page at a time.
- **Ask works like an agent and is honest.** It finds the pages relevant to your question — by meaning and wording, not just exact names — and answers from them in **formatted markdown** that links the **first mention of each subject** inline to that subject's page (and lists each cited page too), every such link a fully-qualified front-door URL (so it opens whether the wiki runs in production or a local deployment, and you can copy it elsewhere); answers from those it has even if a related subject was never ingested; says "nothing here" plainly when the wiki holds nothing on the question; never fabricates from general knowledge; never changes anything.
- **Opening the wiki in a browser is a place you can use, not a placeholder.** A logged-in dashboard user who navigates to the mount root lands on their remembered scope (or `default`) — a small, on-brand page, styled consistently with the rest of the suite, with a header bar carrying the suite brand mark (over to the dashboard's landing), a Home control (back to this landing), a scope selector, and a search box to ask the wiki, and the scope's seven most recently added pages to jump into (everything older stays findable by search); someone without a valid session is turned away from private pages (public-scope pages remain open to them, browse and ask, read-only), and agents are unaffected — they keep working through MCP. A footer names the service and its version.
- **Anonymous visitors to a public scope get the real wiki.** Its home page suggests the scope's newest pages; they can ask it questions and get the same cited answers a signed-in user would, and read every page with its links — but the pages only ever read from that one scope, and nothing they do changes anything. The inference their questions spend is a cost the owner accepts.
- **Every web address says what it shows — and navigation lands on the right one.** A page's URL names its scope (and whether it's the public or signed-in form), so a copied link opens the same content for whoever receives it — it never depends on the receiver's own scope choice. Landing on the wiki and switching scopes always arrive at a public scope's public address (the shareable form) and a private scope's signed-in address. The one cost accepted: flipping a scope's visibility changes its links, so links shared before a flip may stop working.
- **Ask on the web is the same ask as on MCP, and reads like an article.** Type a question and the page becomes the cited answer drawn only from compiled pages — or a plain "nothing here" when the wiki holds nothing — while the header's question box keeps the question you just asked, ready to edit and resubmit; the first mention of each subject in the answer is an inline link to its page, and beneath the answer — laid out just like the lists at the foot of a subject page — sit the pages the answer cited and the subjects it names, each a link you can follow into its page.
- **Every page is a node you can navigate from, and navigation changes nothing.** A subject page shows its compiled prose — with the first mention of each subject it points to rendered as an inline link (never a link to itself) — and a footer of the subjects it points to (named in its prose) and the subjects that point to it; following any of them lands on that subject's page, with the header's Home control and question box as the way back or onward. The web only reads — it never alters a subject, claim, page, or job.
- **Web prose reads like an article, not source text.** On both the answer page and subject pages the compiled markdown is rendered with its formatting intact — headings, bold and italic, ordered and unordered lists, inline code and code blocks, blockquotes, GFM tables, the inline subject links (both the first-mention links the wiki weaves in and any the prose itself contains) — styled with the suite's shared design tokens so it looks like the rest of the suite, never a wall of raw `##` and `**`. The rendering is safe: any HTML or script woven into the LLM-authored prose is neutralized and can never run or alter the page.
- **Each call site runs its own model,** independently configured and inspectable — no single global model; the embedding model is configured the same way.
- **Every pipeline prompt is tunable from its committed folder, with nothing else to build.** For each of the four steps the developer points the suite's standalone tuning tool at that step's committed folder and it runs: the folder carries the current prompt, the operator-reviewed cases, the pinned configuration (the same model the step serves on; `gpt-5.6-sol` proposing revisions), and the step's scoring program. The developer's only local prerequisites are the tuning tool itself with its login, and the standard OpenAI key in the environment for the scorers. Everything a tuning run writes stays in the folder's ignored run workspace; the committed folder — and the service — are untouched by any run.
- **Tuned quality means served quality.** Each step is scored under the same model and call structure production serves it with, so a folder's score predicts the running service's behavior, and a winner confirmed on the held-out cases is a winner in production terms. Adoption is deliberate: the developer manually copies a winning prompt into the code, and until they do, nothing observable changes.
- **Scores are honest.** Extract and analysis scores are fully deterministic — the same candidate against the same cases always scores the same. Compile and synthesis scores hold the mechanical parts of each contract (size caps, well-formed output, no leaked internal identifiers, and — for synthesis — citations that resolve) to deterministic checks, and only the prose quality to a fixed-rubric model judge applied identically to tuning and held-out cases.

## Success criteria (outcomes)

Each is a result the user can confirm against the running service:

- I create a scope, ingest into it, and everything I then list, read, or ask in that scope comes only from it; the same subject name in another scope keeps its own independent page.
- Ingesting, asking, or listing with a scope name that was never created fails with a clear error and creates nothing; omitting the scope on a content operation is likewise an error, never a guess.
- Creating a scope with an illegal name (uppercase, spaces, too long, stray hyphens) or a name already taken fails with a clear reason; `default` exists without my creating it.
- I delete a scope and it disappears with all its content, leaving every other scope untouched; deleting `default` is refused.
- I list scopes and see each one's name and whether it is private or public.
- I flip a scope to public and can then open its pages and ask it questions in a logged-out browser, getting the same cited answers as signed-in; flipping it back closes the public access.
- In a logged-out browser, a private scope's pages are indistinguishable from pages that don't exist, and no page reveals private scope names.
- Opening the wiki's bare address takes me to the scope I last picked in the selector (or `default` if I never picked); following someone else's link to another scope shows me that content without changing where I land next time.
- A link I copy from any wiki page opens the same page for whoever I send it to (sign-in permitting), regardless of their own scope choice.
- I ingest text and get a handle back without waiting for processing.
- During an ingest's multi-minute phase, job-status, jobs/subjects listing, claims, pages, and `ask` all return promptly, and I can submit another ingest immediately.
- A hung or long job blocks no read and no submission, stays abortable, and the service stays responsive throughout.
- A completed ingest's subjects and pages become visible all at once — never partially applied.
- I watch a job go pending/working → done (or failed/aborted) by handle, and list jobs newest-first filtered by any combination of states and/or a time window, paged; one query naming several states (e.g. all but `done`) returns them together.
- I get the **count** of jobs matching a state/time filter without retrieving them, correct across many jobs.
- Filtering by an invalid state yields a clear error naming the valid five; the valid states are discoverable from the jobs surface.
- I abort a pending or working job; it leaves nothing partial and shows as `aborted`, distinct from `failed`.
- I re-run a finished/failed/aborted job; it reprocesses from original text and **replaces** that attempt's claims; re-running a pending/working job is refused with a clear reason.
- Text too large to digest in one pass fails with a clear reason recorded on the job, leaving no partial subjects, claims, or pages.
- On the suite's central inference service I find every call wiki made — extract, compile, ask, and the page/query embeddings, each retry its own entry — labeled with its wiki stage; an ingest's calls all share that job's handle, one question's calls all share one correlation handle, and a call caused by my action is attributed to me.
- I page through jobs, subjects, and a subject's claims via cursor, with any filter applied before paging.
- After an ingest I list the subjects it produced, and for any subject view its claims and its page, referring to it by readable `type/slug` name.
- Every subject shown in a listing, page, or link is named by `type/slug`, and an `ask` citation comes back as a fully-qualified link to that subject's page (built from its `type/slug` and resolving through the front door in production or a local deployment); I never see an internal id.
