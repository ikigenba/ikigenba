# Decision 86 — `ask` tolerates a stale retrieval hit: drop-and-continue

**Decision.** When `ask`'s page-gathering step (D9) meets a retrieval hit whose subject does not resolve in the asked scope, it **drops that hit and continues** with the remaining ones — it no longer fails the whole ask. Concretely, `gatherPages` treats `ErrSubjectNotFound` from `SubjectStore.GetInScope` exactly as it already treats a missing page row (`sql.ErrNoRows` from `PageStore.GetBySubject`): skip the hit, move on. Every other error still propagates — tolerance is scoped to precisely the two "this hit points at nothing" outcomes, never to database failures.

**Why this is not a fail-loudly violation.** The meaning lane reads an in-memory index that cannot be updated atomically with a SQLite transaction, and the design *already* accepts it being momentarily behind: D35 keeps old vectors live until replaced, D38 and D84 evict after commit, best-effort. A hit that no longer resolves is therefore a **design-sanctioned transient**, not a broken invariant — and a behavior that is expected during normal operation must not produce a user-facing error, or the error stops carrying signal. Before this change, the sanctioned transient surfaced as a hard failure on both surfaces (the web's bare "ask wiki" 500; the MCP `internal` error) — observed live for hours after every `scope_delete`/re-ingest cycle. The loud path for *genuine* breaks (a database error, a failed LLM call) is untouched.

**Downstream behavior is unchanged by construction.** Dropped hits simply leave fewer gathered pages; the existing gates then do their work: zero surviving pages → the honest-empty answer (D9 R-BCVP-4LM3's path), some surviving → a grounded, cited answer over what actually exists — which is also the *honest* answer, since a dropped subject no longer exists to cite.

**Rejected.**
- **Keeping the failure fatal so inconsistency surfaces hard.** Punishes the asker for a maintenance race they cannot influence, during windows the design itself sanctions; the operator explicitly chose otherwise (grill Q1).
- **Retrying the whole retrieval on a stale hit.** The window is milliseconds; the remaining hits are already in hand; a retry adds a second embed/search round-trip for a result the skip already produces.
- **Evicting the stale entry from the cache at ask time (read-repair).** The ask path reads through the search seam and has no business mutating the cache; eviction is the deleting writer's job (D84) and the reap's backstop (D85). Read-repair from a reader adds a write dependency to the read path for a window that self-heals anyway.

**Verification.**
- R-RDLX-FD2O — With retrieval returning a mix of live hits and a hit whose subject id resolves to no subject in the asked scope, `Ask` succeeds: the answer is grounded in and cites only the live subjects' pages, and no error reaches either surface — where before the fix the whole ask failed with `wiki: subject not found`. Real temp SQLite + mock provider, the stale hit injected through the retrieval seam.
- R-RETT-T4TD — With **every** hit stale (no subject resolves), `Ask` returns the honest-empty answer — found `false`, the honest-empty text, no citations — never an error and never a fabricated answer.
