# Decision 95 — Job liveness and the honest job surface

**Decision.** A job is always **queued, running, or terminal**. There is no fourth condition in which a document is alive but nothing will ever move it, and the job surface never shows the caller a state it cannot query for.

## Liveness

A handed-off job's next event comes from another service. That service can lose the event: prompts drops the error from its terminal write and its `finish` only accepts an item still marked `running`, so a completion can be left `running` forever, never delivered, never swept. Nothing inside wiki would ever move such a job. Liveness therefore cannot rest on the completion arriving.

**`jobs.deadline_at`** is stamped when a job is handed off and **extended every time a unit lands**, so a large fan-out is not killed for being large while a job making no progress dies within one budget.

**The reaper** runs on the same tick as the drain and depends on nothing but the local clock and the database:

```go
func (s *Service) ReapExpired(ctx context.Context, now time.Time) (int, error)
```

It fails every job past its deadline, recording that its completion never arrived, and clears staging and lease in the same transaction. An **absolute lifetime ceiling** measured from admission applies regardless of progress: a job holds its scope's lease while it runs, so an indefinitely-extending deadline would let one pathological document own a scope. The lease's own expiry is independent of and shorter than the job deadline, so a leaked lease is reclaimed even when its job is gone.

The reaper is a backstop for another service losing work, not a normal-path mechanism. Its firing is a defect signal and is recorded as one. The real fix belongs to the prompts tree and is out of this project's scope.

## The surface

**Five statuses, and only five.** `pending`, `working`, `done`, `failed`, `aborted` are the product constant. The handoff phase is internal (D4) and never surfaces: a handed-off document reads `working`, because that is what it is. This removes by construction the condition where a caller sees a status that the same surface rejects as an invalid filter value — the filter's valid set and the set of statuses a job can report are the same five.

**A job reports what it produced.** `status` returns the job's compiled subjects once it is `done`, and reports fan-out progress (units complete, units total) while it is running, so a long ingest is legibly progressing rather than merely not finished.

## Visibility

The outage that motivated this decision produced **zero log output** for ten minutes, because the drain's error was discarded by its caller. Silence is the defect being fixed, not just the wedge.

- **One structured record per apply decision**, carrying job id, stage, unit, outcome class, reason, and duration. An outcome is never dropped.
- **A per-tick summary** of the drain's counts.
- **A no-progress detector**: consecutive ticks in which items were seen but none were applied, discarded, or failed is the signature of a stalled drain. It is reported as an error within seconds and degrades health if it persists.
- **Health counters** for running jobs, oldest running job age, deferred items, and the no-progress streak, so the condition is visible from the MCP surface without shell access.

**Rejected.**
- **Trusting the completion to arrive** — the assumption the shipped pipeline made; a lost completion left the job alive forever with no error and no log.
- **A timeout with no absolute ceiling** — progress extension alone lets a job hold its scope's lease indefinitely.
- **Exposing `waiting` and adding it to the filter enum** — patches the symptom by growing the product's status vocabulary to six for an internal detail. Collapsing it into `working` fixes the same bug by removing the state instead.
- **Logging the drain error and stopping there** — a log line nobody reads is what the ten-minute silent outage would have produced anyway. The detector and the health counters are what make the condition impossible to miss.

**Verification.**
- R-PA2D-OEYB — A job handed off whose completion never arrives is ended `failed` by the reaper once past its deadline, with an error naming the missing completion, its staging cleared and its scope lease released — and the next pending job in that scope is then admitted.
- R-PBAA-26P0 — A job whose units keep landing has its deadline extended and is **not** reaped while progressing; the same job is still ended `failed` once past its absolute lifetime ceiling measured from admission (an implementation with extension but no ceiling never terminates it, and is the failing case).
- R-PCI6-FYFP — Across a job's whole life — before handoff, handed off awaiting extract, awaiting compile, and terminal — every status the surface reports is one of the five valid values, and the internal phase never appears in a reported status.
- R-PEXZ-7HX3 — A filter naming all five valid statuses returns **every** existing job, including one currently handed off awaiting a completion (a live job invisible to that filter is the failing case), and the count matches the listing.
- R-PDQ2-TQ6E — A `done` ingest reports its compiled subjects from `status`, matching the subjects the job actually created; while running, the same call reports units complete and units total.
- R-PG5V-L9NS — Every apply decision emits one record carrying its outcome class and reason, and a drain that repeatedly sees items while applying, discarding, and failing none reports a no-progress condition rather than staying silent.
- R-PHDR-Z1EH — Health reports the running-job count, the oldest running job's age, and the no-progress streak, and degrades while the drain is making no progress.
