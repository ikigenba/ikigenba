# Decision 4 — The ingest pipeline: handoff and apply over the completion queue

> **Extended by D97.** A `match` phase sits between extract and compile for subjects with correction involvement (D96/D97): the phase vocabulary is now `''` | `'extract'` | `'match'` | `'compile'`, extract apply may ensure match items (key `<jobID>/match/<norm-name>/a1`), match applies stage edge sets and ensure their subject's compile item, and the integrate commit also inserts corrections and suppression edges and builds pages from **effective** claim sets. D97 owns those behaviors' ids; everything below — the handoff/apply split, the staging table, the dedupe rules, the stray rule, the worker shape — stands unchanged and governs the match stage too.

**Decision.** Ingest is fire-and-return at the front door, and the pipeline behind it is **split in two**: a stage that needs inference *hands the work off* to prompts' durable completion queue (D5/research §12) and ends — no goroutine waits on a model — and a separate **inbox applier** collects finished work and applies it. A job therefore rests in exactly two kinds of durable state at all times: its row in wiki's `jobs` table, and its outstanding work items in prompts' queue. Neither process restarting loses anything; nothing is ever "in flight" only in memory.

**Front door — unchanged.** `wiki.Service.Ingest(ctx, …) (jobID, err)`: one `jobs` INSERT (`status='pending'`), nudge, return. No LLM work on the request path. `JobStatus` reads the row.

**Handoff state is an internal phase, not a status.** The durable lifecycle is `pending → working → done | failed | aborted` — the five product-constant statuses, unchanged. "Handed off, awaiting a queue delivery" is a **`phase`** column on the same row (`''` | `'extract'` | `'match'` | `'compile'` — the `match` value is D97's), never a status: from the caller's side a handed-off document is simply still `working`. Design owns the phase; the surface owns the five (D95). One document, one job row, one status vocabulary.

**The two halves:**

*Handoff (producer).* The claim loop admits the oldest `pending` job **under its scope's ingest lease** (D93), renders the extract request from `source_text` (D6's render half), and **ensures** one queue item — key `<jobID>/extract/a1`, context envelope `{job_id, stage:"extract"}` — then sets `phase='extract'` and moves on. Re-ensuring after a boot sweep dedupes against the item already queued, so the claim-crash-reclaim path never double-spends.

*Apply (consumer).* The inbox tick runs every few seconds: `Inbox()` → classify and handle every terminal item (D94), acking last. Applying dispatches on the item's context envelope:

- **`stage:"extract"` done** → decode/validate claims (D6's parse half; semantic failure → ensure a corrective item per D5's recipe, ack the bad one, phase unchanged; attempts exhausted → job `failed`). On success, in one tx: stage the extract output and the compile fan-out plan, set `phase='compile'`; then ensure the compile items — key `<jobID>/compile/<unit-token>/a1`, envelope `{job_id, stage:"compile", subject:<norm-name>, units:N}`; ack the extract item **last**.

  **Ensure only the units that have no staged compile row.** `Ack` deletes the queue row, so a key that was applied and acked no longer exists to dedupe against: blanket re-ensuring on every extract redelivery would mint **new paid items** without bound. A compile unit's staging row commits before its ack, so "has a staging row" is exactly "already paid for and applied". This rule, not Ensure, is what makes extract replay free.

- **`stage:"compile"` done** → decode/validate the page (D7's parse half; semantic retries as above), then **one transaction**: upsert the unit's staged payload, **recount** the job's staged compile rows (never increment a counter), and when the count reaches the fan-out's `units`, run D14's integrate commit over the staged outputs, mark the job `done` guarded by its expected state, clear staging, and release the scope lease. Ack after that commit. Embedding writes (D30/D34/D35) follow the commit and never re-drive the item.

- **any `failed` item** → job `failed` with the queue's error (guarded terminal write), staging cleared, lease released, ack.

- **stray item** — unknown `job_id`, terminal job, stage/units mismatched to staging, or a non-pipeline envelope → **ack and discard**, writing nothing. (Live ask items cannot reach this drain: the live path submits under `service:wiki.ask` (D5), which nothing drains; the non-pipeline arm stays as pure defense.)

**Plans are staged by name, and ids are minted at commit.** `stagedIntegration` carries each subject's `norm_name`, `name`, and `type` — **never a minted subject id**, and its claims are staged against norm_names too. New subject ids are minted **inside the integrate transaction**. Two documents that both introduce the same subject therefore cannot mint two ids for one name: the collision that produced a `UNIQUE (scope, norm_name)` violation is unrepresentable rather than merely serialized against.

`SubjectStore.Save` becomes an upsert — `INSERT … ON CONFLICT (scope, norm_name) DO NOTHING`, then read the surviving row's id — so an already-present subject is adopted instead of erroring.

At commit, every staged norm_name is **re-resolved through the alias-aware `Resolver`** (subjects, then aliases), because a `merge` between staging and integrate legitimately turns a name into an alias for its survivor. A name that resolves to nothing and is not in the plan's new subjects means the plan no longer describes reality: fail the job with that reason. Never guess.

**Attribution.** Every prompts call a job causes carries the job's **correlation id** as its group id — the chain id stored on the job row, or the job's own id when the row stores none. D64 owns the client and the header, D65 owns where that id comes from, and this Decision restates neither. The job id is wiki's key for the job, never the group handle; the `status` tool reports the job's correlation id (D10) so the caller who holds a job id can still reach that job's calls on prompts' record.

**Staging state** — one table, the durable scratch between handoff and integrate:

```sql
CREATE TABLE job_staging (
  job_id   TEXT NOT NULL,
  stage    TEXT NOT NULL,             -- 'extract' | 'compile'
  unit     TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT '',  -- compile: the subject norm-name; extract: ''
  payload  TEXT NOT NULL,             -- extract plan / compiled page JSON
  units    INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 0,
  PRIMARY KEY (job_id, stage, unit)
);
```

Upsert-keyed so every apply is idempotent; rows die in the integrate commit or with the job's terminal write. Counting staged compile rows filters `stage='compile'` — the extract row shares the table and would otherwise integrate the job one unit early. The integrate commit reads **only** staged payloads and the job row: no LLM call inside a transaction, trivially, because wiki makes none.

**Worker shape.** `Spec.Workers` keeps **two** loops: the claim loop (pending jobs → handoff, doorbell + poll fallback) and the inbox tick (~2 s, drains the whole inbox each tick). They stay separate deliberately: merge jobs still run their compile inline through the live path, so folding the applier into the claim loop would let one scope's merge stall completion-apply for **every** scope. **Boot sweep:** `working` with no phase → `pending` (an in-process step died mid-stride), releasing the lease; a job with a phase set is left alone — its state lives in the queue and staging, and the tick resumes it without re-spending.

**Anti-poisoning invariant — unchanged and structural.** Extract renders only from `job.source_text`; compile renders only from claim sets; no page body is ever an input.

**Rejected.**
- **The blocking pipeline (the prior form of this decision)** — held a goroutine through every model call; any completion slower than the chassis's 15 s write deadline killed the job, and a restart vaporized all in-flight work.
- **`waiting` as a sixth job status** — it leaked an internal handoff detail into the product's five-value vocabulary, produced a status callers could observe but not filter on, and made "still running" ambiguous. Phase is internal; the surface keeps five.
- **Blanket re-ensure of the compile fan-out on extract replay** — reads as idempotent and is not: `Ack` deletes the row that would have deduped it, so every replay mints fresh paid items. Staging-row presence is the durable, correct guard.
- **Staging minted subject ids** — two in-flight documents naming one new subject stage two ids for one `(scope, norm_name)`, and whichever integrates second violates the constraint. Names are the identity (D11); ids are an implementation detail that must not outlive a transaction.
- **Persisting queue item ids and resuming polls** — keys are derivable from durable job state; nothing to resume means nothing to recover.
- **A per-stage in-memory fan-in (channels/WaitGroups)** — dies with the process; the staging recount is the same fan-in, durable.
- **Folding the two worker loops into one** — a synchronous merge would then block completion-apply for every scope, reintroducing the cross-scope blast radius D93 exists to bound.

**Verification.**
- R-M8RN-87WV — `Ingest` returns a job id produced by a single INSERT, with no LLM/queue call on the request path (fire-and-return).
- R-K73J-J3W3 — Claiming a `pending` job ensures exactly one extract item (key `<jobID>/extract/a1`, envelope naming the job and stage, request rendered from `source_text`) and leaves the job `working` with `phase='extract'`; re-claiming after a simulated crash re-ensures the **same** key (captured requests show identical keys — no duplicate work).
- R-K8BF-WVMS — Applying a done extract item stages the plan, ensures one compile item per affected subject (envelopes carrying the fan-out count), and acks the extract item only after both; replaying the same delivery leaves staging and the ensured set identical.
- R-OQJZ-K337 — Replaying a done extract item **after** some of its compile units have been applied and acked ensures items **only** for units with no staged compile row: the captured ensure set for the replay is exactly the un-staged units, and is empty when all units are staged (a blanket re-ensure would show the full fan-out and is the failing case).
- R-K9JC-ANDH — Applying a done compile item stages that unit's page; the **last** unit's apply (staged count = `units`) runs the atomic integrate commit: prior claims replaced, affected pages upserted, job `done`, staging cleared — and redelivering an already-applied compile item after that acks-and-discards without altering committed state.
- R-OVFL-361Z — The compile apply's stage-recount-integrate-finish sequence is a **single** transaction: a failure injected after the unit's staged write but before the terminal write leaves **no** staged row for that unit and the job non-terminal (a multi-transaction implementation leaves the staged row committed and is the failing case).
- R-ORRV-XUTW — A staged extract plan contains each subject's `norm_name` and **no** minted subject id, and the ids of newly created subjects are first assigned during the integrate transaction: two jobs staged concurrently against the same absent subject name integrate to **one** subject row, both jobs' claims attached, with no constraint error.
- R-OU7O-PEBA — Saving a subject whose `(scope, norm_name)` already exists returns the existing row's id and writes no duplicate, instead of returning a constraint error.
- R-OSZS-BMKL — A staged norm_name that became an alias for another subject (via a `merge` committed after staging) integrates onto the **survivor**; a staged norm_name that resolves to nothing and is not among the plan's new subjects ends the job `failed` naming the stale plan, with nothing partial written.
- R-KAR8-OF46 — A semantically bad extract or compile result ensures a corrective item (`/a2`, corrective replay per D5) and acks the bad one with the job still in phase; attempts exhausted ends the job `failed` with the semantic error and zero subjects/claims/pages written.
- R-KBZ5-26UV — A `failed` queue item (provider failure, runtime bound, model `error` status) ends the job `failed` carrying the queue's error, clears its staging, releases its lease, and acks the item; nothing partial lands.
- R-KEEX-TQC9 — A stray terminal item — unknown job, terminal job, stage mismatched to staging, or a non-pipeline envelope — is acked and discarded: the inbox drains, no job or staging row changes.
- R-KFMU-7I2Y — The inbox tick drains **every** terminal item present in a tick (a scripted inbox of mixed items empties in one tick), and boot leaves phased jobs untouched while sweeping unphased `working` → `pending`.
- R-MB7F-ZRE9 — When integration errors mid-commit, the job ends `failed` with a non-empty `error` and **zero** subjects/claims/pages are written (atomic commit).
- R-MCFC-DJ4Y — A second job naming an existing subject (same `Normalize(name)`) appends its claims and bumps that subject's page `version`, creating no duplicate subject.
- R-MG31-IUD1 — Compile requests are rendered from claim sets and never receive page-body input (the anti-poisoning invariant, asserted at the handoff render seam).
