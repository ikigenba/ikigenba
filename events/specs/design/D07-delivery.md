# D07-delivery

The delivery loop: `internal/delivery`, which hands every event of the log to each subscriber that accepts it, one at a time and in `seq` order, retries a failed attempt with a doubling wait, pauses a subscriber that fails one event too often, and gives up an attempt still unanswered when the drain runs out. `D04-store` owns the log and the subscribers: `Next` hands back a subscriber's next accepted event and moves its cursor past the ones it does not accept, `Advance` finishes an event, `Pause` stops the subscriber on it, `Skip` and `Resume` unstick it, and `Changed` says something moved. `D05-declarations` decides who is a subscriber and what it accepts. `D06-serve` builds the loop in `cli.Run` with the services path, the run's writer, the settings and the seam's `TimeoutAfter` and `BackoffAfter` (R-9GA0-6UY6, R-CBMM-PF9D), starts it after `service.started` (R-8O8B-E4W7) and drains it at the stop (R-BJKX-WP7E, R-BM0Q-O8OS). What the `subscribers`, `skip` and `resume` tools answer is `D09-tools`; the trail as a whole is `D10-trail`.

## Whom events delivers to

A subscriber (R-YNVI-5RW8) is delivered to while it is `ok`. Before each attempt the loop reads the services file at `Config.Services` afresh, through appkit's `services.Read`, and finds the subscriber's **target**: the entry with its name, enabled, and not events itself. The socket can move between attempts and the loop follows it. A subscriber with no target — its entry gone or disabled since the last refresh, an unreadable file, an empty path — gets no attempt and counts none; the next refresh makes it `gone` when the file it reads no longer lists it enabled, but a file that gives no list after the start makes no subscriber `gone` (`D05-declarations`), and in the meantime the loop looks again a second later or at the next change of the store, so a file that comes back loses nothing. events is never a target: the host's services file lists `events` on its own socket, but events is the broker and takes no deliveries.

A `paused` subscriber gets nothing until an agent skips or resumes it; a `gone` one gets nothing until it is `ok` again, when the loop takes it up from its cursor, in order. An event the subscriber does not accept is passed over without an attempt, and its cursor moves past it.

## An attempt

An attempt is one `POST /events` on the target's socket, made with appkit's `events.Send` through a client from `telemetry.SiblingClient` over `telemetry.SocketTransport`, so its body is the event's record with `attempt` added, and the trail gets a `sibling.called` for it: target the service, `POST`, `/events`, the status of the answer's status line, 0 when none came; appkit records it when the status line arrives, so an attempt cut off while its body is still coming carries the status it had (probed). The answer is read as `events.Send` reads it (proved against the installed module): `{"outcome":"ok"}` with status 200 **succeeds**; `{"outcome":"skip"}` with status 200, and a 404, which a subscriber with no handler for the name answers, are **skipped**; everything else **fails** — an error outcome, any other status or body (400, 409, 502, 503, a 200 with no outcome), a socket nothing listens on, a connection lost before the answer, and an answer not read within the delivery timeout.

Each attempt has its own deadline, `Settings.DeliveryTimeout()`, measured by the timer `TimeoutAfter` gives from the moment the attempt begins, after it has its place among the attempts in flight, so it covers connecting, sending and the answer alike (the setting is how long one delivery may take, and a delivery may wait longer than the drain). When it fires the loop closes the connection and counts the attempt failed.

Every attempt in flight counts against `Settings.InflightMax`, across all subscribers; an attempt due beyond that waits for one to end. Apart from that cap no subscriber waits on another: one stalled, failing or backing off holds back only its own later events.

## Outcomes

A success moves the cursor to the event's `seq` and records `event.delivered`; a skip does the same and records `event.skipped`; each with `event` the event's id and `service` the subscriber's. A failure leaves the cursor where it is and the subscriber `ok`. The loop counts failed attempts per subscriber and event, starting afresh when the loop starts and whenever the subscriber becomes `ok` again — after a `resume`, so the stuck event gets every attempt again from 1, and after a return from `gone` — and puts the count on the wire as `attempt`, one more than the failures before it. After a failure it waits before the next attempt at the same event: one second after the first, doubling after each, never more than five minutes, and never less than the `Retry-After` of a 429 or 503 answer, honored however long it is. The wait begins when the failed attempt has ended, through the timer `BackoffAfter` gives.

The failure that makes the count `EVENTS_DELIVERY_ATTEMPTS` pauses the subscriber on that event instead (`D04-store`'s `Pause`), with the error text, and records `subscriber.paused` with `service`, `event` and `error`. The error text is the subscriber's own message when it answered an error outcome with one, and otherwise one of three copy constants events formats: one carrying the service and the timeout for the deadline, one carrying the status for an answer `events.Send` cannot read as an outcome, and one carrying the service when nothing was read at all.

## Stopping

`Run` delivers until its context is done and then returns at once, starting nothing after: no first attempt, no retry, and a wait for a retry ends there. Attempts already in flight are not cut short by the stop; they run on until answered, until their deadline, or until the drain gives them up. `Drain` waits for them, and handles each answer as it would at any other moment — the cursor moved, the record made — before it returns. When `Drain`'s own context ends first, it gives up every attempt still in flight: the connection is closed, the attempt is not counted, the cursor does not move, and nothing but its `sibling.called` is recorded. The next events starts its count afresh, so stopping never brings a subscriber nearer to a pause.

## Decisions

- **The deadline covers the whole attempt**, connecting included, and starts once the attempt has its place in flight, not while it waits for one: a delivery not answered within the timeout and one that cannot reach the subscriber are two kinds of failure, and the timer's start is what the in-flight cap would otherwise eat into.
- **Backoff** doubles from one second and is capped at five minutes: with the default ten attempts the waits are 1 s to 256 s and the cap is never reached, but `EVENTS_DELIVERY_ATTEMPTS` has no upper bound and the doubling must not overflow. `Retry-After` is honored uncapped, as a floor on the wait; appkit's `events.Send` reports it only for a 429 or 503 with delta-seconds (probed: an HTTP date, and a 500 carrying the header, report none), so the loop never parses the header itself.
- **A 404 is a skip**, as appkit's `events.Send` reports it: appkit's own `DeliveryHandler` answers 404 for a name its service has no handler for, which is "nothing to do with this event". Every other answer that is not an outcome is a failure, so a misbehaving subscriber is retried and then paused rather than passed over. A 503 or 429 with an error body is still reported by `events.Send` without its message (probed), so its text is the one carrying status 503.
- **Error texts for failures without a message** are events' own, fixed, and carry no transport detail (no socket path, no Go error text), so the one piece of text the trail carries is either the subscriber's or one of three known phrases.
- **The attempt count lives in the loop**, not the log: a restart, a `resume` and a return from `gone` start it afresh, so stopping or resuming never brings a subscriber nearer to a pause; a refresh that changes nothing does not.
- **No target, or a store error, is a pause of the loop's own for that subscriber**, not a failure: no request is made, so no attempt is counted and no `sibling.called` recorded (the trail records one per call made). It looks again after one second, through `BackoffAfter`, or at the next store change, whichever is first, so a services file restored between refreshes, or a store that recovers, needs no new event to get going.
- **`sibling.called` per attempt**: each delivery attempt adds a `sibling.called` to the trail beside its `event.delivered`, `event.skipped` or `subscriber.paused`.
- **A success or skip on a subscriber that is no longer `ok`** still records `event.delivered` or `event.skipped`, since the subscriber did answer, but `Advance` leaves its cursor (R-2VE3-OKL3), so a returning subscriber gets the event again: delivery is at least once. A failure that would pause a subscriber no longer `ok` pauses nothing and records no `subscriber.paused`, which the trail records only when a subscriber becomes paused.
- **An error outcome with an empty message** (`{"outcome":"error","error":""}`, which `events.Send` reads as an error with `Message()` empty, probed) takes the text carrying its status, since an empty `reason.error` would tell an agent nothing.
- **A store error after an answer** (`Advance` or `Pause` failing) records nothing for the answer, leaves the cursor, and the event is attempted again after the one-second recheck; so `event.delivered`, `event.skipped` and `subscriber.paused` are recorded only once the store has taken the outcome, and a trail never shows a delivery the log does not.
- **`event.accepted` before the delivery's records** is the store's: `D04-store` records it before any `Next` answers the event (R-6BQQ-0AK1), so an attempt's `sibling.called` always follows it and the loop needs no rule of its own.
- The loop writes nothing to any stream; it has none. That events writes nothing to stdout or stderr for any delivery is `D06-serve`'s at the run level: R-A2DS-7DL6 (nothing on `p.Stdout`, and on `p.Stderr` only appkit's warning over a newer database, undelivered-record lines and the stop's lines) and R-AZ4W-FGNR (nothing through the `log` package's standard logger, a delivery answered with an error included). What its writer does with a record telemetry refuses is R-9XI6-OAME's; a telemetry outage writing such lines is the one exception.
- `Config.Telemetry` must be non-nil: every attempt goes through `telemetry.SiblingClient`, which needs a writer.
