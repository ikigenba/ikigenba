# D10-on-a-space

What campaigns contributes when it runs on a space and in a sandbox: what ships, what a space takes from the manifest, the nginx fragment `cli.NginxConf` and the file under `etc/` that copies it, the services file entries it uses, which of the contract's guarantees the delivery's sandbox walk stands on and which document owns each, the stand-in candidates that walk reads businesses from, the real artifacts it keeps material against, the trust campaigns places in what reaches it, and the finish line's steps on `us` (log D6). Almost every step between a checkout and a visitor's phone belongs to someone else: devctl builds and ships the file, opsctl installs it and generates nginx, auth decides every credential, the host's nginx admits guests and sets the identity and address headers, the gateway relays tool calls, the events app takes the bus events, telemetry keeps the trail, home lists the app, and the tracking page on michaelgreenly.dev forwards a campaign token here (log D10). campaigns' own share is what `D01-layout-and-run-seam` to `D09-trail` state, plus the fragment, which this document declares. Decisions cited as doc N are /home/mgreenly/Documents/ikigenba-campaigns.md; those cited as log DN are /home/mgreenly/Documents/ikigenba-campaigns-decisions.md.

This document declares one name, `cli.NginxConf`, which `D01-layout-and-run-seam` and `D02-cli` assign to it, and states that `etc/nginx.conf` holds its bytes. Everything else it says of a space or a sandbox is behaviour the other documents own; the table below names the owner of each outcome.

## What ships

The release holds four members of campaigns' directory: `bin/campaigns`, `etc/manifest.toml`, `etc/nginx.conf` and `share/icon.svg`, the last Tabler's `speakerphone`, human-authored and already in the tree (log D19). The two files under `etc/` are what the root package's `Etc` embeds (`D01-layout-and-run-seam`, R-A9IF-JX59): `etc/manifest.toml` holds the bytes of `cli.Manifest` (`D02-cli`, R-V162-1KNZ, R-93PO-A5VQ) and `etc/nginx.conf` the bytes of `cli.NginxConf`, which this document states. The templates ship inside the binary (`D01-layout-and-run-seam`, R-A8AJ-65EK), and the database is not in the file: campaigns creates `state/campaigns.db` under its working directory on its first start (`D01-layout-and-run-seam`, R-AJ9M-M32T). The tarball, its member list and its name are devctl's; the icon is never opened by a test. No test reads the checkout, so the release and the icon are outside what a requirement can state; a developer checks them with `tar`.

## The manifest

The manifest is `D02-cli`'s, byte for byte (R-V162-1KNZ). What a space takes from it: the app is `campaigns`; its description is `pages.Description` (R-V2DY-FCEO), which the host publishes in its services file for the gateway; it is not the default app; it is an MCP service (`mcp = true`), so the gateway offers the fifteen tools (`D08-tools`); it serves guests (`guests = true`, log D10); it holds no secrets, since it sends nothing and calls nothing outside the suite (doc 19, doc 20); its `[env]` holds `CAMPAIGNS_DEFAULT_DESTINATION` and `CAMPAIGNS_LINK_PREFIX` with their defaults, which opsctl and the sandbox write into its environment (log D11, log D22); `[database]` names `state/campaigns.db`, which the host keeps and replicates; `[resources]` holds only `memory_max = "128M"`; and `[home]` holds only `group = "application"`, so home lists campaigns under Application. `DRAIN_SECONDS` is the space's.

**What `guests = true` means.** The host's nginx (opsctl's) and the sandbox's put every request at `/` and below through auth's open check, which sets `X-User-Id` and `X-User-Email` when the request carries a credential and lets it through without them when it does not; only `/mcp` and `/api` and the paths under them, and the git smart-HTTP paths (any path ending `/info/refs`, `/git-upload-pack` or `/git-receive-pack`, matched by nginx's regex location), still demand a credential, answered `401` by nginx before anything reaches campaigns. So a guest reaches campaigns on every other path; campaigns serves no git path, so a signed-in request there reaches its not-found page. campaigns sorts them itself (`D03-serve`): `/t/` is the guest ingress, which ignores identity and answers every request a 302 (`D06-links`); `/events` and `/declarations` are the bus's, held back at the public name by the fragment; `/_appkit/` serves the shared files to anyone; and every other path is a page, which sends a guest a 302 to the sign-in address itself, as sites' pages do (`D07-pages`, `D01-layout-and-run-seam` R-BG6W-XW3K), and draws the page for a signed-in user. A page's 302 for a guest is therefore campaigns', not nginx's, where candidates' is nginx's.

## The nginx fragment

`cli.NginxConf` is cron's fragment exactly: two exact-match locations answering `/events` and `/declarations` 404 at the public name, so a bus delivery and the declarations reach campaigns only over its socket (`D05-events`). Nothing else is held back: `/t/` must reach campaigns from the open internet, `/mcp` is guarded by nginx's own locations, and the pages guard themselves. The fragment adds no location for `/t/`, since the open check at `/` already admits a guest there and sets `X-Real-IP`. cron's design observed the two lines' behaviour in a real nginx: every method of `/events` and `/declarations`, with or without a query, answered 404, and `/events/`, `/declarations/`, `/eventsx` and `/` passed on; campaigns' page handler answers those last ones (`D03-serve`, R-Q8D7-UNAL).

## On a space

campaigns on a space is the same binary on the same terms as under a test: a socket passed as descriptor 3 (`/run/ikigenba/campaigns.sock`), `/var/opt/ikigenba/campaigns` as its working directory, its settings from `/etc/opt/ikigenba/campaigns/env`, and `IKIGENBA_SERVICES` naming the host's services file. Who supplies each is opsctl's; what campaigns does with them is `D01-layout-and-run-seam`'s and `D03-serve`'s. Of the services file it uses `candidates`, through appkit's `candidates` client, for `add_cohort`, `add_member`, `merge_list` and the names on the campaign page; `artifacts`, through appkit's `artifacts` client, for `add_material`; `auth`, for the banner's links and a guest's sign-in address; and `events` and `telemetry`, appkit's sinks'. It calls no other sibling and nothing outside the suite (`D01-layout-and-run-seam`, R-077Z-GMKB). A missing entry never refuses the start: without `candidates` or `artifacts` the tools that need it refuse `unavailable` and the pages leave the names and sibling links empty (`D03-serve`, R-QGWI-J1HG, for the tools; `D07-pages`, R-D1SN-LVRE, for the pages; log D19). The host must run candidates with its cohort tools (the document's Prerequisites) and artifacts.

The tracking page on michaelgreenly.dev is the front door for a printed or mailed link: a piece's link is `CAMPAIGNS_LINK_PREFIX` and its token, by default `https://michaelgreenly.dev/c/?t=<token>`, and the page forwards a token of campaigns' shape to `https://campaigns.us.ikigenba.dev/t/<token>`, passing its own referrer as `r` (log D10). That page is the michaelgreenly-dev repository's, committed there and published by the owner only once campaigns is live on `us` (log D7, log D10).

## In a sandbox

The sandbox builds campaigns from the worktree, gives it the two `[env]` settings and `DRAIN_SECONDS`, includes `etc/nginx.conf` in campaigns' server, and, the manifest admitting guests, routes `/` and below through auth's open check and `/mcp` through the credential check, as the host does. campaigns' state is under the sandbox's data directory and survives `down` and `up`. An agent is curl on the developer's machine: the pages at `http://campaigns.<sandbox>.localhost:<port>/` with `Authorization: Bearer $(sandbox token)` or without it for a guest, `/t/<token>` on the same host, and the tools through the gateway. The sandbox's nginx sets `X-Real-IP` to the connecting address and overwrites any value the client sends, so every visit in a sandbox records the loopback address the curl connected from.

The sandbox runs a real candidates and a real artifacts. The walk keeps material against the real artifacts: the delivery puts one small file into the sandbox's artifacts through artifacts' published `import` tool and the upload it plans, and reads its metadata with artifacts' `show`. It reads businesses from a stand-in candidates instead of the real one, swapped in through the services file.

### The stand-in candidates

The real candidates in a sandbox cannot be made to hold a cohort whose members lack an address or an email on demand, or an excluded member, and every real business there carries a real email address, which the merge list would answer and the walk would print; log D8 allows only the owner's fake addresses in anything a check handles. So the walk's businesses come from a stand-in, as candidates' own checks take their rows from a stand-in scrapes.

**What it is.** A small Go program the delivery writes and runs from a temporary directory outside the tree (`mktemp -d "${TMPDIR:-/tmp}/scratch.XXXXXX"`), never committed, requiring appkit at a published release through the module proxy as any module does. It serves appkit's MCP server at `POST /mcp` on a Unix socket in a short temporary directory of its own, with two tools registered, `cohort` and `candidate`, the two appkit's `candidates` client calls (`Members` calls `cohort` with `{"cohort": <id>}`; `Candidate` calls `candidate` with `{"candidate": <id>}`). `cohort` answers a structured result whose `members` is an array, each element an object with `candidate`, the business, and `excluded`, a boolean; `candidate` answers a structured result whose `candidate` is the business. A business is an object with the strings `id`, `status`, `name`, `street`, `city`, `state`, `zip`, `county`, `phone` and `email`, `names`, an array of strings, and `people`, an array of objects with `name` and `role`; an absent fact is the empty string. An id it does not hold is refused as candidates refuses one, an error result whose text begins `not_found: `, which the client answers `candidates.ErrNotFound`. It answers `POST /events` as appkit's `events.DeliveryHandler` with no handlers answers, and 404 on every other path. It appends one line per request to a log in its directory: the tool, the arguments, and the `X-User-Id`, `X-User-Email` and `X-Request-Id` headers.

**Its data.** One cohort, `coh_00000000000000c1`, and five candidates, each `status` `active`, with a distinct fictional name, one other name, one person with a role, a `555-01xx` phone, and, wherever it has an email, `mgreenly+fake00@gmail.com`, the only email address in the stand-in (log D8):

| Candidate | In the cohort | Street, city, state, zip | Email | Pieces an add makes on a mail-and-email campaign |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `cand_00000000000000a1` | yes | all four | fake00 | mail and email |
| `cand_00000000000000a2` | yes | street empty, the other three present | fake00 | email; mail skipped, no postal address |
| `cand_00000000000000a3` | yes | all four | empty | mail; email skipped, no email |
| `cand_00000000000000a4` | yes, `excluded` true | all four | fake00 | none: dropped as excluded (log D17) |
| `cand_00000000000000a5` | no | all four | fake00 | mail and email, through `add_member` |

`cohort` answers the four cohort members in that order; `candidate` answers any of the five; every other cohort or candidate id is `not_found`.

**The swap.** The sandbox's services file is shared, `services.json` in its data directory. The delivery edits its `candidates` entry's `socket` to the stand-in's socket and restarts campaigns' unit, `sandbox-<sandbox>-campaigns.service`, with `systemctl --user restart`, since campaigns builds its candidates client from the entry at the start (`D03-serve`); afterwards it restores the entry and restarts campaigns again. The gateway and the events app read the file too, so while the swap stands the gateway's `candidates` tools are the stand-in's and bus deliveries bound for candidates reach the stand-in's `/events` and are lost; that is accepted in a sandbox, and if events pauses its candidates subscriber the delivery resumes it with events' `resume` after the restore. The URL in the entry is left as it is, so the campaign page's links still name the real candidates' pages, where the stand-in's ids are not found; that is accepted, the link's address being `D01-layout-and-run-seam`'s (R-BIMP-PFKY). **Caution:** `sandbox up` rewrites `services.json` from the manifests, so a redeploy while the stand-in is swapped in silently undoes the swap and campaigns would read the real candidates at its next start; the walk makes no `sandbox up` between the swap and the restore, and if one is needed it makes the swap again, with the restart, before continuing. At cleanup the stand-in is stopped, its directories removed by their literal paths, the entry restored and campaigns restarted.

### The walk

With the sandbox up from the worktree and the swap made, the delivery walks, as the developer, through the gateway's tools and with curl:

1. Before any campaign: home, `/`, `/tools` and `/about` signed in and as a guest, `/mcp` without a credential, `/events` and `/declarations` at the public name and `/declarations` on campaigns' socket, and the gateway's `describe` of campaigns.
2. `create_campaign` A with channels mail and email and a destination of the delivery's choosing; `create_campaign` B with channel email; `edit_campaign` A's description, then the same edit again.
3. `add_cohort` A with `coh_00000000000000c1`, twice; `add_cohort` A with an unknown cohort; `add_member` A with `cand_00000000000000a5`, twice, and with an unknown candidate; `add_member` B with `cand_00000000000000a5`.
4. `reject` A's `cand_00000000000000a3` with a reason, twice; `drop` A's `cand_00000000000000a1` email piece, twice; `deliver` that dropped piece, and `deliver` `cand_00000000000000a3`'s mail piece.
5. `merge_list` A mail, then mail with `all`, then email.
6. `deliver` `cand_00000000000000a1`'s mail piece, twice; `drop` it; `deliver_channel` A email, then mail, then mail again.
7. `add_material` A with the artifact's path and a label, again with another label, with a path artifacts refuses as malformed and with a well-formed path it does not hold; `remove_material` the path, twice; artifacts' `show` of the path.
8. Visits to `/t/<token>` on campaigns' host: `cand_00000000000000a1`'s mail token with a mobile-like `User-Agent` (an iPhone Safari string) and `?r=` an escaped referrer; the same token in capitals with a mail-client-like `User-Agent` (a Thunderbird or Outlook string) and a `Referer` header and no `r`; a `HEAD` of it; a `GET` carrying a forged `X-Real-IP`; and a `GET` of the rejected member's mail token and of the dropped email token. Each with `curl -sD-` and no redirect followed.
9. Unknown answers: a `GET` of a well-formed token no piece carries, a `POST` to a known token, a `GET` of `/t/abc` and of `/t/<token>/x`.
10. `engagement` of the visited piece, of `cand_00000000000000a1` in A, and `campaign` A; A's page `/campaigns/<A>` signed in and as a guest.
11. `delete_campaign` A; `delete_campaign` B, twice; a `GET` of B's piece's token; `campaign` B and B's page.
12. events' `catalog` and `search` for campaigns, telemetry's `catalog` and `search` for campaigns, and the stand-in's log.
13. The swap restored and campaigns restarted.

## Trust

campaigns believes the identity it is sent, as every sibling does: `X-User-Id`, `X-User-Email` and `X-Request-Id` as nginx or the gateway set them on a request to its socket (`D03-serve`, R-PZTX-693Q). It asks auth nothing; a campaign, a member, a rejection, a delivery, a drop and a material are recorded as made by the user the request names, and the calls it makes to candidates and artifacts forward that identity (`D03-serve`, R-QJCB-AKYU, R-QKK7-OCPJ). Every signed-in user sees and may change every campaign (log D19). A process that can reach campaigns' socket can therefore name any user and do anything a tool does as that user; every service on the host is trusted, and nginx and the gateway are the only doors from outside, nginx holding back `/events` and `/declarations` there.

campaigns trusts `X-Real-IP` as the visitor's address because nginx sets it from the connection and overwrites what a client sends; on the socket any value is believed, as every header is. The user agent and the referrer are the visitor's own words, kept as given and never interpreted (doc 13); the `r` parameter is the tracking page's report of its own referrer, and anyone can forge it.

A token is a tracking handle, not a credential (doc 11, log D10, log D12; `D06-links`, `D09-trail`). It grants nothing but a visit row and a redirect to a destination that is public anyway; it is printed on paper and carried in email, sits in nginx's access log and in the trail's request pair, and is compared as any lookup key is. A guest who guesses one adds a visit to that piece, which is what anyone holding the letter could do; 128 random bits make guessing hopeless, and every visit is kept, so a forged one stays visible beside the real ones (doc 13). An unknown token learns nothing: its answer differs from a known one's only in `Location` (`D06-links`, R-28GD-3781), and the destination it reveals for a known token is the one the campaign points every reader at. No answer sets a cookie.

The stand-in candidates and its socket are trusted as the real candidates is: whatever a process at the `candidates` entry answers is the business.

## The finish line

The delivery carries campaigns to `us` itself, after the sandbox walk, in the order the log's sequence derives (log D14) and as log D6 settles it: the steps a shell can make are the delivery's, and the phone's QR scan and the email client's open stay with the owner. The candidates change (doc 17, doc 18) is the log's step 2, with its own design; the finish line's candidate page and cohort filter are its outcomes, not campaigns'.

1. Build: campaigns' build run closes the gap and the gates pass.
2. Sandbox: the walk above; nothing in it reaches a real candidate's email.
3. Land: the branch onto `main` with `git fetch . <branch>:main`.
4. Tag: the next suite release, on `main`.
5. Push: `main` and the tag to origin.
6. Deploy: `devctl deploy us <release>`. The start creates `state/campaigns.db` and migrates it; there is no previous release of campaigns, so no rollback concern beyond removing the app.
7. Check the space: home lists campaigns under Application; the pages answer 200 with the owner's token and 302 to sign-in without it; `POST https://campaigns.us.ikigenba.dev/mcp` without a credential is 401; `/events` and `/declarations` are 404 there; the gateway lists the fifteen tools.
8. Create: as the owner, through the gateway, `create_campaign` with the channels and destination the owner chooses, then `add_cohort` with the owner's real cohort, whose id the owner names; the result's skipped pieces and excluded count are read back to the owner.
9. Merge list: `merge_list` mail answers one row per planned mail piece, each with its link.
10. Visits from a shell: for one piece the owner picks, `curl -sD- -o /dev/null` of `https://campaigns.us.ikigenba.dev/t/<token>` with a mobile-like `User-Agent` and `?r=` an escaped referrer, then with a mail-client-like `User-Agent` and a `Referer` header; each answers 302 to the campaign's destination; `engagement` of the piece answers two opens with both user agents, both referrers and the shell host's public address, and of its member opened with the same counts and times; an unknown token answers 302 to the default destination and adds nothing.
11. Bus and trail: events' `search` finds the `campaign.member.added` events of step 8; telemetry holds the two `campaign.visited` events and no `sibling.called` but to candidates and artifacts.
12. Hand-over to the owner: the owner publishes the tracking page's commit in michaelgreenly-dev (log D7, log D10), then scans one letter's QR code with a phone and opens one email's link in a mail client; both visits show against the right member with their counts and times, as step 10's did. Marking pieces delivered is the owner's, by hand, when the letters go to the post office and the emails are sent (doc 8, Finish line).

The two shell visits of step 10 are kept against a real member of the owner's real campaign, as every visit is (doc 13); they are told from real ones by their user agents and the shell host's address.

## Recorded decisions

- **`cli.NginxConf` is declared here,** as `D01-layout-and-run-seam` and `D02-cli` assign it, and is cron's fragment byte for byte (the brief): campaigns is an emitter-only bus member as cron is, and its guest ingress needs no location of its own, the open check at `/` already admitting a guest and setting `X-Real-IP`.
- **The manifest stays `D02-cli`'s;** this document states no manifest requirement and explains what a space takes from it.
- **`guests = true` is log D10's,** and the pages send a guest to sign in themselves, as sites' do. Departure: candidates' `guests = false` has nginx and auth answer every guest; campaigns cannot keep that and still serve `/t/` to a visitor's phone, and the brief rejects a hand-written host location (log D10, option c).
- **The walk reads businesses from a stand-in candidates,** as candidates' checks read rows from a stand-in scrapes: the real candidates cannot be made to hold a cohort with a member lacking a postal address, one lacking an email and one excluded, and its businesses carry real email addresses, while log D8 allows only the owner's fake addresses in a check. The stand-in uses `mgreenly+fake00@gmail.com` as its only email. **It is built on appkit's MCP server** at a published release, so its results are in the shape appkit's own server writes and the gateway can list its tools during the swap; it serves exactly the two tools appkit's `candidates` client calls.
- **The stand-in is swapped in through the shared services file and campaigns restarted,** as candidates' checks swap scrapes and prompts: campaigns builds its candidates client from the entry at the start (`D03-serve`), so editing the entry and restarting is the one seam that reaches it without a change to the sandbox. Bus deliveries bound for candidates during the swap are lost, accepted in a sandbox.
- **Material is kept against the real artifacts in the sandbox,** not a stand-in: artifacts can be made to hold a chosen file through its published tools, and the walk then exercises `Head` and `Show` against the real service (log D20, log D23).
- **The address check in a sandbox expects the loopback address,** since both nginxes overwrite `X-Real-IP` with the connecting address; that the handler reads `X-Real-IP` and never the connection's address is `D06-links`' requirement over a test's own header.
- **Bus delivery is observed at the bus** (events' `search` and `catalog`), since no sibling accepts the three events until candidates' step lands (log D14, doc 17).
- **The finish line's shell steps are the delivery's and the phone and mail-client opens the owner's** (log D6). The delivery does not mark pieces delivered on `us`: the document's Finish line names the owner as the one who marks them, delivery is by hand for what was actually sent (doc 8), a delivered piece never changes again (doc 7) and makes the campaign undeletable (doc 2), and the visits that prove the tracking path record whatever the piece's state (log D22). The shell visits stay on the real campaign's record (doc 13).
- No release is named in the manifest, the fragment or any requirement; the release the finish line tags and deploys is the delivery's.

## Consumer tasks

An operator installs the release; opsctl reads `etc/manifest.toml`, writes `DRAIN_SECONDS`, `CAMPAIGNS_DEFAULT_DESTINATION` and `CAMPAIGNS_LINK_PREFIX` into `/etc/opt/ikigenba/campaigns/env`, generates campaigns' server with the open check at `/`, the credential check at `/mcp` and `/api`, and `etc/nginx.conf` included, adds campaigns' entry with its icon and group to the services file, and starts the unit; the start creates and migrates the database with no step of the operator's. The owner publishes the tracking page once campaigns is live (log D10).

A test reads `nginx.conf` from the root package's `Etc()` with `fs.ReadFile` and compares it with `cli.NginxConf`; it imports `cli` and references the constant, so it compiles only if the constant exists.
