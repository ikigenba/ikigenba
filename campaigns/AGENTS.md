# campaigns

An app of the Ikigenba platform: outreach campaigns to the businesses candidates ingests, each piece carrying a tracking link of its own, served at `campaigns.<host>`. One Go binary serves on the listening socket it is passed as descriptor 3 (`/run/ikigenba/campaigns.sock` on a host), behind the host's nginx. campaigns is stage eight of the funnel, compose, and the tracking half of stage nine, send. A campaign is a named record the owner creates with a description, the channels it uses (`mail`, `email` or both, fixed at creation) and the destination its links forward a visitor to; deleting one marks it, and a campaign holding a delivered piece cannot be deleted. Its members are businesses named by their candidate id in candidates, each in a campaign once, added from a prospect list or one at a time, and rejected for good. A member gets one piece per channel the campaign uses, except no mail piece when the business candidates answers lacks a postal address and no email piece when it lacks an email; each piece is planned, delivered by hand or dropped, and carries a token, whose link, `CAMPAIGNS_LINK_PREFIX` and the token, the tracking page on michaelgreenly.dev forwards to campaigns' guest route `GET /t/<token>`. That route records every visit whole, the user agent, the referrer and the address, and answers a 302 to the campaign's destination, or to `CAMPAIGNS_DEFAULT_DESTINATION` for a request that names no piece; engagement is read off the visits. A campaign's material is labelled references to files in artifacts.

**campaigns sends nothing.** It sends no email and no mail, by any channel: it has no send path, no mail or email client, no SMTP, no HTTP client to the outside and no call to gmail or to any connector, and delivery is marked by hand. The hard rule binds every later version too: campaigns, and anything built on it, may send an email only when a human has explicitly approved that specific send, in production, and never in a sandbox, a smoke test, a check script or any development work, with no flag, setting or environment that lets it happen there (`D01-layout-and-run-seam`). No test, fixture or check sends an email or a letter, and none reaches gmail or a connector; should a later version send, `mgreenly+fake00@gmail.com` through `mgreenly+fake99@gmail.com` are the only addresses a test send may ever go to.

Among its siblings campaigns calls only candidates, through appkit's `candidates` client over the socket the services file's `candidates` entry names, for `add_prospect_list`, `add_member`, `merge_list` and the names on the campaign page, and artifacts, through appkit's `artifacts` client over the socket the `artifacts` entry names, whose `Head` and then `Show` `add_material` calls; each call carries the caller of the tool call or page request it is made for. A missing entry never stops the start: the tools that need the sibling refuse `unavailable` and the pages leave the names and sibling links empty. Outside the suite it reaches nothing. It keeps its campaigns, members, pieces and their tokens, material references and visits in its own SQLite database, `state/campaigns.db` resolved against the working directory and opened through appkit's `db` package; it is the only writer of it. It emits an event on the bus when a member is added or rejected and when a piece is delivered, accepts none, and records each visit in the trail as `campaign.visited`. Its manifest sets `guests = true`: nginx lets a request without a credential reach every path but `/mcp` and `/api`, the guest route ignores identity, and the pages send a guest to sign in themselves; its nginx fragment answers the bus's two paths 404 at the public name. At `/mcp` it offers the fifteen MCP tools `D08-tools` catalogs, which agents reach through the MCP gateway. At `/` it serves a page of the campaigns, a page per campaign at `/campaigns/<id>`, a tools page at `/tools`, an about screen at `/about` and a not-found page; the pages carry no form and change no record. On a host it runs as `bin/campaigns` of its release with `/var/opt/ikigenba/campaigns` as its working directory and its environment from `/etc/opt/ikigenba/campaigns/env`; a developer runs the same binary from the checkout. The module path is `github.com/ikigenba/ikigenba/campaigns`. It requires appkit (`github.com/ikigenba/ikigenba/appkit`) and uses appkit's packages `page` (the banner and footer, and the shared static files under `/_appkit/`), `identity` (the caller nginx authenticated, required at `/mcp` and optional before the pages), `mcp` (the server mounted at `/mcp`, and the client the tests drive it with), `telemetry` (the event contract, the request middleware and the writer campaigns' events go through), `db` (the database's handle and its migrations), `paging` (the listing tokens), `services` (the services file), `version` (the identity and its display string), `events` (the event bus), `candidates` (the client of candidates' socket) and `artifacts` (the client of artifacts' socket). It requires no other module. The contract is the documents in `specs/design/`. This file restates none of it. campaigns is an app, so it has no stories: its intent is the decisions document that delivered it, or what was agreed in conversation, together with its templates under `assets/`, and the design is the record.

This sub-project is spec-driven: `specs/design/` defines the contract, and the build run writes the Go source, the tests, `go.mod`'s requirements and `go.sum`, the migrations under `migrations/` (`0001_campaigns.sql` and `0002_prospect_lists.sql`), which the root package embeds, and everything under `etc/` (`etc/manifest.toml` and the nginx fragment `etc/nginx.conf`). The delivery that created campaigns seeded `go.mod` on appkit; the build run owns it and `go.sum` from then on and changes them as design says. It never writes `assets/`, `share/`, this `AGENTS.md`, the `Makefile` or `.golangci.yml`. `state/` is where a running campaigns keeps `campaigns.db`; it is created at run time and never committed. See the `spec` and `build-spec` skills. Everything below is what the build run computes the gap and runs the gates against; it is written outside the run and read-only to it.

## Assets

`assets/` holds campaigns' markup: Go `html/template` files, each opening with a comment naming the data it receives: `landing.html`, `campaign.html`, `tools.html`, `about.html` and `notfound.html`. Every page is shown only to a signed-in user; a guest is sent to sign in. They are written by the user or the delivering agent, following the repository's `design/`, and are inputs to the spec: the build run reads them and never writes them. The code parses them into the set appkit's `page.Templates` returns and executes them by template name; it never writes markup of its own, not even a fragment or an error page. Go's `embed` reaches only files at or below the embedding package's directory, so the module's root package (the directory holding `go.mod`) embeds `assets/`, and design names what it exports.

Every word a person or an agent reads, and every class, id and attribute, lives in exactly one place, a template under `assets/` or a named copy constant in the source; a test and a requirement never spell one. Design names each template and the data it receives, never its text, hooks, markup or styles. A test proves a page by executing the named template with the data the design says and comparing, or by checking that a value the test supplied appears in the body; it never looks for a word or a tag. A change to copy or markup is an edit to the asset alone. A needed template that is missing or wrong, or a template that cannot show a state the design names, is an issue: the run files it in `specs/issues/` and never edits the asset to close it.

campaigns holds no copy of the stylesheet, fonts, or licences; appkit's `page` package embeds and serves them. `share/icon.svg` is campaigns' icon, shown in the banner's trail and on home: Tabler's `speakerphone` outline from `design/ikigenba/icons/tabler/`, without its class, width, height, or invisible bounding path, as `design/README.md` asks of a service icon. The build run never writes it. `devctl build` packs it beside `bin/` and `etc/`.

## Test files

The sub-project's tests are all `*_test.go` files in the module: the root package, `cmd/campaigns` and everything under `internal/` (`internal/cli`, `internal/settings`, `internal/server`, `internal/web`, `internal/store`, `internal/trail`, `internal/links`, `internal/pages`, `internal/tools` and `internal/urls`). This is the file set the canonical gap greps for requirement ids:

```
grep -rhoE 'R-[A-Z0-9]{4}-[A-Z0-9]{4}' --include='*_test.go' . | sort -u
```

`specs/`, `assets/` and `share/` hold no `*_test.go`, so the design documents never enter the test side of the grep.

**No id-shaped literal in a fixture.** The grep cannot tell a requirement tag from any other string of that shape: such a literal anywhere in a `*_test.go` file counts as a covered id. campaigns echoes back what callers and its siblings give it — candidate ids and prospect list ids as given, the businesses candidates answers and every field of them (names, other names, streets, cities, states, ZIPs, counties, phones, emails, people), artifact paths and ids, campaign names, descriptions and destinations, material labels, rejection reasons, user ids and emails, request ids, bus event ids, event causes, the services file's URLs, the settings' URLs, a visit's user agent, referrer and address, the tokens and links of pieces, and the `camp_` and `piece_` ids campaigns mints — and tests echo candidate ids above all, since every member, piece and merge-list row names one, so a test that sends an id-shaped value lands one in the test file. No fixture carries one: not a header value, a query parameter, a tool argument, a fake candidates' or fake artifacts' answer, a business's field, a candidate id, a prospect list id, a campaign id, a piece id, a token, an artifact path or id, a URL, a services file entry, a setting, an event's payload, a rejection's reason, a campaign's name or description, a label, a user agent, a referrer, an address, or an expected body.

## Test discipline

These rules govern the unit tests: everything `go test ./...` runs.

- Offline: no network beyond loopback and Unix sockets, no real credentials.
- Deterministic: time, randomness, and environment are injected; no test sleeps to wait for something. Clocks and pauses are injected: a test hands in a `Now`, a `Sleep` and a `Rand` it controls.
- No fixed ports: a test binds `127.0.0.1:0` or a Unix socket in a temporary directory.
- Isolated: a test touches only its own temporary directory, never the developer's home, config, or real state.

**Nothing is sent.** No test sends an email or a letter, reaches gmail or any connector, or reaches anything outside loopback and its own Unix sockets. campaigns has no send path and no HTTP client, and a test proves the absence by use, as design names: the `Process` shape, the keys `Run` looks up, and a services file naming every other sibling on a listener that records each connection, none of which is reached.

**The run seam carries the process.** campaigns binds nothing itself; it serves on the listener it is passed. Arguments, environment lookup and removal, the pid, the inherited listener, the output streams, the clock, the pause between delivery attempts, the random source and the working directory come in through the run seam design declares (`cli.Run` with `cli.Process`), and tests inject them. A `Run`-level test sets the version string to one of its own and `Dir` to a temporary directory of its own, so the database lands there and never in the checkout. A test never reads or changes the real environment, clock, or randomness, and never leaves the inherited-listener step unset, since that would take the test process's real descriptor 3. The one exception is the working directory: a test that proves what an empty `Dir`, or the root package's `Assets`, `Etc` or `Migrations` does whatever the working directory is may `t.Chdir` into a temporary directory of its own, and such a test does not call `t.Parallel`. A test learns that the server is ready the way systemd does: it binds a Unix datagram socket in a short temporary directory (`os.MkdirTemp("", ...)`, since a Unix socket path is limited to 108 bytes and `t.TempDir()` can exceed it), names it in `NOTIFY_SOCKET`, and waits for `READY=1` with a deadline that fails the test. A test waits on the clock in one place only, because there the deadline is the behavior: a drain test, which keeps that wait to a few seconds. The gates run offline as an ordinary user, with no systemd.

**The siblings are faked on loopback.** No test reaches candidates or artifacts. A `Run`-level test writes a services file whose `candidates` entry names a Unix socket in a short temporary directory on which a loopback fake serves the MCP tools appkit's `candidates` client calls, `prospect_list` and `candidate`, with the prospect lists and businesses the test chooses, and whose `artifacts` entry names one on which a loopback fake answers appkit's `artifacts` client's `Head` and `Show` with the artifacts the test chooses; each fake refuses or fails on demand and records each request it receives, identity headers included. A test of the tools or the pages hands them appkit's `candidates` and `artifacts` clients built with `NewClient` over such an entry, or nil clients, as design names. A test proves a call by what the fake received. The bus and telemetry are captures. The exec'ing test never lets the binary reach candidates or artifacts: the tools it calls, `create_campaign` and `campaigns`, read neither.

**The state is an isolated directory.** Each test gives campaigns a working directory inside a test-owned temporary directory, so the database is the test's own. A test that needs a store opens its own database with appkit's `db.Open` at a path in its own temporary directory, with the root package's `Migrations()` and a clock it controls, closes the handle when it ends, and builds the store over that handle with `store.New`. Tests may create filesystem fixtures inside that temporary tree (a regular file named `state`, a file at `state/campaigns.db` that is not a database, a database a test's own `db.Open` made and then changed through `DB.Write`, campaigns, members, pieces, material and visits a test's own store recorded, campaigns deleted); nothing touches `/var/opt/ikigenba/campaigns`, the checkout's `state/` or a shared file. A database failure is provoked with `SetFailing(true)` on the handle the store the test handed the server or handler was built over, or by closing that handle, as design names, never by corrupting the file or removing permissions. Tests prove campaigns' use of the database, its schema, its store and its wiring, never SQLite's own guarantees (atomicity, durability, locking) and never appkit's `db` contract (opening, migrations, transactions, `db status`), which appkit's own tests prove; they prove what a store holds by reopening its path, never by reading tables. A test of a window's plan asks SQLite for it through `EXPLAIN QUERY PLAN` over the store's exported statement, as design names.

**Time is driven, not waited out.** A campaign's created time, a member's added and rejected times, a piece's delivered and dropped times, a material's added time, a visit's time and the order of visits are proved through the injected `Now`: a test advances the clock it handed in, never waits real seconds. The writer's and the emitter's pause between delivery attempts is the injected `Sleep`. A test that sleeps to age a record is a bug. A test waits for something it started to finish (a fake receiving a request, an event reaching a capture) with a deadline that fails the test.

**Events come from the test's own sink.** Where a test observes the events campaigns records or emits, it hands campaigns' writer appkit's `telemetry.Capture`, or a services file whose `telemetry` or `events` entry names a Unix socket the test holds, as design names; it hands the emitter appkit's `events.Capture` and sets an event cause with `events.NewContext`. Every test builds its own database; no test depends on state another test made or on the order the tests run in.

**Environment variables, set by the test.** appkit's constructors read `IKIGENBA_SERVICES` (`services.Variable`) from the process environment; it is the one environment read campaigns cannot route through the run seam, and in the binary only `main` makes it. A test that reaches such a read, directly or through a campaigns constructor, first sets that variable with `testing.T.Setenv` to a services file it wrote or to the empty string, so the developer's environment never decides a result. It is the only variable an in-process test sets for campaigns' own code; `DRAIN_SECONDS`, `CAMPAIGNS_DEFAULT_DESTINATION` and `CAMPAIGNS_LINK_PREFIX` reach `Run` through the `LookupEnv` the test gives it. The exec'ing test also sets `IKIGENBA_COMMIT` and `IKIGENBA_RELEASE` with `testing.T.Setenv`, so it may call appkit's `version.Read()` and the identity's `String()` under the same two values it composes into the child's environment; the commit it sets is longer than seven characters, so a `main` that skipped shortening would fail. Any test that sets a variable with `testing.T.Setenv` does not call `t.Parallel`.

**Process-wide state is not shared.** A test that changes process-wide state, such as the standard `log` package's output, restores it when it ends and does not call `t.Parallel`, so no other test's line lands in its buffer.

**Identity comes from headers the test sets.** appkit's `identity.Require` wraps `/mcp`, and `identity.Optional` the pages and `/_appkit/`; the guest route `/t/` and the event bus's `/events` and `/declarations` take no identity. A test sets the identity headers to be a signed-in user, or omits `X-User-Id` to be a guest: at `/mcp` it exercises the missing-identity answer, on a page the redirect to sign in, and at `/t/` the same answer a signed-in request gets. It sets `Host` and `X-Forwarded-Proto` the way nginx does, and `X-Real-IP` for a visit's address. Where it matters it acts as two users, and it proves that a campaign, a member, a rejection, a delivery, a drop or a material is recorded as made by its caller by its record. What appkit's middleware and writer do is appkit's contract; campaigns' tests prove only that its handlers are wired to them, by use.

**MCP through appkit's client.** A test drives `/mcp` the way a client would: it serves the handler it built on loopback or a Unix socket and calls it with appkit's `mcp.Client`, passing the caller whose identity headers the client forwards. Raw HTTP to `/mcp` is for what the client cannot send (a missing identity header, `server/discover`), never a substitute for the client. Assertions are on the `mcp.Result` and `mcp.ToolInfo` the client returns. campaigns' tests never re-prove appkit's transport.

**Pages and the guest route are raw HTTP.** A test drives `/`, `/campaigns/<id>`, `/tools`, `/about`, `/t/<token>`, the event bus's two paths and any other path as a browser, a visitor or the bus would: raw HTTP requests on the handler it built, served with `httptest` or on a Unix socket, with the identity headers where the route takes them, and never follows a redirect.

**The addresses are the test's own.** The banner's profile and sign-out addresses and a guest's sign-in address are built on the services file's entry `auth`, and the links to candidates' candidate and prospect list pages and to artifacts' artifact pages on its entries `candidates` and `artifacts`, as design names, or, for auth with none, on the request's scheme and host. A test chooses them: it writes a services file into its own temporary directory whose entry names a URL, or none, and hands its path in the config or names it in `IKIGENBA_SERVICES` through the `LookupEnv` it gives `Run`. The link prefix and the default destination are settings, which a test gives `Run` through that `LookupEnv` or hands in with the settings it builds.

**No test runs the page's scripts.** Every page carries appkit's feedback script. The gates have no browser and no JavaScript engine, and adding one is an external dependency no one has approved, so a test asserts what a response body carries and never what a script would do with it.

**No test reads the checkout.** A test opens no file of this directory — no `.go` file, not `go.mod` or `go.sum`, nothing under `etc/`, `share/` or `assets/` — and never parses or inspects source. It proves what the design declares by using it. Handing `cmd/campaigns` to `go build` is not the test reading it.

**One exec'ing test of the binary, and only one.** No test starts a process: tests under `internal/` never start campaigns. The wiring in `cmd/campaigns` can be proved no other way, so exactly one test that execs the binary is admissible, and it lives in `cmd/campaigns`. It builds the binary into a temporary directory and runs it with `--version`, with `manifest`, with `bogus`, and bare with no socket passed in. For the serve case it stands in for systemd: it makes a Unix socket in a short temporary directory, passes it as `exec.Cmd.ExtraFiles[0]` (descriptor 3 in the child), and starts the child through `/bin/sh -c 'LISTEN_PID=$$ LISTEN_FDS=1 exec "$0"' <binary>`, because `LISTEN_PID` must be the child's own pid and `exec` keeps the shell's. The child runs in a test-owned temporary working directory, where it creates `state/campaigns.db`. Its environment is one the test composes, never the developer's, with non-empty `IKIGENBA_COMMIT` and `IKIGENBA_RELEASE`; the test computes the release and commit it expects with `version.Read()` and the display string with its `String()` after setting the same two values with `t.Setenv`, so no test spells a version. Separately it runs `--version` with neither variable in the child's environment and expects exactly one empty line. The test waits for `READY=1`, makes the requests design names for the binary, over the socket, then stops the child with `SIGTERM`, and in a second run with `SIGINT`, asserting what design states. The binary's `--version` output, the MCP `serverInfo` and `service.started` all carry that display string, and the pages' footer the release and commit. Any other test that builds, execs, waits on, or signals a process is a bug.

## Live tests

There are none. No test reaches outside the suite, and none sends anything: candidates and artifacts, the two siblings campaigns calls, are faked in every unit test, so nothing reaches a real candidates or a real artifacts, and campaigns has nothing outside the suite to reach. The sandbox walk reads businesses from a stand-in candidates whose only email address is `mgreenly+fake00@gmail.com`, keeps material against the sandbox's real artifacts, and proves the real chain.

## Gates

Run from this directory (`campaigns/`), in order; every command must exit 0. No skipped tests, no disabled linters laundering a failure. A per-finding suppression comment (`//nolint` and the like) is a skip: the run never adds one; a finding it cannot fix, or believes is wrong, is filed as an issue.

1. `test -z "$(gofmt -l .)"` — fails if any file is unformatted (fix with `make fmt`)
2. `go build ./...`
3. `CGO_ENABLED=0 GOOS=linux GOARCH=amd64 go build -o /dev/null ./cmd/campaigns` — the release build, static and cgo-free, the way `devctl build` builds it, which is why the SQLite driver must be pure Go
4. `go test -race ./...`
5. `GOLANGCI_LINT_CACHE="$(git rev-parse --absolute-git-dir)/golangci-lint" golangci-lint run --allow-parallel-runners` — the cache lives in this worktree's git directory, so worktrees never share it, and `--allow-parallel-runners` lets gates for several sub-projects lint at once; `make lint` runs this form

Gate 5 also flags an undocumented `package main` (`revive`) and an `http.Server` without `ReadHeaderTimeout` (`gosec` G112); both are fixed in code, never suppressed.

## Commit conventions

```
<imperative summary of the phase, <=50 chars>

<optional: one or two lines on what changed and why>

Requirements: R-XXXX-XXXX, R-YYYY-YYYY
```

The `Requirements:` trailer lists the phase's ids so history stays greppable by id.

## Build

`make` builds `bin/campaigns` from the checkout (`make build`). `make fmt` rewrites unformatted files, and `make test` and `make lint` run the test and lint gates. The gates do not go through `make`: they call the Go tool directly, and the one test that needs a binary builds its own into a temporary directory.

## Deploy

campaigns ships in the suite release: `devctl build <sha|tag>` builds it with every other app, its part of the release holding `bin/campaigns`, `etc/` and `share/icon.svg`, and `devctl deploy <space> <sha|tag>` deploys that release. The host must run events, which campaigns emits to, candidates, whose `prospect_list` and `candidate` tools it reads businesses through over the socket, and artifacts, whose files `add_material` checks over the socket; its services file names them, with telemetry and auth. campaigns needs no outbound access, holds no secret of its own and sends nothing. The tracking page on michaelgreenly.dev forwards a campaign token to `https://campaigns.us.ikigenba.dev/t/<token>`; it is published by the owner only once campaigns is live on `us`. The host's backup archives `state/` whole, so `campaigns.db` is taken with it; it holds the campaigns, their members by candidate id, their pieces and tokens, material references and every visit with its user agent, referrer and address, and no secret.
