# D19-hosts

The accounts service runs hosts, EC2 machines of their own, from the one space whose role AWS lets do so: the hosts operator. Its role carries the tag `HostsOperator=true` and has the customer-managed policy `<root>-hosts-operator` attached, the policy Terraform made for it, named after the root as the permissions boundary is. `hosts` is the command that makes one space the hosts operator and stops it being one, from the developer's machine, and `internal/hosts` owns it. The vocabulary is D06's: `space.HostsTagKey`, `space.HostsTagValue`, `space.HostsPolicyName`, and `space.RoleName`; the IAM operations are D03's `IAM` methods; and the detach that `clear` shares with `space destroy` is D06's `space.DetachHostsPolicy`, so the two can never disagree about what undoing the hosts operator means.

Nothing here touches a host. No ssh connection is opened, no host command is issued, and no instance, address, record, parameter, or object is read or changed beyond the two EC2 listings `cloud.LookupSpace` reads. The tag and the attachment are IAM state on the space's role, so a space may be the hosts operator while it is running, stopped, or left with only its address by a failed replace.

`set` needs the space: it finds it with `cloud.LookupSpace`, not `LookupInstance`, because it needs no host. Its preflight refuses before anything is printed or changed, in this order: the space exists; its role exists; the managed policy exists, looked up by name as create looks up the launch template and the permissions boundary; and no other space is the hosts operator. The last check reads every role carrying the tag; a tagged role named after another space, one label under the root, is the hosts operator, and `set` refuses to take the role from it, because a set aimed at the wrong space would strip production of its rights. A tagged role that is not named after a space was not devctl's doing and does not stand in the way. Then two steps: `tag` tags the role first, so a set interrupted before the attachment already stands in the way of a set on another space, and `role` attaches the policy. Both run on every `set`, whether or not the space is already the hosts operator, since re-tagging and re-attaching change nothing, and the last line is the space's domain.

`clear` needs only the role: it never looks the space up, so it also clears a role that a destroy which failed part-way left with no instance and no address. It is `set`'s reverse in the reverse order: `role` detaches the policy first, so the role loses its rights before it stops being marked as holding them, and then `tag` removes the tag. Each step that finds nothing to undo says so and succeeds, and there is no final line.

A failure stops the command at that step: the completed lines stay on stdout, the failed step's error is the diagnostic, and nothing is undone; a `*cloud.Error` is returned unchanged. The two refusals the stories fix and no other design declares, a space with no role and another space being the hosts operator, are this package's error types.

Memory is bounded by D03: `ManagedPolicy`, `AttachedPolicy`, `RoleTag`, and `RolesTagged` each hold one page of their listing at once, and `RolesTagged` returns only matching names. `set` holds that result, the names of every role in the account carrying the tag, whether named after a space or not, and nothing else listed; devctl tags only the hosts operator's role, so the result is at most a few names unless something outside devctl tags roles in bulk.

Ordering and the cli mapping are the shared rules and are not restated here: usage errors first, then the root file, then the operand, then the first cloud call (D04 R-N1LD-IX1I and R-ST4K-APZN); `cloud.Connect` with the root as the profile and the root file's region (D02 R-Z8MF-4H88, which lists `hosts` among the commands that read the root file); a `*cloud.NoSpaceError` to `devctl: no space at '<domain>'`, exit 1 (D03 R-YTFS-2EAU); a `*cloud.Error` or `*cloud.NotFoundError` to one line and exit 1 (D03 R-QZSL-ZMJL); the checkout and root-file errors to exit 2 (D04 R-EX3T-CTOO); an error carrying `ExitCode()` and `Detail()` rendered as D05 R-D4G2-IO81 states, with the detail separated as D02 R-C52Z-FBPW states; the fallback that renders any other error as one line with exit 1 (D05 R-0D99-FN33); usage errors as D06's `*space.UsageError` (R-SZP9-8199); and every step line written through `space.Step` (D06 R-SR5Y-JN2E). The top-level usage line `  hosts     make one space the hosts operator, or stop it being one`, the top-level command set, and the per-command help list are D02's.
