# D03-serve

The serve path: what happens between `cli.Run` being called with no arguments and the process being gone, the database it opens before it is ready and closes when it stops, the trail home records of itself from its start to its stop, and the rules its handler keeps on every route — the identity gate, the request's id, the request's two events and the two bookmark events that are the only events home records of its own. `D01-layout-and-run-seam` declares the names this design behaves through: `cli.Process` with its clock, random source, directory and HTTP client, `cli.Run`, the root package's `Migrations`, the display string, the database path `state/home.db` under `Dir` and the User-Agent; `D02-cli` declares the exit codes and decides that an empty `Args` means serve. This document declares `internal/server`. `D04-pages` declares `pages.Handler` and `pages.Config` and states what is answered at `/`, at `/about`, under `/_appkit/` and at a path that is not a page; `D06-bookmarks` declares the store `Run` builds over the database; `D07-bookmarks-page` states what is answered under `/bookmarks` and the two bookmark events; `D08-favicon` declares the fetcher `Run` builds over `Process.HTTP`. appkit's `db` package owns opening the database and bringing it up to date, its `telemetry` package the writer and the request middleware, and its `identity` package the gate; this document wires them and restates none of their contracts.

## The terms home serves on

home serves on the platform's common terms. It serves only on a listening socket it inherits and never opens one of its own; on a host that is `/run/ikigenba/home.sock`, held by `ikigenba-home.socket` beside the `Type=notify` `ikigenba-home.service`. A deploy restarts the service alone, so the socket and the connections queued on it outlive every restart; home's part is to finish what it accepted and leave the socket where systemd put it.

home takes the socket the way `sd_listen_fds(3)` documents: `LISTEN_PID` is its own process id, `LISTEN_FDS` counts the sockets passed, and the first is file descriptor 3. It removes `LISTEN_PID`, `LISTEN_FDS` and `LISTEN_FDNAMES` from its environment once it has taken the socket, as that manual page recommends. Once it is ready it sends the datagram `READY=1` to the Unix datagram socket `NOTIFY_SOCKET` names, as `sd_notify(3)` documents, a name beginning with `@` being in the abstract namespace. Taking descriptor 3 in the binary is `net.FileListener`, which, as Go's `net` documentation of `FileListener` and `UnixListener.SetUnlinkOnClose` states, leaves the socket's path in place when the listener is closed.

The suite is a closed system: home trusts `X-User-Id`, `X-User-Email` and `X-Request-Id` as nginx sets them. Every request has an id: the one nginx set, or, for a request that came without one, the one appkit's request middleware makes up for it, 32 lowercase hexadecimal digits drawn from `Process.Rand`. `Process.Rand` is also where the store draws bookmark ids, so `Run` hands both one reader that never lets two reads of `Process.Rand` overlap (`D01-layout-and-run-seam`).

home's standard error holds only trouble: a start it refuses, a socket it cannot take, a database it cannot open, appkit's one warning line over a database a newer home upgraded, a readiness it cannot report, a socket that stops accepting, a stop that cut requests off, and the lines for an event it could not deliver. A request answered with any status, the missing-identity 500 and the answer to a database that cannot be reached included, and a favicon fetch that finds no icon, are handled, recorded in the trail or answered to the caller, and earn no line. So the handler has no stream, and while telemetry takes every event and the clock reads a year 0 through 9999 a running home writes nothing to either stream.

## Checking the start

With `Args` empty, `Run` checks, in this order: `DRAIN_SECONDS`, then whether a socket was passed in; only once it has taken the socket does it open the database. `DRAIN_SECONDS` is spelled as opsctl writes an integer — one or more ASCII digits, no sign, no leading zero, no unit, no white space — and is 5 when unset or empty; home sets no upper limit. A value that is not acceptable is a usage error, quoted back verbatim, and is reported whether or not a socket was passed in. A socket is passed in only when `LISTEN_PID` is exactly the decimal spelling of `Process.Pid` and `LISTEN_FDS` is a count of at least one; anything else is no socket passed in, and a count above one is a unit home will not guess about. Both are usage errors, exit 2, with the detail line saying how to run home, and none of the refused starts takes the descriptor, unsets a variable, creates or opens anything under `Process.Dir`, reads `Process.Rand`, sends a request through `Process.HTTP`, serves, tells systemd anything, or records an event.

With exactly one socket passed in, `Run` removes the three `LISTEN_*` variables through `Unsetenv` and takes descriptor 3 as its listener, through `Process.Inherit`. A descriptor that cannot be made into a listener is trouble on the host: `home: ` and the error, exit 1; like the refusals, it leaves `Process.Dir` as it found it, reads no `Process.Rand` and sends nothing through `Process.HTTP`.

## The database, the run's writer and readiness

`Run` opens the database with appkit's `db.Open` at `state/home.db` under `Process.Dir`, with the root package's `home.Migrations()`, `Process.Now`, the service `pages.ServiceName`, and a writer for appkit's warning that reaches `Process.Stderr` in one write. `db.Open` creates `state/` and the database on a first start and applies every migration the database has not had, so a first start serves an empty list of bookmarks. Every `db.Open` failure that returns while the context is not done is `home: cannot open database state/home.db: ` and the error's text with its newlines made spaces, exit 1, before anything is served, before systemd is told anything and before any event is recorded; the path is always this relative spelling, whatever `Dir` holds. A missing database is not this error: it is a first start. A regular file named `state` is one, and so is a file at `state/home.db` that is not a database.

A database that records a migration this home does not carry is not refused: data never rolls back, so older code runs on newer data. appkit's `db.Open` applies nothing to it and writes one warning line, beginning with the service name it was given and naming the lowest version this home does not carry; `Run` passes that line to `Process.Stderr` in one write, before `READY=1`, and the trail records nothing about it. home states nothing about the line's wording: a test compares what `Run` wrote with what its own `db.Open` call, given the same service name and a buffer, writes over the same database.

Then `Run` builds the store with `bookmarks.New` over the handle, `Process.Now` and the guarded `Process.Rand` (`D06-bookmarks`); the fetcher with `favicon.New` over `Process.HTTP` and the User-Agent `D01-layout-and-run-seam` states (`D08-favicon`); and the run's writer with appkit's `telemetry.New`, naming the service `pages.ServiceName` and the version `Process.Version`, delivering to `Process.Sink` (appkit's socket sink when it is nil), writing the line for an undelivered event to `Process.Stderr` through the same guard as its own lines, stamping events from `Process.Now` and drawing the request ids it makes up from the guarded `Process.Rand`, as cron's writer does. It looks up `IKIGENBA_SERVICES` once and builds the handler with `pages.Handler` over `Process.Banner`, that value as the services path, the writer, the store and the fetcher. Nothing about `IKIGENBA_SERVICES` or the file it names can stop a start.

A signal that arrives before `Run` has begun to report ready — before the open, during it, after it, or before `Run` was even called — is a stop, not a failure, so it is exit 0: whatever `db.Open` returned, unless it had already failed before the signal arrived, `Run` sends no `READY=1`, accepts nothing, records neither `service.started` nor `service.stopping`, and returns.

The socket has been listening since systemd made it, so ready is true before the first `Accept`, and sending it first means a failure to send is reported before anything was served: `home: ` and the error, exit 1. Once `READY=1` is sent, or there is no notification socket to send it to, and before any connection is accepted, `Run` calls `Writer.Ready`, so `service.started`, carrying `Process.Version`, is the first event of a run.

appkit's writer queues and returns, so neither readiness nor any answer waits on telemetry; an event it cannot deliver goes to standard error as `home: undelivered event: ` and the event's JSON, in the order recorded, and home keeps serving. That a telemetry installed after home started is found without a restart is appkit's socket sink, which reads the services file afresh on every delivery.

This design assumes a clock that reads times in the UTC years 0 through 9999, the years an RFC 3339 time can spell; appkit's writer delivers no event stamped outside them and writes a malformed-event line to standard error instead, and what home records or writes under such a clock is not contract.

## Stopping

When `ctx` is done — in the binary, on `SIGTERM` or `SIGINT`, the signal's name being the context's cause — `Run` drains the server through `Serve`, and once every request has finished, or the drain deadline has come, it shuts the writer down with the reason, the text of `context.Cause(ctx)`, which records `service.stopping` and delivers what is queued, inside the same window. With nothing pending the stop is immediate: `service.stopping` is delivered and home exits 0 at once. Last, once `Serve` has returned, `Run` closes the database's handle, whether the drain ended in time or cut requests off; it closes it too when it returns for any other reason after the database opened.

The **drain deadline** is `DRAIN_SECONDS` in seconds, measured in real time from the cancellation, so a drain test runs with `DRAIN_SECONDS=1` and waits about a second. When the requests use the whole window, every event still queued, `service.stopping` included, is written to standard error before the stop line, which is always `Run`'s last; `Serve` cuts the remaining requests off only after its `stop` has returned, and the client's connection closes without the rest of its answer. `Run` returns less than a second after the deadline and says `home: stopped with <n> requests unfinished` (`1 request` for one), exit 1. A cut-off request's `request.finished`, recorded after the deadline, is written out or not at all, never delivered.

## Serving and stopping the listener

`Serve` owns `ln` from the call. It serves plain HTTP/1.1 with `h` and blocks. When `ctx` is done, `Serve` closes the listener so nothing new is accepted — new connections wait in the socket's queue for the next home — drops connections that carry no request, and lets every request already being handled finish and receive its whole response, for at most `drain`. If they all finish, it calls `stop` and returns nil as soon as `stop` has. If some are still being handled when `drain` runs out, it calls `stop`, then ends those requests' contexts, closes their connections without the rest of their responses, and returns a `*DrainError` counting them, without waiting for the handlers themselves to return. `stop` is called once, when the drain is over, with a context that ends at the deadline. An `Accept` error that reports itself temporary is retried, as `net/http`'s `Server.Serve` retries it; any other ends serving, and that is trouble: `home: ` and the error as `Run`'s last line, exit 1. `Serve` gives its server an `ErrorLog` that goes nowhere, since Go's `net/http` documents that a nil one writes to the real standard error. No read, write or idle timeout is contract; the build sets `ReadHeaderTimeout` because gosec `G112` demands it.

## The handler's rules on every route

The handler `pages.Handler` returns is appkit's `telemetry.Middleware` over `Config.Telemetry`, wrapping appkit's `identity.Require`, wrapping home's routes (`D04-pages`, `D07-bookmarks-page`). So every request records its two request events, and a request without `X-User-Id` is answered 500 with `identity.MissingBody` whatever its path or method — never 404 or 405, and no page and no shared file for it. home's manifest serves no guests, so nginx never lets such a request through; when one arrives the gate or the host is misconfigured. Routing is on the request's `URL.Path` as `net/http` decodes it, compared exactly, and never redirects: the only `303` answers, and the only `Location` headers, are those `D07-bookmarks-page` states for `POST /bookmarks` and for a `POST` to a delete path, a delete of an unknown id included.

home calls no sibling while serving a request: the events a request causes go to telemetry apart from it, and no answer waits for them. The one outbound request a request can cause is the favicon fetch of a `POST /bookmarks` (`D08-favicon`), sent through `Process.HTTP` to wherever the bookmark's URL points, a sibling's page included; it fetches a page and its icon, and calls no sibling's API. A request records its two request events and, when it adds or deletes a bookmark, the one bookmark event `D07-bookmarks-page` states; those two bookmark events are the only events home records of its own.

## Recorded decisions

- `Serve` takes a `stop` function, called once inside the drain window, so the writer's shutdown, and with it `service.stopping`, lands before any cut-off connection is closed.
- The writer is shut down after the requests drain, so `service.stopping` follows every finished request's `request.finished`.
- An accepted `DRAIN_SECONDS` whose seconds are too large for a `time.Duration` has an unspecified drain: no permitted test can establish which drain `Run` uses for such a value, since `Run` exposes no drain duration and a test may wait only a few seconds, and no host writes such a value.
- What becomes of the requests in progress at an accept failure, and whether `service.stopping` is recorded then, is left open, as in the precedent.
- The database is opened only after the socket is taken, so every refused start, the failure to take descriptor 3 included, leaves `Process.Dir` as it found it, reads no `Process.Rand` and sends nothing through `Process.HTTP`. That is stated in a requirement of its own rather than by widening what `touches nothing` denotes, so the requirements using that phrase keep their ids (root correction 8.4).
- A `db.Open` that returns while `ctx` is done, whatever its error, is a stop, exit 0, not the database line, as in the precedent.
- The run's writer takes `Process.Now` and `Process.Rand`, as cron's does (root ruling 7.1). One requirement states that every event's `Time` comes from `Process.Now`. appkit's writer delivers no event stamped outside the UTC years 0 through 9999: it marks the event malformed and writes a malformed-event line to its standard error instead (appkit's `telemetry` source). So every requirement about the run's writer, the handler's events, the lines written for them, and a standard error kept quiet carries cron's clause limiting it to a clock reading those years, and those requirements are re-minted with it.
- `Run` closes the database's handle before it returns, and a requirement says so (root ruling 7.8). appkit's `db.Open` opens SQLite in write-ahead-log mode and sets nothing that keeps the log; SQLite removes `home.db-wal` and `home.db-shm` when the last connection to the database closes. A probe outside the tree, over appkit's `db` from the module proxy, found both files present while a handle was open, still present after a second handle was opened and closed beside one left open, and gone once every handle was closed; so a test that lists `state` after `Run` returns tells a closed handle from a leaked one. The requirement holds only when no accepted request is still being handled, since a request cut off at the drain deadline may still hold a connection when `Run` returns, and when nothing else, a test's own handle included, holds a connection to the database.

## Consumer tasks

`main` hands `cli.Run` the real process (`D01-layout-and-run-seam`). With `Args` empty, `Run` reads `DRAIN_SECONDS`, checks `LISTEN_PID` and `LISTEN_FDS`, calls `p.Unsetenv` for the three `LISTEN_*` names and `ln, err := p.Inherit(3)`, opens `d, err := db.Open(ctx, db.Config{Path: filepath.Join(p.Dir, "state", "home.db"), Migrations: home.Migrations(), Now: p.Now, Service: pages.ServiceName, Stderr: stderr})`, builds `st := bookmarks.New(d, bookmarks.Config{Now: p.Now, Rand: rand})`, `fav := favicon.New(favicon.Config{HTTP: p.HTTP, UserAgent: pages.ServiceName + " (" + pages.Description + ")"})`, `w := telemetry.New(telemetry.Config{Service: pages.ServiceName, Version: p.Version, Sink: p.Sink, Stderr: stderr, Now: p.Now, Rand: rand})` and `h := pages.Handler(pages.Config{Banner: p.Banner, ServicesPath: path, Telemetry: w, Store: st, Favicon: fav})`, `rand` being the one guarded reader over `p.Rand` and `stderr` the one guarded writer over `p.Stderr`; sends `READY=1`, calls `w.Ready()`, and runs `err = server.Serve(ctx, ln, h, drain, stop)` with `stop` calling `w.Shutdown(c, context.Cause(ctx).Error())`; it answers a `*server.DrainError` with `home: ` and its text, exit 1, closes `d`, and returns.

A test of `Run` builds a `cli.Process` with a temporary `Dir`, a `Now` and a `Rand` it controls, an `HTTP` whose transport is the test's own (it answers every request itself, records it, and never reaches the network), a `LookupEnv` over a map holding `LISTEN_PID` (the `Pid` it sets), `LISTEN_FDS` `1` and a `NOTIFY_SOCKET` it binds in a short temporary directory, an `Inherit` returning a Unix listener it bound, a `Version` of its own, a `*telemetry.Capture` (or a sink that fails or blocks) as `Sink`, and a `Banner` closure of its own. It runs `cli.Run` in a goroutine with a context from `context.WithCancelCause`, waits for `READY=1`, drives the listener with raw HTTP (`GET /` with and without `X-User-Id`, `GET /bookmarks`, a form-encoded `POST /bookmarks`), cancels with `errors.New("SIGTERM")`, and inspects the exit code, `Dir`, the capture in order, the requests its transport saw and the streams. A database test makes `Dir`'s `state` a regular file, or writes a `state/home.db` that is not a database, or prepares the database with its own `db.Open` over `home.Migrations()` and a later migration of its own (a `fstest.MapFS` holding both) and adds a bookmark through a store of its own; it compares what `Run` writes with what its own `db.Open` call writes, and reads the database once `Run` has returned with `db.Status` and a store of its own. A refused-start test hands `Run` an empty `Dir`, a `Rand` that counts its reads and an `HTTP` that counts its requests, and finds all three untouched. A drain test sets `DRAIN_SECONDS` to `1`, holds a request open with a client that sends a request whose declared `Content-Length` exceeds the bytes it sends, and finds `home: stopped with 1 request unfinished` last on `Stderr` within about two seconds. A test of `server.Serve` serves a handler of its own on a Unix listener, cancels, and checks the drain, the `stop` call and the `*server.DrainError`. A test of the handler builds its own writer with `telemetry.New` over a `*telemetry.Capture`, its own store with `bookmarks.New` over a `db.Open` in a temporary directory, and its own fetcher with `favicon.New` over an `HTTP` of its own, builds `pages.Handler`, drives it with `httptest`, calls `Writer.Flush`, and asserts on the events by name, attributes, request id and user.
