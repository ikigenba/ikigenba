# D05-scheduler

Time: `internal/scheduler`, which knows each active trigger's next slot, waits for the earliest of them on the injected timer, fires the triggers whose slot has come, makes every change a tool asks of a trigger, and stops. `D01-layout-and-run-seam` declares `cli.Process`, whose `Now`, `After` and `Rand` reach the scheduler through `cli.Run`, and says why the timer is a seam; `D03-serve` builds the scheduler once the store is open, before cron reports ready, and stops it at the signal; `D04-store` declares the store, the `Trigger` and `Draft` records, the `when` rule and the errors this package passes on; `D07-trail` declares `trail.Lifecycle`, `trail.Fire`, `trail.Name` and the kinds they take, which say what a change and a fire record in the trail and emit on the bus; `D08-tools` declares what each tool checks before it calls the scheduler and what it answers. This document declares every exported name of `internal/scheduler` and states what each does.

## Slots and the next slot

A trigger's **slots** are the moments its `when` names, read in UTC, to the minute. `NextSlot` is the one rule that finds them: the first slot strictly after a given time, found by the standard parser of `github.com/robfig/cron/v3` and its schedule's `Next`, always handed a UTC time, since that parser reads a schedule in the location of the time it is given. A `when` the parser accepts but that names no moment that will come, such as `0 0 30 2 *`, has no next slot: the parser's `Next` answers the zero time for it, and `NextSlot` answers false. Such a trigger is kept, listed and shown, and never fires.

A trigger's **next** is the slot the scheduler will fire it for, which the tools answer as `next` and the landing page shows; `Next` reads it. An active trigger's next is worked out **from** a moment: it is the first slot after that moment, or after the trigger's `last_fired` when that is later, so a trigger never fires a slot it already fired, even under a clock that was set back or a database restored from the future. A paused trigger has no next. The scheduler keeps the next slots in memory, not in the database: each start works them out afresh from the moment it starts, which is how the slots that passed while no cron ran are gone and why a restored `last_fired` older than the real last fire makes nothing fire again.

## Waiting and firing

The scheduler waits on the timer seam: it calls `After` with the time until the earliest next slot, but never more than a minute, and wakes when the channel of its most recent call delivers. The minute is a bound on lateness, not a tick anyone can see: a timer in Go is measured on the monotonic clock, which on Linux does not count time the host spends suspended, so a single wait for a slot weeks away could end weeks late after a suspend; waking at least once a minute means a slot reached late is fired within about a minute of the host running again, as the late-fire story asks. A test learns each wait from its own `After`, moves its own `Now`, and delivers on the channel; nothing waits in real time. Between the return of `Start` or a change and the scheduler's next wake, it makes no new call of `After`, so the channel the test saw last is the one it is waiting on.

At a **wake** the scheduler reads the clock and fires every active trigger whose next slot has come. It fires each one once, for the latest of its slots that has come: the slot it was waiting for when only that one has come, late or not, and the latest of several when the machine was away long enough for more than one to come, the earlier ones never firing. It sets the trigger's `last_fired` to that slot and its next to the slot after it, which is the first slot after the wake. A wake at which nothing has come — a timer that delivered before the clock reached any slot — fires nothing and changes nothing; it only waits again.

A fire runs as the trigger's owner and is no request: its context carries an `identity.Caller` with the owner's user id and email and a request id of its own, 32 lowercase hexadecimal digits from 16 bytes of `Rand`, and no event cause, so its bus event has an empty cause and depth 0. What a fire records in the trail and emits on the bus is exactly what `trail.Fire` records and emits (`D07-trail`); the scheduler's part is when it calls it, as whom, and for which slot. Two triggers due together each fire once, each with its own request id, in an order that is not fixed.

A fire is all or nothing. Its `last_fired` is written before its events go out, and the events cannot fail once queued, so a fire whose write fails — a database the scheduler cannot reach, or a random source that fails — has not happened: nothing is recorded or emitted for it, `last_fired` and the next slot stay as they were, and the scheduler waits a full minute before it tries again, so a database that is down is not hammered. When a later wake finds the database back, the trigger fires as any late trigger does, for the latest of its slots that has come, so a long outage costs every slot but the last, exactly as a suspended host does. No line is written: the scheduler has no stream, and a database that is down is answered to tools and pages, where it is seen.

## Changes

The tools change a trigger only through the scheduler, never the store directly, so every change and every fire happen one at a time and in an order the trail and the bus agree with: once a pause or a delete has returned, no fire of that trigger follows its `paused` or `deleted` event, and once an update has returned, no fire carries the old schedule. `Update`, `Pause`, `Resume` and `Delete` name a trigger by slug and act only for its owner: another owner's trigger is not found, exactly like a slug no trigger has. Each answers the trigger as it is after the call (`Delete`, as it was), records and emits under the caller's context through `trail.Lifecycle` when it changes the trigger's existence or status, and changes nothing, records nothing and keeps the next slot when it fails.

- `Create` stores the draft and works out its next from the moment of the call, strictly after it: a trigger created two seconds before `09:45` fires at `09:45`, one created at `09:45:00` exactly first at `10:00`. It records and emits `created`.
- `Update` with a `when` byte-identical to the current one changes nothing and keeps the next slot. A different `when` is stored and, for an active trigger, its next is worked out afresh from the moment of the call; a paused trigger stays paused with no next. `Update` records and emits nothing.
- `Pause` of an active trigger pauses it, takes away its next and emits `paused`; of a paused one it changes nothing and emits nothing.
- `Resume` of a paused trigger makes it active, works out its next from the moment of the call, so the slots that passed while it was paused never fire, and emits `resumed`; of an active one it changes nothing, keeps the next slot and emits nothing.
- `Delete` removes the trigger, which then never fires again, and emits `deleted` with the `when` it had.

## Stopping

`Stop` is the end of firing, called by `D03-serve` the moment cron is told to stop: once it has returned no fire begins, a fire already under way has finished with its events emitted, and the scheduler calls `After` no more. Changes still work after it — a `create` accepted before the signal finishes during the drain and emits its `created` — but nothing fires again in that process. A stop changes no trigger: each keeps its schedule, its status and its `last_fired`.

## Recorded decisions

- The scheduler holds no copy of the triggers but their next slots; the store is the record, read at `Start` and on each change and fire.
- The wait is capped at one minute (see above). The cap is observable at `After`, and a test that delivers on every channel sees a wake at least once a minute of its clock; that is the price of bounded lateness after a host suspend, judged worth it.
- A fire whose store write fails is retried at a later wake rather than emitted unrecorded, so that every fire on the bus has its `last_fired`; the stories do not cover a database failing at a slot, so this is a decision of this design, open to the root.
- `Start`'s context bounds only its first read; the scheduler runs until `Stop`.
- Precedence when a change meets several conditions: a done context, then a failing store, then a trigger not found (unknown slug or another owner's), then an invalid `when`. This matches the order in which the tools check (`D08-tools`), so no consumer can tell the difference.

## Consumer tasks

`cli.Run` (`D03-serve`) calls `sch, err := scheduler.Start(ctx, scheduler.Config{Store: st, Events: em, Telemetry: w, Now: p.Now, After: p.After, Rand: rand})` once the store is open and before it reports ready, hands `sch` to `web.Config.Scheduler`, and calls `sch.Stop()` the moment its context is done. The tools (`D08-tools`) call `sch.Create(ctx, store.Draft{Slug: slug, When: when, OwnerID: u.UserID, OwnerEmail: u.Email})`, `sch.Update(ctx, u.UserID, slug, when)`, `sch.Pause(ctx, u.UserID, slug)`, `sch.Resume(ctx, u.UserID, slug)` and `sch.Delete(ctx, u.UserID, slug)` with the request's context, and answer `next` from `sch.Next(t.ID)`; the landing page (`D06-pages`) shows `sch.Next(t.ID)` for each row.

A test opens its own database with `db.Open` and `cron.Migrations()`, builds `store.New` over it with a clock it controls, creates the stories' triggers through the store, builds a `telemetry.New` writer over a `*telemetry.Capture` and an `events.New` emitter over an `*events.Capture` with `trail.Emits()`, and calls `scheduler.Start` with its clock and an `After` that records each call's duration and returns a buffered channel the test keeps. It reads `Next`, moves its clock, delivers on the most recent channel, waits for the next `After` call (which follows every wake) with a deadline that fails the test, flushes the writer and the emitter, and inspects the captures, `store.Get` and `Next`. It calls the five changes with an `identity.NewContext` of its own, calls `d.SetFailing(true)` on the handle before a wake to see a fire not happen and `SetFailing(false)` to see it happen at the next, calls `Stop` and delivers again to see nothing fire, and compares `scheduler.NextSlot` with the stories' slots.
