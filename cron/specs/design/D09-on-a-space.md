# D09-on-a-space

What cron itself contributes when it runs on a space and in a sandbox, and what it does not. The path from a checkout to a browser, curl and an agent on a space or in a sandbox is long, and almost every step on it belongs to someone else: devctl builds and ships the file, opsctl installs it, writes the environment, publishes the socket, bounds the unit, keeps and replicates the database and generates nginx, auth decides every credential and whether a request reaches cron at all, the host's nginx terminates TLS, sends a guest to sign in, challenges `/mcp` and answers cron's fragment, the mcp gateway relays tool calls, the events app takes, stores and delivers the bus events cron emits, scripts runs what subscribes to them, and the sandbox stands the same arrangement up on a developer's machine. cron's own share is what the earlier designs already state — the layout, the run seam, the paths, the display string and the binary's wiring (`D01-layout-and-run-seam`), the command, the manifest, the nginx fragment and the two files under `etc/` (`D02-cli`), the serve path, the event bus's two paths and the quiet standard error (`D03-serve`), the store (`D04-store`), the firing (`D05-scheduler`), the pages (`D06-pages`), the trail and the bus events (`D07-trail`) and the tools (`D08-tools`) — plus one guarantee a space and a sandbox lean on that no single earlier document owns: neither the credential a caller presents nor the caller's email lands in anything cron records, and the credential lands nowhere cron keeps or writes. This design states that guarantee; for every other outcome on a space or in a sandbox it names the owner, and it says plainly what can be checked only on a running space.

This design declares no name. Everything it states is behaviour of names `D01-layout-and-run-seam` declares: `cli.Run` and `cli.Process`.

## What ships

The release file has four members: `bin/cron`, `etc/manifest.toml`, `etc/nginx.conf` and `share/icon.svg`. Two of those are cron's contract and are already designed: the manifest's and the fragment's exact texts are the constants `cli.Manifest` and `cli.NginxConf` (`D02-cli`, R-TA28-URWT, R-8V5V-M0IX), the root package's `Etc` holds exactly `manifest.toml` and `nginx.conf` (`D01-layout-and-run-seam`, R-7PPA-LTBB, R-7QX6-ZL20), and their contents are exactly those constants (`D02-cli`, R-901H-53HP, R-919D-IV8E); together those prove "nothing under `etc/` but the manifest and the fragment" and that the committed manifest is byte for byte what `cron manifest` prints, which is what `devctl build` checks. The manifest's own lines carry the rest of what a space asks of it: `guests = false`, so the host's nginx admits no visitor without a credential; `mcp = true`, so the host's services file marks cron for the gateway; no `[env]`, since cron has no settings of its own and `DRAIN_SECONDS` is the space's; `[database]` naming `state/cron.db`, which the host keeps and replicates; and `[resources]` holding only `memory_max = "128M"` (`D02-cli`, R-TA28-URWT, with its description tied to the about screen's by R-8YTK-RBR0).

That the binary in the file, asked `--version` with neither `IKIGENBA_COMMIT` nor `IKIGENBA_RELEASE` set, prints one empty line — it carries no identity of its own, and the commit in the file's name is nowhere inside it — is `D02-cli`'s `--version` (R-92H9-WMZ3) over the display string `D01-layout-and-run-seam` defines and the binary's wiring (R-8FB6-MZVW, R-8GJ3-0RML); that the binary prints the manifest byte for byte is `D02-cli`'s R-93P6-AEPS through the same wiring. That the binary is static `linux/amd64` and cgo-free is the release build gate of `AGENTS.md`, not a requirement. That no `assets/` directory or font ships beside the binary follows from the templates being embedded (`D01-layout-and-run-seam`, R-7N9H-U9TX, R-7OHE-81KM) and appkit's shared files being served from inside it (`D06-pages`). That the database is not in the file, and that cron creates `state/cron.db` under its working directory on its first start, is `D01-layout-and-run-seam`'s (R-86RV-YLP1, R-8IYV-SB3Z) and `D03-serve`'s (R-ITDK-3AEO).

The rest of the file is not cron's to state. The tarball, its member list and its name `cron-<sha>.tar.xz` carrying the full sha of the clean commit it was built from, with no tag read, are `devctl build`'s; `share/icon.svg` is human-authored (`AGENTS.md`), never written by the build run and never opened by a test, and that its presence lists cron in the launcher is opsctl's services file. No test reads the checkout, so the tarball and the icon are outside what a requirement can state; a developer checks them with `tar`.

## The credential

On a space every request cron receives has been admitted by auth, and the request that reaches cron may still carry the client's `Authorization` header; whether and how the host's nginx passes it on is opsctl's, and cron does not rely on either answer. cron treats every credential form alike, and the guarantees are proved for a `Bearer` and, defensively, a `Basic` one. cron never needs the header — the caller is appkit identity's `identity.Caller`, from the identity headers nginx sets — and a space and a sandbox rely on the token's secret being in no record any of them leaves and on no event holding the owner's email. The trail and the bus events are cron's to keep clean, and so is everything cron writes to standard error and keeps on disk; nginx's logs are opsctl's.

A request carries a **credential header** when its `Authorization` header is `Bearer <p>`, or `Basic ` and the standard base64 of `<u>:<p>`, the test choosing `<u>` and `<p>` long, with a letter other than `a` to `f` in every 8-character stretch of each and of their base64 value so that no hexadecimal id or run of digits can match them, and occurring nowhere else in what it sends. Its **secret strings** are every run of 8 or more consecutive characters of `<p>`, of `<u>` and of the base64 value, so a truncated or partial leak counts as a leak, and every comparison ignores ASCII case, so a lowercased or uppercased copy counts too. The caller's email is marked the same way: an `X-User-Email` value of letters and digits around one `@`, whose every run of 8 or more characters is an **email string** (R-HHUN-PFL8). A secret written in another encoding than a change of case is outside what these tests can detect; the guarantees are about the credential's and the email's own characters. A chosen value could collide with what cron writes whatever the credential — `finished`, `additive`, `undelivered`, a column name in the database — so a run counts only when its values are **admissible**: none of their strings is held by anything the test's reference run writes or names on disk. The reference run is the same sequence made before it with the same clock, timer, random source, environment, user id and slugs, but with no `Authorization` header at all and a marked email that shares no such string (R-HJ2K-37BX). A correct cron writes nothing that depends on the credential, so a test that checks admissibility, and picks other values when it fails, always finds a run the guarantees speak about, and a cron that leaks still fails.

The guarantees are proved over one sequence of requests, the **credential sequence** (R-HJ2K-37BX), made once with every request carrying a `Bearer` credential and once with every request carrying a `Basic` one. It reaches every kind of route cron serves — the landing page, the about screen, an appkit path, a path that is no page, the event bus's two paths, and requests with no `X-User-Id` that cron refuses with appkit's 500 — and every tool, a refused call among them, and it makes the trigger it creates fire once, so the fire's events, which run as the owner and carry the owner's identity on their context (`D05-scheduler`), are inside it. Four guarantees follow. First, no trail event — name, envelope or attribute — holds a secret string or an email string (R-Q84O-CC6Z). Second, no bus event does either, its id and cause included (R-Q9CK-Q3XO). Each event's exact keys are already fixed by the documents that own them (`D03-serve` for the request pair and the service events, `D07-trail` for every trigger event, appkit's MCP server for `tool.called`), the envelope's user is the caller's `X-User-Id` (`D03-serve`, R-JSQN-6MWT), so these are the end-to-end statement of what those already imply, proved through `Run` with capturing sinks. Third, nothing `Run` writes to `Stderr` holds one, including the lines appkit's writer writes for a trail event it could not deliver and the lines appkit's emitter writes for a bus event it dropped (R-QBSD-HNF2). Fourth, nothing cron leaves on disk holds a secret string: after the sequence, no file under the test's temporary directory that holds `Dir` — the working directory, the database and its SQLite sidecar files included, and anything beside `Dir` — holds one unless it is byte for byte as it was before `Run` was called, and no entry there that the run made holds one in its path (R-HKAG-GZ2M). `D01-layout-and-run-seam`'s R-87ZS-CDFQ keeps everything outside `Dir` unchanged for a run without a credential; this guarantee extends the check to the credential sequence, so a defect that writes beside `Dir` only when an `Authorization` header is present is caught. The disk guarantee is about the credential only: cron keeps the owner's email in its database by design (`D04-store`) and answers it in tool results and on the landing page (`D08-tools`, `D06-pages`), by design; nothing asks that an email stay out of a response, and the responses are no record.

That cron sets no cookie is already `D03-serve`'s (R-KC91-AYRX).

## In a sandbox

In a sandbox cron is the same binary in the same arrangement: the sandbox builds it from the worktree, runs it under its own units with a working directory of its own (`<data>/apps/cron`), gives it the environment of a host — `DRAIN_SECONDS` and `IKIGENBA_SERVICES` — with `IKIGENBA_COMMIT` set to the worktree's commit, `-dirty` appended when the tree has changes, no `IKIGENBA_RELEASE`, and variables a host never sets, puts its own nginx in front with cron's `etc/nginx.conf` from the checkout included in cron's server, and routes every request through auth's `/check` because the manifest has `guests = false`. All of that is the sandbox's. cron's contributions are the ones it makes on a space:

- It serves under the sandbox like any app; its database is `state/cron.db` under its working directory (`D01-layout-and-run-seam`, R-86RV-YLP1), so it is created on the first `up` (`D03-serve`, R-ITDK-3AEO) and survives `down` and `up` because the sandbox keeps that directory until `wipe`.
- It reads nothing from its environment beyond what a host gives it, so the variables only the sandbox sets change nothing it does: every key `Run` looks up is one of `D01-layout-and-run-seam`'s five (R-897O-Q56F). `IKIGENBA_COMMIT` and `IKIGENBA_RELEASE` are read by `main` for the display string and never through `LookupEnv` (R-8FB6-MZVW, R-8GJ3-0RML), and appkit's constructors and socket sinks read `IKIGENBA_SERVICES` from the real environment by appkit's published design.
- Its footer, its about screen, its MCP `serverInfo` and its `service.started` carry the display string `main` builds from `IKIGENBA_COMMIT` and `IKIGENBA_RELEASE` (`D01-layout-and-run-seam`, R-8FB6-MZVW, R-3NY2-4C7B, R-3P5Y-I3Y0, R-8MMK-XMC2, R-PD6S-T4SE, R-8RI6-GPAU), which under the sandbox is the worktree's short commit with `-dirty` after it when the tree had changes; the shortening is appkit's `version.Display`, and the value it shortens is the sandbox's.
- It emits to the events app the sandbox's services file names, through appkit's socket sink (`D01-layout-and-run-seam`, R-8CVD-VGEI): `cron.tick.created`, `paused`, `resumed` and `deleted` under the gateway request's id and the caller (`D07-trail`, `D03-serve` R-JSQN-6MWT), and one `cron.tick.fired` per minute slot under a request id of its own, as the owner, with no cause and depth 0 (`D05-scheduler`, R-HJ1D-K08A; `D07-trail`), none for a slot that passed while the trigger was paused (`D05-scheduler`, R-HK99-XRYZ, R-HFDO-EP07) and none after the delete (R-HFDO-EP07), in the order the tools and the slots made them (`D07-trail`).
- The same credential and email guarantees hold; `sandbox logs cron` showing no line is `D03-serve`'s quiet standard error (R-3XP9-6I4V).

The landing page, the gateway's `create`, `list`, `pause`, `resume` and `delete`, and the two bus paths in a sandbox are the outcomes on a space again, with the sandbox in place of opsctl and the host's nginx, and the same owners. The script `on-tick`, its runs, their `input.json` with the events app's `seq` and `received`, repos' `tick-watch`, events' `search`, and `sandbox token` are the sandbox's and the siblings'; cron's part of each is the bus event it handed its sink.

## What only a space can show

These outcomes are space-level checks: no test of cron can make them, and they are verified on a running space or sandbox. They are recorded here, not required.

- The release file holds exactly `bin/cron`, `etc/manifest.toml`, `etc/nginx.conf` and `share/icon.svg`, the binary is a static `linux/amd64` executable, the icon is an SVG, the commit is in the file's name and nowhere in a member or the binary (devctl; cron's part is `Etc`, the constants and `--version`, above).
- A guest is sent to sign in, a refused credential is answered `403`, and a `POST /mcp` with no credential is answered `401` with the bearer challenge and nginx's one-line body, in each case nothing reaching cron's socket and cron recording no event (nginx and auth; in a sandbox, the sandbox's nginx).
- auth's own records of each request (`check.allowed`, `check.refused`) and their sharing a request id with cron's events (auth, nginx).
- The host's nginx log holds no token: its format is opsctl's, and cron neither writes it nor affects it.
- The answers arrive over HTTP/2 and TLS on a space and over HTTP/1.1 in a sandbox, with `X-Forwarded-Proto` `https` or `http` (nginx).
- The host's and the sandbox's nginx include cron's `etc/nginx.conf` in its server, so `/events` and `/declarations` answer 404 at the public name for every method, with or without a credential, and nothing of such a request reaches cron's socket. cron's part is the fragment's text (`D02-cli`). Observed while designing with a real nginx: the fragment's two lines included in a server holding a prefix location `/` and a regex location answered `GET`, `POST`, `PUT`, `DELETE` and `HEAD` of `/events` and `/declarations`, and `/events?x=1`, 404, and passed `/events/`, `/declarations/`, `/eventsx`, `/Events`, `/mcp` and `/` on.
- The gateway offers cron's seven tools with their kinds, runs `create`, `update`, `pause`, `resume` and `delete` through `mutate` and `list` and `show` through `call`, forwards the caller and the request id, and relays cron's results under its own `serverInfo` (mcp).
- The events app delivers cron's bus events to scripts, which runs `on-tick` once per fire with the event as its input; events' `search` finds them (events, scripts, repos, the sandbox).
- The 128 MiB ceiling, the database's replication, and the restart a deploy makes (opsctl, the kernel, litestream).
- The launcher a browser opens lists cron with its icon, and the page's style, button-feedback script and favicon are fetched from cron's own host and from no other origin (appkit's scripts; no test runs them).

## REQUIREMENTS

- R-HHUN-PFL8: cron's design defines a request as carrying a **credential header** when it has exactly one `Authorization` header whose value is either `Bearer <p>`, or `Basic ` followed by the standard base64 encoding (RFC 4648, with padding) of `<u>:<p>`, where `<u>` and `<p>` are each at least 16 ASCII letters and digits, and defines that request's **secret strings** as every substring of 8 or more characters of `<p>`, of `<u>`, and of that base64 value; defines a request's **marked email** as its one `X-User-Email` value when that value is at least 16 ASCII letters and digits with exactly one `@` inserted, not first or last, and that request's **email strings** as every substring of 8 or more characters of its marked email; requires that every secret string and every email string hold at least one ASCII letter that is none of `a` to `f` and `A` to `F`; defines that a text **holds** a secret string or an email string when the string occurs in the text with ASCII letters compared case-insensitively; and requires that no secret string and no email string occur, compared that way, anywhere else in what the test hands cron — no other header, URL path or query, request body or tool argument of any request in the same test, and no slug, schedule, user id or fixture it places in its temporary directory; every requirement of cron's design that names a credential header, a marked email, their secret strings or email strings, or a text holding one MUST denote that.
