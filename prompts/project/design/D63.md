# Decision 63 — Queue observability: the completion queue's state is visible without opening the database

## Decision

The completion queue is the one part of prompts whose failure is **silent by construction**: a consumer that submits work and collects nothing cannot tell a slow model from a wedged queue, and neither can the operator. D29 removes the mechanisms that produced silent wedges, but it cannot make the queue's state *legible* — that requires a surface. The queue therefore reports its own depth beside the service's existing health.

The surface is the chassis **health** response, extended with one `completions` object:

```json
{
  "completions": {
    "queued": 3,
    "running": 1,
    "terminal": 12,
    "oldest_queued_age_seconds": 41,
    "reclaimed_items": 0
  }
}
```

Every field is a **derived query** over the existing `completions` table, served at request time:

- the status counts and `oldest_queued_age_seconds` come from the `completions_status_created` index;
- `reclaimed_items` counts rows whose `reclaims` column is non-zero.

**No new counter storage exists** — no counter column, no metrics table, no in-process accumulator. A running tally would have to be persisted, kept correct across restarts, and reconciled after a crash, all to report something the rows already know.

`reclaimed_items` is the field that earns the surface. D29 accepts that a reclaim may duplicate provider spend, and records the truth of that in two places that disagree by design: `calls` holds a row per executed round trip (both executions), while the item's own `cost_usd` reflects only its winning execution. Exposing the reclaim count is what makes that divergence **attributable rather than invisible** — an operator who sees non-zero reclaims knows to trust `calls` over the item, and knows something abandoned work.

The queue store gains one read-only method returning the snapshot; the composition root wires it into the health payload. The store method is the seam — the health handler holds no SQL.

Because health is an **externally reachable surface**, its proof runs through the composition root: the real binary, `serve` on a loopback port, the actual health route. A test that constructs the handler and calls it directly would prove the query and not the exposure.

## Rejected

**A separate metrics or status endpoint.** Health is already the box's "is this service working" surface and is already reached by the operator's tooling. A second endpoint would be a second thing to remember, a second thing to route, and a second thing to forget to look at.

**A persisted reclaim counter (a counter column, a metrics table, or an in-process accumulator).** It buys a cheaper query at the cost of state that must survive restarts and be reconciled after a crash — durability work in service of a number that a `COUNT` over an indexed column already answers correctly by construction.

**Per-consumer breakdown of the counts.** The queue is small and one-box; a total tells the operator whether the queue is moving, which is the question. A breakdown is easy to add later against the same index if a second heavy consumer ever makes it a real question.

**An MCP tool for queue state.** `/mcp` is the product surface for agents (D29); queue depth is operator plumbing. The `calls` and `usage` tools (D32) already cover the agent-facing question of what inference happened.

**Alerting or thresholds on the counts.** prompts reports; it does not decide what depth is alarming. No spend budgets or alerting is an existing product scope boundary and this Decision does not cross it.

## Verification

- R-07YK-W9KU — The assembled binary's health response carries the `completions` object with the queued/running/terminal counts and `oldest_queued_age_seconds`, and the counts track the queue: after seeding a known mix of items through the real surface, each count equals the number of rows in that state, and `oldest_queued_age_seconds` reflects the oldest `queued` item's age rather than the newest (injected clock; proven through `cmd/prompts` serving on a loopback port, never by constructing the handler).
- R-15VT-PVYT — `reclaimed_items` counts exactly the items whose lease was reclaimed: it is 0 for a queue whose items were only claimed and released gracefully, and becomes non-zero after an item's lease expires and is reclaimed, without any counter being persisted (the value is recomputed from the rows after a restart).
